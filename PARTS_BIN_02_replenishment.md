FIELDTAG (thermal media for installed printer fleets), FITFLEET 365 (respirator fit-test kit rental) —
supplied explicitly as COMPONENT DONORS, not candidates.**

## G — Parts from the replenishment models

### G1 · THE INSTALLED-BASE ANNUITY — attach to an object someone else already sold

> *"EdgeLoop turns every approved industrial cutting component inside a factory into a digitally
> identified, automatically replenished physical-goods annuity."*

Razor-and-blades, with one crucial refinement: **you do not sell the razor. Someone else already did. You
attach to it afterward.**

**Why this is new here:** every candidate in this file starts from *find a buyer.* This starts from **find
an installed object that has already committed its owner to future purchases.** The commitment exists
before the seller arrives.

### G2 · THE GOLDEN PASSPORT — expensive once, free thereafter ★

> *"The first transaction is technical. Transactions two through fifty should be nearly lights-out."*
> *"Perform expensive identification once; collect recurring product contribution many times."*

A permanent record — customer, machine, specification, drawing version, **SHA-256 hash**, manufacturer
approval, customer approval, first-article result, commercial terms, and **operating history**. Once it is
Green, an ordinary repeat requires **no technical rediscovery**.

**This is the sharpest single mechanism in all three documents, and it is the direct answer to her one
failing key.** The book costs four hours *every time.* A Passport structure makes the **first** one
expensive and every subsequent one nearly free.

**Applied to candidate 70:** qualify a **funeral home** once — their voice, their format, their package
structure, their intake form, their turnaround, their approval path. **Every family after that is a
lights-out repeat.** The expensive step happens once per *partner*, not once per *order*.

### G3 · THE TRIGGER GRAPH — hunt the event that CREATES the purchase ★

> *"FieldTag does not merely search for 'label bids.' It searches for every event that creates a future
> thermal-media account."*

The example is the whole idea: a 2025 Iowa Department of Public Safety purchase bundled a mobile printer
with **a $6.550 pallet of thermal paper.** The printer was a few hundred dollars. **The consumable dwarfed
the hardware, and the hardware purchase announced it in advance, publicly.**

> **THE LEADING-INDICATOR RULE — do not hunt the purchase. Hunt the observable event that COMMITS someone
> to a future recurring purchase, and arrive before the need does.**

A printer deployment creates media demand. A machine install creates blade demand. **We have never once
searched this way** — every list source in this file has been a list of *buyers*, never a list of
*commitments already made*.

### G4 · THE WEAR CLOCK — a moat built from accumulated transaction history

Consumption is **inferred**, not instrumented: *"purchase history, reorder spacing, production schedules,
maintenance emails, historical run hours, expected blade life, seasonal demand."* Over time it learns
*"Plant A processing HDPE normally consumes one approved blade set every 47 days."*

> *"Manufacturers know how to make blades. Customers know their machines. EdgeLoop gradually knows which
> exact part, from which manufacturer, lasts longest in each machine and application — and when the next
> one should ship."*

**A moat neither the supplier nor the buyer can build, made only of your own transaction history.** It
does not exist on day one and cannot be bought. It compounds.

### G5 · THE INVISIBLE DEPARTMENT — now triple-confirmed

> EdgeLoop: *"You own the machine relationship. We become your invisible, white-label blade supply
> department."*
> FieldTag: *"You deploy and support the printer fleet. FieldTag invisibly operates the entire media
> lifecycle under your brand or ours."*
> Full-Circle (funeral aftercare, found independently by agent 6): *"We do the hard work. You get the
> credit."*

**Three unrelated industries, one sentence.** Parts bin D3/D4 said partners want inventory, not
commissions. This is the *pitch* for it, and it is now validated across three sectors. **Use this wording.**

### G6 · THE FREE RECONSTRUCTION — give away the inventory of their own situation ★

> *"Free Blade-Room Reconstruction."* The plant uploads photographs, box labels, machine nameplates, old
> invoices, parts lists. It receives back a **machine-by-machine blade register, missing-information list,
> likely cross-references, duplicate SKUs, annual usage estimate, replacement calendar, sample-qualification
> plan.** *"EdgeLoop earns only when the customer orders approved replacements."*

The free thing is **an organised map of what they already own** — which they do not have, cannot easily
build, and which is simultaneously the sales qualification, the product specification and the lock-in.

**AND IT REFINES THE INVERSION CHECK, which I have been applying too bluntly.** I killed PERMITCLOSE
because *"the information is on the buyer's side of the table."* That was right there — a deposit is one
cheque and one receipt. It is **wrong** where the buyer owns two hundred machines and forty years of
invoices in a filing cabinet.

> **THE INVERSION CHECK, CORRECTED — the test is not "does the buyer possess the raw data." It is
> "CAN THE BUYER PRODUCE THE ANSWER IN AN AFTERNOON?" Possessing data and being able to use it are
> different things, and the gap between them is a real, sellable arbitrage.**

### G7 · THE ROUTER POSITION — the Stamp Law solved structurally, not by hiring ★★

> *"Grok may not independently decide: blade material, hardness, heat treatment, bevel angle, tooth
> geometry, coating, rotational-speed rating, permitted tolerances… Those decisions belong to the approved
> blade manufacturer and the customer's qualified technical representative. The AI is the information,
> orchestration, and replenishment system. It is not the metallurgical engineer."*

**PERMITCLOSE hired the stamp. STONEPROOF hired the stamp. EdgeLoop routes to someone who already holds
it and never touches it.**

> **THE ROUTER POSITION — where a business requires a stamp you cannot hold, you can stand between the
> buyer and someone who already holds it, provided you never make the stamped decision yourself. You are
> paid for knowing WHICH, never for certifying WHAT.**

**And this reopens something the file closed.** THE STAMP LAW §5b killed selling introductions into
licensed professions on referral-fee rules — AICPA, ABA Rule 7.2(b), Cal. B&P § 5061, 19 CFR 111.36(b),
ISO 17065 4.2.3. **Every one of those regulates a FEE FOR AN INTRODUCTION.**

> **None of them regulates RESELLING.** EdgeLoop is the **merchant of record** and earns an embedded resale
> margin on a physical good. There is no referral fee, so there is no referral-fee rule. **The escape from
> §5b is to take the transaction rather than to broker it.**

*(Recorded as a mechanism to test, not as a settled legal conclusion. It would need checking in any
specific regulated vertical before it is relied on.)*

### G8 · GIVE AWAY THE SOFTWARE, MONETISE THE GOODS

> *"EdgeLoop is the merchant of record. It does not charge the customer for software… The customer
> receives Blade Passport, reorder portal, QR tracking, inventory alerts, order history, performance
> record. These are included because they make EdgeLoop's physical product relationship harder to replace.
> The money stays attached to the product transaction."*

**The software is the retention mechanism, not the revenue.** The exact inverse of every SaaS candidate in
this file, and it sidesteps the $5–50/month price anchor that killed most of them.

### G9 · THE REGULATORY REBOOKING CLOCK ★

> FITFLEET: *"OSHA mandates annual fit-testing, turning every customer into an automatic annual rebooking."*

**A statute that forces a repeat purchase on a fixed calendar.** This is the recurring mechanic this file
has failed to find in seventy candidates — and note that agent 11 independently identified the *only*
recurring hook in the entire compliance family as **"forced re-documentation on annual audit cycles and
scheme-edition changes."**

**Two independent sources, one conclusion: where recurrence exists in a document or service business, a
statute usually put it there.**

### G10 · THE EXPERT BUYER — never be believed as an expert again ★★

> FITFLEET: *"Target audience: service providers, EHS consultants, and enterprise safety teams **who
> already know how to operate the machines** and only need temporary surge capacity."*

> **THE EXPERT-BUYER RULE — sell capacity, throughput or supply to a buyer who is ALREADY the expert.
> Then you are never required to be believed as one.**

**This directly dissolves her hardest veto** — *"anything requiring her to be believed as an expert."* It
is not worked around; it is designed out. The buyer supplies the credibility; you supply the capacity.

**And it is the same shape as candidate 70's channel:** the funeral director is the expert and holds the
family's trust. She supplies capacity behind them.

### G11 · RENT BEFORE YOU BUY — validate on borrowed capacity

> *"Validate demand first with partner/sub-rented inventory (aiming for 10 paid bookings in 30 days)
> before financing equipment at ~$16K/unit."*

**Prove demand on someone else's asset before acquiring one.** Generalises well beyond hardware: borrow
the capability, sell it, and only then build it.

### G12 · REFUSING CUSTODY AS A PRODUCT FEATURE

> *"Strictly zero medical evaluations, testing administration, or worker-data retention. Tablets are wiped
> clean after every rental."*

They **designed custody out deliberately, to kill liability** — and then sold that as a feature.

**C4 stops being a limitation and becomes positioning.** *"We never hold your credentials, your records or
your data"* is a sales line in any market where the buyer is nervous, which is most of them.

### G13 · GREEN / YELLOW / RED PRODUCT UNIVERSE

Explicit lists: allowed at launch · requires separate qualification · **permanently excluded**. And a named
promotion path — Yellow becomes Green only after engineering review, a controlled drawing, dimensional
inspection, first-article approval, a customer test, and **three clean live production orders**.

**A scope-discipline instrument this file has never had.** Our candidates drift because nothing forbids
drift.

### G14 · THE STANDING AUTHORITY ENVELOPE

FieldTag lists ~17 simultaneous conditions under which a repeat executes with **no human review** —
existing customer, Green Passport, unchanged version, unchanged artwork hash, price at or above floor,
contribution at or above minimum, no overdue receivable, no open incident, cost refreshed, capacity
confirmed, exposure inside limits.

> *"Any failed condition generates an exception rather than an order."*

**A precise operational definition of "automated"** — a bounded envelope with explicit exit conditions,
rather than "AI runs the business."

### G15 · THE THREE-STRIKES RULE ★

> *"When the same manual intervention occurs three times: automate it, outsource it under an SOP, or
> discontinue that transaction type. **There is no permanent fourth option in which the principal handles
> it manually.**"*

**A forcing function for operator leverage**, which is the key her business keeps failing. Every manual
touch gets a reason code; three occurrences trigger a mandatory decision.

### G16 · ZERO-INVENTORY DOCTRINE, as named structures

**Permitted:** build to customer PO · customer-funded production (deposit/prepay) · supplier-held finished
goods under a committed customer programme · supplier-held raw material against contracted usage.
**Prohibited:** buying in anticipation · owning finished custom goods with no committed customer ·
guaranteeing volume without a matching customer obligation · *"we will probably sell it later"* treated as
demand.

The import/export hunt died on inventory risk. **This is the catalogue of inventory-free ways to run a
physical-goods business** — and note that print-on-demand is simply the consumer-scale case of the first
structure.

### G17 · GATE 0 AS TWENTY REAL INSTANCES WITH A COUNT

> *"At least 16 of 20 products can be identified sufficiently to quote. At least 10 can be requoted without
> fresh engineering after initial qualification. At least 8 appear capable of clearing 25% contribution."*

**Test twenty real cases and count how many clear a numeric bar.** Far better validation design than
"get three customers" — it measures the *rate*, which is what predicts everything afterwards.

### G18 · CONCENTRATION LIMITS WITH DATES

Supplier: 60% of COGS permitted at launch → below 45% at twelve months → below 40% mature. Customer:
largest direct below 12%, largest partner below 20%, top five combined below 55%. **Limits that loosen and
tighten on a schedule, rather than a static rule nobody follows.**
