# HUNT — 14 AUG 2026: six transposition probes on the candidate-64 line

**Method: take a mechanic proven at $59–199 in one vertical and find the vertical it never crossed
into. Six probes. Five kills, one real find, and the find has a competitor that shipped five days
ago.**

**Net result: candidate 64 got stronger, because I tried hard to beat it and could not.**

---

## 1. The law this hunt was built on

Candidate 64 survived because of a specific structure, and it is worth stating as a law because it
is the only reliable generator this record has produced:

> **THE TRANSPOSITION LAW — a gap exists where two mature industries sit adjacent and neither has
> crossed into the other, because each is branded, priced and staffed for its own side.**

Pregnancy was empty because **life-story services (StoryWorth, Remento, Memoirji) are branded for
elders and legacy**, while **pregnancy keepsakes are a paper-goods industry selling blank journals.**
Two mature businesses, touching, neither crossing. That is what an exploitable gap looks like.

**Corollary, and it is the filter that did most of the work today:** the deliverable must be
something **a chat window cannot produce** — a physical object, a third party contacted, or custody
held. Any "AI writes text about your own life" idea dies on the chat-window test before research
starts. That single filter killed the wedding-speech and vow-writing branch without a search.

## 2. The five kills

### PROBE 1 — Pet memorial book. **DEAD: saturated.**

[Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/market/pet_memorial_book) carries entire market categories for it, AI-
illustrated pet storybooks already exist there, and
[petventuresbook.com](https://petventuresbook.com/products/pet-memorial-keepsakes-personalized-pet-book)
already sells the **done-for-you** version — *"expert team helps you design, print, and ship."*

Not a gap. A commodity race with better-established sellers.

### PROBE 2 — Family heirloom cookbook from handwritten recipe cards. **DEAD: occupied at both ends.**

- [EverPresent](https://everpresent.com/services/custom-photobook-design/cookbooks-and-recipe-books/)
  runs the genuine done-for-you version with a design team, Wall Street Journal coverage and
  *"thousands of custom design projects."*
- [CreateMyCookbook](https://createmycookbook.com/inspiration/heirloom) already sells **"WeTypeIt"** —
  the exact transcription service that would have been the wedge.
- [ReciScan](https://reciscan.app/) and
  [Family Cookbook Project](https://www.familycookbookproject.com/) hold the DIY end.

Both ends taken, and the middle is the incumbent's upsell.

### PROBE 3 — Multi-contributor tribute book (retirement, milestone birthday, memorial). **DEAD: dense at the exact price point.**

[Kudoboard prints hardbound books](https://www.kudoboard.com/print/) ·
[Memorygram](https://memoirs.memorygram.com/tributes) at **$99 with unlimited storytellers and a
colour hardcover** · [Newlywords](https://www.newlywords.com/) ·
[FromABirdie](https://www.fromabirdie.com/retirement-memory-book) ·
[Tribute.co](https://www.tribute.co/) at **$149 concierge** · Tribute Technology.

Six named competitors straddling $99–149. This is the most crowded space I found all day.

### PROBE 4 — Grandparent's letters and diaries turned into a published book. **DEAD: Law 1 fires on the core capability.**

The enabling technology is free or nearly free: [Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/letters-notes)
gives 50 credits a month free, [Parsli](https://parsli.co/solutions/handwriting-to-text) is
"free to start" at 95%+, [Handwriting OCR](https://www.handwritingocr.com/) claims **under 1% word
error rate**, and [Anderson Archival](https://andersonarchival.com/services/digital-preservation-scanning/preserve-the-past-with-precision-ai-powered-handwritten-text-recognition-services/)
holds the premium hybrid end.

**The honest note against my own kill:** free transcription is her *input cost going to zero*, not
her competitor — the finished object is still unbuilt by anyone. What actually kills it is the
**buyer-finding problem**: there is no way to reach "person who just found a box of letters." No
dated trigger, no community, no search intent. **Filed as SHAPE-dead on channel, not on product.**

### PROBE 5 — Wedding speeches, vows, toasts. **DEAD without a search: chat-window test.**

One user, one account, one moment, no third party, no artifact. A chat window does it free and
instantly. Killed on doctrine in ten seconds, which is what the doctrine is for.

## 3. PROBE 6 — the one real find, and the thing that closes it

### The gap is real and it is enormous

| Tier | What exists | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **DIY** | Export a GEDCOM → [Ancestral Author](https://www.cyndislist.com/writing/publications/) or [Family Book Creator](https://www.familybookcreator.com/en/) → hyperlinked PDF → print it yourself | ~$0, and it produces a *data dump*, not a narrative |
| **THE GAP** | — | **nothing** |
| **Done-for-you** | [Legacy Tree](https://www.legacytree.com/hire-genealogist-family-history-narratives), [The Writers For Hire](https://www.thewritersforhire.com/services/ghostwriting/family-history/) — interviews, research, project management | **from $3.500 for the writing alone**, book billed separately |

A search for the integrated product returned, in as many words: *"there doesn't appear to be a
single integrated service that automatically handles the entire process from GEDCOM to printed
hardcover."*

**A $3.500-to-$0 gap with nothing in it**, and unlike the pregnancy book — which fought a $96 blank-
journal anchor — here the anchor is **$3.500**. She would be twelve times cheaper than the only
done-for-you option in the market.

### The buyer is unusually well-defined

Someone who has spent **years and hundreds of dollars** building a tree and has no way to turn it
into something their family will read. The tree is invisible and dies with them. It is a **known
unfinished project with emotional weight and heavy sunk cost** — and one of the search results is
literally a person asking *"I have been with Ancestry for years and am now ready to write a family
history book. Since Ancestry really doesn't have…"*

### The channel is the best I have seen in 65 candidates

Everything else on this list starts cold. This does not:

- **[r/Genealogy: 206.000 members](https://gummysearch.com/r/Genealogy/)** who *"frequently
  recommend/review DNA tests, websites, and software."*
- **[Over 16.000 genealogy Facebook groups](https://www.genealogyandthesocialsphere.com/post/facebook-groups-where-the-genealogy-community-comes-alive)** as of 2021.
- **[GeneaBloggers](https://geneabloggers.substack.com/)**, a 501(c)(3) whose entire purpose is a
  community of genealogy bloggers — people who review products constantly, in writing.
- **[RootsTech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RootsTech)**, the world's largest family-history event,
  drawing **millions of virtual participants from 200+ countries**, free to attend online.
- A **[$2,25 billion North American market](https://www.globalgrowthinsights.com/market-reports/genealogy-products-and-services-market-101977)**, growing, with 25 million DNA kits sold in the US in 2025 alone.

Written, faceless, concentrated, review-hungry, and old enough to have money. **This is the Kaching
seed pattern with a ready-made venue.**

### The economics are roughly double candidate 64

| Line | |
|---|---|
| Price (150-page hardcover) | **$299** |
| Print + fulfilment + shipping | **−$53** *(anchored on Lulu's $32,50 at 100pp)* |
| Payment processing | **−$9** |
| **Contribution, first copy** | **$237** |
| Extra copies at $99 (siblings, cousins, children) — ~2,5 average | **+$115** |
| **Average order** | **≈ $352 ≈ R$2.040** |

> **R$30.000/month = 15 orders. R$12.000/month = 6 orders.**
> At ~90 min per book that is **~22 hours a month** at the three-year target.

Candidate 64 needs 39 orders. This needs 15.

## 4. And then: FamilySearch shipped into it on 9 August 2026

**Five days before this hunt.**

[**Memories Book**](https://lds365.com/2026/08/09/new-memories-book-website-turns-familysearch-photos-and-stories-into-a-family-history-book-in-just-a-few-minutes/)
— connects to a FamilySearch account, picks a starting ancestor, covers one to five generations,
and *"turns FamilySearch photos and stories into a family history book in just a few minutes."*
Read online, save as PDF, **order printed copies.** Premium adds an editable DOCX.

**LAW C fires exactly as written: hunt the free competitor by funding model.** FamilySearch is
funded by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, gives its products away, and has just
shipped into this vertical. **Corrected Law A1 leg (a) — FREE — fires on its face.**

[MyCanvas](https://www.mycanvas.com/familysearch-book) also already accepts a FamilySearch login to
build a family history book, and [48hrbooks](https://www.48hrbooks.com/genre/genealogy-printing)
holds the printing end.

### The wedge that might survive — and it is narrow

**Memories Book compiles; it does not write.** It assembles *"photographs with titles and
captions, and written stories from FamilySearch"* — content someone already uploaded. **A tree with
800 names and no uploaded stories produces an empty book**, and that is the majority case.

So the surviving proposition is precise:

> **"Your tree has 800 names and no stories. We write them."** — occupations, migrations, the
> village, the crossing, the war, the world those people actually lived in.

That is the same distinction that made candidate 64 work: **blank versus written.**

### But three things push against it, and they are not small

1. **The community's stated objection is exactly this product.** In its own words: *"The AI-drafted
   narrative family history is the newest temptation… but it will also invent detail and give
   ancestors personalities they may never have had."* The defence — every sentence sourced, nothing
   invented, context labelled as general and cited — is real, and it is also a discipline she must
   prove to sceptics with no credential to lean on.
2. **She is not a genealogist and cannot claim to be** — which collides with her own veto on
   anything requiring her to be believed as an expert. **The only honest position is: the customer
   is the researcher, she is the publisher.** That works, and it must never slip.
3. **GEDCOM parsing is a real build.** It is an old, messy, inconsistently implemented format. This
   is materially harder than candidate 64's form-plus-template, against her own *"build yes, bugs
   and such after the build."*

### Verdict on probe 6

**ALIVE BUT DEMOTED — second to candidate 64, not first.** Better economics, better channel, worse
competitive weather and a harder build. It is the only one of the six worth a second look, and it
needs **one specific check before anything else**:

> **Feed Memories Book a tree with names and dates and no uploaded stories. If the output is
> empty or trivial, the wedge is real. If it writes decent narrative from bare facts, this is
> dead and there is no repair.**

That check is free and takes an afternoon.

## 5. What the hunt actually proved

Six probes across the emotional-keepsake space returned five kills, and the sixth is contested by a
free product from a $100-billion-endowed organisation that launched last week.

> **The emotional-keepsake category is one of the most thoroughly farmed consumer spaces on the
> internet.** Pet loss, cookbooks, tribute books, memory books, memorials, letters — every one of
> them has a done-for-you incumbent, a free tool, or both.

**Candidate 64 did not survive because keepsakes are easy. It survived because pregnancy sits in the
one seam nobody crossed** — and a deliberate six-way attempt to find its equal produced exactly one
contender, immediately contested.

**That is the strongest evidence yet that candidate 64 should be built, and the cheapest way to
learn it was to spend an afternoon trying to beat it.**

---

## 6. Recommendation

1. **Build candidate 64.** One book, one volunteer, $30. Nothing found today displaces it.
2. **Run the Memories Book check on a story-less tree** — one afternoon, free. If the wedge holds,
   the genealogy book is the strongest *second* product she could ever launch, because it shares
   the entire spine: a form, an AI draft, a text-only approval gate, a deterministic template, and
   one Lulu API call. **Same machine, different vertical.**
3. **Stop hunting the keepsake category.** It is farmed. The next hunt, if there is one, should
   apply the transposition law somewhere else entirely.
