## What You're Actually Building

A company that produces certified, liability-bearing land-title paperwork — chain-of-title runsheets, mineral reports, heirship research, curative work — for the lawyers and developers racing to build solar farms, transmission lines, and data centers on rural land. An AI pipeline reads the county records and drafts the work; a licensed human signs off on every single deliverable. You get paid per tract and per month of ongoing monitoring. The asset you're really building isn't the software — it's a permanent, growing archive of certified title research on specific counties that only gets built by doing paid work, which is exactly what stops a funded competitor from just copying you.

## The Problem You're Solving

Energy developers need proven land title before they can close financing — today that means landmen billing $400–600/day, producing 1–3 tracts a week, at $300K–$600K per 200-tract project, while every day of delay costs the developer $10K–$300K. The professional abstractor workforce is aging out just as 2,600+ GW of projects sit in interconnection queues waiting on exactly this work. The money is already budgeted; you're not creating a new category, you're taking share from slow, expensive incumbents.

## Why 50 Funded Copycats Still Lose

1. **The title plant compounds — and it can't be bought.** A third of relevant rural county records have no online index before 1970; someone has to physically go into the courthouse. Raw scanning isn't the moat — a copycat can blanket-scan 100 counties with enough money. The moat is the *adjudicated chain graph*, which only gets built by delivering paid, certified work. Repeat orders in the same survey reuse 60–80% of prior effort. A copycat has to win that same paid volume, county by county, earning nothing while catching up. **~2 years to replicate, and capital cannot compress it.**
2. **Courthouse grit + counsel trust.** Handwritten pre-1970 deeds, county-specific quirks, a vetted runner network, and buyers who switch vendors only on their attorney's recommendation. **18–36 months to replicate.**
3. **Monitoring lock-in.** Default-on in every contract, embedded in the customer's financing diligence chain. Ripping it out mid-project jeopardizes the deal. 1%/mo churn.

Licensing (E&O coverage, credentialed signers) is cleared in year 1 as an operating requirement, not sold as a moat by itself — the real regulatory barrier (Oklahoma/North Dakota abstract-plant certification) is a genuine multi-year, $400K–$1M+ project, deliberately deferred to year 2–3 and funded from cash flow, never from the starting $250K.

## The Discipline That Keeps This Airtight

- **A real kill line for the mandate itself**, not just for quality gates: if by month 18 the trailing run-rate doesn't project to at least $500,000/month pre-tax by month 30, new county expansion halts and the mandate gets reassessed before another dollar of capital moves.
- **Concentration caps:** no single customer above 15% of trailing-12-month revenue; no single geographic corridor above 40%.
- **Cyclicality, named openly:** revenue tracks interconnection-queue timing and energy policy; a contraction scenario delays the trajectory by 2–3 quarters, it doesn't break it — every published stress branch stays cash-positive.
- **E&O is treated as the single point of failure it is:** carrier gate cleared month 1, before any client document is touched, with a fallback ladder (parallel E&S quote → fronting through a licensed title agency → reserve-funded high-retention structure) if the primary carrier balks.

## What's Still Needed Before This Is Fully Airtight

Three concrete gaps, named rather than hidden:

1. **A real month-by-month depreciation schedule for capitalized plant-capture spend.** The month 43–44 after-tax crossing above uses a flat continuation of the month-36 amortization charge — a reasonable approximation, not a modeled fact. A full schedule could move that date earlier or later.
2. **A published, month-by-month cash curve for TITLE FORGE specifically**, with an explicit trough dollar figure and month — the certified plan states the breakeven milestones (month 13 accrual-positive, month 16 cash breakeven) but not a full curve between them.
3. **A month-42+ product-mix projection**, so the "recurring lines carry more of the margin over time" mechanism can be stated as a number instead of a direction.

None of these three are reasons to doubt the business — they're reasons to build the three missing models before wiring the next dollar, exactly the discipline this whole exercise exists to enforce.
