## ★★★ CORRECTION 1 — MY AMENDMENT TO `THE REPLACEMENT-COST CEILING` WAS WRONG, AND THIS IS THE IMPORTANT ONE

Yesterday I amended that law to add a fourth escape: *"AN ACCESS RIGHT OWNED BY HER OWN RESIDENCE… a foreign
buyer cannot commission residency at any price."* The reviewer's answer:

> *"Brazilian residence is a defensible access right — **WRONG. Thousands of residents, IP agents and law firms
> can provide it. It is abundant, not proprietary.**"*

**Correct, and the error is precise: I conflated *the buyer cannot self-supply it* with *it is scarce*.** Those
are different properties and only the second one prices.

> ### **THE AMENDMENT IS WITHDRAWN AND REPLACED: an input escapes the replacement-cost ceiling only if the buyer cannot commission it FROM ANYONE. "The buyer cannot BE this" is worth nothing when ten thousand other people can be it instead. Non-self-suppliability is necessary and nowhere near sufficient; SUPPLY SCARCITY is what prices.**

**And the price evidence the reviewer produced confirms it, at exactly the level the law predicts:**
- **123 Marcas: R$810** for appointment **plus a full year of monitoring.**
- **Global Trademark Company: US$460 ONE-TIME**, including filing, representation, monitoring and procedural
  responses.

**Against my US$149/month (US$1.788/year) — that is 2–22× the observed market for the same seat.** My "observed
US$400–800/month" was the *entity* representative role, and I had already flagged that as a category error;
the reviewer supplied the actual comparables for the trademark seat and they are annual and cheap. **`THE
OBSERVED-PRICE CEILING` should have caught this and I ran straight past it on my own candidate.**

*(The seat was already dead by founder decision. This records **why** it was right, and repairs a law that
would otherwise have mispriced the next candidate too.)*

## ★★ CORRECTION 2 — MY POOL FIGURE IS SOUND PER PROCESSO AND MY ANNUALISATION IS NOT

The reviewer challenged the pool with **WIPO: 10.910 Brazil designations in 2025**, against my ~33.000/year
annualised. **I re-ran the parse checking for double counting: 2.529 Madrid processos over four weeks are
2.529 DISTINCT processo numbers — no inflation from summing editions.** So the raw count is clean.

**The reconciliation is a unit mismatch, and both numbers are right:** WIPO counts *international registrations
designating Brazil*; INPI's gazette carries *Brazilian processos*, which split per class. ~10.910 designations
at ~3 classes each ≈ 33.000 processos. **The two agree once the units are aligned.**

**But the reviewer is right about the inference, and this part is my error:**
- **Defensible, measured:** **1.007 distinct COMPANIES** with a granted Brazilian mark and no procurador, and
  **313 distinct companies** with a hard-deadline event and none, **in one four-week window.**
- **Not defensible:** multiplying either by 13. **Companies recur across months**, so the true annual figure is
  materially below 13.100 and 4.100. Those two numbers are withdrawn.
- **And the sharper point, which is entirely correct:** *"313 deadline events prove a stream of events and
  leads — not recurring buyers. Many will appoint counsel for one response."* **A lead is not a subscriber.**

## What the reviewer got right that I had not said
- **Mercado Livre gives sellers a window to challenge a report, and an unanswered challenge can reactivate the
  listing** ⇒ the cadence is not a weekly sweep, it is a docket. *(Not independently verified by me — flagged.)*
- **No guaranteed 24–72 h removal SLA exists** at either platform. The proposal's figure was its own.
- **The brands worth counterfeiting at volume already have counsel or software; the brands she can reach easily
  may not have enough Brazilian infringement to justify a monthly fee.** This is the pincer, and it is the same
  shape as `THE MONITORING PINCER` already in the file. **It should have been run on this candidate and was not.**
- **A trademark registration is not a licence to remove:** genuine resale, grey imports, legitimate
  collaborations and unauthorised-but-lawful distribution are not infringements.

- The reviewer keeps the buyer definition at *"brands with Brazilian revenue and 20+ manually verified
  actionable listings."* **Agreed, and it is strictly better than mine**, which qualified on trademark
  registration alone — a filter that selects for brands with rights rather than brands with a problem.
