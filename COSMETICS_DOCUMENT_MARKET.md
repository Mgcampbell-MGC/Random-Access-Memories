## 3. Why the regulatory-document business is probably NOT hers

The agent's own demolition is strong and I accept most of it.

1. **The one document that matters is licence-reserved.** CPSR Part B must be signed by someone with
   a university degree in pharmacy, toxicology or medicine — **Annex I, Regulation (EC) 1223/2009**.
   Every $30–80 "CPSR" gig without a real assessor is either quietly reselling someone else's
   signature or selling something the buyer cannot legally use. **The accredited assessor is the
   business. A non-credentialed intermediary sits on top of nothing.**
2. **The analytical layer is free.** The EU's **CosIng** database is free and public, and
   **INCIkit, CosIng Checker, COSMILE Europe** already let anyone paste an ingredient list and get
   instant restriction flags. *"The hard analytical work — is this ingredient banned — costs
   nothing."*
3. **Free competitors exist at the task level.** Some UK firms give SCPN notification away free to
   upsell CPSR work. Manufacturers hand over CoA, MSDS and GMP certificates free with the purchase.
4. **She has no edge.** Pakistani, Indian and UK sellers already hold this niche at **$20–50**, and
   several carry genuine regulatory credentials she does not have. **Portuguese buys her nothing
   here; English is their working language too.**
5. **It is one-off, not recurring.** Once a SKU is filed there is no reason to buy again until the
   next SKU — and a small indie brand launches rarely.

**Also permanently closed to her:** being the EU/UK Responsible Person (must be an EU/UK-established
entity), being Brazil's Responsável Técnico (pharmacist or chemical engineer with CRF), issuing a
CoA (needs an ISO/IEC 17025 lab), issuing halal or vegan certificates, and authoring any real
patient's clinical record (CFBM 241/2014 and 363/2023).

> **Verdict on the regulatory-consulting business: it is a licensed profession with a commoditised
> assembly layer beneath it, already occupied by credentialed sellers at $20–50. She has no
> credential and no edge. Not hers.**

## 4. But one thing inside it is exactly candidate 66, in its best vertical

Read this, from an Amazon seller, verbatim:

> *"**I cannot complete the application to sell a Topical product because I haven't obtained a
> COA... Amazon's requirements are vaguely stated, Amazon's support staff doesn't have an answer,
> and every lab I speak with can't make sense of what Amazon requires.**"*

And this one, which is a pure document problem stated as such:

> *"**97% of factory SDSs are written for road freight… not small FBA quantities.**"* — the gap
> between the manufacturer's document and the one the marketplace will accept **is reformatting, not
> chemistry.**

Plus the pattern in the forum thread itself: sellers being **pointed straight at paid consultants,
by other sellers, inside Amazon's own support forum.**

> ### THE PIECE THAT SURVIVES
> **Not "cosmetics regulatory consulting." The cosmetics marketplace documentation package** — the
> set of documents Amazon and the European marketplaces demand before a beauty listing goes live or
> after they suspend one: PIF reference, notification proof, a CoA formatted to the platform's
> spec, an SDS rewritten for small-quantity fulfilment rather than road freight, and label text that
> matches the physical product.
>
> **That is the same machinery as the catalogue business — a per-platform rule set, a document
> package, an acceptance test — pointed at the most restricted, highest-margin, fastest-growing
> category on the marketplace.**

**And it fixes the weakest part of candidate 66.** Its problem was that nothing recurred and the
trigger was rare. Here the triggers are **statutory and dated** — August PPWR, October PFAS, the
allergen glossary, biennial MoCRA — **and Amazon can demand documentation at any point in a
listing's life and suspend on the spot.**

## 5. The free diagnostic, again — and this time the tooling is free too

Every strand of this project has converged on the same funnel, and here it costs nothing to run:

> **Paste the INCI list. Screen it against the free CosIng database for Annex II–VI restrictions.
> Screen the label copy for the claim words that trip FDA, EU and Amazon rules — "cures",
> "anti-aging", "hypoallergenic", "dermatologist tested" without substantiation. Return a one-page
> red-flag report, free.**

**Built entirely on public, no-cost databases.** It is the diagnostic that the merchant council seat
asked for, the competitor seat said would make him take her seriously, and the blue team costed at
four hours — and in this vertical the underlying data is already free and public.

## 6. What is still open

**The second agent is measuring the one question that decides whether any of this is more than a
Fiverr gig:** does a **preparer layer** exist beneath the licensed roles — do Responsible Person
firms, safety assessors and consultancies subcontract document assembly, and at what rate? If they
do, the buyer is a regulatory firm rather than a brand, which changes the trust problem completely.
If they do not, this reduces to competing with credentialed sellers at $20–50, and it is dead.

**Until that lands, the honest position is:** the vertical has the best-dated demand in the file,
the free-diagnostic funnel is cheaper here than anywhere else, and **the marketplace documentation
package — not regulatory consulting — is the part she could actually own.**
