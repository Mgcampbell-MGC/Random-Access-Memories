# THE AD-REP INDUSTRY ALREADY EXISTS — 19 Aug 2026

**Leg 2 of the newsletter-advertising-representation hunt. Twenty-five observed rate cards, all primary.**

> **My premise was that small trade publications have no sales team and nobody represents them. The first
> half is true. The second half is FALSE. The outsourced advertising-representative industry is roughly forty
> years old and has already consolidated exactly this market.**

## 2. ★★ THE FATAL LAYER — it is not selling the ad, it is GETTING APPOINTED

**Buying a placement is often genuinely written-only.** The agronomy societies publish a **printable insertion
order with tick-boxes and a signature line** — `☐ Top Leaderboard @ $3.000` — and take orders at a generic
`advertising@sciencesocieties.org` inbox. NTEA, Maritime Executive, AALL, OGJ and NAIOP all publish full
numeric rate cards with email workflows.

**But the layer above it does not.**

> **Zero evidence was found that any publisher or association has EVER appointed an advertising representative
> through a written-only process, and no association RFP for ad-sales representation could be opened**
> *(the one candidate document 403'd — **UNVERIFIED rather than disproven, but every observable points the
> wrong way**)*.
>
> **The incumbents compete on decades of named relationships. The rep's entire pitch to a publisher is
> *"I know these advertisers and they take my call."* That sentence is a SPEAKING claim and an EXPERTISE
> claim at the same time. It collides with C1 and the expert veto simultaneously.**

**And insurance — the sector I would have guessed richest — is flatly phone-gated.** Insurance Journal and
Carrier Management publish **no rates at all**: *"Call us now at 800-897-9965 x125."*

## 3. My arithmetic rested on 30%, and that number is now in dispute

**This agent could not find a published take rate anywhere** — not on Paved's publisher page or blog, and not
from Elliott, Russell Johns, Kelman, AdBoom, Spilman or Association Revenue Partners. It correctly refused a
"35%" figure sourced only to a sample business plan.

> **CONTRADICTION, RECORDED.** The S6 agent cited **Paved's own publisher pricing help article: 30% on
> Marketplace bookings, 40% on Ad Network performance earnings.** That is a specific document at a specific
> URL. This agent looked at a different page and found nothing. **I resolve in favour of the S6 agent — a
> named help-centre article beats absence on a marketing page — but the sell-side REP commission, as distinct
> from a marketplace take rate, remains UNVERIFIED.**

**And a trap worth keeping:** AALL's kit states *"A 15% commission is allowed to recognized advertising
agencies."* **That is the BUY-SIDE agency discount, not the sell-side rep commission. Do not conflate them.**

### ★ The one genuine rescue in this report — sell the CONTRACT, not the placement

> **NAFA sells a 24× *Fleet Pulse* contract as a SINGLE $10.920 booking.** NTEA sells monthly banner
> contracts at $2.200–2.900. NAIOP's 12-pack is **$10.500 against $14.340 at single rate — a 27% discount.**
>
> **That reframes the entire hour problem. Three or four annual contracts a month clears the target instead of
> seven or eight individual placements — and one contract is one sale, not twenty-four.**

**Minnesota Dental's kit even enforces repetition contractually:** *"Frequency contracts not fulfilled are
subject to short-rate billing at four-time or one-time rate."* **Publishers claw back the discount if you do
not repeat. Nobody writes that clause unless the repeat contract is the standard unit of sale.**

*(Repeat-booking RATE itself remains **UNVERIFIED** — no kit publishes retention. Frequency tiers and
short-rate clauses are strong indirect evidence, not a measurement.)*

## 6. The escape hatch this report accidentally reveals

**She cannot get APPOINTED by a publisher without relationships and a telephone. But AALL's own kit grants
15% automatically to any *recognized advertising agency* — a BUY-SIDE commission that requires no exclusive
appointment from anyone.**

> **An agency represents ADVERTISERS, not publishers. It places their buys across trade media and collects
> the standard agency commission from the publisher. No appointment, no exclusivity, no relationship pitch.**
>
> **The costs: it is 15%, not 30% — so the flow required roughly doubles to ~$74.000/month. "Recognized
> agency" almost certainly has criteria nobody has checked. And acquiring advertisers as clients is a sales
> job with the same hour problem.** *Flagged as a lead, not a candidate.*

## 7. What this does to the candidate

**The ad-representation business is not dead, but it is badly wounded, and the wound is in a predictable
place: THE ONBOARDING LAW.** Selling the placement works in writing. **Winning the mandate does not.**

**Two things survive regardless of whether this candidate lives:**

1. **THE APPOINTMENT LAYER TEST** — in any intermediary business, ask separately: *can the TRANSACTION run in
   writing, and can the MANDATE be won in writing?* They are different questions with different answers, and
   the second one is where intermediary businesses die.
2. **SELL THE CONTRACT, NOT THE UNIT.** Where an industry already prices 12× and 24× frequency contracts, one
   sale can be worth twenty-four placements — which is the only observed mechanism that collapses the
   per-unit hour cost in a sales business.
