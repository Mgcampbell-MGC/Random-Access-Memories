## 1. How it was generated

> **THE VERIFIABILITY LAW — a faceless seller can only sell work whose correctness the buyer can
> check faster than they could do it themselves.**

Combined with the arbitrage law (hard-currency buyer), the B2B buyer law (no procurement, no calls),

the search space collapses to something very specific: **work with a machine-checkable right answer,
a statutory deadline, a file-in/file-out shape, and a buyer paying in euros.**

**That points at digital accessibility, and inside it at documents rather than websites** — because
websites need code access and documents do not.

## 2. The trigger is real, in force, and it is already pulling revenue

> **The European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) came into force on 28 June 2025.
> Enforcement powers are active in all 27 member states.**

- It applies to **e-commerce, banking, transport, telecoms and digital services**, and explicitly to
  **e-books**.
- **It applies regardless of where the business is headquartered** — it follows the sale to an EU
  consumer.
- **Penalties, by member state:** up to **€100.000** generally · **France €5.000–250.000**, plus
  **€25.000 a year** for a missing accessibility statement · **Spain up to €1.000.000** for very
  serious infractions.
- Authorities can **order withdrawal, ban products from a national market, require an audit, and
  publicly name the organisation.**
- **Microenterprises are exempt** — under 10 employees *and* under €2M turnover — which conveniently
  removes the buyers too poor to pay.

**And the consequence for a publisher is not a fine, it is lost revenue:** titles that cannot be
made compliant *"may need to be temporarily or permanently removed from sale in the EU."* **A
product being delisted is a far better motivator than a hypothetical penalty.**

## 5. THE QUESTION THAT DECIDES IT — and my sources disagree

> **Does a backlist title first sold before 28 June 2025 have to be remediated NOW, or does a
> transitional provision run to 28 June 2030?**

- One source: *"all eBooks sold in the EU must be accessible… this applies to both new and existing
  titles, regardless of your location."*
- Another: *"compliance deadlines beginning June 28, 2025, for new content, and **a broader backlist
  remediation deadline set for 2030**."*
- And the EAA does contain a transitional provision letting service providers continue using
  **products** lawfully in use before 28 June 2025 until **28 June 2030**.

**If the backlist has until 2030, the urgency collapses and this becomes an ordinary competitive
market with five years to prepare. If it does not, three and a half million titles are non-compliant
right now.**

**I am not picking the exciting reading.** I did that this afternoon with a quote from an Asia
capital-markets newsletter, and it was wrong. An agent is reading Directive 2019/882 Article 32
directly, along with national transposition and any settled professional reading, and it has been
told plainly: **if the backlist has until 2030, say so and the candidate downgrades.**

## 6. The risks I can already see

1. **Vendors exist.** PaperbackKit takes bulk EPUBs through an API; eBook Partnership, S4Carlisle,
   KnowledgeWorks Global and Kitaboo all sell this. **Not an empty field.**
2. **The offshore production houses may bundle it free.** If S4Carlisle or KnowledgeWorks include
   accessibility inside a typesetting contract they are already paid for, **that is the clearing
   party and it kills this.** The agent has been told to report that finding early and plainly.
3. **Alt text is the hard part, and it is judgement.** Describing a chart or a diagram in a
   scholarly book needs subject understanding. Trade fiction is easy; academic and technical are not.
4. **Automated tooling is improving.** Adobe auto-tag, PDFix and Equidox are closing the gap.
5. **Publishers buy from known production partners.** Cold entry into a relationship industry is the
   same wall candidate 66 hit.
