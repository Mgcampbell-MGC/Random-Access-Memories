## 1. ★★ THE PDA LAW — BRAZIL NEEDS NO UKGWA BECAUSE ITS AGENCIES ARCHIVE THEMSELVES

Under the open-data decree regime (each agency's *Plano de Dados Abertos*), Brazilian federal agencies publish
**outcome, history, and in one case the derived statistic itself** — all verified today, first rows printed:

| Agency | What it publishes that kills the archive play |
|---|---|
| **ANTT** | Monthly RNTRC snapshots **back to 12/2020 at date-stamped URLs** — the publisher ships the archive |
| **ANS** | A dedicated **cancelled-operators file** + a penalties file |
| **CVM** | Cancelled registrants **retained in-file** with `DT_CANCEL`, `MOTIVO_CANCEL` — and an `EMAIL` column |
| **ANVISA** | Expired GMP certificates retained in-file (STATUS=VENCIDO, dated, daily refresh) — **and it publishes the queue-wait statistic itself** (mean wait per sub-queue, monthly, back to 2018) |
| **IBAMA** | The embargo dataset ships its own **cure register** (`decisao.csv`, Desembargo + dates) |
| **CADASTUR** | Quarterly snapshots kept back to ~2012 |
| **INPI** | The RPI **is literally a weekly gazette** — permanent deltas |
| **Everything else** | **LAI (Lei 12.527) is the front door**: every register inspected is held by a PUBLIC body, and the FOIA law transposes intact — any past version is retrievable in writing for R$0 |
