# WHAT THE BUSINESS IS — plainly, 2 Sep 2026
**Founder: *"stop pushing the outreach, you have not even made it clear what the business is."* Fair. This
document has no tests, no probes and no kill conditions in it. It is only what the thing is, what it looks like
from the inside, and what the ideal version would be.**

---

## 1. It in one paragraph

**Brazil requires that a foreign company which owns anything here has a real person here who answers for it.**
Not a lawyer. Not a company. A person, resident in Brazil, named on the file, to whom the government sends its
letters. A foreign company cannot be that person and cannot buy its way out of needing one. **Sol is that
person, for as many foreign companies as will have her, for a flat monthly fee.** She receives what the
Brazilian state sends, translates the deadline into English, forwards it the same day to the company and its
lawyers, and files nothing, decides nothing and advises nobody.

> ### **She is not selling work. She is selling a seat — a permanent, legally required chair in someone else's Brazilian affairs, which they must fill and cannot fill themselves.**

## 2. What she is actually selling, precisely

Not advice. Not effort. **A legal qualification plus reliability.**

- **The qualification:** residence in Brazil and civil capacity. INPI's rule and two independent sources agree
  the role may be held by *"an IP agent, a specialized lawyer, or **any Brazilian citizen**"* — since 2014.
  She has it by birth. **A foreign buyer cannot commission it at any price, and it never expires.**
- **The reliability:** that when a letter arrives in Portuguese with a 60-day fuse, it reaches the right people
  in English the same day, with the date on top.

**That is the whole product.** Everything else — the weekly report, the log, the summary — is packaging around
those two things.

## 3. This is not a new idea. It is an industry that exists in every comparable country except this one.

Wherever a state says *"you must have a local person,"* an industry forms to be that person at scale, and it is
never the legal profession that ends up owning it:

| Jurisdiction | The compelled seat | Who sells it | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | Registered agent, required in **every state** for every LLC and corporation | Northwest, Harbor Compliance, ZenBusiness, LegalZoom, CSC, CT Corporation | **US$89–125/yr** retail · **US$249–450/yr** enterprise |
| European Union | Fiscal representative for non-EU sellers | specialist firms, per country | per country, annual |
| United Kingdom | Registered office address | hundreds of providers | £50–200/yr |
| Hong Kong | Company secretary, statutory | corporate services industry | HK$1.500–5.000/yr |
| **Brazil** | **Procurador / representante legal, LPI art. 217 + IN RFB 2.119** | **law firms, as an unpriced line item** | **nobody publishes** |

> ### **Every country with this rule has a productised operator. Brazil has 707 individual procuradores, no product, and no published price. That gap is the business.**

**Why Brazil hasn't got one:** the role arrived attached to trademark filing, so the people holding it are the
people who file — law firms and IP agents. For them it is a courtesy line on an invoice, not a product. Nobody
whose main business is the seat itself has ever built it here.

## 4. ★★ THE DEEPEST RISK, and it is not the one I have been writing about

**Look again at the US column. A registered agent costs US$125 a year.** If the Brazilian seat becomes a
product, its price should fall toward that — and **the person who productises a statutory seat captures volume,
not margin.** Northwest survives at US$125/yr because it has hundreds of thousands of customers. **She cannot
have hundreds of thousands. There are only a few thousand foreign trademark holders a year here.**

**So the business only exists in the space between "law firm afterthought" and "US$125 utility." Three things
hold it there, and all three are real:**
1. **The work is triage, not mail.** A US registered agent forwards an envelope. She reads a Portuguese
   administrative notice, identifies which of the client's marks it touches, states the deadline, and says who
   has to act. **That is a translation and a judgement about timing** — it cannot be reduced to a scanner.
2. **The exposure is real.** The US agent has none. A procurador acts inside a power of attorney and is named
   on the file. Somebody accepting that is paid more than somebody forwarding post.
3. **★ The market is too small to attract a utility.** This file's own note, made at a much larger revenue
   target: ***"too small to matter is a real moat."*** At a few thousand holders a year, no venture-funded
   operator will ever enter. **At US$2.500/month that moat is wide. At US$10.000/month it would not have been.**

**⇒ The honest positioning: this is a US$99–199 per month service, not a US$125 per year utility, and it must
never be sold as a mailbox.** The moment it is sold as a mailbox it is worth mailbox money.

## 5. What the week actually looks like

**Monday morning.** One script runs against the week's INPI gazette. It does two things at once: it flags
anything that touched a client's marks, and it lists foreign holders who just hit a deadline with nobody
appointed. *(One parse, both halves of the business — this is why the delivery cost does not grow with clients.)*

**Most weeks, for most clients: nothing happened.** The report says so, which is itself the product — the
client knows somebody looked.

**When something did happen:** a notice arrives, in Portuguese, with a fuse on it. She logs it with a
timestamp, writes three lines in English — *what INPI did, which mark, by what date somebody must respond* —
and sends it to the company and its lawyers in the same message. **She does not say what they should do.** Then
it is theirs.

**Once a month:** a one-page summary per client and an invoice. **Once a year:** a renewal, and a list of their
Brazilian marks with the next dates on each.

**Total, at twenty clients: ten to twelve hours a month.** No calls. No deadlines of her own. Nothing that can
be delivered wrong, because nothing is produced.

## 6. There are two versions of this business, and they are not the same business

### Version A — sell the seat to the company
Twenty to fifty foreign companies, US$149/month each. Straightforward, and it is what v1 described.
**The trouble: twenty to fifty separate relationships to find, close and renew**, each worth US$1.800 a year,
each needing its own power of attorney. It works, and it is a lot of small sales.

### ★ Version B — sell the seat to the FOREIGN LAW FIRM. **This is the real business.**
A US, UK or German trademark firm with a Brazil practice needs a Brazilian address of record **on every matter
it handles here.** Today it gets one from a Brazilian law firm.

> ### **And the Brazilian law firm is its competitor.** It practises the same law, it is now on the file, and it can see the client. **Sol structurally cannot compete with them, because she cannot practise law at all — and that inability is the entire pitch.**
>
> ***"Your Brazilian correspondent is a law firm that would be delighted to have your client. We can't be. We don't practise law. We're just the address — and we send you a docket report every Monday."***

| | Version A | **Version B** |
|---|---|---|
| Relationships to reach the target | 15–20 companies | **5–8 firms** |
| Who talks to the end client | her | **the firm — she never does** |
| Growth after the sale | find another company | **the firm files new matters every month** |
| What she is against | a law firm's courtesy line | **a law firm's conflict of interest** |
| Price shape | US$149/company/month | **US$400–600/firm/month, unlimited matters** |
| At target | 15–20 clients | **5–8 firms** |

**Version B is better on every axis that has ever killed a candidate here.** It needs five relationships
instead of fifty, so distribution stops being the binding constraint. The firm handles all client contact, so
the constraint on speaking is protected by the structure rather than by discipline. New matters arrive without
new selling. And the reason to choose her over the incumbent is not price or effort — **it is a structural
conflict the incumbent cannot remove.**

## 7. Why clients do not leave — the part nobody has written down

Once she is the procurador of record, **changing her requires the client to execute a new power of attorney and
file a revocation.** That is not hard, but it is friction, and friction now works for her instead of against
her. Meanwhile:

- **A Brazilian trademark registration lasts ten years and renews indefinitely.** The seat is needed for as
  long as the mark exists.
- She holds no data they need back, so there is nothing to fight over on exit.
- Nothing she delivers can go visibly wrong, so there is no quality event that triggers a review.

> **Long asset life, real switching friction, no failure mode. Client lifetimes here should be measured in
> years, and that is rare in anything this file has looked at.**

## 9. Honestly: what makes it a hell yes, and what makes it thin

**What is genuinely rare about it**
- **The buyer is compelled by law and cannot self-supply.** In roughly 130 candidates examined here, this is
  the only business where the thing being sold cannot be rebuilt by the buyer at any price.
- **Nothing is produced, so nothing can be wrong.** Every other candidate on the board has a quality risk.
- **It gets cheaper per client as it grows**, because one gazette parse serves everyone.
- **It survives all her constraints without needing any of them relaxed** — no employees, no credential, no
  audience, no expertise claimed, no capital beyond a domain and a lawyer's opinion.

**What is thin, and should not be dressed up**
- **It is boring.** Not stressful, not heavy, not emotionally costly — genuinely boring. Ten to twelve hours a
  month of forwarding letters. Whether that is a feature or a slow death is her answer, not mine, and it is the
  same question that ended GateLedger.
- **She would be one of 707.** This is not an empty market; it is an unproductised one. The wedge is a
  published price, a same-day promise, a weekly report and English — a better version of an existing thing,
  not a new thing.
- **The bare role's price is still unproven.** Every observed figure belongs to the heavier company-representative
  role.
- **A regulator could delete the duty.** It happened next door in 2024 for small foreign investors.

## 10. The ideal version, if it were built deliberately

> **A tiny, English-language Brazilian representation company that never practises law — selling standing seats
> to foreign IP firms, at a published flat rate, with a weekly docket report attached, and a written promise
> that it will never contact their clients.**

Five deliberate choices make it that:
1. **Trademarks only in year one.** The company and tax role pays more and carries real liability; it is a
   year-two upsell to firms that already trust her, taken with counsel.
2. **Sell to firms, not companies.** Five relationships, not fifty. The firm owns the client; she owns the seat.
3. **Publish the price.** Nobody in this market does. It is the cheapest possible differentiation and it
   removes the sales conversation entirely.
4. **The weekly docket report is included, never sold separately.** It costs one script run for all clients at
   once, and it is what makes a monthly fee honest instead of a mailbox charge.
5. **The refusal is the product.** *"We do not advise, we do not file, we do not appear, and we will never
   contact your client."* Every one of those is a limitation she cannot escape — and stated together they are
   the reason a foreign firm would pick her over a Brazilian law firm.

**The one thing to establish before anything is built** is not distribution and not the pool, both of which are
measured. **It is whether the seat, sold on its own, is worth a monthly fee at all** — because if firms give it
away with a filing, this is a beautifully shaped answer to a question nobody pays to have answered.
