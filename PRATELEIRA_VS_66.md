# PRATELEIRA PERFEITA vs CANDIDATE 66 — is it the same idea?

**14 Aug 2026.**

**Short answer: yes, it is the same business, aimed at a different buyer in a different currency.
It is better than mine in five specific ways, worse in two structural ones, and it breaks three of
her hard constraints in its go-to-market.**

---

## 1. "We had an older idea — is it the same?"

**No. Nothing in this record matches it.** Searched every markdown file for *prateleira*,
*Nuvemshop*, *cadastro*, *catálogo*, *SKU*, *catalog*. Three hits, all unrelated: *cadastro e
homologação de fornecedor* (the supplier-onboarding gate that killed a different candidate), a
graveyard quote about keeping a register updated, and one Nuvemshop mention inside a post-purchase
app memo.

**But the document itself says where it came from, and it is honest about it.** It carries a table
titled *"The idea DNA from the previous concepts should be kept, but redirected into a safer
operating zone"*:

| Pattern from earlier ideas | Keep | Remove |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnostic wedge | Mini Resgate + Prateleira Score | Fear-based compliance audit |
| Outcome/payment logic | Pay for approved cadastros | **Tax recovery success fee** |
| Dossier format | Catalog Pack with maps, logs, pendências | **Regulatory file needing signatures** |
| Case credits | Error Rescue credits | **Pix, refunds, appeals, financial disputes** |
| Safety rails | No invented facts, no customer outreach | **Government, legal, tax, health exposure** |

**That right column is VOLTA, GLOSA and Conferência de Crédito.** So: **the mechanism is new, the
skeleton is inherited from the dead ones** — the same diagnostic-wedge-plus-dossier-plus-outcome-fee
architecture, with every source of liability surgically removed. It is a *repair* of this record's
own dead thinking, pointed at a target that cannot generate a fine.

That is a legitimate and rather good move, and it is the first repair in this file that produced a
living candidate rather than another corpse.

### The prices agree once the units are normalised — which is a good sign

Their unit: **1 cadastro = 1 parent product on 1 platform, up to 10 variations.** Mine: one row.

| Their tier | Per cadastro | In USD |
|---|---|---|
| Mini Resgate R$390 / 10 | R$39 | $7,50 |
| Coleção Express R$1.490–1.900 / 30 | R$50–63 | $9,60–12,20 |
| Marketplace Batch R$3.900–4.900 / 60 | R$65–82 | $12,50–15,80 |

A fashion parent with ~10 variations is ~10 rows on my ladder: **10 × $1,80 = $18.** Their
$9,60–15,80 sits just under it. **Two independent price builds landed within a factor of 1,5 of
each other.** Neither of us is wildly wrong.

## 3. Five things theirs does better — steal all of them

1. **"Você só paga pelos cadastros aprovados."** Payment for *approved* listings, not for work
   done. For a faceless seller with no reputation, **this is a better risk reversal than any
   guarantee I wrote.**
2. **Mini Resgate: R$390 for 10 products, and if fewer than 8 of 10 pass the checklist, the client
   pays nothing.** Stronger than my $149 sample, and it replaces the sales call more completely.
3. **"Pendências da Dona"** — a named, separate list of the facts only the owner can supply
   (exact measurements, barcodes, materials). **This is the single best idea in the document.** It
   pre-assigns blame for missing data before the job starts, which is the exact failure mode that
   makes catalogue work go bad.
4. **Error Rescue as its own product** — R$900 minimum, then R$39–69 per error, for sellers whose
   import has already failed. **That buyer is urgent**, which is the best kind, and my version has
   no equivalent.
5. **Dated trigger moments**: a new collection stuck, expansion to a new marketplace, a failed
   import that duplicated everything, Dia das Mães / Black Friday / Natal. **Buyer-finding is the
   weakest part of my version and the strongest part of theirs.**

Also worth keeping: **the Prateleira Score** (transparent before/after grade) and the
`ready / pending client fact / out of scope` status on every product.

## 4. Two structural problems — and the arithmetic is the serious one

### a) The customer-acquisition maths is much harder

A brand with 30–300 SKUs runs perhaps **4–6 drops a year**, so one client is worth roughly
**R$6.000–10.000 a year.** Reaching R$360.000/year therefore needs **40–60 active clients** —
acquired and retained by a faceless founder with no ad budget.

**Compare: one 5.000-product backfill in the English version is $4.999 ≈ R$25.900. One client is
most of a month.**

### b) The tier that fits her week is seasonal; the tier that is year-round eats it

| Tier | Jobs for R$30.000/mo | Est. hours each | Hours/month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coleção Express (R$1.700) | **~18** | 4–8 (30 parents ≈ 180 rows, images, pendências, a validation round) | **~108** |
| Marketplace Batch (R$4.400) | ~7 | 8–14 | ~77 |
| **Mutirão Sazonal (R$8.400)** | **~3,6** | 12–20 | **~54** |

Her ceiling is ~130 h/month. **Coleção Express at target consumes 83% of it, leaving almost nothing
for selling.** Mutirão is comfortable — **but it is explicitly seasonal** (Black Friday, Natal, Dia
das Mães). *It is not a monthly product.*

**Also note the tax line:** a Brazilian service company with no payroll falls to **Anexo V at
15,5%** unless pró-labore is set at ≥28% of revenue to reach **Anexo III at 6%.** The document's
financial section should be checked against this; it is worth ~9,5 points of margin.

## 5. Three hard-constraint violations in its go-to-market

The delivery design is clean. **The selling design is not.**

| In the document | Constraint |
|---|---|
| *"Loom walkthrough"* | **Her voice, probably her face. FACELESS IS PERMANENT.** |
| *"seasonal webinars"* | **A live speaking event. Absolute veto.** |
| *"junior operator time"* in Cost Structure, and §13 *"Hiring, training and operating cadence"* | **NO EMPLOYEES, NO CONTRACTORS. EVER.** |

And a softer one: **"woman-to-woman concierge"** and *"Instagram DM"* describe a *relational* sale.
Written DMs are fine. A concierge relationship that expects a voice note or a call is not.

**All three are removable** — written walkthroughs instead of Loom, written case posts instead of
webinars, no hiring plan — but the document as written assumes a semi-visible, relational founder,
and that is not her.

## 6. Verdict

**Same idea. Their offer design is better than mine; my buyer is better than theirs.**

- **Their pricing architecture, risk reversal, pendências concept, Error Rescue product and trigger
  list should be adopted wholesale into candidate 66.** They are strictly better and cost nothing
  to take.
- **My buyer size should be kept.** 30–300 SKUs at 4–6 drops a year needs 40–60 clients; 500–25.000
  SKUs needs 4–7 jobs a month. **For a founder who cannot make a single sales call, fewer and
  bigger is the only workable direction.**
- **The Loom, the webinars and the junior operator come out**, or it is not her business.

### The version I would actually run

> **Candidate 66's buyer and volumes, priced with Prateleira Perfeita's offer ladder: a paid
> sample that is free if it fails, payment only for approved products, a named pendências list, a
> separate rescue product for failed imports, and dated seasonal triggers — sold in writing, to
> merchants with real catalogues, with nobody's face anywhere on it.**

**One open question worth deciding before the test:** whether to run it in **Portuguese to Brazilian
sellers** — where she is native, the buyer profile matches the "female-friendly" vision exactly, and
the acquisition maths is brutal — or in **English to US merchants**, where the maths is nine times
kinder and the cultural fit is weaker. **The offer design above works identically in both. Only the
buyer changes.**
