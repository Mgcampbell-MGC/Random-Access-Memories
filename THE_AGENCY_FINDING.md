## 1. THE ANTI-DISINTERMEDIATION STATUTE — the finding

**Three jurisdictions independently give an exclusive-territory commercial AGENT commission on transactions in
her territory THAT SHE DID NOT TOUCH:**

| | Provision | Verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| **Brazil, Civil Code** | **art. 714** | *"Salvo ajuste, o agente ou distribuidor terá direito à remuneração correspondente aos negócios concluídos dentro de sua zona, **ainda que sem a sua interferência**"* |
| **Brazil, special law** | **Lei 4.886/1965 art. 31** | Commission on business in the zone *"**ainda que diretamente pelo representado ou por intermédio de terceiros**"* — and it applies even where the contract is silent |
| **United Kingdom** | **SI 1993/3053 reg. 7(2)** | Commission where *"he has an exclusive right to a specific geographical area or to a specific group of customers"* — **non-derogable** |
| **European Union** | **Directive 86/653/EEC art. 7(2)** | The UK regulations implement it verbatim — **non-derogable** |

> ### THE ANTI-DISINTERMEDIATION STATUTE — the reason a matching business normally fails is that both sides, once introduced, transact directly and stop paying. **In commercial agency that failure is answered BY STATUTE rather than by contractual goodwill.** She is owed commission on business in her zone whether or not she touched it.

**And the distinction that decides it, which is one word in one contract:**

| | Brazilian Civil Code | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **AGÊNCIA** | arts. 710–721 | **art. 714 pays her on deals she never touched** |
| **CORRETAGEM** | arts. 722–729 | **art. 726: *"Iniciado e concluído o negócio diretamente entre as partes, nenhuma remuneração será devida ao corretor"*** unless written exclusivity |

> **Every contract she signs must be an *agência*, never a *corretagem*. One word decides whether she is
> disintermediated by default or protected by default.**

**Two honest limits.** UK reg. 2(1) confines "commercial agent" to **goods only**, so services agency gets no
UK/EU protection. Brazil's CC art. 710 says *"certos negócios"*, which is broader and appears to reach
services — but whether Brazilian law governs a contract with a US principal depends on choice of law, which a
US counterparty may refuse. **UNVERIFIED, needs Brazilian counsel.**

## 2. THREE CORRECTIONS TO THIS FILE

### CORRECTION 1 — the CORE registration gate does not exist. I briefed the agent that it did.

**Lei 4.886/1965 art. 2º** makes registration obligatory and **art. 5º** says *"Sòmente será devida
remuneração… a representante comercial devidamente registrado."* On its face, a hard gate. **It is not.**

> **STJ, REsp 1.678.551/DF, 3ª Turma, Rel. Min. Paulo de Tarso Sanseverino, j. 06/11/2018, DJe 27/11/2018** —
> *"Pacífico o entendimento do STJ de que o **artigo 5º da Lei 4.886/65 não foi recepcionado pela Constituição
> Federal de 1988**, pois, por se tratar de profissão que não exige qualificação técnica específica, o
> condicionamento ao recebimento de qualquer valor por serviços efetivamente prestados violaria à garantia de
> 'livre o exercício de qualquer trabalho, ofício ou profissão'."*

**She may lawfully earn and sue for commission WITHOUT registration.** What registration buys is the statutory
package (art. 27(j) indemnity of ≥1/12 of lifetime commissions, art. 43 prohibition of *del credere*, art. 44
labour-credit ranking in the principal's bankruptcy), not the right to be paid. **Unregistered, she still gets
the Civil Code: art. 711 zone exclusivity, art. 714, art. 716, art. 720.**

**And the strategic reading is the reverse of the obvious one:** the 1/12 indemnity is a real liability that
may make principals reluctant to appoint her. **She can simply not register — keeping art. 714, the clause that
actually matters, and dropping the indemnity that frightens the principal.**

### CORRECTION 2 — the escape from the S2 tax kill is written into the same decree that created it

**`CLAUDE.md` records RIR/2018 art. 162 §1º as closing resale.** It does. But the agent read further:

> **Decreto 9.580/2018 art. 162 §2º III** — *"O disposto no inciso II do § 1º **não se aplica** às pessoas
> físicas que… III — **agentes, representantes e outras pessoas sem vínculo empregatício que, ao tomar parte em
> atos de comércio, não os pratiquem, todavia, por conta própria**"*

> ### THE AGENT EXEMPTION — the *equiparação à pessoa jurídica* that closed S2 EXPRESSLY DOES NOT APPLY to an agent or representative who never trades on her own account. She stays *pessoa física*: no CNPJ obligation, **no PIS/COFINS on gross**, taxed on IRPF (carnê-leão, to 27,5%) plus INSS contribuinte individual plus municipal ISS.

**This is the exact escape hatch THE COMMISSION-AGENT RESOLUTION predicted, now verified at decree level
rather than inferred.** *(ISS treatment **UNVERIFIED** — where the principal is foreign but the buyer is
Brazilian, the *resultado* occurs in Brazil, so the LC 116/2003 export exemption probably does NOT apply.)*

publishes email for an entire national economy.**

## 3. THE LEAD CANDIDATE — exclusive ad representation for niche B2B newsletters

**Shape S6. Revenue structure B — a slice of someone else's flow. A genuinely new shape for this file.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Population A (supply)** | Niche B2B newsletters and podcasts, 20–60k engaged subscribers, in expensive verticals — devtools, fintech, healthcare IT, logistics, semiconductors. Enumerable from platform directories; **contactable because every one of them publishes a sponsorship address — it is their entire business model** |
| **Population B (demand)** | B2B advertisers **already observed buying slots in adjacent titles.** She does not guess at demand; she reads it off ads that already ran |
| **Rate — OBSERVED, from the intermediary's own schedule** | **Paved charges publishers 30% on Marketplace bookings and 40% on Ad Network performance earnings.** That is the market-clearing intermediation rate, published by the incumbent |
| **Flow required for $13.000/month** | **$43.333 at 30% · $32.500 at 40%.** At a $5.000 average slot: **~9 placements a month** |
| **Capital** | **Under $500.** A domain, an email sender, a spreadsheet |
| **Licence** | **None, in any of the six markets.** Not a regulated activity anywhere |
| **C4** | Clean. Money flows advertiser → publisher → her commission. **She is never merchant of record and never takes custody** |
| **THE ONBOARDING LAW** | **Passes.** Publisher signs a PDF; advertiser signs an insertion order. **Paved proves the whole transaction runs self-serve with no call** |
| **Expert veto** | **Passes.** Advertisers evaluate the newsletter's audience data. She asserts nothing about herself |
| **Chicken-and-egg** | **Solved, and this is the elegant part: unsold inventory is worth exactly zero once the send goes out.** A publisher joins an empty book at zero cost and zero risk — commission-only, non-exclusive, converted to exclusive *agência* after she has sold twice |

### The arithmetic is tighter than the agent stated, and this is the thing to watch

The agent computed ~14 hours per closed placement against 130 monthly hours. **Against the 78 DELIVERY hours
in `THE_REBUILD.md`, it is 9 hours per closed placement** — to source, pitch, negotiate and close a $5.000
sponsorship, cold, with no name.

> **So the model does not work on new business alone. It works only if ADVERTISERS REBOOK** — a repeat booking
> costs almost no incremental hours, which is what collapses the hour cost and turns a sales job into an asset.
> **The real question is not "can she close nine a month?" It is "what fraction of advertisers book a second
> time?"** That single rate decides the business, and it should be the first thing measured.

**Honest failure mode, stated by the agent and not softened:** Paved already sits at exactly 30% and is
self-serve, so publishers can monetise without her. **Her only wedge is that Paved is passive inbound and she
is outbound.** If her close rate is half what she assumes, the model produces $6.500/month, not $13.000.

## 5. A dead lead worth recording as one

**MANA's own page states its commission survey ran in 1999, 2002 and 2005 and stopped because "participation
in subsequent years dropped below an acceptable level"; the PDF sits behind member authentication.**

> **There is no current, citable, primary-source industrial sales-agency commission rate in the public domain.
> Any figure quoted for "typical rep commission" traces to a members-only 2005 survey or to vendor marketing.
> Do not accept one without a filed contract behind it.**

**This is why industrial agency ranks second, not first:** at an unverifiable 5–10% she needs **$130.000–260.000
a month of brokered flow**, and a manufacturer appoints an agent *for her existing customer relationships* —
which she does not have, and C7 bars the ones she does. **That is the expert veto in disguise.**
