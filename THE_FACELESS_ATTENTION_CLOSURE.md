# FACELESS YOUTUBE — HUNTED AND CLOSED, 4 Sep 2026

**The founder asked for this hunt specifically, after supplying YouTube-automation transcripts. It deserves a
straight answer in either direction, and the answer is no.** But **not for the reason the archive would have
guessed**, and the reasoning that got it wrong is worth more than the verdict.

---

## 1. ★ THE ATTENTION TRILEMMA DOES NOT CLOSE THIS, AND SAYING SO IS THE HONEST STARTING POINT

The standing law says an attention asset has three exits and a faceless operator is barred from all three.
**Its ad-sales blade was derived about a local newsletter whose one sponsor was won in person** — a negotiated
relationship per sponsor. **YouTube Partner Program AdSense revenue is fully programmatic: no sponsor, no
negotiation, no discovery call. That is a fourth door the trilemma never considered.**

**And the deeper point stands: a faceless channel is the ONE attention business C2 actively PERMITS rather than
tolerates.** No camera, no personal brand, no recognition — company-branded by construction. **The constraint
is satisfied, not dodged.** It was right to hunt this rather than assume it away.

It dies on arithmetic, on a tax gate nobody had checked, and on a portfolio requirement her hours forbid.

## 3. ★ THERE IS NO PRIMARY SOURCE FOR YOUTUBE RPM. THAT IS ITSELF THE FINDING.

**Every RPM table on the internet is an SEO blog, a course launch or a tool's marketing** — all three barred by
this archive's evidence rule. Google publishes no per-view payout data; its own advertiser page on video costs
gives bidding *models* and no dollar figures.

**So the judge derived a ceiling from figures Alphabet publishes itself:** YouTube revenues *"surpassed $60
billion across ads and subscriptions"* (Alphabet Q4 2025 CEO letter); assume US$45bn is watch-page advertising
*(UNVERIFIED apportionment)*; creator share at 55% = US$24,75bn; against ~4,38 trillion long-form views/year
*(from a **2017** 1-billion-hours/day figure that could not be re-verified — **UNVERIFIED and stale-low**)*
⇒ **≈US$5,65 per 1.000 views as a GLOBAL AVERAGE CEILING.**

That brackets the folk figures sensibly — ~$1 Brazilian/Indian, ~$4 general English, $12–20 US/UK
finance — **all UNVERIFIED, and the conclusion is invariant across the entire range**, which is why the missing
source does not rescue the candidate.

## 4. ★★ SHORTS IS ARITHMETICALLY CLOSED BY YOUTUBE'S OWN DENOMINATOR

YouTube's press page: **"YouTube Shorts is now averaging over 200 billion daily views"** = **73 trillion/year.**

> **If Shorts were 100% of YouTube's ENTIRE US$60bn and creators took the full 45%, the pool would pay
> US$0,37 per 1.000 views. That is a physically impossible best case.** At a realistic US$0,06–0,15,
> US$2.500/month needs **17 to 42 MILLION Shorts views every month.**

## 5. ★★★ THE TAX GATE NOBODY HAD CHECKED — and it inverts the strategy

**I verified both halves at source today.**

- **YouTube withholds US tax on a non-US creator's earnings from US viewers.** With no treaty the rate is
  *"30% of earnings from viewers in the U.S."*; with no tax info submitted at all, *"up to 24% of your total
  earnings worldwide."* → `support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362`
- **Brazil is NOT in the IRS list of countries with a US income tax treaty in force.** Verified by fetching the
  list and reading the B-section: **Bangladesh, Barbados, Belarus, Belgium, Bulgaria — no Brazil.**
  → `irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z`

> ### ⇒ THE SINGLE HIGHEST-RPM AUDIENCE IS THE ONE TAXED AT 30% AT SOURCE, FOR HER SPECIFICALLY.
> For a US-heavy channel ~90% of revenue is US-sourced ⇒ **~27% of gross vanishes before Brazil sees it.**
> **The whole strategy was "chase the US audience for the RPM." The tax gate points the other way.**

**Two open questions flagged, both for an accountant, both UNVERIFIED:** whether that credits against IRPJ/CSLL
under reciprocity (ADI SRF 28/2000, Lei 9.249/95 art. 26) inside Lucro Presumido; and **AdSense's Brazilian
contracting entity and payment currency — if she is paid in BRL the PIS/COFINS export exemption's *ingresso de
divisas* condition may fail and the domestic 15,5% load applies instead of 8,1%.**

**Corrected bar: US$2.750–3.850/month gross for the US-audience strategy**, not US$2.200–2.800.

## 7. ★★★ THE KILL: SHE CANNOT HOLD ENOUGH LOTTERY TICKETS

**This is structural rather than arithmetic, and it is the actual answer.**

The founder's own transcript supplies the base rate as an admission against interest: *"more than 30 successful
channels, but also **hundreds of total flop channels**"*, and *"three months, close to 500 bucks for six videos
and **only one** blew up."*

> **A 10–20% channel hit rate is a viable business ONLY IF YOU CAN RUN FIVE TO TEN CHANNELS AT ONCE.** That is
> exactly what the man in the transcript does — 30 successes on top of hundreds of attempts, in parallel,
> funded by a tool-subscription business. **A serious channel consumes 40–60 hours a month. At 87 hours a month
> SHE CAN HOLD ONE TICKET AT A TIME.**

## The laws

> ### ★★★ THE PORTFOLIO REQUIREMENT — A LOW HIT RATE IS NOT A RISK, IT IS A CAPACITY REQUIREMENT. COMPUTE HOW MANY SIMULTANEOUS ATTEMPTS THE BASE RATE DEMANDS, THEN CHECK SHE CAN HOLD THEM.
> A 10–20% hit rate is a fine business for someone running ten attempts and a coin-flip disaster for someone
> running one. **The visible operators in any lottery-shaped category are portfolio operators, and their hours
> per attempt are the number that matters — not their success stories.** ⇒ **Ask of every candidate with a hit
> rate below ~50%: how many simultaneous attempts does the base rate require, what does one attempt cost in her
> 87 hours, and can she therefore hold enough?** *(Generalises past YouTube: app launches, product launches,
> prospecting-heavy sales, any speculative-capture archive.)*

> ### ★★★ THE WITHHOLDING GATE — FOR A BRAZILIAN, THE HIGHEST-PAYING AUDIENCE IS THE ONE TAXED HARDEST AT SOURCE.
> **The US withholds 30% on US-source earnings for residents of countries with no income tax treaty, and BRAZIL
> HAS NO US INCOME TAX TREATY** *(verified: the IRS A-to-Z list runs Bangladesh, Barbados, Belarus, Belgium,
> Bulgaria)*. ⇒ **Any candidate whose plan is "earn from US consumers through a US platform" must price
> withholding BEFORE choosing the audience** — it inverts the usual "chase the US RPM" instinct. ⚠ **And check
> the CONTRACTING ENTITY AND PAYMENT CURRENCY: if a platform pays a Brazilian in BRL, the PIS/COFINS export
> exemption's *ingresso de divisas* condition may fail and the domestic 15,5% load replaces the 8,1% export
> rate.** *(Both open questions are for an accountant, and both are cheap to settle.)*

> ### ★★ WHEN NO PRIMARY SOURCE EXISTS FOR A LOAD-BEARING RATE, DERIVE A CEILING FROM PUBLISHED TOTALS AND SHOW IT IS INVARIANT.
> No primary source for YouTube RPM exists — every table is a blog, a course or a tool's marketing.
> **Alphabet's own US$60bn, the published 55% share and a views denominator give ≈US$5,65/1.000 as a global
> ceiling; YouTube's own "200 billion daily views" makes Shorts pay US$0,37/1.000 in a physically impossible
> best case.** ⇒ **Bound the unknown from figures the counterparty publishes about itself, then show the verdict
> holds across the whole plausible range.** A missing source is not a reason to accept a folk number, and not a
> reason to stop.
