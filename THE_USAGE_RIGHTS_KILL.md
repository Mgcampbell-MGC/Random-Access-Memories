# THE USAGE-RIGHTS OVERRUN LEDGER — KILLED, 19 Aug 2026

**The best domain-fit idea generated for her: archive which images were live on which brand pages on which
dates, and sell it to the agencies whose model licences expired. Killed twice independently, and it is a
scissors — the two possible buyers fail in opposite directions.**

---

## 1. ★★ THE KILL — THE TWO-DATES TEST

> ### **The overrun interval is bounded by two dates the rights-holder ALREADY OWNS. The start is the licence expiry date — it is in their own contract. The end is the date of their own screenshot, taken the day they look.**
>
> **An overrun is a CONTINUING STATE, so the normal discovery case has the image still live: present tense,
> free, one screenshot.** The dated archive only adds *"and it was also up on the days in between"* — which
> nobody contests.

**The incumbents state the evidentiary standard themselves.** Pixsy's own guidance to rights-holders:

> *"Take screenshots that clearly show the unauthorized use of your work… **Print the page as a time-stamped
> PDF to secure conclusive evidence of the exact date** that the image was published on the webpage.
> **Additionally, save the webpage to archive.org so it's also held by a third party.**"*

**ImageRights** describes its demand as *"sending out one or more emails and/or letters, **including screenshots
of the Recovery Asset**, license terms, an invoice."* **Nothing in either firm's process needs a historical
series.** That is `THE SUBSET LAW`: producing what the buyer must produce anyway — the contract plus a
screenshot — already produces her artefact.

**And the mirror side fails too.** Selling defensively to the *brand* (*"prove we took it down on time"*) dies on
`THE KNOWLEDGE-LIABILITY TEST` inverted: a finding hands them a breach they now knowingly hold, not money. Their
DAM already logs it — Adobe's own documentation says the expiry field *"ensures that the published asset is
unpublished when it expires, **which prevents license violation**."*

## 3. ★★ THE TWO LAWS — and they are the reason this file keeps the kill

> ### ★★ THE SPECULATIVE-CAPTURE LAW — **an archive must capture BEFORE it has a customer, so screen every archive candidate on THE SIZE OF THE UNIVERSE IT MUST CAPTURE SPECULATIVELY.**
>
> Value accrues only to the tiny subset ever asked about; **cost, blocking and legal exposure scale with the
> whole universe.** The licensor only tells her which pages matter *after* subscribing — by which time the past
> is gone. **So this candidate must speculatively capture the commercial web. `THE_WORKING_DAY_LEDGER` must
> speculatively capture ~17 terminal pages a day. That is the whole difference between them.**
>
> **⚠ COROLLARY, AND IT BITES THE LIVE CANDIDATE TOO: on launch day the archive is empty, so there is no head
> start. Anyone pointing a $29/month screenshot service at the same pages that morning holds the identical
> asset.**

> ### ★★ THE TWO-DATES TEST — **before selling a dated series, ask whether the interval is already bounded by two dates the BUYER owns.**
>
> **Where a breach is a CONTINUING STATE, the present tense is sufficient and history is decoration.** This is
> the sharper, cheaper form of `THE SUBSET LAW`, and it should be run **before any S3 proposal.**

## 4. ★ RUNNING THE TWO LAWS AGAINST CANDIDATE #1 — it passes both, and one caveat is real

| Test | Usage-rights ledger | **THE WORKING-DAY LEDGER** |
|---|---|---|
| **Universe to capture speculatively** | **The commercial web** | **~17–20 terminal pages/day.** Passes cleanly |
| **Is the interval bounded by two dates the buyer owns?** | **Yes** — contract expiry + their own screenshot | **NO.** The buyer owns the invoice and the charge period, **but not which days within it the gate was open.** That fact belongs to a third party and is in the past |
| **Is the breach a continuing state?** | **Yes** — the image is still live; look today | **NO.** A demurrage charge is a **closed historical interval.** There is no present tense to inspect |
| **Access** | 3 of 7 target domains block a plain client | **Verified readable, no login, including the terminal operating system itself** |

> **★ BUT THE COROLLARY LANDS ON #1 AND MUST BE RECORDED: the raw capture is a commodity. Stillio would point
> at the same seventeen pages for $29/month.** What is not commodity is (a) **starting today rather than the day
> a competitor notices**, and (b) turning raw pages into a **per-terminal, per-shift, per-day parsed calendar**
> joined to the carrier tariff definitions. **The archive is the moat only in combination with the parse. The
> screenshots alone are worth $29/month to anybody.**

## 6. ⚠ LEGAL EXPOSURE — worse in Brazil than the US framing suggests

**Lei 9.610/98 art. 29** requires prior express authorisation for *"a reprodução parcial ou integral"*, and
**art. 46 is a CLOSED list** — **Brazil has no general fair use, so the US "thumbnails are transformative"
position has no counterpart in the statute.** Art. 46 VII exempts use *"para produzir prova judiciária ou
administrativa"*, but whether that covers a **commercial archive built years before any proceeding** is
**UNVERIFIED and is exactly the question C5 cannot afford to ask a lawyer.**

**The one distinction that survives: a perceptual HASH is not a reprodução; a thumbnail is.** A hash-only ledger
is defensible — and cannot show a human what the image was, which is what a demand letter needs. **And she must
copy speculatively, before knowing whose work it is, so authorisation always arrives after the copying.**

speculative-capture problem, smaller universe. Not proposed — flagged.**
