# CANDIDATE 70 — the written memorial, sold wholesale to funeral homes

**17 Aug 2026. Eight agents converge. This is the first candidate in the file where every finding points
the same way instead of cancelling out.**

> **Sell a human-written memorial narrative to funeral homes as a wholesale product, delivered as a
> document, from a written questionnaire, on a deadline.**
>
> **No Etsy. No printing. No shipping. No border. No inventory. No physical object at all.**

## 1. The finding that makes it a candidate

Agent 6 mapped the entire death-admin and funeral-aftercare supply chain — twelve consumer incumbents and
roughly twenty vendors selling into funeral homes — and returned this:

> **"Funeral directors demonstrably WILL sell a written life story (Life Story Network proves demand and
> willingness), the channel demonstrably DOES accept third-party memorial vendors (Eterneva through
> Batesville), and yet nobody has built the vendor. That is the actual finding."**

Three legs, each independently verified.

### Leg 1 — funeral homes already sell written life stories, and charge for them

**Life Story Network** (Kalamazoo, MI), their own words: *"Each life has a story. Life Story Funeral Homes
are the only ones who write that story so it can be shared, saved, and passed on from generation to
generation."* The service includes **the reading of the Life Story** at the service.

**But it is a brand licence and a staff-training system, not a vendor.** The funeral home's own trained
staff writes it — *"our staff is trained to focus on the life that was lived."* Scale, counted from their
own directory: **~39 funeral-home businesses, ~60 locations, 8 states.** One member listed defunct
(1933–2018). Their funeral-home-facing site is nearly contentless — they are not recruiting.

**Price: bundled, never itemised.** Heritage Life Story packages run **$2.300–6.800**, with the Life Story
inside. Tellingly, on the cheapest tier the family is told to **write it themselves.**

**And the white-label posture that works here is already named, by Full-Circle:**

That is exactly the wholesale relationship the parts bin's **D3/D4** describe, in the channel's own words.

## 3. What the red team destroyed, and what it saved

**Agent 10's verdict on the Etsy book: FALSE as written.** Not because a faceless Brazilian cannot sell on
Etsy — **the faceless, no-phone, no-photo and POD parts are all SOUND** — but because two clauses cannot
coexist: made-to-order as that plan defined it, and 4–20 orders a month.

### The three fatal mechanics, all from Etsy's own documentation

1. **"Processing times begin the day after your item is purchased by a buyer."** Max processing time is
   capped at **10 weeks** (verified in Etsy's OpenAPI spec: `minimum 1, maximum 10`, unit days or weeks).
   Personalisation is capped at **5 fields per listing**, so a 28-question intake must happen off-platform
   after payment. **The Etsy buyer is the giver; the labour is the mother's.** If she stalls, the order can
   never ship and the giver wins a non-delivery case 7 days after max EDD. Breach costs search visibility,
   Purchase Protection eligibility, and ultimately *"selling privileges may be temporarily or permanently
   revoked."*
2. **AI disclosure is mandatory.** Etsy: *"Seller-prompted AI creations must disclose the use of AI."*
   Disclose and the premium dies; don't and the shop does.
3. **Purchase Protection covers up to $250; the rest is charged to the seller** — including when *"their
   item matches the listing description and photos, but a buyer claims it does not."* **The buyer is not
   required to return the item.** On an $899 book: Etsy covers $250, she is charged **$649**, and the buyer
   keeps a hardcover containing a stranger's ultrasound scans with zero salvage value. The case-rate metric
   *"doesn't include orders over $250"* — the entire regime is calibrated sub-$250.

### Corrections to this file

- **Contribution at $149 is $107,80, not $116** — before tracked outbound shipping, which the file omitted.
  **~$85 with an Offsite Ads attribution.**
- **Brazil Etsy Payments processing is 6,5% + $0,30 versus 3% + $0,25 for a US seller** — a permanent
  3,5-point handicap. Full stack ≈13,2% base, ≈28% with Offsite Ads.
- **Offsite Ads, parts bin D1 is wrong:** 15% *below* the threshold, **12% at or above**; the threshold is
  **$10.000 in any 365-day period**; below it opt-out is allowed, at or above **"required to participate
  for the lifetime of your shop."** She would cross it in ~2 months.
- **Lulu cannot be pinned to a plant.** *"Customers cannot choose their printing facility"* — France,
  India, Australia, UK, Canada, US. Etsy allows one origin per profile. Every misroute breaks the EDD.
- **Etsy is growing, not declining** — Q2 2026 Marketplace GMS **$2,6B, +7,5%**, third consecutive growth
  quarter, 87M buyers. **The real signal is extraction: take rate 25,9%, +130bps, alongside a 12% workforce
  cut.** A healthy channel taking a rising share with fewer humans to adjudicate disputes.
- **GMS per active buyer is $124 per year, platform-wide.** A $299 first purchase is 2,4× a typical buyer's
  entire annual Etsy spend; $899 is 7,3×.
- **My "no phone is a dispute problem" fear FAILS.** Etsy cases are entirely written and in-platform, and
  Etsy support *"won't make unrequested phone calls."* No procedural disadvantage.
- **My "Etsy computes delivery from São Paulo" fear FAILS.** EDD uses the shipping profile's
  `origin_country_iso`, not shop location. **But shop location IS displayed to foreign buyers and IS a
  promoted search filter** keyed on shop location regardless of shipping origin. Magnitude **UNVERIFIED**
  and the agent refused to invent one.

## 5. The four real risks, named

1. **The chatbot.** A funeral home can generate this itself, and **Tribute Technology already
   auto-generates it from obituary data.** Her entire premium is *"a human wrote this."* The only evidence
   that premium holds is the Association of Ghostwriters' finding that 61% of writers use AI but **only 7%
   use it to generate content** — a perception with an expiry date, not a moat. **Anyone building here is
   racing a clock.**
2. **Direct cremation removes the ceremony**, and a eulogy is read at a ceremony. The cremation shift helps
   the memorial *artefact* and may cut the eulogy *occasion*. These two findings pull opposite ways and the
   net is unresolved. *(Partial offset: NFDA finds 58,3% have attended a funeral at a non-traditional
   location — the ritual is decoupling from the building, not vanishing.)*
3. **C1 at the channel level.** Aftercare.com sells at $150/month with *"no contracts,"* which proves
   low-friction purchase exists — but whether a funeral director will onboard a vendor **entirely in
   writing** is unverified. This is the sales-motion risk and it is the one that killed the referral play.
4. **The funeral home may want the writing in-house.** Life Story Network works precisely *because* the
   director's own staff writes it and gets the relationship. **Counter-evidence, and it is strong:
   Full-Circle sells into the same channel on the explicit promise *"We do the hard work. You get the
   credit."*** White-label is an established posture here.
