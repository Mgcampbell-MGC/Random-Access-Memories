**The hunt:** deliverables whose correctness the buyer can check **objectively, themselves, without trusting
her.** This attacks the expert veto head-on — *"anything requiring her to be believed as an expert"* — by

## 1. ★★ THE TWO LAWS

> ### **★ THE FREE-VALIDATOR SCREEN — run it BEFORE anything else.**
>
> Of seventeen objective-acceptance-test spaces examined, **the commonest cause of death by a wide margin is
> that the body which DEFINES the standard also PUBLISHES A FREE VALIDATOR**: EPUBCheck, Ace, Pinnacle 21
> Community, the EN 16931 Schematron, Google Merchant Center diagnostics, GFW Pro.
>
> **This is structural, not accidental — a standards body's job is to make conformance testable, so it gives
> the test away.** *(It is `THE RENEWAL SCISSORS` and `THE FREE FRONT DOOR` arriving at the same place from a
> third direction.)*
>
> **★ The corollary is the useful half: SELL WHERE THE CHECK IS FREE BUT THE RULE IS SCATTERED.** A rule
> spread across a statute, four rate tables, a taxonomy of deductions and six years of history has no owner
> with a reason to ship the tool.

> ### **★★ THE KNOWLEDGE-LIABILITY TEST — a new screen, and it belongs beside `THE CONSTRAINT-SIDE LAW`.**
>
> **An objective acceptance test makes a deliverable SELLABLE IN WRITING. It does not make it WANTED.**
>
> Where the finding creates a legal duty to self-correct — minimum-wage arrears, duplicate payments already
> written off, royalty underpayments, a bordereau that was wrong for three years — **the buyer's rational
> response to a perfect detector is to look away.**
>
> ### **ASK OF EVERY RECONCILIATION AND COMPLETENESS CANDIDATE: "WHEN THIS FINDS SOMETHING, DOES THE BUYER GET MONEY, OR A PROBLEM?"**
>
> **Money:** duplicate-payment detection · carrier invoice recomputation · commission reconciliation · royalty
> recomputation. **A problem:** NMW recomputation · holiday-pay recomputation · bordereaux conformance · SDS
> completeness.
>
> **That is a sharper split than price, than pool size, and than any constraint in the file.**

## 3. THE LEAD — UK NMW recomputation, sold as a LICENSED ENGINE

**The acceptance test, in the buyer's words:** *"Pick any twenty rows. Look up the rate on gov.uk for that date
and that worker's age, divide their NMW pay by their NMW hours yourself. Twenty of twenty reproduce. Then run
it over a period HMRC has already reviewed and closed: it must return zero FAILs."* **A historical replay and a
machine check at once. No credential, no opinion, no résumé.**

**The exposure is primary-sourced.** Department for Business and Trade, NMW Naming Scheme **Round 23**
educational bulletin (19 Mar 2026): **389 employers named, £7,3m arrears, £12,6m penalties.** Derived from
those figures: **~£18.766 average arrears and ~£32.391 average penalty — roughly £51.000 per named employer**,
plus the naming. Penalties run at 200% of arrears; NMW is strict liability; records kept **six years**.

**And DBT publishes the failure taxonomy — every category is deterministic arithmetic, not judgement:**

| Cause | Employers named |
|---|---:|
| Deductions taking pay below NMW (uniform, PPE, parking, salary sacrifice, training, till shortages) | **157** |
| Unpaid working time (pre/post shift, rounded clock-ins, travel, trial shifts, sleep-ins) | **130** |
| Wrong apprentice rate | **71** |
| Uprated rate not applied in the first PRP after 1 April, or after an 18/20/21 birthday | **57** |
| Accommodation offset above the statutory rate | **26** |
| Worker-status / work-type errors | **6** |
| Other | **62** |

**★ Is it already free? Partially — and the incumbent documents its own gaps.** Staffology's payroll guide
states its built-in NMW check computes on **NIC rules** (the wrong basis), that **net deductions *"will not
currently reduce the pay amount"***, that tips and premium pay *"may incorrectly inflate the pay amount"*, and
that ***"employers are responsible for verifying NMW compliance independently."*** Sage's knowledge base
confirms salary-sacrifice pension deductions are **not** included by default. **The clearing party ships an
incomplete check and says so. The 157 deduction cases and 130 unpaid-time cases are precisely those gaps.**

**★ The architecture is the interesting part: a LICENSED ENGINE THAT RUNS ON THE CUSTOMER'S OWN MACHINE.**
The UK has **no adequacy finding for Brazil** — a UK→BR transfer needs an IDTA plus a transfer risk assessment.
So the payroll file never crosses a border and **never reaches her at all.** **C4 solved by architecture, GDPR
solved by architecture, and the transfer objection never arises.** *(Same move `THE_SEAM.md` identified as the
only agency-family variant that cleared the hour ceiling.)*

**Arithmetic.** Build 250–400 h once; **~0,25 h/month per licence** after. **21 licences at £399/month or 11 at
£750** clears the target — **5,25 h/month of delivery against a 78 h budget.** *(USD/GBP ~1,34, approximate.)*

**The seeded-defect benchmark, which replaces every claim about her:** ~50.000 synthetic worker-pay-period rows
across weekly/fortnightly/monthly PRPs over six years of real published rates, with planted instances of each
of the eight DBT categories — birthdays crossing 18/20/21 mid-PRP, apprentices passing month 12, a £4,80
uniform deduction, accommodation £1 above the offset, 7-minute clock-in rounding, sleep-ins, a salary sacrifice
that dips one worker below in one month only. **Publish: *"detected X of Y seeded breaches across 8 categories;
Z false positives on 40.000 clean rows."***

## 4. ★★ WHY THE LEAD IS ON THE WRONG SIDE OF ITS OWN LAW — and the test that settles it

**The knowledge-liability objection is not a footnote here; it may be fatal.** Finding a breach obliges
self-correction across six years **plus a 200% penalty**. A rational employer with real exposure may prefer not
to look — **so demand is suppressed exactly where the product is most valuable.**

**And it is worse on the wholesale route than direct.** A bureau that runs this across its book may discover
breaches **in payroll it processed itself**, creating its own professional-indemnity claim. **The bureau's
incentive to buy may be NEGATIVE.**

**Two further honest kills:** the ceiling holder has the credential — Fair Work Solutions is founded by people
with HMRC NMW enforcement and Big 4 backgrounds, and what is bought in this market is partly *defensibility in
front of an inspector*, which is bought from a name. And **supplier assurance**: even a locally-run licence
bought by a firm that touches payroll data triggers a vendor security questionnaire. `THE ONBOARDING LAW`,
still standing, though C1's relaxation makes it friction rather than a wall.

> **THE TEST, BEFORE A LINE OF CODE — free, no phone, ~2 hours.** Email 40 UK payroll bureaux one question:
> ***"if a tool showed you which of your clients' workers fell below NMW in any pay period in the last six
> years, would you want to know?"*** — **and count "yes" against "we'd rather the client ran that themselves."**
> **That measures the knowledge-liability objection directly.** It is the same shape as every good test in this
> file: twenty-to-forty real instances, count how many clear a bar.
