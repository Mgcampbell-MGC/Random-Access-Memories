Directed by the file's own highest-yield instruction: **hunt poster ≠ invoice-issuer splits inside

## KILL 1 — EU261 flight-delay compensation. ★★ THE BURDEN-OF-PROOF SCREEN *(new, 30 seconds)*

A mature contingency-fee recovery industry (AirHelp, Flightright, Bott & Co) on ~25–35% success fees —
exactly the "recovery industry already sitting there" this file demands. It dies before any data question:

> **"The burden of proof is on the airline, not the passenger… Passengers do not need to provide evidence
> to establish their claim; the airline must affirmatively prove that extraordinary circumstances apply."**

> ### **THE BURDEN-OF-PROOF SCREEN — ask WHO BEARS THE BURDEN. Where the DEFENDANT must prove the exculpatory
> fact, the claimant needs no evidence at all, so no evidence market can exist on the claimant's side.**

Sharper than `THE DSAR FRONT DOOR`, which says discovery is free. This says **discovery is unnecessary.**

**★ AND THE INVERSION IT HANDS YOU: the evidence market flips to the DEFENDANT.** The airline must prove an
ATC regulation was live, that weather closed the field, that the measure was unavoidable — per claim, often
years later. *(Not pursued: the buyer is a global airline, i.e. enterprise sales, i.e. C1. Recorded because
the inversion is reusable — **when the burden sits on the defendant, ask what the DEFENDANT must buy.**)*

**★ AND IT VINDICATES CANDIDATE #1 rather than threatening it:** the FMC's own guidance names *screen
captures of gate closures* as evidence the **complainant** brings. The burden there is on the claimant, which
is the side of the line that supports an evidence market.

## KILL 2 — Inland rail ramp storage under a through bill of lading. The split was REAL; erasure was not.

The tournament flagged this as the best unexplored column. **The legal hook is genuine** — Union Pacific's own
tariff (announcement PR2023-141, 1 Dec 2023, ITC §C.12, effective 1 Feb 2024), fetched and quoted:

> *"If an Intermodal Terminal is closed for an entire calendar day for any reason, that day will not be
> included in the Free Time or Storage Charge calculations."*

**Terminal closure is the denominator of the storage charge** — binary, machine-checkable, `46 CFR 541.6`'s
shape in a private tariff. And under a through B/L the **ocean carrier** issues the invoice while the
**railroad** posts the closure: **poster ≠ issuer, confirmed.**

**It dies anyway, on a five-minute fetch.** UP archives its closure notices permanently:

| Announcement | Posted | Fetched 21 Aug 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| `IM2020-82` — Yard Center IL closed, Dolton street barricades | **1 Jun 2020** | **HTTP 200**, 38.475 B, verbatim |
| `IM2020-140` — Houston ramps closed, Hurricane Laura | **25 Aug 2020** | **HTTP 200**, 38.787 B, verbatim |
| `PR2023-141` — the storage tariff itself | 1 Dec 2023 | **HTTP 200**, 39.703 B |

Six years and two months, at stable URLs, on **sequential integers** (`IM2020-82`, `IM2020-140`) — so the
whole back series is enumerable by anyone in an afternoon. `THE DATE-STAMPED FILENAME IS A BACK-FILL
INVITATION`, in its purest form: not even a date to guess, just a counter.

## ★★ THE UNIFICATION — THE SPLIT TEST AND THE ERASURE MOAT ARE THE SAME TEST

Why does UP keep six years of one-off closure notices while YTI erases in seven days?

> **Because UP's own tariff makes closed days non-billable, UP must be able to prove which days were closed
> — to defend its own invoices. YTI has no invoice to defend; the ocean carrier issues it.**

> ### **POSTER = ISSUER ⇒ the poster archives permanently to defend its billing ⇒ NO MOAT.**
> ### **POSTER ≠ ISSUER ⇒ nobody has a reason to retain it ⇒ ERASURE COMES FREE.**

These were being run as two independent screens, with erasure checked by hand each time. **They are one
screen, and the split PREDICTS the erasure.** This is `THE INCORPORATED-SCHEDULE COROLLARY` generalised from
"the poster archives to defend its invoices" to its contrapositive — **and it explains candidate #1's moat
structurally instead of merely observing it.**

## KILL 3 / FIND — chassis per diem. Not a business; a SECOND BUYER for the archive that already exists.

Chassis pools (TRAC, DCLI, CCM) bill **$12–55/day** from their own records — poster = issuer, so the pool's
own billing data is not archivable value. **But the world-state fact that clears a disputed charge — whether
the approved return location was accepting that day — belongs to the TERMINAL.** Poster ≠ issuer again, and
it is published on **the same pages already being captured**, at zero marginal cost.

Measured demand signals *(SECONDARY — trade/vendor sources, flagged)*: **per-diem disputes run a 15–20% audit
hit rate**; drayage is described as **the highest error-rate freight mode**; automated drayage audit recovers
**3–5% of drayage spend.**

⚠ **The caution that comes with it:** the market's own recommended fix is *"an independent GPS device gives
you an audit trail nobody can argue with"* — i.e. **claimant-conduct evidence, self-supplied.**
`THE CLAIMANT-EVIDENCE LAW` therefore applies here exactly as it does to candidate #1, and the same 30-email
probe must resolve it. **Add per-diem auditors and drayage 3PLs to that probe's recipient list — they are a
larger population than ocean D&D recovery firms.**
