# Brazil → USA / EU import-export, hunted — 17 Aug 2026

**At the founder's instruction: a physical import-export model, female-friendly, ~$7.500 of product
capital, recycle the cash. Explicitly relaxing C5 ($300) and the standing veto on inventory, to see
whether anything is there.**

> **Verdict: the trade window is real and the timing finding is genuinely good. The business fails
> the founder's own four cash-flow keys harder than anything else on the board — 0,5 of 4. But it
> produced one law worth more than the candidate, and that law says the model's single best property
> is available without buying any inventory at all.**

---

## 1. THE FINDING — two doors moved in opposite directions in twelve months

This is the cleanest "why now" this file has produced.

### The USA slammed shut

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **5 Aug 2025** | **40% IEEPA tariff** on Brazilian goods signed 30 July, plus the **10% reciprocal** tariff = **50% total** |
| **29 Aug 2025** | **$800 de minimis exemption eliminated for ALL countries.** Every parcel, any value, owes duty and needs customs processing |
| **Nov 2025** | EO carves out **238 agricultural tariff lines** from the 40%. Food partly spared; non-food is not |
| **24 Jun 2026** | **CBP Interim Final Rules indefinitely suspend** the de minimis exemption for all modes, and create a new informal entry and bonding process for international mail |
| **22 Jul 2026** | **New 25% ad valorem Section 301 tariff** on many Brazilian-origin goods, under Trade Act §§ 301(b) and 304(a) |

**Measured effect, not projection:** Brazilian footwear exports fell **3,6% in volume and 23,6% in
revenue** in H1 2026 versus H1 2025.

> **Brazil → USA is structurally dead for non-agricultural consumer goods.** 50% plus a possible 25%
> on top, plus duty on every single parcel. No product margin survives that.

### The EU swung open

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **25 Feb 2026** | **Brazil completes ratification** of the EU-Mercosur Partnership Agreement |
| 27 Feb 2026 | Argentina and Uruguay ratify |
| 17 Mar 2026 | Paraguay ratifies — all four Mercosur states done |
| **23 Mar 2026** | **The EU notifies Mercosur of provisional application of the interim Trade Agreement** |

**The caveat, and it is real:** on 21 Jan 2026 the European Parliament adopted a resolution asking the
**CJEU for an opinion** on the agreement's compatibility with the EU Treaties, which **suspends
Parliament's ratification for 16–18 months.** So provisional application is proceeding under live
legal challenge.

**And a counter-current on the EU side:** the Council voted **13 Nov 2025** to phase out the **€150 de
minimis**, rollout from **1 July 2026** — a flat **€3 per HS code** on low-value parcels initially,
moving to standard tariff rates once fully implemented. Import VAT has applied from €0 since July 2021.

> **So: the American door closed and the European door opened, in the same twelve months, and both
> are verifiable to the day. That is a real arbitrage window. It is also the last good news in this
> document.**

## 2. What the EU structure actually requires

Two paths, and both bite.

### Path A — bulk import, hold stock in the EU

- Under the **Union Customs Code**, a non-EU business must have an **EU-resident representative** for
  customs clearance and import VAT wherever the importer cannot present an EORI tied to EU residency.
- The **IOR or appointed fiscal representative owns the EORI** — *"not typically a task a forwarder or
  3PL takes on unless contracted to act as importer of record directly."*
- **More than half of the 27 member states oblige** a non-EU business to appoint a fiscal
  representative. **Germany and the Czech Republic have withdrawn that requirement** — that is the
  opening. Italy is the opposite extreme: mandatory rep, financial guarantees, quarterly filings.
- Holding stock inside the EU requires **VAT registration in the country of storage**, then
  pan-EU selling via **OSS**.

**Verdict on Path A:** it exists, and Germany is the door. It is also an ongoing paid compliance
relationship with filings, on top of 3PL fees, freight, duty and VAT — against $7.500 of total capital.

### Path B — direct to consumer from Brazil

- **IOSS** covers consignments under €150; a non-EU seller must register **through an intermediary.**
- Per-parcel duty now applies (€3/HS code, rising to standard rates).
- **Transit from Brazil is 3–8 weeks by Correios, with no practical return path.**

**Verdict on Path B:** no importer of record needed, and it dies on customer experience. Etsy penalises
late delivery, and a customer in Munich will not wait six weeks for a bikini they cannot send back.

## 3. Scored against the founder's own four keys

These are the criteria he gave me two turns ago. This is the worst score anything has received.

| Key | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring demand | **FAIL** | One-off product sales. Consumables would recur — and consumables are food or cosmetics, i.e. FDA/MoCRA/EU novel-food regulation |
| High margins | **UNPROVEN, probably thin** | Landed cost = product + freight + duty + import VAT + 3PL + Etsy fees. Import-export gross margins are a volume game |
| **Low reinvestment** | **FAIL BY DEFINITION** | *"Recycle cash"* **is** the reinvestment treadmill. Every unit of growth is another unit of stock. This is the exact opposite of key 3 |
| Operator leverage | **FAIL** | Sales scale with inventory and with her hands. No software does the work |

**0,5 of 4.** The book scores 2. Fiverr's channel scores 2.

### And the constraint collisions

| | Problem |
|---|---|
| **C1 — never speaks** | This is the hardest C1 test in the whole file. Fiscal-representative onboarding, customs brokers, freight forwarders — and Brazilian suppliers, who work by **WhatsApp voice** as the default. Written-only sourcing is possible with some suppliers; it is not the norm |
| **C3 — no contractors** | A fiscal rep and a 3PL are vendors, same class as Lulu, so C3 probably survives. Recorded, not a kill |
| **C5 relaxed to $7.500** | But $7.500 is not *risk* capital for her. It is most likely a large share of everything, and inventory that does not sell has no salvage value. **There is no second attempt** |
| **Crash timing** | She would be holding EU-facing discretionary stock precisely when EU discretionary demand contracts |

## 4. THE ONE THING WORTH KEEPING — and it is worth a lot

The model's single best feature has nothing to do with importing.

Her costs are in **BRL**. Revenue would be in **EUR**. In a global crisis the real has historically
depreciated hard against hard currency — roughly **−38%** through 2008 (1,55 → 2,50) and **−32%** in
2020 (4,0 → 5,9).

> **A global crash EXPANDS the gross margin of a BRL-cost, hard-currency-revenue business.**

That is a genuine countercyclical hedge, and almost nothing else in this file has it.

> **THE CURRENCY DIRECTION LAW — earn in a hard currency, spend in a soft one. For a founder whose
> costs are in BRL, any business billing in USD or EUR receives a free margin expansion in exactly
> the crisis that damages everyone else. This is a property of the BILLING CURRENCY, not of the
> product. Every candidate from here on gets scored on it.**

### The corollary, which is the actual zag

**She does not need to import anything to get this property.** Any business that bills in USD or EUR
already has it:

- The book business: Etsy receipts in **USD**, her labour in **BRL**. Already hedged.
- The Fiverr channel: receipts in **USD**, labour in **BRL**. Already hedged.

> **The import-export model's best feature is available without the inventory, without the tariffs,
> without a fiscal representative, and without $7.500 at risk. The currency arbitrage is in the
> BILLING, not in the CARGO.**

---

## 5. Verdict

**Do not build it.** Not because the window isn't real — it is, and it is precisely dated — but
because:

1. It fails **three of the founder's own four keys**, one of them by definition.
2. It converts a $300 no-loss experiment into a **$7.500 single-shot bet with no salvage value.**
3. It is the hardest C1 collision in the record, at the sourcing layer where she has least leverage.
4. And its one great property — the currency hedge — she **already has** in two candidates that
   require no capital.

### What to do with the finding instead

The tariff asymmetry is genuinely valuable information, just not as a goods business. Worth noting for
later: **anything she sells to the USA now faces a 50%+ tariff wall if it is a physical Brazilian
good** — which, incidentally, is another argument for the print-on-demand structure, where the object
is manufactured **in the destination country** and never crosses a border at all. Lulu printing in the
US for a US buyer pays no Brazilian tariff, because nothing Brazilian is imported. Only the text
crosses, and text is not cargo.

**That is the quiet structural advantage nobody noticed until the tariff question was asked.**
