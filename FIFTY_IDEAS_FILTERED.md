## 1. His filter is right, and it is ours

He states it up front and it is the correct standard:

> *"Businesses where the buyer already feels the pain, already spends money, and already understands
> the outcome."*

That is **sell into an existing budget line** — the same rule this file arrived at after sixty-seven
candidates. **No disagreement on method.** The disagreement is entirely about what a *faceless
solo operator who never speaks and holds no credentials* can execute.

### Why 38 die on the same word

Every high-value idea on his list works by **living inside the client's stack**: their CRM, their
inbox, their helpdesk, their Slack, their spreadsheets, their Google profile, their ATS, their
booking system.

> **That is the integration tax, and this list is a fifty-item proof of it.**
>
> His most valuable ideas are valuable *because* they are wired in. Sol's model requires never
> being wired in. **The same property that makes them worth $5.000 a month makes them impossible for
> her.**

## 3. The five that survive on shape, and why four still fail

| Idea | Why it survives | Why it still dies |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer research sprint** (#42) | Public data in, report out. No access | **Chat-window test.** Anyone can point an AI at their own reviews. And it must be *believed* — the expertise veto |
| **Customer feedback report** (#32) | Their tickets in, themes out | Same. And the tickets are usually inside a helpdesk |
| **Deal research desk** (#19) | Public data in, memo out. Genuinely access-free | **Trust gate at its maximum.** Nobody buys research underpinning a financial decision from an anonymous stranger. Investors buy known analysts |
| **Audit prep** (#21) | Evidence mapping, document-shaped | The evidence lives in their systems, and it is a compliance sale — the buyer wants somebody accountable |
| **RFP / tender response** (#20) | The tender is public, their boilerplate is sent as files, output is a document, **and the deadline is dated** | **Already assessed.** A 60-page response is 4–8 hours of founder labour, caps at ~15/month, and a company betting a $2M contract will want to meet someone |

**The RFP one is the closest miss on the whole list**, and it was the operator council seat's own
runner-up. It fails on labour and trust, not on shape.

## 4. What is genuinely worth taking

### a) Three of his ideas independently confirm candidate 66

**#26 software switch service · #40 CRM cleanup and migration · #29 spreadsheet-to-app.**

All three are the same observation from a different direction:

> **A migration is the moment messy data becomes urgent.** *"Switching tools sounds easy until the
> team has years of messy data, duplicate records, random templates, and nobody wants to own the
> cleanup."*

**That is candidate 66's central thesis, arrived at independently by someone with no knowledge of
this file.** Our version — *sell to the launch event, never to the mess* — was confirmed by the
merchant council seat and by the red team, and now by a third unrelated source.

**It is the strongest external validation candidate 66 has received.**

### b) One line worth stealing for the sales page

From his audit-prep idea, and it is the best sentence in the video:

> *"The buyer is not paying for a cute automation. They're paying to **reduce risk, save expensive
> team time, and avoid scrambling when a customer, investor, or auditor asks for proof**."*

**That reframing belongs on candidate 66's page.** Nobody buys "a cleaned catalogue." They buy not
scrambling five weeks before a launch.

### c) His closing pattern is what 66 already is

> *"The path to a million is not random 50 prompts. It is **one valuable business system repeated**."*

Correct — and it is why the rule library, the validation layer and the monitor matter more than the
individual job.

## 5. The honest verdict

**No new candidate.** Fifty ideas produced zero that Sol can run, because they share one property
she cannot have: **they are all installed inside somebody else's business.**

**That is not a criticism of the list.** It is a good list, for an operator who can take a call, sign
an MSA, hold an API key and hire a second person. **It is a precise description of the business Sol
is not building.**

**What it did produce is worth more than a candidate:** independent confirmation that the migration
event is the moment to sell into, and a better sentence for the page.

> **The next move is unchanged: thirty dollars, one volunteer, one printed book.**
