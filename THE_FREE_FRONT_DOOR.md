# THE FREE FRONT DOOR — why "knowing which part" is not a business

**17 Aug 2026. S2 hunt, agent 4 of 6. The occupancy check on the replenishment middleman position.**

> **The position is not merely crowded. It is NON-MONETISABLE STANDALONE, and the evidence names the
> business model as a line item in a Grainger flyer.**

---

## 1. THE LAW

> **THE FREE FRONT DOOR — "knowing which one" is never a revenue line. It is the customer-acquisition cost
> of whoever holds the stock. Every incumbent gives it away, because the margin is in the product that
> follows. A business whose entire value is knowing WHICH has no product to follow — so it is selling the
> thing everyone else uses as bait.**

**Three independent confirmations, from three directions:**

**1 · The giants sell it as a service line.** From a Grainger **KeepStock Replenish** flyer the agent
extracted and read in full (doc ID W-CTKS174 G0184), the assigned Grainger resource will:

> *"Restock Grainger products in designated areas • Handle Grainger purchasing and purchase orders •
> Support spot buys • Expedite invoices and inquiries • **Source hard-to-find items** • **Cross-reference
> Grainger products to add**"*

and on setup: *"Your dedicated Grainger rep will work with you to determine the products and levels that
need managing."*

**That is "identify once, record the spec, reorder forever," staffed by a human, sold by Grainger.** Tiered
as CMI (self-serve tools) → Vending → Onsite (*"dedicated resource with daily presence"*) → Managed. *(One
crack: the footer says KeepStock is *"subject to customer eligibility and agreements"* — there is a spend
threshold, and it is **unpublished, UNVERIFIED.**)*

**2 · Free auto-reorder is now table stakes everywhere.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grainger** | Auto-Reorder, self-serve, choose frequency, billed only on ship, *"cancel any Auto-Reorder without penalty"* |
| **Zoro** (Grainger's no-rep arm) | **Order Subscription** — weekly / bi-weekly / monthly, self-serve, launched 28 Aug 2019 |
| **Fastenal** | FAST Solutions vending *"free of charge on a simple and straightforward service agreement"* |
| **RS** | ScanStock (VMI), VendStock, ControlStock |
| **Amazon Business** | Subscribe & Save, plus **Restock Managed Inventory** where *"technicians restock… and can automatically reorder items"* (announced 24 Sept 2024) |

**3 · The closest analogue to the idea beats it, and says so.** **CrossFilters.com** offers **"Free manual
cross-referencing"** answered *"within one business day"* — and their stated differentiator is
**"Real inventory, not just data."**

> **That is an explicit swipe at the data-only position.** The incumbent has already named the weakness and
> priced it at zero.

**And the negative evidence is as strong as the positive:** the agent searched **Fiverr and Upwork for
part-identification and part-sourcing gigs and found none.** The "parts" gigs are CAD and 3D modelling.
**Nobody sells this anywhere, at any price, because it is not a product.**

> CrossFilters 1-888-630-4430 · Cleaning Parts Direct 866-766-5553 · Zoro 855-289-9676.

**What the small specialists actually compete on, ranked by evidence:** stock and same-day dispatch ·

instrument) · relationship · **phone** · and technical knowledge, **given away free as acquisition cost.**

> **Strip out the stock, the van, the grinder and the phone, and what remains is precisely the component
> every incumbent hands over for nothing.**

## 3. Category verdicts, least bad to worst

**1 · Thermal labels and ribbons — the only plausible crack, and it is narrow.**
Why it is least bad: media selection is genuinely confusing (facestock, adhesive, size, core diameter,
wound-in versus wound-out, direct thermal versus thermal transfer, and ribbon chemistry that must be matched
to the facestock); **there is no dominant free cross-reference tool** the way filterxref exists for filters;
the spec is stable and repeats forever once set; it is **fully describable in writing**, so written-only is
not disqualifying; and the incumbents are **regional MPS firms with technicians** (ExtenData T-MPS,
Secur-Serv MPowerPrint, NDD Orbix), so a remote operator is not competing against a van.
Why it may still fail: Zebra and Honeywell dealer channels plus Amazon cover stock sizes, custom sizes
require converter MOQs, and stock-size media is a thin-margin commodity.

**2 · Industrial filters — closed.** Free cross-referencing everywhere, including free *manual* crossing in
one business day, a saturated subscription layer (Filterbuy, Discount Filters, Remember the Filter,
Filtrete…), and the incumbent advantage is same-day stock she cannot hold.

**3 · Batteries — closed, plus a physical blocker.** Free finders down to VIN level. And **lithium cells are
UN3480/UN3481 dangerous goods while lead-acid is heavy freight** — both make faceless cross-border
drop-shipping structurally bad and both favour local suppliers.

**4 · Machine wear parts and blades — closed and mismatched.** Incumbents own grinding and resharpening
loops. **The knowledge that matters is metallurgy and tolerance, communicated through drawings and
engineering conversations** — not knowing a number.

**5 · Safety consumables — worst, and treated as disqualifying:**

> *"A faceless offshore merchant of record specifying respirator cartridge change schedules is inserting
> itself into an OSHA-mandated written program (29 CFR 1910.134) where **being identifiable and accountable
> IS the product.**"*

### CROSS-AGENT CONTRADICTION, resolved

**Agent 2 ranked formaldehyde respirator cartridges as its strongest row** — per-shift replacement,
no service incumbent, downloadable CLIA register. **Agent 4 ranks safety consumables as the worst and
disqualifying.**

**Agent 4 is right, and the reason is the Stamp Law.** A written respiratory protection program has a named
accountable person, and 1910.134(d)(3)(iii)(B) requires the employer to implement a change schedule *"based
on objective information or data."* **Supplying the cartridge is fine; being the party whose judgement sets
the schedule is the stamp, and she cannot hold it.** Agent 2 measured the mandate correctly and did not
apply the accountability test.

## 4. The structural conflicts that sit underneath all of it

**Merchant of record with no stock has exactly two configurations, and both bite:**

1. **The manufacturer drop-ships domestically under her name** — which requires a trade account, credit
   references, and **per-state US resale certificates**, and suppliers *"don't have to accept your
   certificate."* Whether any manufacturer grants that to a Brazil-resident, zero-employee entity is
   **UNVERIFIED** — the agent found drop-ship reseller programmes exist but confirmed no requirements.
2. **Goods cross a border on every order** — which **destroys the next-day expectation that is the actual
   buying trigger for a consumable.**

> **A consumable is bought because it is needed NOW. That is the whole purchase. Cross-border shipping does
> not slow the business down — it removes the reason the business exists.**

Plus, unresearched and flagged: UK/EU merchant-of-record VAT and IOSS registration, and for PPE or
respirators, importer-side **UKCA/CE conformity duties.**

## 5. Where S2 stands after two of six agents

**Both agents point the same way.** Agent 2 found real mandates but then found the good ones are owned by
service companies with routes. Agent 4 found the middleman position itself is a lead magnet, not a product.

**One narrow crack survives: thermal labels and ribbons** — no dominant free cross-reference, spec fully
describable in writing, incumbents are technician-based regional firms rather than data-only competitors.

**Three agents still out**, and one of them is load-bearing: **whether a Brazil-resident sole trader can get
a blind drop-ship reseller account with no minimum at all**, and **whether she can be merchant of record for
goods delivered inside the US, UK or EU without registering for domestic tax there.** If the answer to
either is no, **S2 is dead on structure regardless of category.**
