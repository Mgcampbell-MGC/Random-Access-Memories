================================================================================
FIND A BUSINESS — BRIEF V5
For an independent LLM with subagent capability.
Written 13 August 2026, after 63 dead candidates, 8 dead business models,
and 6 measured marketplace ecosystems.
================================================================================

READ THE WHOLE THING BEFORE GENERATING ANYTHING.

Most of this file is the record of what has already been tried and exactly why
it failed, plus the laws each failure bought. Proposing something already in
here is the single most common failure mode of previous attempts: five external
LLMs have contributed candidates, and all five died — four of them to laws
written below.

Section 7 tells you how to deploy your subagents. Do not skip it. The
orchestration pattern matters as much as the search space, and it was learned
expensively.

================================================================================
1. THE JOB
================================================================================

Find a business a specific person can start, alone, with a few hundred dollars,
that nets her R$12.000-15.000/month (about US$2.100-2.600) within roughly a
year, and R$30.000/month within three.

You are NOT being asked for a list of ideas. You are being asked for ONE to
THREE candidates, each with:
  - a measured buyer population, with source and date
  - a price and a customer count that multiply to the target
  - a named competitive field found in the VENDOR's vocabulary
  - an honest statement of how it dies
  - the single cheapest check that would settle it

A candidate without a real number in it is worthless. Sixty-three have already
been produced and killed.

================================================================================
2. THE FOUNDER
================================================================================

~26 years old, woman, based in Sao Paulo, Brazil. Has a Brazilian CNPJ and
access to a US company. Stripe, Pix and boleto all work.

WHAT SHE HAS (do not design for a blank slate — this was an error):
  - Strong AI and agent skills. She can direct AI to build things.
  - A Google Project Management certificate and a Google Marketing certificate.
    Paid acquisition, SEO and listing optimisation are genuinely available.
  - About 30 hours a week.
  - Fluent written English. Native Portuguese.
  - A few hundred US dollars. Total. This is real, not conservative.

WHAT SHE WANTS TO DO ALL DAY, in her own words:
  "writing, designing, organising, digging through data — with AI doing the
  heavy lifting while she supervises that the system is working."
  And: "prefer a business where I provide a deliverable" (rather than renting
  software by the seat, though monthly SaaS is acceptable).

HARD CONSTRAINTS — these have never moved and will not:

  1. SHE WILL NOT SPEAK TO ANYONE. Ever. No calls in any language, no video, no
     meetings, no demos, no discovery calls, no sales calls. This is now THE
     binding constraint on the whole search.
     - WRITTEN support IS fine. Email, docs, tickets: all acceptable.
     - She writes English fluently and selling in WRITING to US/UK/EU buyers is
       explicitly fine, "more than fine."
  2. FACELESS, PERMANENTLY. No camera, no personal brand, no creator or
     influencer work, no webinars, no audience-building as a route to market,
     no name on anything. An ANONYMOUS or COMPANY-BRANDED presence IS allowed.
  3. NO EMPLOYEES, NO CONTRACTORS. Ever, at any revenue level.
  4. NO CUSTODY of any customer credential, certificate, password or login.
     This closed an entire category. A faceless unknown asking a stranger for
     their login inverts the trust gradient and cannot be sold.
  5. Her personal Sao Paulo network is NOT a sales channel.
  6. BUILD CAPABILITY, stated precisely by the person who knows her:
     "Yes but not highly. Build yes. Bugs and such after the build."
     => She can ship a first version with AI. She is NOT a strong engineer and
     ongoing maintenance is a real risk. FAVOUR LOW-MAINTENANCE SHAPES OVER
     CLEVER ONES. A product requiring constant platform-API firefighting is a
     worse fit than a simpler product that sits still.

RELAXED CONSTRAINTS (these moved on 13 August 2026 — use the room):
  7. INCOME TARGET: was R$30.000/month in 12 months. Now R$12.000-15.000/month
     near-term, R$30.000 over about three years.
  8. FOUNDER LABOUR: was "no per-transaction founder labour", absolute. Now a
     per-customer human step is permitted, provided total labour at target
     volume stays under about 10 hours/week. Onboarding, setup, manual
     exception review, and hand-delivery before automation are all now legal.

THE TRAJECTORY BAR — this is a hard filter, apply it to every candidate:
  - Month 3 at zero revenue: ACCEPTABLE.
  - Month 8 at R$3.000 (about US$520): FAILING. Too little.
  - Reaching full target at month 14-18: ACCEPTABLE.
  So: something that plausibly clears roughly US$1.000/month by month 8 and
  US$2.400/month by month 14-18.

SCOPE: she wants ONE business. However — a smaller thing earning US$500-700/
month is explicitly welcome as a starting point or side project while the main
one is found. So do not discard a candidate merely because it tops out below
target. Say what it tops out at and let that be judged.

FOUNDER VETOES (permanent, do not propose):
  - Construction as a buyer segment.
  - Labour-compliance document chasing (the gauntlet converged on it three
    times in three different segments; she declined all three).
  - Physical goods and inventory, unless it is a clear home run.
  - Anything requiring her to be believed as an expert.

3. THE ARITHMETIC — the most important section
================================================================================

Target is about US$2.400/month gross.

    Price/month     Customers needed      Segment where that is <=1%
    US$29           83                    8.300
    US$49           49                    4.900
    US$99           24                    2.400
    US$199          12                    1.200
    US$500          5                     500

    One-off US$300  8 sales/month
    One-off US$1000 2-3 sales/month

THE RULE: required penetration must be at or under roughly 1% of the real buyer
pool. Above that she needs market leadership, which a solo founder with no
capital, no face and no audience does not get.

TWO PRICING LAWS, both measured:

  THE PRICE-FLOOR LAW (from Shopify data): review-to-install runs 1:30 to
  1:100, but install-to-paid is only 8-18% with strong onboarding and 2-5% with
  weak onboarding, and churn is 5-12%/month. The median listed Shopify app
  earns under US$1.000/month.
  => THE BINDING VARIABLE IS PRICE, NOT INSTALLS. Anything priced under US$29
  is disqualified before it starts. At US$29 with weak onboarding, 100 reviews
  yields about US$400/month. At US$49 the same app clears target.

  THE SELF-SERVE CEILING (the NO-SALES-CALL TEST, below): annual contract value
  must stay under roughly US$3.000/year. Above that a human buyer appears and
  demands a conversation, and she cannot have one.

  => The viable band is therefore roughly US$29-250/month. Narrow, and it
  eliminates both cheap-consumer and enterprise at a stroke.

4. THE GAUNTLET — run in this order, cheapest killer first
================================================================================

Three candidates passed every other test and then died on a number obtainable
in an afternoon. TEST 1 RUNS FIRST, ALWAYS.

TEST 1 — POOL / VOLUME
  How many real buyers exist? Then: customers-needed <= ~1%?
  Two traps that have each killed multiple candidates:
    - THE SCALE SCISSORS: the segments where the job is big enough have too few
      firms; the segments with enough firms have firms too small to have the
      job. Measured in Brazil: every visit-based service segment has a mean
      firm of 2-5 people. CHECK THE SIZE DISTRIBUTION, NOT JUST THE COUNT.
    - MEAN IS THE WRONG STATISTIC; THE BAND COUNT IS THE RIGHT ONE. In one
      measured population the median claim was tiny but 619 entities still
      cleared the threshold, because the tail was fat rather than the top
      being thin. Count the band you can sell to, never the average.

TEST 2 — NO-SALES-CALL (new, and now the hardest filter in the file)
  She cannot speak to anyone. THE PRODUCT MUST BE BOUGHT FROM A PAGE.
  This kills essentially all mid-market and enterprise B2B regardless of how
  good the product is. If the category sells after a demo, a pilot, or a
  procurement process, it is dead. Say so plainly and move on.

TEST 3 — NO-TRUST-GATE (new)
  A faceless one-person company with no track record cannot pass vendor
  security review, SOC 2, HITRUST, a BAA, or an MSA negotiation.
  Anything touching insurance, health, lending or payroll data is presumptively
  dead UNLESS a platform vouches for her (see the Atlassian finding in §6).
  Corollary learned today: FACELESSNESS IS A FEATURE IN DELIVERY AND A
  DISQUALIFIER IN DILIGENCE. It helps where nobody needs to know who built it;
  it is fatal where somebody must trust who built it.

TEST 4 — PAIN
  Is there a first-person account, from a real buyer, in the buyer's own words?
  Not vendor marketing. Two candidates died because six separate interrogations
  could not find one person describing the loss.

TEST 5 — SHIPPED (apply the CORRECTED law)
  CORRECTED LAW A1: an incumbent kills only when (a) its version is FREE, or
  (b) its measured adoption inside the specific target segment exceeds roughly
  20%. "A vendor exists and has a landing page" is a price and positioning
  constraint, NOT a kill.
  Supporting data: Cetic.br 2025 puts Brazilian ERP adoption at 36%; Sebrae
  2025 puts 53% of small firms still on spreadsheets, in the best year ever
  recorded.

  BUT SEARCH PROPERLY. LAW A: "no competitor found" is a claim about the
  SEARCHER, not the market. EVERY WRONG NULL IN THIS ENTIRE RECORD WAS A SEARCH
  IN THE BUYER'S VOCABULARY INSTEAD OF THE VENDOR'S:

      the buyer says             the vendor says
      -------------------------  ----------------------------------
      supplier follow-up         diligenciamento
      warranty claims portal     gestao de pos-venda
      document collection        gestao de terceiros
      artwork proofing           prova digital / aprovacao de arte

  Find the vendor's word FIRST, then search. This has fired five times.

TEST 6 — FREE (hunt separately, by funding model)
  LAW C: a competitor search is implicitly a search for VENDORS, and a vendor
  is someone who charges — so a free competitor has no pricing page, no
  Capterra row and no keywords. It CANNOT be found by searching for a product.
  It must be found by asking "who would give this away, and why?"
  This law was bought expensively: a candidate cleared by 17 agents including a
  dedicated adversarial verifier running 20+ vendor-vocabulary queries was
  killed by the founder with ONE LINK — a competitor whose own copy read "Free
  isn't a trial or a teaser for a paid plan — there is no paid plan", funded by
  a 5% margin on the goods flowing past it.
  FREE KILLS AT ANY PRICE AND ANY TARGET. Zero excludes nobody, and the
  adoption correction in Test 5 has no force against it.

TEST 7 — REGULATOR (LAW B)
  Does the regulator build and mandate its own tool, free? Killed at least six:
  Inmetro's PSIE, the US National Board's EDT, Brazil's SINIR MTR system
  ("eletronico, numerado, autodeclaratorio e gratuito"), the ANTT insurance
  webservice, Lei 14.133 art. 141 s3 publishing the public payment queue by
  statute, and BACEN's SVR which has already returned R$15,81 billion of
  R$20,93 billion identified to 38,73 million people.
  THE ENUMERABILITY TRAP: a population enumerable BECAUSE a regulator registers
  it has usually already had its task systematised free by that same regulator.

TEST 8 — HUMAN SUBSTITUTE (LAW 6)
  Brazilian benchmarks: secretaria R$1.600-2.500/month; contador R$300-800;
  analista ~R$3.400; Analista de Documentacao R$4.324 average (~R$7-9k loaded).
  US benchmarks are 5-10x these.
  Note this test WEAKENED when the price band moved: a US$199/month product
  against a US$4.000/month analyst is a fair fight; a US$29 product never was.

TEST 9 — CLEARING PARTY (LAW 0)
  Whoever earns margin on goods, payments, bookings, credit, insurance or
  advertising flowing past the product WILL give that software away free,
  permanently.
  Predictor: does this buyer's customer pay across a counter terminal? If yes,
  an acquirer funds vertical software to zero.
  TWO CORRECTIONS, both bought expensively:
    - IT IS A TEST, NOT A PRE-FILTER. Run as a pre-filter it closed 6 of 7
      candidate segments before any candidate existed and produced two
      consecutive zero-yield waves.
    - A CLEARING PARTY KILLS SOFTWARE AND PRICES GOODS. Mercado Livre earns
      margin on goods flowing past, which is exactly why its seller tooling is
      free and its take rate is 12-19% plus shipping. It prices you; it does
      not kill you. Software has zero marginal cost so it gets given away;
      goods have a COGS so they get taxed instead.

TEST 10 — THE CHAT WINDOW (and read the correction, it is important)
  A general chat model was measured against a candidate's core value: 38/38
  planted discrepancies found, 0 false positives, across ~380 checks. And
  OpenAI shipped a Scheduled Tasks hub on 17 June 2026, so "it runs on a timer"
  and "it remembers" are consumer features now.

  THE BOUNDARY:
    A chat window has exactly ONE USER, ONE ACCOUNT, ONE MOMENT. It acts only
    inside its user's account, with its user's consent, on its user's data.

  A candidate survives only if its value depends on at least one of:
    (a) THIRD-PARTY OBLIGATION — someone who is NOT the customer, and who
        agreed to nothing, must be made to act.
    (b) SHARED STATE — a status a second party reads and is bound by.
    (c) CUSTODY FOR A LATER PARTY — a record a court, auditor, insurer, client
        or platform DEMANDS as evidence. (Corollary: an audit trail nobody
        demands has no price.)
    (d) AN IRREVERSIBLE ACT — money released, access revoked, a batch shipped.

  ONE-LINE SCREEN: if the whole product still works when only the buyer ever
  touches it, it is a chat window.

  *** THE CORRECTION, made by the founder and it matters enormously ***
  An AI that REACHES OUT TO THIRD PARTIES does not fail this test — it is leg
  (a) PASSING. A chat window cannot contact eight hundred strangers.
  AI IS DEAD WHEN IT WORKS INSIDE THE BUYER'S OWN ACCOUNT. IT IS THE STRONGEST
  THING AVAILABLE WHEN IT WORKS ON SOMEBODY ELSE'S.
  It also needs NO credential custody: the agent messages third parties from
  her own infrastructure on the customer's behalf, never logging in as the
  customer.

5. THE REST OF THE LAW SET — each bought with at least one corpse
================================================================================

LAW 1 — THE LEAD-MAGNET MECHANISM (7 kills, the deadliest in the file)
  Content costs nothing to produce, so a funded incumbent's free tier IS its
  marketing budget. Anything that is content — a guide, a template, a
  checklist, a corpus, a course, a report, a swipe file — is dead on arrival.
  IT IS STRONGER IN ENGLISH MARKETS, NOT WEAKER. The US content-marketing
  machine is vastly bigger than Brazil's.

LAW 7 — THE VISE (6 appearances)
  The legally safe version has no differentiation; the differentiated version
  is reserved work. In Brazil watch CFC/DL 9.295 (accounting judgement, art.
  25(c), art. 15 "sob qualquer forma"), OAB (legal theses), CONFEA Res.
  218/1973 (technical equivalence), CRQ, CRM, CRO, CRECI (Lei 6.530/78 art. 3
  reserves property intermediation), SUSEP (insurance broking), CVM
  (securities). In the US watch CPA, bar admission, PE licensure and state
  broker licensing.
  NOTE: the relaxed labour rule makes this MORE dangerous, not less — the human
  check she could now perform is often precisely the reserved work.

THE SELLER-SIDE FUNDING LAW (3 appearances)
  If the party you chase is competing to SELL to your buyer, someone will
  charge that party for access and give the chasing tool to the buyer FREE.
  Object & Order (5% goods margin), Mercado Eletronico ("a empresa fornecedora
  paga uma taxa... o que faz a solucao totalmente gratuita para compradores"),
  Neogrid, SAP Ariba, Nimbi Private, ISNetworld, Avetta, TraceGains, EcoVadis.
  One mechanism, not nine coincidences.
  => THE CHASED PARTY MUST BE A PAYEE OR OBLIGOR ALREADY UNDER CONTRACT, never
  a seller competing to win the order.

THE COUNTERPARTY-MOTIVATION RULE
  A COORDINATION job wants a motivated counterparty. A CHASING job wants an
  UNMOTIVATED one. If the document is how the counterparty gets paid, they are
  motivated and there is no chasing job.
  Exception that matters: motivation is insufficient when the thing owed is
  BURDENSOME or INCRIMINATING to produce.
  The purest form found: a franchisee who reports more sales PAYS more royalty
  — structurally adverse, not merely lazy.

THE GATEABLE-COUNTERPARTY LAW
  Enumerability and solvency are properties of the BUYER. Gate-holding is a
  property of the buyer's COUNTERPARTY. Check them together.
  826.570 Brazilian government suppliers are enumerable, solvent and
  phone-reachable — and useless, because their only counterparty is the State,
  which cannot be gated and publishes its own gate-state by statute.

THE THREE-CONDITION RULE FOR ASSEMBLY BUSINESSES
  A business built on assembling scattered parties needs three things at once:
  a statute that PUBLISHES the tail, a statute that makes the tail PIVOTAL, and
  a SOLVENT party who must satisfy it.
  Without pivotality, a published list of people owed money is just a list of
  poor people, and every buyer for it is the State, a saturated fund, or a
  lawyer.

THE PLACEMENT SCISSORS
  Where supply is ACTIVE, the matching layer is already free on both sides.
  Where supply is PASSIVE, filling the slot IS twenty hours of outbound calling
  — agency work with the invoice reshaped. There is no third region.

PRICE AND POOL MOVE IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS
  When the competitor is per-seat, escaping the crossover means raising price,
  which raises the minimum client headcount, which drops the qualifying
  population into the top 2-7% of the segment.

THE FIELD-OWNERSHIP LAW
  If what you sell can be described as one field changing value, ask who owns
  the record before you ask who the buyer is. An approval gate is a status
  field on a record some system already owns — structurally a feature, not a
  product. Tested across eleven segments; survived none.

THE PRECONDITION TRAP (3 appearances)
  If the product requires the discipline whose absence is the problem, the
  qualified and the needy populations are disjoint.

THE LIABILITY-EVIDENCE LAW, with its boundary
  Before building anything that records who worked when under whose
  instruction, check whether the record becomes evidence AGAINST your buyer.
  Brazilian boundary, established: it becomes evidence of vinculo only when
  FOUR conditions hold at once — the operator is the tomador who denies
  employment; a named, non-substitutable person (pessoalidade, Sumula 331 III);
  command rather than a refusable offer; and persistence proving habitualidade.
  Remove any one and the record flips to DEFENSIVE. MEI/CNPJ does NOT cure it
  (STF Tema 1389, undecided, ~74.000 actions paused). ETT employment (Lei
  6.019/74 art. 10) and intermitente (CLT art. 443 s3) DO.

THE ALERT FINDING
  Alerts sell only to whoever PROFITS FROM KNOWING — never to the subject of
  the alert.

GATE 0
  Measured solvency as a DISTRIBUTION. An aggregate or a count is not a
  distribution. One candidate died because its buyers averaged R$2.815/month
  personal income.

RECURRENCE LAW
  An idea that keeps coming back is telling you about its APPEAL, not its
  viability. One idea surfaced four times in four costumes and consumed four
  rounds of analysis before anyone checked the boring number.

THE CNAE-MIRAGE LAW (Brazil-specific but generalises)
  A registry count is not a buyer count. Check what the classification actually
  contains, and check whether the entities in it are the buyer or the
  workforce. One code showed 179.702 entries; the real buyer count was 108-500.

6. WHAT HAS ALREADY BEEN SEARCHED — do not repeat any of this

63 CANDIDATES, 8 BUSINESS MODELS, 6 MARKETPLACE ECOSYSTEMS.

CLOSED CATEGORIES — do not propose anything inside these:
  - Information or content products of any kind (Law 1, 7 kills)
  - Analysis, verification, reconciliation or report products (chat window,
    measured 38/38)
  - Pure AI wrappers operating inside the buyer's own account
  - Government filing tools (Law B)
  - Authenticated action on Brazilian government systems: gov.br's Termo de Uso
    bans robots AND bans "acessos realizados atraves de servicos de
    hospedagem", which is what any cloud agent is. Eight candidates died here.
  - Two-sided marketplaces (cold start, no capital, no audience)
  - Agency or service work sold by the hour
  - Reselling foreign SaaS into Brazil
  - Order or shipment tracking, in any vertical
  - Booking, scheduling, payments or course platforms where the customer pays
    at a counter (Law 0 fires by construction)
  - Supplier onboarding, qualification, RFQ chasing, PO confirmation, delivery
    follow-up (seller-side funding, five named free-to-buyer incumbents)
  - Approval gates in any segment (field-ownership law, eleven segments tested)
  - The Brazilian public-procurement supplier population (gateable-counterparty)
  - PO expediting in any geography
  - Road freight document gates: TST 5a Turma, September 2024 held transport
    contracts commercial, so Sumula 331 IV does not apply and the tomador has
    no subsidiary liability. The legal engine that makes document gates
    valuable is judicially excluded from freight.
  - Labelling and signage packs (see below)
  - Construction as a buyer segment (founder veto)
  - Labour-compliance document chasing (founder veto)
  - Generic/horizontal document chasing: Content Snare US$35-215/month with
    1.900 customers, Suralink US$17/user/month, FileInvite. The horizontal is
    gone. ONLY VERTICAL ADVERSARIAL CHASING IS OPEN.
  - Certificates of insurance (COI) chasing: BCS has a genuinely free tier;
    Certificial is free for insureds, agents, brokers and requestors, funded by
    25.000+ agencies and carriers. Law 0 executed exactly.
  - W-9 / vendor tax onboarding: free from Avalara Track1099, TaxBandits,
    TaxZerone, funded by e-filing fees.
  - US healthcare prior authorisation: BAA + SOC 2 Type 2 + HITRUST are table
    stakes. Fails the no-trust-gate test fatally.
  - Cold outbound prospecting to strangers: spam law, deliverability, and a
    funded crowded market (Apollo, Clay, Instantly, Smartlead, Lemlist).

THE 8 BUSINESS MODELS ALREADY TESTED AND THEIR KILLS:
  Success-fee origination — works only at US$1.500-3.000 per closing at 1-2
    closings/month; below that it is full-time sales, above it the cycle is
    6-12 months and she cannot fund it. THE CASH CYCLE IS THE FILTER.
  Lead generation — every signal derivable from a national company registry is
    worth about one cent (Casa dos Dados sells CNPJ records at R$0,01). Signals
    from specialist registers have unenumerable beneficiaries.
  Physical product / own brand — working capital, freight, and unmeasured COGS;
    founder has since vetoed inventory.
  Data / list licensing — the two highest-assembly-cost datasets in Brazil are
    given away FREE by open-source and philanthropic actors (minhareceita /
    BrasilAPI for the company registry; Querido Diario for 27 states of
    official gazettes). And the one genuinely missing field, verified WhatsApp
    numbers, died on a MECHANISM: Meta sunset the On-Premises API on 23 October
    2025, taking the /contacts number-existence endpoint with it, and the Cloud
    API has no equivalent. Every surviving validator is an unofficial
    WhatsApp-Web wrapper, i.e. a permanent ToS breach as the product's core.
  Placement / recruiting — the placement scissors.
  Acquiring cash flow — a 135x capital gap, plus art. 133 CTN making the
    acquirer of a Brazilian fundo de comercio inherit the seller's taxes
    integrally (STJ, Feb 2024).
  Demand aggregation — wherever pooled buying pays in Brazil the aggregator
    seat is already held by a licensed or association-owned institution;
    wherever it is not held, the volume discount is already delivered without
    any pool (atacarejo: R$327,7 billion, 29% of food retail, any CNPJ walks in).
  White-label / arms dealer — it INVERTS her only asymmetry, converting many
    low-trust transactions into few high-trust senior partnership sales. And
    the specific instance died on Telecontrol: 30 years, "aprovada pelos 120
    maiores fabricantes", 35.000 postos autorizados, 1M+ service orders/month,
    while Garanzia sells the identical product at R$159-359/month.

THE MARKETPLACE ECOSYSTEMS, MEASURED AND RANKED BY WHETHER MONEY CAN BE
COLLECTED:

  ATLASSIAN FORGE — native till (billed on the invoice the customer already
    pays); 0% revenue share to US$1M lifetime, then 16%; apps free at 1-10
    seats so a 100-seat app is US$600-1.900/year, UNDER the self-serve ceiling
    so no human buyer appears; 53.017 customers above US$10k cloud ARR.
    *** THE KEY FINDING: "Runs on Atlassian" (launched 26 May 2026) means Forge
    apps run on Atlassian's infrastructure, data never leaves Atlassian Cloud,
    and — Atlassian's own words — there is NO LENGTHY VENDOR SECURITY REVIEW.
    This is the no-trust-gate problem SOLVED AT THE PLATFORM LEVEL, and nothing
    else in the record does that. ***
    Search long tail exploded: top-5 search terms fell from 57% of all searches
    in 2021 to about 12% in 2026.
    Free Marketplace Reporting API gives every listed partner real search
    terms, impressions and installs — a demand verdict in six weeks for zero
    dollars, from inside the channel.
    ITS SEARCH RULE, an ECONOMIC moat rather than a hoped-for one: Atlassian
    structurally will not build workflows for people who do not have a seat,
    because that cannibalises per-seat revenue. SO HUNT ANYTHING WHERE THE
    PERSON WHO MUST ACT IS UNLICENSED.

  SHOPIFY — native till; 0% revenue share on the first US$1M LIFETIME gross
    (changed 16 June 2025; it used to reset annually); US$19 to list; ~5,5M
    merchants, 2,90M live verified stores growing 11%/year; 17.891 active apps
    from 11.352 partners, 34% with zero reviews.
    Platform risk is REAL AND FAST: on 2 April 2026 Shopify shipped native B2B
    to every paid plan and gutted the wholesale-pricing app category overnight.

  CHROME WEB STORE — NO native till; store payments shut down 1 February 2021;
    you bill via Stripe/Paddle/Lemon Squeezy/ExtensionPay; 0% store cut and any
    price you set. ~251k extensions, median 9-18 users, ~90% never pass 1.000
    installs, 0,7% over 100k. Every documented indie win came from Product Hunt
    or a founder's blog — i.e. an AUDIENCE, which is permanently closed to her.

  FIGMA — native till, flat 15%, but it bills the INDIVIDUAL USER at consumer
    prices (US$2 minimum, whole dollars). US$2.400/month needs ~300 subscribers
    = 12-15k installs. Stark, one of the best-known plugins in the store, has
    73k installs. Top 1% of 12.686 plugins by month 8. FIGMA PAYS BUT CANNOT
    PAY ENOUGH. Paid FILES are also paused for new sellers.

  CANVA — a discretionary usage fund, not a price you set. Not a business.

  NOTION — NO payment layer at all. Zero.

  monday.com — native till (payments, renewals, taxes, compliance), 0% until
    US$200k lifetime then 15%; 250.000+ customers; 49% of accounts and 87% of
    enterprise accounts have installed at least one app.

  SALESFORCE APPEXCHANGE — US$999 security review per paid app, 6-9 weeks, 15%
    cut, and contract values that summon a procurement human. Fails
    no-trust-gate and no-sales-call outright.

  ZAPIER / MAKE / AIRTABLE / CLICKUP / SLACK — no monetisation of integrations.
    They are distribution for a SaaS you already sell, not a revenue channel.

  HUBSPOT — 0% take is a trap: you must build billing, tax, dunning and licence
    enforcement yourself.

KILLED WITHIN THE MARKETPLACES, with the measurement:
  Shopify resale-certificate custody — passed the chat-window test on all four
    legs and had genuinely low platform risk, but the niche generates well
    under 2 reviews/month: Exemptify 37 reviews total with three visible across
    Dec 2025-Jun 2026 at US$4,99; Tax Exempt Manager 24 reviews with the most
    recent visible from May 2022 at US$9,99. A market of roughly 2.000 stores.
  EU accessibility (EAA/WCAG) monitoring — the US$79-249/month self-serve band
    is genuinely empty (accessiBe from US$490, AudioEye US$24-75k/year
    sales-assisted, UserWay enterprise). It dies on HER constraint: A FACELESS
    COMPANY ISSUING CONFORMANCE EVIDENCE IS A CONTRADICTION. Buyers of
    compliance proof want a NAMED auditor.
  Atlassian external sign-off for unlicensed users — cleared every structural
    test better than anything in 63 candidates and STILL only reached
    US$720-1.080/month by month 8, in the most crowded category on the
    marketplace (Appfire owns the Confluence half; Atlassian shipped approvals
    into Premium). RIGHT CHANNEL, WRONG APP.
  Franchisee royalty-report chasing — the purest adverse-counterparty found
    anywhere (reporting more sales means paying more royalty), a real gap
    (FranConnect US$1.000-2.000/month demo-gated, every competitor gated), and
    still probably dead: it is a trust gate wearing a US$129 price tag, the
    self-reporting segment SHRINKS as POS integration spreads, and the search
    volume implies about one customer/month, i.e. month 8 at US$650.

*** THE SEEDING LAW — the most important finding in the whole marketplace file,
    from a study of documented solo builders ***

  ZERO documented cases exist of a cold marketplace listing reaching US$1k MRR
  on in-marketplace search alone. NOT ONE.

  But every documented winner seeded a first cohort BY HAND, and the channels
  they used were WRITTEN, FACELESS AND CALL-FREE:
    - Kaching Bundles: gave the app away free in Facebook dropshipping groups.
      Posts went semi-viral -> 500 merchants in week 1, 1.000 in month 1, THEN
      ranked #1 for its keyword. US$1k MRR IN FOUR MONTHS.
    - Storemapper: emailed his own freelance clients, then answered "store
      locator" questions in Shopify forums. Later: "a big portion comes from
      searches inside the app stores."
    - DropCommerce: cold-emailed brands to seed supply, then 18 installs/day
      organic from the App Store, which he credits as "everything."
    - "Credible": one Shopify New & Upcoming -> Featured placement "netted
      about 180 new recurring customers."

  => MARKETPLACE-AS-SOLE-DISTRIBUTION IS DEAD.
     MARKETPLACE-AS-COMPOUNDING-CHANNEL-AFTER-A-WRITTEN-SEED IS WELL SUPPORTED.
     The seed is forum writing and free giveaways in exchange for reviews. All
     of it is inside this founder's constraints.

  THE REVIEW COLD-START is the specific mechanism: ranking weights installs,
  uninstalls, reviews and billing usage. Listings under 25 reviews convert at
  1-2% versus 5-8% at 200+. Kaching solved it by giving the app away FOR
  REVIEWS. Any candidate must state how it crosses this threshold.

*** MAINTENANCE BURDEN, RANKED — and this REVERSES the obvious choice ***

  Given "build yes but not highly, bugs and such after the build", maintenance
  matters more than economics. Measured:

  SHOPIFY — KINDEST. Quarterly API versions, 12-month support, 9-month
    overlap. Support load measured at 5-10 tickets/week across 1.800 merchants
    = about 1-2 tickets per 100 merchants per month, and ~80% of tickets are
    merchant misconfiguration rather than bugs, answerable with saved snippets.

  ATLASSIAN — BRUTAL. Connect reached end-of-support 30 April 2026, and
    post-EOS breaking changes and security fixes become the DEVELOPER'S
    problem. The legacy Forge runtime is deprecated and state is no longer
    guaranteed cleared between invocations. Forge is not at feature parity with
    Connect. The community expects a wave of apps to disappear.

  CHROME — BRUTAL. Manifest V3 was "a complete structural redesign, not a
    port", and MV2 extensions were auto-disabled from Chrome 127.

  *** CONSEQUENCE: Atlassian has the best economics in the file (0% to US$1M,
  host collects, and "Runs on Atlassian" solves the trust gate) AND the worst
  maintenance profile for a non-expert. Only ~50 businesses have EVER grossed
  more than US$1M lifetime on that marketplace — about 2,8% of its ~1.800
  vendors. Worse: THE PUBLIC RECORD FOR ATLASSIAN IS MARKETING, NOT DATA. The
  single most-cited Atlassian solo success never states how it got one
  customer, and no Atlassian solo developer publishes a channel breakdown.
  TREAT ATLASSIAN DISCOVERY AS UNVERIFIED. Shopify is the only one of these
  marketplaces with a genuine first-person record. ***

*** WHAT KIND OF APP ACTUALLY WON, and it fights the price band ***

  Uniform pattern across documented solo winners: NARROW, SINGLE-PURPOSE,
  CHEAP, HORIZONTAL UTILITIES. Kaching quantity-breaks at US$14,99-59,99.
  Storemapper, a map widget, at US$5-20. A guarantees-and-features icons app at
  US$5,99/month. TEAMS won the complex multi-sided products; SOLO winners
  priced LOW TO MANY, not high to few.

  This qualifies the price-floor law in §3: under US$29 is disqualified ONLY IF
  you cannot seed. With a seeded base of 500+ merchants, US$15/month x 160
  merchants clears target. So state your seeding plan and let it set the price,
  rather than the reverse.

WHY MOST MARKETPLACE APPS EARN NOTHING — top three, with evidence:
  1. The listing is not distribution. Median Shopify app has 4 reviews; 25%
     have zero; 75% of all reviews sit in the top 5% of apps. 87% of Chrome
     extensions have under 1.000 installs and 50% have under 16.
  2. The review cold-start (above).
  3. Built on assumptions then abandoned. Shopify's own post-mortem: no user
     research, built "in a bubble", competitor already ahead. About 30% of
     listed Shopify apps never add a paid plan at all.

KILLED — THE FACELESS AFFILIATE/SPONSORSHIP NEWSLETTER (tested 13 Aug, and it
died on arithmetic rather than doctrine):
  Sponsorship needs 12.000-37.500 subscribers for US$2.400/month at measured
  vertical CPMs (generalist B2B US$5-12, devtools US$15-40, cybersecurity
  US$25-60 at the ceiling, priced per thousand OPENS not sends). Affiliate needs
  43.000-125.000 at a 0,5-1% CTR and US$1,20-3,50 EPC. Paid subscription needs
  38.700 free subscribers at beehiiv's median 0,62% free-to-paid conversion.
  Her capital buys 80-250 subscribers. THE GAP IS 32x TO 500x.
  And the structural finding, which generalises:
    *** CPM AND CONTESTEDNESS ARE THE SAME VARIABLE. A niche uncontested enough
    for a faceless newcomer to win has too little advertiser demand to pay
    US$2.400/month. There is no gap in the middle. ***
  Law 1 confirmed in the field: cybersecurity's leading free newsletter,
  tl;dr sec with 50.000+ subscribers, is written by Semgrep's Head of Security
  Research. A vendor employee, produced as marketing.
  KEPT BUT DEMOTED: a newsletter as DISTRIBUTION for a product she owns dodges
  Law 1 (the money comes from the tool, not the words) but not acquisition
  cost. It is a tactic to attach to a product, never a business on its own.

FIVE MORE FACTS WORTH CARRYING:
  - DELIVERABILITY IS THE FRAGILE CORE of any chasing product. Sending on
    behalf of hundreds of small customers is exactly the reputation profile
    ESPs terminate, and one customer repurposing it for prospecting torches the
    pool for everyone.
  - EMAIL ONLY, NEVER SMS, for third-party outreach in the US. TCPA class
    filings are up ~95% year on year at US$500-1.500 per message. A
    transactional message about an existing obligation is a genuinely different
    legal object from marketing under CAN-SPAM — but SMS is litigated
    regardless.
  - LGPD ENFORCEMENT CAME FROM THE COURTS, NOT THE REGULATOR. The Brazilian
    ANPD's total private-sector fines were R$14.400 — but MPDFT's acao civil
    publica obtained an injunction, upheld by TJDFT, ordering Serasa Experian
    to STOP commercialising its "Lista Online" and "Prospeccao de Clientes"
    products, and the court EXPRESSLY REJECTED the legitimate-interest defence.
    The largest data company in the country, enjoined out of that exact
    category.
  - THE OAB BANS PAID INTERMEDIARIES. Lei 8.906/94 art. 34 and CED art. 7 ban
    "intermediarios e agenciadores comissionados" by name, and Provimento
    205/2021 bans unsolicited outreach offering legal services. Legal-services
    lead generation and claimant sourcing are permanently closed.
  - THE US SEARCH BIAS. Of 63 candidates, ~45 were aimed at professional
    services, health, beauty, events, education, culture, property and
    government paperwork. ZERO of those survived. Only 5 were aimed at the real
    economy (industry, commerce, transport, agriculture) and 1 of 3 Brazilian
    real-economy candidates cleared the gauntlet.

================================================================================
7. HOW TO DEPLOY YOUR AGENTS — read this, it was learned expensively
================================================================================

The orchestration pattern matters as much as the search space. What follows
worked; the alternatives failed measurably.

RULE 1 — FIX THE DIMENSION, NEVER LET AGENTS FREE-ASSOCIATE.
  Give each agent ONE fixed thing — one business model, one marketplace, one
  vertical, one shape — and tell it that if its best answer resolves to
  something outside that fix, it has FAILED THE TASK and must go back.
  When 12 agents were given open briefs they returned zero candidates. When 8
  agents were each handed a fixed business model, they returned 8 measured
  verdicts and 2 conditional survivors.

RULE 2 — SEPARATE MEASUREMENT AGENTS FROM IDEATION AGENTS.
  The single most valuable agent run in this entire project was a pure
  measurement task with the instruction "Do not propose any business. Measure."
  It machine-parsed nine documents, validated its parser against stated totals
  to the centavo, and settled a question that had blocked two candidates.
  When a candidate hinges on one number, spawn an agent whose ONLY job is that
  number. Forbid it from ideating.

RULE 3 — EVERY AGENT MUST RETURN "WHAT I ELIMINATED AND WHY".
  Twelve agents once returned zero candidates and their elimination reasoning
  survived only in transcripts. The eliminations are frequently worth more than
  the survivors, because they are where the laws come from.

RULE 4 — MAKE EVERY AGENT DESTROY ITS OWN PROPOSAL IN THE LAST THIRD.
  Instruct explicitly: "the last third of your answer must be your best attempt
  to destroy your own candidate." The strongest results in this project came
  from agents that killed their own work. One agent's self-destruction —
  "this is a trust gate wearing a US$129 price tag, and I may be rationalising"
  — was more useful than its candidate.

RULE 5 — POOL FIRST, ALWAYS, AND STOP EARLY.
  Instruct agents to run the tests in order and STOP at the first failure,
  reporting which test killed it. Three candidates in this record passed every
  other test and then died on a number obtainable in an afternoon.

RULE 6 — REQUIRE THE ONE FREE CHECK.
  Every candidate must arrive with the single cheapest observation that would
  settle it. Real examples that each cost under an hour and settled a
  candidate: "open dgtec.com.br and look at the price list"; "count reviews
  posted on these four apps in the last six months"; "open Porto Seguro
  Corretor and look for a prospecting module"; "walk into an Atacadao with a
  CNPJ". If an agent cannot name the check, it does not understand its own
  candidate.

RULE 7 — SEND MID-FLIGHT CORRECTIONS.
  When one agent returns a finding that sharpens the brief, message the agents
  still running. A finding from a solar-signage kill ("if the artifact carries
  no per-job information, digital-in-artifact-out degenerates to
  nothing-in-commodity-out") was pushed to a running agent and improved its
  output materially.

RULE 8 — RUN 4-8 AGENTS IN PARALLEL, NOT 1 AND NOT 20.
  Below 4 you learn too slowly. Above 8 you cannot read the results carefully,
  and reading them carefully is where the laws come from.

THE AGENT SET TO DEPLOY, in order:

  WAVE 1 — TERRITORY HUNTERS (4-6 agents, one fixed territory each)
    Suggested fixes, given what is already closed:
    a) Atlassian Forge apps, using the "unlicensed person must act" search rule
    b) Shopify apps, in categories Shopify structurally will not enter
    c) monday.com apps (250k customers, host collects, 0% to US$200k)
    d) WordPress plugins (largest install base, but establish how money is
       actually collected before anything else)
    e) VERTICAL ADVERSARIAL CHASING in English markets — the shape where an AI
       contacts an obligated, unmotivated third party. The horizontal is
       commoditised; only verticals are open.
    f) One wildcard: a territory nobody has named. Instruct it to justify the
       territory before proposing anything in it.

  WAVE 2 — MEASUREMENT AGENTS (spawn as needed, no ideation)
    For each Wave 1 survivor, one agent whose only job is the single number the
    candidate depends on. Give it the exact sources to try, tell it to validate
    its own extraction, and tell it to report "unmeasured" rather than estimate.

  WAVE 3 — ADVERSARIAL VERIFIERS (1 per surviving candidate)
    Brief: "Here is a candidate that survived. Your only job is to kill it.
    Hunt the FREE competitor by funding model, in the vendor's vocabulary, in
    the local language. Check who earns margin on the flow. Check whether the
    host platform can ship this. Report the single link that kills it."
    NOTE: a candidate cleared by 17 agents including a dedicated adversarial
    verifier was still killed by the founder with one link. Verifiers reduce
    the failure rate; they do not eliminate it.

  WAVE 4 — THE CASE-STUDY AGENT (1, and do this early)
    Not "what should we build" but "who has actually done this, and how did
    their first hundred customers arrive?" Demand named people, named products,
    revenue figures with dates, and specific acquisition accounts. Instruct it
    explicitly not to let survivorship bias produce a hopeful answer, and to
    say "the public record here is marketing, not data" if that is the truth.

================================================================================
8. WHAT A GOOD ANSWER LOOKS LIKE
================================================================================

For EACH candidate — one to three maximum:

  1. One sentence describing the product as the BUYER would search for it.
  2. The buyer, precisely — and HOW THEY BUY IT WITHOUT ANY CONVERSATION.
  3. VOLUME, measured, with source and date. Then the arithmetic:
     price x customers = US$2.400/month, and customers as a % of the pool.
     If that percentage is above ~1%, do not send the candidate.
  4. PAIN: a link or a quote from a real buyer, or an explicit "unverified".
  5. SHIPPED: named vendors found in the VENDOR's vocabulary, with prices, and
     an explicit judgement under corrected Law A1 — is any of them FREE, and is
     measured adoption in THIS segment above 20%?
  6. FREE: the answer to "who would give this away, and why?" — reasoned by
     funding model, not by product search.
  7. CLEARING PARTY: name them, or state that you searched by class and found
     none.
  8. CHAT WINDOW: which of the four survival properties does the value rest on?
     Quote the property.
  9. MAINTENANCE: how much ongoing engineering does this need, and what happens
     when the platform ships a breaking change? She is not a strong engineer.
  10. THE MONTH-8 NUMBER: show the ramp. Is it plausibly above US$1.000/month
      by month 8? Name the acquisition channel and cost it.
  11. HOW IT DIES, and the ONE FREE CHECK that settles it.

MANDATORY CLOSING SECTION: "WHAT I ELIMINATED AND WHY" — everything considered
and rejected, with the reason. And the last third of your answer must be your
best attempt to destroy your own proposal.

================================================================================
9. WHAT A BAD ANSWER LOOKS LIKE
================================================================================

  - Any number without a source and a date.
  - A registry count presented as a buyer count.
  - "There is no competitor" without evidence you searched in the vendor's own
    vocabulary, in the relevant language.
  - A competitor analysis that only looks for companies THAT CHARGE (Law C).
  - Anything in the closed-category list in section 6.
  - A product priced under US$29/month, or above US$3.000/year.
  - A product whose value is analysis, a report, a template, advice, a
    dashboard, an alert, or an AI acting inside the buyer's own account.
  - Anything requiring her face, her name, her network, an employee, a
    credential, an audience, or a spoken conversation.
  - A candidate that needs her to be believed as an expert.
  - Enthusiasm. Six candidates were cleared enthusiastically by large agent
    fleets and killed later by a single link.

================================================================================
10. THE ONE-LINE VERSION
================================================================================

Find a business bought from a page for US$29-250/month by 5-50 customers in an
English-speaking market, whose value depends on somebody OTHER THAN THE BUYER
being made to act, built and maintained by one faceless person with AI, no
staff, no capital, no audience and no telephone — and then spend the last third
of your answer trying to kill it.
