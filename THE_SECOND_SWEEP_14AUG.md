## 1. THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST — I was solving the wrong half of the problem

The binding constraint was recorded as **$150–300/month for domains, mailboxes, warmup and
enrichment**, exhausting her capital in month 2–3 of a 6–9 month ramp.

> **Look at what that cost actually is. NONE of it is cost of goods. ALL of it is customer
> acquisition cost.**

**Print-on-demand solves COGS timing perfectly and solves the binding constraint NOT AT ALL** — a
Lulu-printed book sold from her own website still needs traffic, and traffic bought in advance is
exactly the arithmetic that killed twenty-four candidates.

**There are two independent timing axes. I collapsed them into one.**

| | COGS before revenue? | **CAC before revenue?** |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory + cold email | Yes — fatal | Yes — fatal |
| **POD on her own site** | No — good | **Yes — FATAL** |
| **POD on a marketplace** | No — good | **No — good** |

### The instrument that closes it

> **ETSY OFFSITE ADS: *"you only pay when a sale happens."*** 15% of an attributed order for shops
> under $10.000/365 days, 12% above, **capped at $100 per order**, 30-day attribution window.
> Opt-out available below $10k; mandatory above.
>
> **Etsy buys the Google, Meta, Pinterest and Bing traffic with its own money and bills her only out
> of completed revenue.** That is the structural inverse of the $200/month mailbox stack. The cost
> structure that killed two dozen candidates is not reduced — it is DELETED and replaced with a
> success fee.

**THE LAW:**
> **BOTH TIMING AXES MUST CLEAR. A supplier that bills after the sale is necessary. A CHANNEL that
> bills after the sale is what actually saves her.** Etsy is the only instrument found that does
> both, that a Brazil-resident can be paid through, that permits a made-to-order physical product,
> and that never requires her to speak.

## 2. THE OTHER NEW LAWS

### THE AUTOMATION–VERIFIABILITY IDENTITY
> **A check the buyer can verify in seconds against a document they wrote themselves is a check a
> machine can perform. The property that makes facelessness free is the same property that makes the
> work automatable — and therefore free. Maximum verifiability and durable pricing power are
> OPPOSITES, not complements.**

This explains why the Verifiability Law kept generating near-misses across eighty candidates. It is
**necessary** for a faceless seller and, pushed to its maximum, becomes a kill.

**Hold both correctly: verifiability solves the TRUST problem — can she sell at all. The marginal-cost
moat solves the MONEY problem — can she keep it. They are different problems, and the adjudication
shape only ever solved the first.**

### THE MARGINAL COST MOAT, CORRECTED TO TWO PARTS
> (a) The product must cost real money to produce EVERY time — that stops it being free lead-gen.
> (b) **The value must be CONTENT-dominant, not DESIGN-dominant.** A design is clonable in an
> afternoon.
>
> Verified wholesale: a 252-piece jigsaw **$14,95** · throw blanket **$28,78** · framed canvas
> **$61,20** · woven tapestry **$15,75** · wall calendar **$19,33**. All have real unit costs. All
> are thousand-substitute commodities needing bought traffic. **Marginal cost alone buys nothing —
> it just makes you an expensive commodity.**

### THE TEMPLATE-VERSUS-WRITTEN DISTINCTION — corrected to THREE tiers
My two-tier version was too coarse. The middle tier is where the money already is:

| Tier | Who does what | Verified prices |
|---|---|---|
| **Template** | Buyer does everything | Canva downloads $10–30 · StoryWorth $59–199 · Remento $99 · zapptales €37,90 |
| **DESIGN + PRINT** | **Buyer writes, seller designs and prints** | Serena's **85p/copy** min 30 · Artful Pixels **$145–549** · The Occasional Press **from £695** · Scripted Ever After **$4,75/copy** min 50 |
| **Written** | A human writes the prose | The Love Dispatch **$1.000–5.000** · StoryTerrace **$3.250–24.000** |

> **$199 to $3.250 is empty.** That is the gap, with prices on both sides of it.

**And the tension between my own two laws, which must be held explicitly:**
> **Content-dominance is what protects her from cloning, and it is also what makes her INVISIBLE in a
> listing photograph.** Every sub-$1.000 competitor sells from pictures of a beautiful object.
> "Forty thousand characters of original prose about you" cannot be photographed.

### THE NARROW-INPUT RULE
> **The multi-copy lever is about DISTRIBUTION. The content INPUT must stay narrow.**
> One buyer plus two or three siblings is feasible. **Forty colleagues is not** — that is an
> aggregation problem, and FromABirdie gives aggregation away free in digital and charges $125
> hardcover. This kills retirement and reunions outright, and it sorts every event.

### AND ONE PROPAGATION RULE, LEARNED THE HARD WAY
> **When a correction lands, propagate it to every law that depends on it.** The graveyard contained
> a falsified law for weeks *after* the same file had corrected half of it sixty lines below.

## 3. CORRECTIONS AGAINST MY OWN CLAIMS

**1. I propagated a fabricated statistic.** I cited *"≤50-recipient campaigns average 5,8% reply
versus 2,1%"* repeatedly. The source page was fetched: Belkins describes **7,5 million** emails, not
16,5M, and **contains neither figure and no segmentation by campaign size at all.** Its only claim in
that direction is "lower send volumes produce higher reply rates." **This is the FOURTH fabricated
cold-outreach statistic this file has caught** — after Baird Hall/iTunes API, Jake Atwood/Indeed, and
the entire rank-and-rent literature. Do not use it in arithmetic.

**2. I quoted the Newspaper Club ladder wrong.** I said "100 copies $267" and "500 at $1,80 each."
Their price table loads by AJAX defaulting to 4 pages, and the ladder I passed on was one row each
from six different page counts. **Two agents re-queried the endpoint and both confirm 8pp/100 =
$348.** They disagree on what $267 buys — one says 70 copies at 8pp, the other says 100 copies at 4pp
on 80gsm — a paper-weight variable neither fully resolved. **Verify by hand before pricing anything.**

Also from the real grid: **pages are the expensive axis, not copies.** 8pp→16pp at 100 copies costs
**+$258**; 70→100 copies at 8pp costs **+$81**. The format punishes exactly the thing that gives the
moat.

**3. I overstated the ISO 17065 kill.** Clause 4.2.3 NOTE 1 names referral commission as a **risk to
be managed** under 4.2.4, not a prohibition. European Accreditation's Certification Committee FAQ
Q34.4 works a numeric example of an external sales agent and states no requirement prevents it. QIMA
settles it commercially with a public affiliate programme paying **5% for five years**, explicitly
open to individuals, with no call.

**4. The binding-constraint section of the brief is wrong for hand-built lists.** The
$150–300/month figure is correct for ~2.400 emails/month with **bought enrichment**. At ≤215/month,
hand-built, there is no enrichment line: one domain and one mailbox is **under $120 for a year.** The
constraint binds the VOLUME channel, not the NAMED-LIST channel.

**5. The RFP kill was recorded against the wrong target.** The file rejected it because "it caps at
~15/month" — but 15 × $799 = **$11.985/month, more than double her three-year target.** Her near-term
target is **2,9 responses/month = 12–24 hours.** The cap was never binding. *(It dies anyway: Fiverr
sells identical done-for-you RFP writing at **$15–150**, plus free DoD-funded APEX proposal
assistance, plus 17+ AI platforms from $49/month. Record the right reason — the wrong one would
generalise into future false kills of every per-deliverable service.)*

### A. THE MILESTONE-BIRTHDAY MULTI-COPY BOOK

about the honouree's life — childhood, working life, the marriage, the era — written from written
questionnaires completed by the buyer and two or three siblings. One copy per guest, on the tables.

**Why it is live where the wedding is dead:** the buyer does not believe he can write eight pages
about his mother's life, **and nobody expects a biographer to have attended the birthday party.**
That deletes the presence kill that ends the wedding version, where the entire written tier
($1.000–5.000) is priced on on-site interviews and reception coverage.

**Wedding also fails on budget:** the average full paper suite is **$518**, invitations alone
**$226**, rising only to **$276** at 101+ guests. A $599 newspaper exceeds the entire stationery
budget of the average American couple.

**$899 is MANDATORY, not optimistic.** At 8–12 hours per order, $599 needs 25 orders/month at year
three — 200–300 hours. $899 needs eleven, at ~12 hours each.

**The honest weakness:** every milestone-birthday party budget figure found came from SEO content
farms, several apparently machine-generated. **Demand for this object is inferred, not evidenced.**

### B. THE GENEALOGY HARDCOVER — a resurrection, and the kill was factually wrong
FamilySearch's Memories Book **compiles** content already uploaded; the vendor concedes *"sections
with limited FamilySearch content may be shorter."* A tree of names and dates with no uploaded
stories yields a thin book. **It never touched the written layer.**

The competitor the original hunt missed is **MyHeritage AI Biographer** — which does write, but is
**PDF only, one ancestor at a time, no physical object.**

Market: free compile → free AI per-ancestor PDF → **THE GAP** → $3.500 human ghostwriting.

**And it is the only candidate in eighty with a channel that already exists and is written and
faceless:** r/Genealogy at **206.000 members** who review products · **16.000+** genealogy Facebook

**Structurally clean:** the buyer supplies their own content (their GEDCOM), so there is no gift code
and **no redemption failure** — the hidden kill candidate 64 still carries. The gift moment is extra

**Two real risks:** GEDCOM parsing is a genuine build. And the community has openly objected that AI

## 6. SUPPLY CHAIN — a second source, and it halves the print cost

**Bookvault runs a public, unauthenticated price API.** `quote.bookvault.app` issues an anonymous
token on page load; `POST api.bookvault.app/v3/CalculateTitle` returns live per-copy cost for all four
plants. *(Same technique as the Lulu GraphQL probe already in this file.)*

> **Her exact object — 40pp, premium full colour, case-bound, 8.25″ square — is $8,26 US / £5,53 UK
> against Lulu's $19,57.**

**But the per-title setup fee inverts it.** Bookvault charges setup PER TITLE, and in a bespoke
business **every customer is a new title.** At £19,95 on the free plan a Bookvault hardcover costs
~$34 against Lulu's $21,32 landed. On Pro (£89,95/month) setup is free, saving **$11,56/book**, so
**break-even is ~10 books/month.**

> **Below 10 books/month: Lulu, on its zero per-title fee. Above 10: Bookvault Pro. And £89,95/month
> is a recurring pre-revenue cost — so it is a post-proof move, never a starting move.**

Bookvault is also more explicit about white-label than Lulu. Three constraints found only in API
responses, not marketing: **the US plant requires 40pp minimum for case binding, rejects 100gsm
coated stock, and saddle stitch is UK-plant only.**

**The multi-copy winner is NOT the newspaper.** A **40-page full-colour perfect-bound paperback from
Bookvault US at $2,92/copy** — **$322,26 landed for 100 copies including UPS Ground.** At $699 that
is **$356 contribution → 7 orders/month for $2.300, 17 for $5.780.** Forty pages instead of eight, at
a lower unit cost than Newspaper Club.

**Only two suppliers charge nothing before a customer pays, with no minimum, no setup fee and no
subscription: LULU and AMAZON KDP.** Peecho, Gelato, Prodigi, Cloudprinter and IngramSpark publish no
usable prices — all quote- or login-gated, and marked unavailable rather than estimated.

## 7. WHAT CLOSED

**Kickstarter and Indiegogo are both unavailable to a Brazil-resident individual.** Neither lists
Brazil among eligible campaign-creator countries; Indiegogo additionally requires a bank account in
the campaigner's country of residence. **Delete pre-orders from the plan.**

**Amazon KDP has a 30% tax leak specific to her** — US-source royalties to a country with no US income
tax treaty, and **Brazil has none**, so a W-8BEN cannot reduce it. It is also a public catalogue, so
it cannot be personalised. **Best cash structure found, worst unit economics: 314 hardcovers/month to
hit target.** Keep as a free secondary shelf, never the business.

**Gumroad is not a rail for her** — Stripe direct deposit is available in all Stripe countries EXCEPT
Brazil (and India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Mexico, Philippines, Thailand), leaving PayPal, which is
reportedly disrupted.

**The language-gate adjudication hunt returned an honest zero.** Five candidates, all dead on the
automation–verifiability identity. **Intercom's Batch Test is FREE** — *"you won't be charged for AI
answers generated through the Batch test"* — which was BR-GOLD's only defensible step. **GlobalVision
CompareText is a free online tool.** **Smartling's LQA Agent** runs fully automatically at 90%
agreement with human reviewers; **Phrase QPS** is bundled at every tier. And **ANVISA requires the
notification holder to be a Brazilian legal entity with an AFE number printed on the label** — so the
party legally required to be right about the Portuguese label is Brazilian by law and paid in reais,
which kills the GLOSSPROOF flip too. **Testlio pays Brazil-based testers $10–20/hour** against a
required $63–74/hour. A pricing inversion, not a gap.

**Nothing resurrects from the channel re-audit.** No corpse in this file had, as its load-bearing
cause of death, the belief that written cold outreach was unlawful, toxic or infeasible.

**But one genuinely new list source was found**, and it is the first free list with contactable
emails in the entire record: **15 U.S.C. §45f, the INFORM Consumers Act**, requires every online
marketplace to publish, for each high-volume third-party seller, *"the full name of the seller,"*
*"the physical address of the seller,"* and *"a current working phone number; a current working email
address; or other means of direct electronic messaging."*
**GATED ON ONE FREE PRE-CHECK:** the statute compels the marketplace to PUBLISH; it does not grant
anyone a right to COMPILE. DeckProof died on exactly this trap — a means-neutral harvesting clause in
the marketplace's terms, barring *"data mining or similar data gathering or extraction activities"*
with **no mention of automation**. Read the Conditions of Use in full first. Any means-neutral
harvesting clause and the list source is closed.
