# CARTA — verified at source, 3 Sep 2026

**The candidate: Sol becomes an IRS Certifying Acceptance Agent in São Paulo.** A Brazilian who needs a US

## 1. The gate is real, and it is physical

**Notarised copies are worthless.** Form W-7 accepts only original documents, or *"a certified copy … that the
**original issuing agency** provides"*. Notarised copies are permitted for exactly one class of applicant —
dependents of US military personnel. A Brazilian *cartório* authentication, the cheap local substitute for
everything else in Brazilian life, **does not work here.**
→ https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7

**And the interview requires the documents in hand.** *"AAs and CAAs must conduct an in-person interview with
each ITIN applicant (primary, secondary, and dependent) in order to complete the application."* Video
conferencing is permitted for the interview — **but the CAA must have the original identification documents, or
certified copies from the issuing agency, in their possession during that interview.**
→ https://www.irs.gov/individuals/new-itin-acceptance-agent-program-changes
→ https://www.irs.gov/individuals/itin-acceptance-agent-program

**This is why "remote, worldwide" competition is weaker than it looks.** ITIN Abroad is a real CAA that
publishes prices and advertises *"Based in US & Czech Republic … Remote process"* — but the possession rule
means a Brazilian client must still courier their passport to Prague or the United States and wait without it.
**A São Paulo agent replaces an international courier round-trip with a domestic Sedex or a motoboy.** The moat
is not knowledge, software or speed. It is that no model can hold a passport, and hers is the only pair of
hands in the country's largest city that is also authorised to certify what it is holding.
**Gate: (b) physical access, compounded by (a) permission and (c) accepted liability.**

## 2. She is eligible — this was the hunt's biggest open question and it is now closed

The hunt agent flagged that all four Brazilian incumbents are US LLCs, so *"whether a Brazil-resident
individual can hold the agreement is untested."* It is not untested. It is written down.

**Rev. Proc. 2006-10 §4:** *"An eligible person may be a U.S. person **or a foreign person**."*

**Rev. Proc. 2006-10 §7**, on what the agreement records: *"the organizational status of the applicant (e.g.,
corporation, partnership, **sole proprietorship**, etc.), and the place of residence or organization of the
applicant (i.e., inside or **outside the United States**)."*
→ https://www.stayexempt.irs.gov/pub/irs-tege/revenue_procedure_2006-10.pdf

**Publication 4520 goes further and names her exact profile as a category that gets an *easier* path.** The
persons exempt from the background-check requirement include *"an ERO in good standing with the IRS, and
**foreign nationals without a Social Security Number (SSN) residing outside the United States**."*
→ https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4520.pdf

Pub 4520 also confirms an *individual* may apply — eligible persons include *"Persons that provide assistance to
taxpayers in the preparation of their tax returns"* and *"Any other person or categories of persons who are
involved in providing tax related services."* The four incumbents chose to be LLCs. **Nothing required it.**

And the expert veto survives intact: *"provide proof of professional/ERO status, **if applicable**"* — that
clause bites only if the responsible party is an attorney, CPA or enrolled agent. She is none of those, so it
does not apply to her. **She is not sold as an expert. She is sold as a certified pair of eyes on a document,
and the IRS certifies her, so nothing about her has to be believed.**

## 4. The population passes, and the Taxpayer Advocate makes the argument for her

**Brazil: 3,288 ITIN filers, TY 2022** — seventh of the top fifteen foreign countries, ahead of France, Mexico,
Australia, India and Switzerland. Source: IRS Taxpayer Advocate, *Annual Report to Congress 2024*, Figure
5.3.8, built from the IRS Individual Returns Transaction File.

**The IRS's own advocate names undersupply as a defect, and gives the comparator:** *"in some states and
foreign countries, only a small number of CAAs exist in proportion to the number of ITIN applications submitted
by individuals residing there … taxpayers in the United Kingdom submitted over 15,000 ITIN applications but had
only 34 CAAs."*

| | Filers | CAAs | Filers per CAA |
|---|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom — cited by TAS *as a problem* | 15,085 | 34 | 444 |
| **Brazil** | **3,288** | **4** | **822** |

**Brazil is 1,85× worse than the case the Taxpayer Advocate holds up as evidence of a shortage.**

**And the report supplies the demand argument, from an IRS source rather than vendor marketing:** *"Taxpayers

→ https://www.taxpayeradvocate.irs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/ARC24_RR_Research_3.pdf

and I am not annualising anything. But the same caveat cuts the other way — **every one of those 3,288 owes a
US return every year, and that is the US$550 recurring line, not the one-time US$350.**

## The next action, and it costs nothing

**The only open item is the credit history check.** Rev. Proc. 2006-10 §6.01(2)(a) imposes *both* a credit
history check and an FBI background check on the applicant. Pub 4520 waives the **background** check for
*"foreign nationals without a Social Security Number (SSN) residing outside the United States."* **It is silent
on the credit check, and she has no US credit file.** Circular 230 practitioners are exempt from both — she is
not one.

**Settle it in writing, before anything is spent, before the forensic-document course is bought.** No telephone.

> United States"* among the persons exempt from the background check requirement. Rev. Proc. 2006-10
