# CANDIDATE 66 — THE CATALOGUE DEPARTMENT

**Worked out in full, 14 Aug 2026, with the expansion ladder.**

> **"Your product content department. Send a CSV, get it back written, attributed and categorised in
> 48 hours. $999/month. No integration, no onboarding, no call."**

---

## 1. The job, concretely

Every merchant with more than a few hundred SKUs has the same rotting asset: **a catalogue full of
product data that costs them money every day.**

| What their data looks like | What it costs them |
|---|---|
| Titles that are manufacturer part numbers — `BRK-4420-B` | Invisible in on-site search and Google |
| Descriptions copied verbatim from the manufacturer | Duplicate content — the same paragraph as 40 competitors |
| Missing attributes: size, material, finish, compatibility, dimensions | **No filters work.** A shopper who can't filter leaves |
| Category left at the import default | Wrong shelf, no browse traffic |
| No GTIN, no required per-category fields | **Google Merchant disapprovals, Amazon listing suppression** |
| Nothing written for the channel it's selling on | Amazon, Shopify and Google want different fields and different lengths |

**This is not a cosmetic problem and they know it.** It is the reason a product with stock, a price
and a photo still gets zero sales. And it never ends: **new SKUs arrive every month, forever.**

Today they solve it three ways, all bad: a junior hire at $45–60k, an offshore data-entry team on a
contract with a three-week turnaround, or — most commonly — **not at all**, because it is nobody's
job.

## 2. What she actually sells

A file. That is the entire product surface.

**They send:** a CSV export, their platform, their taxonomy, a brand-voice sample, three products
they like the look of.

**They receive, 48 hours later:** the same CSV with the empty columns filled —

- Title, written to the platform's character limit
- Short description and long description, in their voice, not the manufacturer's
- Five bullet points
- **Attributes extracted and normalised** — the single highest-value column, because it is what makes filters work
- Category mapped to the target taxonomy
- SEO title, meta description, keywords
- Formatted for direct import: Shopify CSV, Amazon flat file, or Google Merchant feed

**Nothing is connected to anything.** No OAuth, no webhook, no store access, no password. That is
not a limitation — **it is the reason it can be bought at 2am from a Stripe link with no
onboarding**, which no competitor in the AI-department market can offer.

## 3. The real asset is the validation layer, not the writing

**Anyone can generate a product description. That part is nearly free and will be entirely free
soon.** The part that is not free, and that compounds:

> **A rules engine that knows what each platform actually requires** — Amazon's per-category
> character limits and prohibited claims, Google Merchant's required attribute set, Shopify's
> metafield structure, which category needs `voltage` and which needs `thread pitch` — and refuses
> to emit a row that would be rejected.

**Offshore BPO writes what you tell them. Her pipeline writes what the platform will accept.** That
is the difference between a file and a file that imports cleanly the first time, and it is the
sentence the sales page should lead with.

Every catalogue she processes adds taxonomy mappings and category schemas to that layer. **Client
five is cheaper to serve than client one.** That is the moat, and building it is exactly
*"organising"* and *"digging through data"* — her own words for the work she wants.

## 4. Her week

- **Morning:** check overnight runs, look at flagged rows
- **Per job:** review a 25-row QA sample from each 500, correct the rule rather than the row, re-run
- **Ongoing:** extend the validation layer as new categories appear
- **Written client contact only** — a shared thread, no calls, ever
- **Marketing:** before-and-after posts in seller groups, updating the page

**1–3 hours per client per month once the pipeline is built.** This is the closest match in 66
candidates to *"writing, designing, organising, digging through data — with AI doing the heavy
lifting while she supervises that the system is working."*

## 6. THE EXPANSION LADDER

**Climb it in order. Each rung uses the same pipeline and adds no new risk until rung 4.**

### Rung 1 — Depth in the same client *(do this first, it is free revenue)*

| Move | Price | Why it is nearly free to deliver |
|---|---|---|
| Volume tiers | 1.500 SKUs **$2.499** · 5.000 SKUs **$5.999** | Same pipeline, same QA sample size |
| **The backfill project** | **$0,40–1,00/SKU on their existing 20.000** = **$8.000–20.000 one-off** | The catalogue is already there. **One backfill is two to three months of target from a single client** |
| Multi-channel | +$400–800/mo per extra channel | Same content, re-emitted through a different rule set. **3× the value, ~1,2× the work** |
| Translation | +$500–1.000/mo per language | **The content is already written.** This is the highest-margin line on the page |

> **One client can go from $999 to $3.000–5.000/month plus a five-figure backfill without a single
> new customer.** At $2.500/client the target needs **3 clients, not 7** — and the concentration
> problem inverts into a depth advantage.

### Rung 2 — Adjacent deliverables, same shape, same buyer

Everything below is file-in, file-out, zero wiring, and sells to the customer she already has:

- **Category and collection page copy** — the pages that actually rank
- **Buying guides** — "how to choose a X", built from her own attribute data
- **Image alt text and A+ / enhanced content copy**
- **Product Q&A and FAQ**, generated from their reviews and specs
- **Review mining** — *"what customers complain about, per SKU"*, which is a genuine merchandising insight and the easiest thing here to charge $1.500 for
- **Assortment and price gap reports** vs named competitors — public data, document output

**Each one is a line item on an existing invoice**, which is the cheapest revenue in any business.

### Rung 3 — Adjacent buyers, same pipeline

| Buyer | Catalogue size | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Shopify / Amazon merchants** | 500–5.000 | **Start here** — the only genuinely self-serve-native B2B buyer |
| Brands and manufacturers going direct | 1.000–10.000 | They own the source specs and have the worst content |
| Distributors and wholesalers | 20.000–200.000 | **Biggest cheques**, slowest to buy, may want a call — approach only once there is a public track record |
| **E-commerce agencies, white-label** | 10–30 catalogues behind one relationship | **The real leverage rung.** One conversation, twenty catalogues — but it is a partner sale and probably a call. **Take it only if it comes inbound** |

### Rung 4 — Productise it and remove herself *(only after the rules are proven)*

Upload a CSV → pay **$0,30–0,80/SKU** → download. No human in the loop, no minimum, instant.

**This is where the ceiling disappears entirely**, because revenue stops being a function of her
hours. It is also the Shopify App Store play the marketplace case studies already validated —
including the seeding pattern that worked: **Kaching gave the app away in Facebook dropshipping
groups, hit 500 merchants in week one and $1k MRR in four months.**

**Do not start here.** Self-serve competes directly with tools, which is Law 1 territory; the
service does not. The service earns the rules that make the product possible.

### Rung 5 — The accumulated asset

After thirty catalogues she owns something nobody can copy quickly: **taxonomy mappings, per-category
attribute schemas, platform rule sets, and a corpus of what good content looks like in each
vertical.** Every new client is faster and cheaper than the last. **Margin compounds instead of
flattening**, which is the one thing 65 previous candidates never had.

## 7. What kills it — unchanged and honest

1. **It is a price fight with offshore BPO**, who have done catalogue data for twenty years at
   $0,30–1,50/SKU. **Her defence is not price — it is 48-hour turnaround, deterministic platform
   validation, and a Stripe link instead of a contract.** If she competes on price per SKU she loses.
2. **Seven clients is fragile** until rung 1 is climbed. Climb it fast.
3. **Quality is publicly blamable.** 500 wrong rows on a live storefront is visible. A written
   accuracy standard and a free-redo policy are mandatory from day one.
4. **The sales call.** It escapes three of the four B2B buyer properties. Whether it escapes the
   fourth is the only real unknown, and it is what the test buys.

## 8. The test — two weeks, under $100, no build

1. **Publish the page**: price, scope, 48-hour turnaround, accuracy standard, redo policy, Stripe
   link. **No call offered anywhere on it.** The Penji motion, copied exactly.
2. **Three free catalogues, 100 SKUs each**, for sellers found in Shopify and Amazon seller groups.
   Measure her real hours and the error rate.
3. **Post the before-and-after** in those groups — written, faceless, Kaching's move.
4. **Close two paying clients at $999 without ever speaking to anyone.**

**Kill if:** any buyer insists on a call before paying · her time exceeds 6 h/client/month by the
third catalogue · more than one redo round is needed · two free catalogues convert to zero paid ·
a buyer names an existing vendor doing it under $400/month.
