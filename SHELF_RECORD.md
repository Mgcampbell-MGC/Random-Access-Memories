# SHELF RECORD — the business, plainly
**2 Sep 2026. The candidate the ground hunt produced, written the way the founder asked: what it is, not how
to test it.**

---

## 1. What it is

**A brand in London, Los Angeles or Milan sells its products in Brazil. It has no office here, no staff here
and nobody here.** It ships to an importer, and from that moment it is blind. Everything it knows about the
Brazilian market arrives in an email, once a quarter, **written by the distributor being judged in it.**

**Sol walks into the shops and looks.**

Once a month she covers a fixed route of São Paulo retailers and photographs the shelves — systematically, in
order, timestamped. Is the product there at all. At what price. In what condition. Beside which competitor.
Is the display current. Is it turning up in a channel the contract forbids. She writes down what she saw,
attaches the photographs, and sends it.

> ### **She is the brand's eyes in a country it cannot see. She offers no opinion, no strategy and no advice — she reports what was on the shelf, on a named day, with the picture attached.**

## 2. ★★ The thing that makes it a business instead of a job

**She photographs the whole category once, and sells each brand in it its own view of the same trip.**

Standing in one beauty retailer she captures the entire shelf. In that photograph are twelve brands. **Eleven
of them are somebody else's client.** The walking, which is the expensive part, is paid for once and sold many
times.

| | Her time |
|---|---|
| The route itself — 15–25 shops, photographed properly | **12–16 h/month, and it does not grow** |
| Each additional client's report, extracted from the same capture | **+2–3 h/month** |
| **Four clients** | **~24 h/month** |
| **Six clients** | **~30 h/month** — still inside her limit |

**Every business this file has killed had costs that grew with customers. This one's largest cost is fixed.**

## 3. ★★★ And what accumulates — the part that cannot be bought later

Month after month, the same shelves, photographed the same way, dated. After a year that is **a
month-by-month photographic record of who was on the shelf in Brazil, at what price, next to whom.**

> **A brand that signs in month twelve can buy the previous eleven months immediately. It cannot commission
> them at any price, from anyone, because nobody else was standing there with a camera.** Not scraped data,
> not a public register — **a physical record of a physical fact that expires the moment the shelf is
> restocked.**

This is the first genuinely un-rebuildable asset in the file that does not require a credential, a licence, or
somebody else's permission. **It is built by walking.**

## 4. Who buys it

**Foreign consumer brands with Brazilian distribution and no Brazilian presence** — and the first vertical
should be the one she knows: **beauty, skincare and personal care**, sold through Brazilian perfumerias,
pharmacy chains and department stores. Brazil is one of the largest beauty markets in the world; the brands
selling into it are numerous, foreign, and blind.

**The person who signs is a regional or export manager** — someone with a Brazilian distributor, a number to
hit, and no way to check anything they are told.

**Why they cannot solve it themselves:**
- **Asking the distributor is asking the defendant.** It is the only channel they have and it is the one being
  evaluated.
- **A Brazilian merchandising agency is paid by the distributor**, and works for the big accounts.
- **Retail measurement panels** sell scanner data at enterprise prices, do not photograph anything, and will
  not track one small foreign brand's SKUs.
- **And nothing remote works.** No amount of software puts a person in a shop in Moema on a Tuesday.

## 5. What she actually sends, every month

A short document. Route and date. For each shop: present or absent, shelf price, facings, condition,
neighbours, promotional material, anything out of place. Every claim carries its photograph. A one-page
summary at the front: *what changed since last month.* **No recommendations. No analysis. No opinion.**

**And two things sold on top, on request:**
- **Test purchase** — she buys a suspect item, documents the seller, photographs the batch codes, keeps chain
  of custody and ships it to the brand or its lawyers. *(This is exactly what the counterfeit work needed and
  could never do from a desk.)*
- **Proof of use** — a dated evidence pack showing the mark genuinely in commerce in Brazil, for a brand
  defending its registration against cancellation. **Measured: 79 such cancellations were notified against
  foreign holders in four weeks.** She gathers the evidence; their lawyer argues it.

## 6. The shape of the money

*Price is the one thing not yet verified — nobody in this market publishes a rate. What follows is the shape,
not a forecast.*

| | |
|---|---|
| Monthly retainer, one brand, one category, one city | **US$600–900** |
| Test purchase | **US$150–300** plus the cost of the goods |
| Proof-of-use evidence pack | **US$500–900** |
| **At the target** | **4 brands** |
| Comfortable | 6 brands ≈ US$4.000/month gross, ~30 h |

Paid in dollars. Taxed as an exported service. No inventory, no employees, no premises. Her costs are a metro
card, a camera she already owns, and coffee.

## 7. Why this one fits her when the others did not

- **She leaves the house** — which she has now said she wants, and which is the entire source of the advantage.
- **It is her world**: beauty and retail, shops she would walk into anyway, a visual and aesthetic job.
- **She is never the expert.** She is the witness. *"This is what was on the shelf"* needs no credential, no
  qualification and no one's belief.
- **The work is looking and writing**, which is the pair of things she asked for at the very beginning.
- **Nothing she delivers can be wrong**, because it is a photograph.

## 8. What is genuinely uncertain — the three things that decide it
1. **The price.** No published rate exists anywhere for this. Everything in §6 is a shape.
2. **★ Whether Brazilian retailers permit photography in store.** Many do not, and store staff will intervene.
   **This is the operational fact the whole method rests on and it is unverified.** If open photography is not
   possible, the method changes — discreet capture, or written observation and no image — and a written note
   is worth materially less than a picture.
3. **Whether a foreign brand cares enough about Brazil to spend US$700 a month watching it.** For a brand
   doing serious Brazilian volume, obviously. For one testing the market with a small distributor, unknown.

## 9. The honest summary

**A dated photographic record of what is actually on Brazilian shelves, sold to the foreign brands that appear
on them and cannot come and look.** One trip serves every client on the route, the archive compounds into
something nobody can reconstruct, and the whole thing rests on the one advantage she has that no competitor
can copy, automate or undercut: **she is here, and they are not.**
