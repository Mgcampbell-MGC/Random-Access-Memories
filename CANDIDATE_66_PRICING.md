# CANDIDATE 66 — FIXED PRICE PER DELIVERY

**14 Aug 2026. Question: can this be a fixed price per job instead of a monthly fee, and what
should that price be?**

**Answer: yes, and it should be. Fixed price is the better primary model here, for three reasons —
and the subscription comes back later as the second sale, not the first.**

---

## 1. The economic fact that decides the pricing shape

**Her cost is per JOB, not per SKU.**

| Work | Time | Scales with size? |
|---|---|---|
| Set up the job, tune rules to their category and voice | 45–90 min | **No** |
| Review a QA sample (25 rows, whatever the job size) | 30–45 min | **No** |
| Export, format, deliver | 15 min | **No** |
| Handle flagged rows | 0–45 min | Slightly |
| **Tokens** | **$0,03–0,10/SKU** *(up to $0,30 when the source is photographed spec sheets needing OCR and vision)* | **Yes** |

**A 200-product job and a 2.000-product job both cost her roughly 1,5–2,5 hours.** Only tokens
scale. Therefore the price must carry **a real minimum and a steeply declining per-product rate** —
a flat per-SKU price would either bankrupt her on small jobs or leave money on large ones.

---

## 2. The two market anchors — both measured

### Upper anchor: what writing alone costs

[Product description copywriters](https://www.susangreenecopywriter.com/articles/how-to-set-pricing-for-writing-product-descriptions.html)
charge **$3,00 to $18,00 per description**, with ~$5 typical at a $50/hour rate. Per-word rates run
$0,05–0,50.

### Lower anchor: what enrichment alone costs — and it describes this business exactly

> **"Backfile enrichment costs roughly $0,40 to $1,50 per SKU depending on attribute depth and
> source difficulty, with a monthly retainer of $1.500 to $6.000 for ongoing new-SKU onboarding and
> supplier catalog updates."**
> — [enrichment services rate guidance, 2026](https://hootcore.io/blog/ecommerce-product-data-enrichment-services-guide-2026)

Plus two facts from the same body of sources worth keeping:

- **Outsourcing saves 60–70% against an in-house product data specialist at $38k–45k/year US.**
- **"While PIM adoption is rising, the enrichment remains the bottleneck"** — the container-versus-
  filled distinction, confirmed by the market rather than asserted by me.

### Two corrections to my own numbers

1. **I underpriced the retainer.** I proposed a $999/month entry tier. **The market retainer floor
   is $1.500.** I was below the floor of a market I had not measured.
2. **My implied $2,00/SKU monthly rate sits above the pure-enrichment band of $0,40–1,50.** That is
   defensible *only because she also writes the copy*, which a $0,40 data-entry vendor does not.
   **The page must make the combined scope explicit or she simply reads as expensive.**

### The position that follows

> **A copywriter writes but does not structure: $3–18 per product. A data-entry vendor structures
> but does not write: $0,40–1,50. She does both, in one pass, in 48 hours — so she belongs above
> enrichment and far below copywriting.**

That sentence is the whole pricing argument, and it belongs on the sales page.

## 3. THE PRICE LADDER

| Job | Fixed price | Per product | Her hours | Effective rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **50 — the sample** | **$149** | $2,98 | 1,25 | $116/h |
| **250** | **$549** | $2,20 | 1,5 | $354/h |
| **500** | **$899** | $1,80 | 2,0 | **$432/h** |
| **1.000** | **$1.549** | $1,55 | 2,5 | **$592/h** |
| **2.500** | **$2.999** | $1,20 | 3,5 | **$807/h** |
| **5.000** | **$4.999** | $1,00 | 5,0 | **$930/h** |
| **10.000+** | **$0,80/product** | | 8,0 | ~$875/h |
| **25.000+** | **$0,65/product** | | | quoted from a form — still no call |

**Add-ons, priced as a percentage of the job so they need no separate arithmetic:**

| Add-on | Uplift | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Each extra sales channel | **+25%** | Same content re-emitted through a different rule set |
| Each extra language | **+40%** | The content already exists — highest margin line on the page |
| 24-hour rush | **+50%** | Pure scheduling |
| Photographed or PDF source material | **+30%** | **Real cost** — OCR and vision passes cost several times plain text |

**The $149 sample is deliberately the worst hour she works.** It is not a profit line, it is the
trial that replaces the sales call — and at 50 products a buyer can judge the quality in ten
minutes.

## 4. What it takes to hit the targets

R$30.000/month ≈ **US$5.780** · R$12.000/month ≈ **US$2.312** *(at 5,19)*

| Working at this size | Jobs for R$12.000 | Jobs for R$30.000 | Hours/month at target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 500 products ($864 net) | 3 | **7** | 14 |
| 1.000 products ($1.479 net) | 2 | **4** | 10 |
| 2.500 products ($2.824 net) | 1 | **2** | 7 |
| 5.000 products ($4.649 net) | — | **1,25** | 6 |

**One 5.000-product job is 80% of a month's target from a single delivery.** That is the number
that makes the fixed-price model work, and it is exactly the backfill job every new client needs
first.

---

## 5. What she gains and what she loses

### Gains — and the first one is the important one

1. **The first sale gets radically easier.** *"Try 50 products for $149"* is a card purchase.
   *"Commit to $999 a month"* is a decision. **The single biggest risk in this candidate is whether
   it can be bought without a call, and a one-off sample is the lowest-friction version of that
   test that exists.**
2. **It matches how the demand actually arrives.** Catalogue work is lumpy — a big cleanup, a new
   supplier line, a seasonal drop. Not a smooth 500 a month.
3. **No churn to manage**, and no cancellation to fear.
4. **It matches her stated preference** for a deliverable over a monthly subscription.

### Loses — one thing, and it is real

**Every month starts at zero.** She must land 4–7 jobs a month, forever, and month 6 does not open
with month 5's revenue already booked. Against her own trajectory bar — **month 8 at R$3.000 is
failing** — recurring revenue is the safer instrument and she is giving it up.

---

## 6. The resolution: project first, retainer second

**Do not choose. Sequence them.**

> **Fixed price is the front door. The retainer is the second sale, offered only after the first
> job has been delivered and judged.**

*"Your catalogue is current as of today. New products arrive every month — the keep-up plan holds
the line for $1.500/month, cancel any time."*

This is better than leading with the subscription on every count:

- They have already seen the quality, so the retainer needs no promises
- It is sized to their **real** new-product rate rather than a guessed tier
- **$1.500 is the measured market floor**, not my invented $999
- And it converts a one-off buyer into recurring revenue **without ever having required a
  commitment up front**

**Every productized service that works grows this way — project, then retainer.** The mistake was
starting at the retainer.

---

## 7. What this changes in the plan

- **The sales page leads with the ladder**, and the $149 sample is the primary call to action.
- **The two-week test gets easier and sharper**: instead of *"close two clients at $999/month"* it
  becomes **"sell five $149 samples and convert two into jobs of 500+."** More signal, faster,
  from smaller commitments.
- **The retainer is priced from $1.500**, not $999.
- **Nothing else changes** — same pipeline, same validation layer, same zero integration, same
  ladder above it.
