# THE DEPARTMENT PLAY

**14 Aug 2026. Written in response to: "how can we give a company another employee — no, a team —
no, an entire department."**

**The seed is right, it is already proven at exactly her price point, and there is one specific
reason every existing version needs a salesperson. That reason is the whole opportunity.**

---

## 1. The frame was right and I under-used it

The single most valuable thing in the push is the **price anchor**:

> **A tool is priced against other tools ($5–50/month). A department is priced against payroll
> (R$4.000–8.000/month loaded).** Same work, same buyer, a 20–100× difference in what can be
> charged — decided entirely by what the buyer compares it to.

That is not a positioning trick. It is the reason the B2B price ceiling — one of the four properties
in the B2B buyer law — **can be escaped.** I treated the ceiling as fixed. It is not: it is a
function of the comparison the buyer makes, and the comparison is chosen by the seller.

### And it is proven, self-serve, at her exact price point

[**Penji publishes $499/month**](https://penji.co/design-pickle-alternative/) — unlimited design
requests, async queue, 24–48h SLA. Design Pickle, ManyPixels, Flocksy and Superside run the same
model. The category's own description of why it works:

> *"Design subscriptions productize creative work into a flat monthly fee, an async queue, and a
> dedicated designer or team, replacing agency proposals and hourly rates with **a Stripe link, a
> dashboard**"* … *"transparent pricing as a customer-acquisition lever; it **lowers friction in
> evaluation, removes proposal cycles, and lets buyers self-serve**."*

**A department, sold from a page, at $499/month, with no call.** The exact thing the record said
was impossible. It has been a category for a decade.

### The AI version already exists and is priced

- **$399/month** DIY setup · **$999/month** done-for-you
- [**AIQ Labs "Managed AI Employee"** — from **$599/month**, spanning **99 distinct roles**](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/36503892/aiq-labs-launches-managed-ai-employee-service-bringing-fortune-500-automation-to-small-business-for-under-1-500-month)

So the market exists, the price band is $499–1.500/month, and buyers are already trained.

## 2. THE INTEGRATION TAX — why all of them still need a salesperson

Here is the fact that turns this from an observation into a wedge:

> *"Once you add your own setup hours, an internal owner, and the month-one correction load, **the
> first-year total typically lands at roughly double the advertised subscription.** If you hire a
> basic setup contractor to help you link the webhooks, it costs between **$1.000 and $10.000**."*

Every AI-employee product on the market must be **wired into the customer's systems** — CRM, inbox,
phone, helpdesk, bank. That wiring is what creates:

- the **scoping call** (what do you use, what do we connect)
- the **credential custody** (OAuth tokens into their stack)
- the **trust gate** (security questionnaire, DPA, MSA)
- the **bespoke deployment** (SingleBrain's five days, per client)
- and the **$1.000–10.000 hidden cost** that makes buyers hesitate and demand a human

> ### THE INTEGRATION TAX LAW
> **Every AI-department product is sold on a call because it must be wired in. The only version a
> faceless solo founder can sell is the department that needs no wiring at all: public or
> client-supplied input, document output, zero access to anything.**

**That is not a compromise. It is the differentiator** — it is also the only version that can be
bought at 2am from a Stripe link with no onboarding, which is a genuine advantage over every
competitor listed above.

## 3. The elimination — which departments survive zero wiring

| Department | Needs wiring? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Sales / SDR | CRM + sending domain | **Dead** |
| Support | Helpdesk + inbox | **Dead** |
| Reception | Phone | **Dead** (and calls) |
| Bookkeeping | Bank + ERP | **Dead**, and regulated |
| Content / social | No | **Dead** — Law 1, free tools, R$800 freelancers |
| Design | No | **Dead** — Penji owns it, Canva does the safe version free |
| **Intelligence / monitoring** | **No** | Survives wiring · **often fails the expertise veto** |
| **Finding (bids, grants)** | **No** | Survives wiring · **usually has a free or cheap incumbent** |
| **File-in / file-out production** | **No** | **Survives everything** |
| **Deadline drafting (RFP, reports)** | **No** | Survives wiring · **heavy founder labour + trust gate** |

Two more filters kill most of the survivors:

- **Anything requiring claimed expertise is out** — regulatory watch, ESG questionnaires,
  compliance briefings. All commercially attractive, all require her to be *believed as an expert*,
  which is her own veto. This is what kills the intelligence plays, not the market.
- **Anything the client's existing software already contains is out** — clearing party.

**One column survives all of it: file-in, file-out production at a volume no human wants and no
chat window can hold.**

## 4. THE PLAY — the catalogue content department

**"Your product content department. 500 SKUs a month, written, attributed and categorised.
$999/month. No integration, no onboarding, no call."**

The client exports a CSV. She returns a CSV. Nothing is connected to anything.

### Why this one and not the others

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| **Payroll anchor** | A PIM/data specialist is $45–60k/year. **$999/month is a fifth of one** |
| **Zero wiring** | CSV in, CSV out. No OAuth, no webhook, no credential, no setup cost |
| **No expertise claimed** | She is not advising anyone. She is producing volume. **The veto never fires** |
| **Chat-window test** | 500 SKUs a month with attribute mapping and category rules is not one user, one moment |
| **Law 1** | Nobody gives away 500 written SKUs |
| **Clearing party** | **Akeneo, Salsify and Plytix are containers, not labour** — the same container-versus-filled distinction that made candidate 64 work. PIM software gives you a place to put product content. **It does not write it** |
| **Recurring by nature** | New SKUs arrive every month, forever. The job never completes |
| **Founder labour** | The most automatable work assessed in 65 candidates. Pipeline plus spot QA |
| **Faceless** | Nobody wants to meet their data vendor |

### Arithmetic

| | |
|---|---|
| Price | **$999/month** |
| Token and tooling cost at volume | **−$40–80** |
| **Contribution per client** | **≈ $920 ≈ R$4.775** |

> **R$30.000/month ≈ US$5.780 ≈ 7 clients.**
> **R$12.000/month ≈ 3 clients.**

At 2 hours per client per month once the pipeline is built: **~14 hours a month at the three-year
target.** Seven customers is the entire business — which is also the risk, since losing two is
losing 30% of revenue.

### The channel already exists in this file

Buyers with 500+ SKUs who purchase from a page: **Shopify brands, Amazon and Walmart sellers,
manufacturers going direct.** The marketplace case studies already established Shopify as the
kindest platform and the seeding pattern that works — **Kaching gave the app away in Facebook
dropshipping groups and hit $1k MRR in four months.** Same groups, same written faceless motion,
and here the offer is stronger because it replaces a hire rather than adding a tool.

## 5. What actually kills it — stated plainly

1. **The commodity race.** Offshore BPO does catalogue data cheaply and has for twenty years. Her
   defence is speed, price and a Stripe link rather than a contract — but **this is a price fight,
   not a moat**, and it is the single most likely cause of death.
2. **Seven customers is a fragile business.** Two churns is a third of revenue. Concentration risk
   that candidate 64 does not have.
3. **Quality liability at volume.** 500 wrong SKUs on a live storefront is a visible, blamable
   failure. A written accuracy standard and a redo policy are mandatory, not optional.
4. **B2B is still B2B.** It escapes three of the four buyer properties — no diligence at $999
   self-serve, no incumbent doing the *labour*, payroll anchor instead of SaaS anchor. **It only
   escapes the fourth (the sales call) if the Penji model holds at this price for this buyer**, and
   that is the thing to test.

**Runner-up, kept on the shelf:** deadline drafting — RFP and proposal responses at $799 per
response. Higher price, real pain, no wiring. Rejected as the lead because a 60-page response is
4–8 hours of founder labour, it caps at ~15 a month, and a company betting a $2M contract will
eventually want to meet someone.

---

## 6. The test — under $100, about two weeks, no build

1. **Publish the page.** Price, scope, turnaround, accuracy standard, redo policy, Stripe link.
   No call offered anywhere on it. This is the Penji motion copied exactly.
2. **Do three catalogues free**, 100 SKUs each, for three sellers found in Shopify and Amazon
   seller groups. Measure her real hours and the error rate.
3. **Post the before-and-after** in those groups. Written, faceless. Kaching's move.
4. **Sell two paying clients at $999 with no call.**

**Kill conditions:** any buyer insists on a call before paying · her time exceeds 6 hours per client
per month at the third catalogue · error rate needs more than one redo round · two free catalogues
produce zero paid conversions · a buyer names an existing vendor doing it under $400/month.

---

## 7. Honest ranking against candidate 64

**Candidate 64 remains first**, on three grounds: a consumer buyer cannot demand a call, 45 orders
is a more robust base than 7 clients, and it has no commodity competitor.

**But this is the first B2B candidate in 65 that escapes three of the four B2B buyer properties**,
and it escapes them for a reason that is structural rather than hopeful: **the integration tax.**
Everyone else has to wire in. She does not, and that is precisely why she can sell from a page.

**It is worth the two-week test, and the test costs nothing but her time.**
