## 1. ★★ THE STATE-TRIGGER LAW — the hunt's chief output, verified twice independently

Two different insurers' full policy wordings were fetched and read at source — **Save the Date v5 Jan 2026**
(my probe, `THE_WEDDING_INSURANCE_PROBE.md`) and **WeddingPlan/Accelerant Feb 2026** (the hunt, 438 KB PDF).
**Both define the trigger identically and both name the same validator:**

> *"Financial Failure: The bankruptcy, compulsory liquidation, insolvency, compulsory winding-up and/or the
> appointment of a receiver or administrator…"*
> *"We may, at our discretion, rely on publicly available records (such as **Companies House or The Gazette**)
> to validate the financial failure of your supplier."*
> *(Both wordings, near-verbatim — shared underwriting language across the market.)*

> ### **A CLAIMS POLICY THAT DEFINES ITS TRIGGER AS A FORMAL LEGAL EVENT HAS OUTSOURCED ITS EVIDENCE TO THE STATE. Where the trigger is "bankruptcy / liquidation / administration," `THE GAZETTE LAW` eats the entire evidence market before it exists.**

**And the perimeter completes the kill:** suppliers *"not registered on Companies House"* — the sole traders who
actually vanish with deposits, the only population where perishable web-state evidence would matter — **are
excluded from cover entirely. The perishable half is the uninsured half.** Every other evidentiary demand in
either wording is claimant-owned (written contract, deposits, police report). `THE CLAIMANT-EVIDENCE LAW` holds
at 100% across both documents.

## 2. ★ THE RECOVERY INDUSTRY IS THE CARD SCHEME, PRICE ZERO

The domain's biggest money-at-risk is deposits — and the recovery layer already exists, free, by statute:
**chargeback plus s.75 Consumer Credit Act** (issuer jointly liable, £100–£30.000). **That is why no success-fee
deposit-recovery trade exists at wedding ticket sizes.** `THE STATUTE IS NOT THE BUSINESS; THE RECOVERY INDUSTRY
IS` — and here the recovery industry is the card rails.
