## First: what the other model's three have in common — and it is the thing that decides them

**All three are ONE shape: a high-ticket intelligence retainer, US$3.500–4.500/month, sold to a foreign or
large enterprise buyer, delivered by contractors, with "accumulated history" as the claimed moat.** Each
reaches Scenario A on paper with 4–5 clients. **Each admits its price is invented.**

That shape has a binding constraint, and it is not economics — it is **whether a 26-year-old with no track
record, no firm and no name can close a brand-protection team at Galderma or a compliance director at
bet365.** That is `THE ONBOARDING LAW` (enterprise vendor onboarding is a procurement process, not a sale)
plus `THE OBSERVED-PRICE CEILING` (MarqVision and Rightlander hold the anchor because they have things she
lacks). **The other model saw this on every one and said so.** It is the untested assumption in all three,
and no amount of structure fixes it.

**And the arithmetic is optimistic in the same way each time.** Hunt 2 measured the honest ceiling of a
contractor-delivered retainer at **US$4.500–6.000/month net** — three agents, three verticals, no
coordination. These three model COGS at 25–30%; investigation and human-validated monitoring run higher.
**Expect Scenario A on paper to land at US$5–7k net in practice. Still far better than Scenario B. Not A.**

## The four, screened

### VERA Brasil — counterfeit-injectable evidence for foreign manufacturers · *(other model: 7,1)* · **MINE: 5,5 — best of its three**
**Verified:** the problem is real and recurring — Dysport P22686 seized 27 Aug (RE 3.356/2026), and the
pattern of new lots every few weeks is exactly the second-purchase clock. **Her domain.** Foreign payer.
**The structure is the right one** under `THE REPLACEMENT-COST CEILING`: seller-identity history is world-state
evidence a manufacturer genuinely cannot reconstruct — the *right* side of `THE CLAIMANT-EVIDENCE LAW`.
**Kills it:** (1) `THE SPECULATIVE-CAPTURE COROLLARY` — *the archive is empty on launch day*; the moat it sells
does not exist until year two. (2) Enterprise sale to big pharma with no track record. (3) Test purchases of a
**prescription** toxin need a licensed partner she does not have. (4) US$3.500 is invented; MarqVision holds
the ceiling. **Realistic path: white-label to MarqVision/Red Points as their Brazil layer — Scenario B, and
their margin.**

### RAIZ Brasil — SisGen biodiversity desk for foreign beauty companies · *(7,0)* · **MINE: 4,0**
**Verified:** the clock-start is real and imminent — Decreto 13.014/2026, SisGen 3.0 launching September.
That is the rarest property in this file (`THE HEAD-START ASYMMETRY`: only a clock-start is level).
**Kills it:** it is a **compelled-document business** — SisGen registrations, product notifications, annual
benefit-sharing filings. The other model argues *"it's a portfolio control system, not a form."* **That is
verbatim the argument CONFORMIDADE v2 made ("17 interlocking documents, not a template"), and the family died
36 times on `THE TEMPLATE FLOOR TEST`.** Brazilian SisGen consultancies already exist. The client owns every
input to the "ledger" and can commission it. Market size explicitly unverified. **Watch the activation; do
not build.**

### VIGIA 18 Brasil — betting-affiliate advertising control · *(7,2)* · **MINE: 4,5**
**Verified:** the rule is real and sharp — 10% warning, effective 17 July, fines up to 20% of revenue, and
liability extends to affiliates. Finite public buyer list with CNPJs. **Best structural properties of the
three.** **Kills it:** (1) **C8** — gambling compliance, three years, alone. GateLedger died on exactly this
after passing every structural test, and the other model flagged it as *"not cosmetic."* (2) Rightlander is a
funded incumbent in this exact niche. (3) **It is priced above the internal hire** — US$4.200/month ≈ R$22k
against a Portuguese-speaking compliance analyst at ~R$8–15k all-in. `THE POSTO CONSTANT`'s cousin. (4)
Enterprise sale to bet365.

### REPRESENTANTE — Brazilian legal representative for foreign companies · *(mine)* · **5,0 — the only one with OBSERVED prices and a COMPELLED buyer**
**Verified, all primary:** **IN RFB 2.119/2022 art. 6 §1** makes a Brazil-resident *procurador* mandatory for
every foreign-owned CNPJ. **LPI art. 217** requires one for direct foreign INPI filings. **Registro.br** requires
one for any foreigner wanting a `.com.br`. Qualification: *"natural person resident in Brazil with full civil
capacity"* — **no credential. She has it by birth, and a foreign buyer cannot self-supply it at any price.**
Liability: **CTN art. 135 III** attaches only on excess of powers or violation of law; case law shields a mere
procurador. **Observed prices: US$800/month (Lematt) · US$400/month · R$500–1.500/month local tier.**
**Why it matters:** this is the class the file closed on *"the faceless constraint and credential custody are
structurally incompatible"* — **and C2 and C4 are both relaxed.** It reopens cleanly.
**Kills Scenario A:** not sub-linear — a standing role, 8–10 client ceiling ⇒ **US$3–7k/month**. **Reaches
Scenario B with 3–6 clients at observed prices.** **Weakest link: distribution** — law firms own the referral
channel and no directory exists. Partial answer: INPI's weekly RPI XML carries `titulares` with a `país`
field, so **every foreign trademark applicant is enumerable for free**; foreign-CNPJ volume is UNVERIFIED.
