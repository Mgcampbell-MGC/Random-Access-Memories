## 2. The new door: celebrants

### The list source is the cleanest in this entire file

**Australia's Attorney-General's Department publishes the register of Commonwealth-registered marriage
celebrants at `marriage.ag.gov.au`.** In the department's own words, the register *"lists the **name,
contact details and address** of each Commonwealth registered marriage celebrant and is published on the
department's website."*

**Over 10.000 registered celebrants.** Named, addressed, contactable, government-published, free.

> **D6 and G3 in one artefact.** Every list source this file has chased — forced-publication notices,
> licensing databases, FDD filings — is worse than this one. This is a government-maintained directory of
> ten thousand sole traders who each own a customer and each face an immovable date.

## 3. The wedge — and it is deliberately narrow

**Do not sell the ceremony.** For many celebrants, writing the ceremony *is* the craft they sell — that is
the same reason Life Story Network keeps its writing in-house, and it will be the first objection.

> **Sell the LOVE STORY section only.** 600–1.000 words of narrative about two strangers, built from the
> questionnaire the celebrant already sends. It is the most labour-intensive part of the script and **the
> part that is not ritual craft.**
>
> **The celebrant keeps the ritual, the legal words, the delivery, the room and the relationship. She
> writes the story.**

The section is standardised — the love story is conventionally delivered in the first half of the ceremony,
in the segment called the invocation — which makes it a **scopeable, repeatable deliverable** rather than a
bespoke commission.

### The counter-seasonality finding

**Australian wedding season runs roughly October–April. UK and Northern Hemisphere runs May–September.**

> **They are opposite. Serving both hemispheres fills each other's trough** — which turns a seasonal
> business into a year-round one, with no extra product and no extra engine. **That is a structural
> argument for going non-US that has nothing to do with the US.**

## 5. Arithmetic

**Wholesale at $80–150 per love story**, against $250 retail (Summit) and £445 for a full script (Speechy).
Leaves the celebrant room to charge $200–300 or fold it into their package.

- A working celebrant does **20–80 weddings a year** — call it 40, so **~3 a month**
- At $120 wholesale: **~$360/month per celebrant**
- **Her floor needs roughly 8 active celebrants**, out of **10.000+ in Australia alone**

**Honest comparison:** 3 units/month per partner is **worse than a funeral home's ~8**, so this needs more
onboarding relationships to reach the same revenue. That is the real cost of the switch.

## 6. The risks, in order

1. **The craft-identity objection.** Many celebrants believe the writing is what they sell. The love-story
   scope is designed to dodge it, but **it will still be the first thing said back.** Unverified.
2. **More partners needed** — ~8 celebrants versus ~3 funeral homes for the same revenue.
3. **The chatbot, and it may be worse here.** Celebrants are less regulated and less risk-averse than
   funeral directors, so they may adopt AI drafting faster. Racing the same clock, possibly at speed.
4. **C1 at the channel level.** Celebrants are extroverts who chose a people business. **Mitigating
   evidence: their intake is already a written questionnaire** — the best sign this file has seen.
5. **Weddings are not a downturn market.** Agent 8 verified average spend held at **$34.000** and **77% of
   couples adjusting budgets INCREASED** them. Good for durability, but the recession is not an
   acquisition channel here — and 52% cite vendor responsiveness, which a written-only vendor must
   answer with speed rather than availability.

## 8. Status and the free first test

**Two facts verified** — the Australian register with its 10.000+ named entries, and a $250 retail price
point for celebrant script writing. **Everything else in this file is structure, not evidence.**

**The test, at $0, and it is a count rather than a pitch:**

Write **one** love story from a real, public couple's story. Send it to **twenty Australian celebrants**
pulled straight off `marriage.ag.gov.au`, with one written question:

> *"Do you write the couple's story yourself, outsource it, or reuse a template — and if you'd pay someone
> to write it, what would that be worth to you?"*

**Count the written replies containing a number.** That settles the craft-identity objection, the price and
the C1 channel question simultaneously. **G17 — twenty real instances, count how many clear.**

**And the second test, which decides whether this is a machine or a job:** write two love stories for the
**same** celebrant's format and time both. **If the second takes under half the first, G2 is real.**
