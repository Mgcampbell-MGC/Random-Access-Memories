# Royalty asset marketplaces — hunted and killed, 17 Aug 2026

**Candidates #1, #2 and #14 from the 17 Aug board: Canva Creators, Envato Elements, Creative Fabrica,
Adobe Stock. Upload once, earn royalties forever.**

> **Killed. Two new laws, both general, and between them they close the entire family of
> "use AI to mass-produce sellable digital assets." That is the value of this run — not the
> candidate, which was never close.**

---

## 1. Why it looked good

Of everything on the board it had the best structural fit to the four cash-flow keys:

| Key | Score |
|---|---|
| Recurring demand | Royalties recur monthly as long as anyone keeps using the asset |
| High margins | 100%. No COGS at all |
| Low reinvestment | $0 to start |
| **Operator leverage** | **Perfect.** One asset, unlimited uses, zero marginal labour forever |

And every constraint passed: no calls, faceless, solo, no credential, no customer credentials, $0,
30 h/week. On paper it was the purest form of key 4 available to one person.

## 2. THE AI SUPPLY VISE — the kill, and it is general

The whole thesis rested on her strongest skill: **AI, to produce assets fast.** So the question is
whether platforms accept AI-generated work. Both answers are fatal.

### Door 1 — the platform bans AI

**Envato** (Elements and Market) prohibits AI-generated content **as the main component of an item**,
policy in force since 2023 and still active in 2026. The stated reason is legal, not aesthetic: under
the Author Terms a seller must warrant that the buyer's use will not infringe, and Envato cannot make
that warranty for models trained on other people's work. AI may be used in **preview images only**,
never in the download.

**AudioJungle** bans AI music outright on the same reasoning.

> **If AI is banned, her AI skill is worth nothing here and she is competing on hand-craft against
> career designers. Marginal labour per asset goes from minutes to hours, and operator leverage —
> the entire reason this candidate existed — evaporates. It becomes a design job she is not trained
> for.**

### Door 2 — the platform allows AI

**Adobe Stock** accepts labelled AI work, and is the counter-example in real time. Q1 2026: the
traditional stock business declined **steeper than expected**; the platform is *"being flooded with
AI-generated submissions, further diluting the earning potential of human photographers"*; and
contributors allege Firefly was trained on their submitted images with no separate notice or
compensation.

**Creative Fabrica** is the most permissive of the majors — Gemini integrated into "Edit-to-Earn,"
generative-AI files explicitly sellable. And the contributor economics are the worst of the set:
*"most users are subscription members, meaning the price you set for each product rarely matters…
a very low commission."*

> **If AI is allowed, supply is unbounded and the per-asset price trends to zero. The platform's own
> revenue line is already bending from exactly this cause.**

### The law

> **THE AI SUPPLY VISE — on any marketplace for AI-producible assets, exactly one of two things is
> true. Either AI is banned, in which case her AI skill is worthless and she competes on hand-craft.
> Or AI is allowed, in which case supply is unbounded and the per-unit price trends to zero. There is
> no third door. Her AI capability can never be an advantage on an asset marketplace: it is either
> forbidden or universal.**

**This is the important output of the run.** It closes, in one stroke, every future variant of
*"she has AI skills, so let her mass-produce sellable digital things"* — templates, stock, fonts,
SVGs, presets, music beds, prompt packs, LUTs, mockups, icon sets. Do not re-open any of them.

## 3. THE ROYALTY POOL LAW — the second kill, and it fires even without AI

**Canva Creators** is open worldwide with no follower minimum — a portfolio application, 5–10 samples
at a public link. But three facts together make it unbuildable:

1. The flagship **Template Creator track is still in beta and invite-limited**, with approval taking
   *"up to a couple of months."*
2. The **Element/Contributor track closed to new applications in 2024.**
3. **The rate is confidential.** Canva pays *"usage-based royalties from a shared royalty pool"* and
   **does not publish a per-use rate**; the pool size is stated outright to be *"confidential business
   information."*

Point 3 is the killer, and it generalises:

> **THE ROYALTY POOL LAW — when a platform pays from a confidential pool instead of a published
> per-unit rate, it sets your revenue *after* you have done the work, and can change it without
> telling you. Unit economics cannot be computed, so GATE 0 cannot be passed. Never build on an
> unpublished rate.**

Envato is better on this axis and still bad: 50% of net subscription revenue, split across authors by
a subscriber-share model — so her share depends on **every other author's downloads**, which she
cannot see. Plus a **two-month reporting lag** and a **$50 payout floor**. Brazil is payable (only
Russia, Belarus, Afghanistan, Sudan and Libya are excluded), so payment was never the problem.

## 4. The ceiling — it is not a scale problem, it is a ceiling

| Platform | Documented earnings |
|---|---|
| Canva Creators | **$50–500+/month** |
| Creative Fabrica, success cases | **$500–600/month** |
| Creative Fabrica, other contributors | *"the platform exploits creators"* |
| Adobe Stock | Declining, by Adobe's own reporting |

Her near-term floor is **US$2.300–2.900/month.**

> **The best documented outcome anywhere in this family is 17–26% of her floor.** Not "needs scale."
> A ceiling, roughly one fifth of the way up.

### Against the benchmark

The book business is now the number to beat, and this is not close:

| | Best month documented | Orders needed for her floor |
|---|---|---|
| Royalty marketplaces | $500–600 | Not reachable at documented rates |
| Milestone-birthday book, $899 | $555 contribution **per order** | **4–5 orders** |

**One birthday-book order equals a documented-best month on Canva.** Four orders a month beat the
best month anyone publicly reports on Creative Fabrica by roughly 4×.

## 5. The 2027–28 crash filter, applied

New standing filter, at the founder's instruction: assume a global downturn in 2027–28.

### Royalty marketplaces fail it worse than almost anything

The pool is funded by **subscriptions from freelancers and small businesses** — the first line cut in
a downturn. And her income is *a share of that pool*.

> **Double compression: the pool shrinks as subscribers cancel, while the contributor count swells
> with laid-off designers looking for passive income. Numerator down, denominator up, simultaneously.**

That is the worst possible structure for a recession, and the direction is already visible — Adobe's
traditional stock line was declining faster than expected in Q1 2026, *before* any crash.

### The book business passes it

- **Deaths do not fall in a recession.** Funeral spending is famously inelastic; memorial is the most
  downturn-proof of the three books.
- **70th birthdays do not fall.** The population reaching a milestone year is fixed a
  lifetime in advance.
- **Family-history research historically rises** when people have more time and less money for travel.
- **Print-on-demand means zero inventory to be caught holding** when demand drops. For a solo
  operator with no capital that is the single most valuable recession property there is.
- Real exposure: it is discretionary gifting at $299–899, and that compresses. The defence is
  multi-copy — at **$12,84 a head** the birthday book reframes from *an expensive book* to *cheaper
  than the party favours*, which is a recession-resistant frame rather than a luxury one.

## 6. Verdict

**Dead. Not "needs work" — dead on a ceiling, a hidden rate, and a vise that has no third door.**

What the run bought:

1. **THE AI SUPPLY VISE** — closes the entire "AI mass-produces sellable assets" family permanently.
   Probably 10–20 future candidates' worth of hunting saved.
2. **THE ROYALTY POOL LAW** — never build on an unpublished rate. Applies to every platform-mediated
   revenue share from here on.
3. **The crash filter now has teeth**, and its first use showed that pooled-royalty income is
   double-compressed in a downturn while print-on-demand is structurally protected.

**Next on the board:** #5, the $19/month tool for a sole-trader trade — because the B2B buyer law's
30+ corpses were all $500+ services sold to firms, and a sole trader paying $19 on a card has no
procurement, no diligence and no committee. If that distinction holds, a whole continent reopens.
