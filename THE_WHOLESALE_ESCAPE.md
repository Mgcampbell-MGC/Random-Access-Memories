## 3. THE STRONGEST DEADLINE MECHANIC FOUND ANYWHERE — and it is Brazilian

**Verified verbatim from Planalto, Lei 9.279/96 (LPI):**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Art. 226** | *"Os atos do INPI nos processos administrativos referentes à propriedade industrial só produzem efeitos a partir da sua **publicação no respectivo órgão oficial**."* |
| **Art. 221** | *"Os prazos estabelecidos nesta Lei são **contínuos, extinguindo-se automaticamente o direito de praticar o ato**, após seu decurso."* |
| **Art. 158** | Oposição within **60 days** of publication |
| **Art. 159 §1º** | *"**Não respondida a exigência, o pedido será definitivamente arquivado**."* |
| **Art. 212** | Recurso within **60 days** |

> **Read art. 226 with art. 221: INPI NEVER NOTIFIES ANYONE. Publication in the weekly RPI *is* the notice, and
> missing it extinguishes the right automatically.** That is the cleanest possible answer to *"why pay when
> alerts are free"* — **there is no alert. There is only a gazette, and not reading it loses the right.**

**Brazilian procurement is the same shape:** Lei 14.133/2021 **art. 164** (impugnação, 3 dias úteis before
opening) · **art. 165** (recurso, 3 dias úteis) · **art. 54** and **art. 174** making PNCP publication mandatory
and central.

**CORRECTION TO MY OWN BRIEF.** I told two agents the RPI was "a large weekly PDF, and the difficulty IS the
moat." **False.** INPI publishes the RPI in **XML** with a documented layout, and mirrors it on dados.gov.br.
**The moat I assumed does not exist**, and the domestic price proves it: **Velador sells the watch at R$19,90.**

**And a licence trap worth carrying forward:** **PNCP's content is CC BY-ND 3.0 — *SemDerivações*.** A derived
data product built on Brazilian procurement data has a real licensing problem. Nobody had checked this.

## 6. The free test, and it needs no customer

**Take four weeks of INPI RPI XML and USPTO daily bulk files. For twenty randomly chosen small IP boutiques
with international dockets, count how many published events touched a client mark, and how many started a
clock shorter than 60 days.**

> **If the median firm has fewer than two clock-starting events a month, it cannot justify $650/month and the
> candidate dies before anything is built. Free data, no customer, no call, no capital.**

**Second test:** email thirty boutiques one written question — *"what do you pay per month for trademark watch
outside the US, and to whom?"* — and **count the replies containing a number.** That measures the price anchor
and whether written-only outreach reaches this buyer at all, simultaneously.
