## #1 — A CLÍNICA · the scan keepsake machine, wholesaled to Brazilian imaging clinics

**What it is.** A machine turns a mother's own 2D/3D ultrasound export into a small personalised printed object
— enhanced portrait, heartbeat-waveform print, birth star chart — with **zero generated prose**. The clinic buys
it wholesale and sells it at the counter under its own name. She never sees an identified record: the
sonographer captures one overlay-off frame (already normal practice at the one incumbent in this channel) and
the pipeline composes it.

**Why it works — five for.**
1. **It is the founder's own pick.** Shown ten candidates on 4 Sep she chose three; this is one of them, with a
   Brazilian channel welded on instead of UK studios.
2. **A machine makes the unit.** The pipeline already exists and runs at 264 ms/unit; her hours are QA (~3
   min/unit), not production. Passes all three limbs of `THE MACHINE TEST`.
3. **The willingness-to-pay anchor is real and expensive.** Alta Diagnósticos (DASA) sells its 3D face print at
   **"Valor promocional: R$ 350,00"** — verified by me today at `altadiagnosticos.com.br/impressao-3d-ultrassom`.
   It requires the baby's face visible and a 3D segmentation by Biodesign Lab, i.e. it is the expensive tier;
   the cheap 2D/typeset tier underneath it is unoccupied.
4. **The buyer list is free and 76% emailable.** CNES open-data API, no key: `codigo_uf=35&codigo_tipo_unidade=39`
   — **re-measured by me today: 455/600 = 75,8% email, 85,3% phone.** One chain signature (DASA, Fleury, Sabin)
   covers many sites, so the 15–20 conversations are 3 chains + ~12 independents.
5. **Nothing has to be believed.** The mother verifies the object by looking at it; the clinic verifies the
   COGS by ordering one.

**What is honestly against it — five against.**
1. The pipeline has processed **one image**, a synthetic render. Never a real GE/Samsung/Philips/Mindray export
   with burned-in overlay text. *"Built and runs"* is a claim about code run once.
2. CNES tipo 39 (*SADT isolado*) is wider than imaging — the first row of my own sample was an acupuncture clinic
   whose email is its accountant's. The list needs a service-code join (obstetric ultrasound) before it is a
   prospect list. **A count of structures is not a check of content.**
3. Chains are enterprise procurement (`THE MONITORING PINCER`): DASA already has Biodesign Lab in-house and may
   simply extend it downward. Independents are the realistic first signatures.
4. Print fulfilment in Brazil is unpriced — Lulu's US$21,32 landed is a US/UK figure; a Brazilian lab (Printi
   fotolivro / Digipix R$64,90–139,90 retail) must be quoted for the actual object.
5. Arithmetic is wholesale-price sensitive: at US$65 wholesale, ~70 units/month clears the target; **at US$40 it
   needs ~130**, because tax runs on gross and COGS is not deductible under Lucro Presumido.

**Arithmetic.** 70 units × US$65 = US$4.550 gross → contribution ≈ US$3.060 → after 8,1% on gross ≈ US$2.690 net.
Hours ≈ 14–16/month. Capital ≤ US$1.000.

**First thing this week.** The test already written and never run: **20 real de-identified exports from ≥2
vendors → run `pipeline/scan_pipeline.py` → stopwatch.** PASS: median ≤3 min/unit AND ≥18/20 ship clean. KILL:
>5 min OR <14/20. Cost ≈ US$0–100, no phone, one sitting.

## #2 — MOLDE PÚBLICO · made-to-order teaching models, sold through *dispensa de licitação*

**What it is.** Small anatomical and simulation models (muscle groups, organ sections, silicone skins, suture
pads) cast in silicone/resin from a home workshop and sold to federal institutes, universities and teaching
hospitals through the non-competitive small-purchase channel the law forces the buyer to advertise **and to push
automatically to every registered supplier.**

**Why it works — five for.**
1. **The money is measured, not estimated.** `dadosabertos.compras.gov.br`, PDM 9837, 12 months to 31 Aug 2026,
   re-fetched by me today: **1.348 homologated line items, R$30,7 M/year in total; the *dispensa* segment
   (modalidade 6) is 197 items, median R$958, R$613.916/year** — exactly the judge's figures.
2. **Suppliers her size are already winning it.** Verified in the raw rows: **RUBENS GONCALVES** sold IF Sergipe
   five models on 20 Aug 2026 (arm R$1.040 · leg R$1.440 · digestive R$770 · liver R$510 · 2× R$1.490);
   **MIRELLY HENRIQUE LEAL PESSOA** has 8 line items across UNESP, UFRB, UF Catalão, IF São Paulo, an army
   battalion and the SP civil police, R$137–1.281 each. Both are single-person CNPJs.
3. **Distribution is a statute.** IN SEGES/ME 67/2021 art. 7, read by me at gov.br today: *"encaminhado
   automaticamente aos fornecedores registrados no Sicaf, por mensagem eletrônica, na correspondente linha de
   fornecimento."* The state emails her the order. Zero sales conversations.
4. **Nothing has to be believed.** The buyer inspects the object against the CATMAT spec it wrote itself.
5. **The moulds accumulate.** Each mould is a sunk cost that pays out on every reorder; single-unit orders
   (qty ≤2, n=663) have a median of **R$1.205**.

**What is honestly against it — five against.**
1. **It fails limb 3 of `THE MACHINE TEST`** — her hands on every unit, 8–12 min each. The founder rejected six of
   ten candidates on exactly this shape. *"The mould is the machine"* is rhetoric. **You gave free rein to loosen
   parameters; this is the parameter it loosens. It is her call, put as that question.**
2. The two named winners look like **resellers of catalogue PVC models**, not casters: 706 of 1.348 line items
   specify PVC, 154 silicone. Her made-to-order silicone unit competes with an imported PVC catalogue item at
   the same spec line — `THE CATALOGUE SCISSORS` in a government costume, unless she targets the silicone lines
   (skins, pads, soft organs) where catalogue supply is thin.
3. Nobody has cast a unit or priced silicone — margin is unmeasured.
4. A missed delivery risks CEIS/impedidos listing, which closes the whole channel at once.
5. Capital: one mould is under US$2.000; a 4–6-mould portfolio presses on the whole US$3.000 cap.

**Arithmetic.** 9–16 units/month × R$958–1.450 = R$12.300–15.400 (the domestic bar, zero-COGS version; real
COGS unknown). Hours 24–30/month all-in.

**First thing this week.** SICAF registration (free, written) so the art. 7 emails start arriving; pull the 154
silicone line items and price two of them from a silicone supplier's quote.

## #3 — O MARTELO · the clearing-price archive for used Brazilian farm machinery

**What it is.** Brazil publishes what people *ask* for a used tractor (FIPE, dealer listings) and nobody
publishes what one *sold* for. A cron captures auction results before they are masked or erased, seals each one
with a free RFC 3161 timestamp under a Merkle root cut at the unit of sale, and sells dated comparables to the
people who must defend a number in writing: NBR 14653-5 appraisal engineers and bank credit-recovery desks.

**Why it works — five for.**
1. **The erasure is real, and I measured it two ways today.** The closed competitive event the hunt cited
   (`superbid.net/evento/234911`, *Encerrado*) shows **60 lots, every one "Lance atual: R$ \*\*\*\*\*\*\*"** to an
   anonymous visitor. And on the *unmasked* auctioneer's lots, the winning bid **ages out**: lot 4848376 (sold
   5 Aug) still shows *"Lance atual: R$ 265.000,00"*, but lots sold 8 Jul, 29 May and 23 Apr now show only
   *"Lance inicial"*. **The page persists; the price does not.** That is the `STATE KEEPS STATE, NOT HISTORY`
   shape on a private record-holder — the one place `THE PDA LAW` says a Brazilian erasure moat can exist.
2. **A buyer with a legal duty exists.** NBR 14653-5 laudos need market comparables; engineers earn
   R$5–12.000 per laudo and cannot cite a masked number.
3. **The incumbent sells a model, not a record.** Via Consulti's plans page, re-fetched by me: it sells
   *"laudos de precificação por estimativa"* and CONAB depreciation matrices — an estimate. A sourced, sealed
   transaction is a different product.
4. **The seal replaces recognition with arithmetic** — the GateLedger mechanism, intact, in a domain that is
   coffee-farm vocabulary (*tratores, colheitadeiras, leilão, arremate*) rather than a Los Angeles container yard.
5. **Zero marginal minutes per subscriber.** One cron, one archive.

**What is honestly against it — five against.**
1. **⚠ CORRECTION — the price anchor was misread.** Via Consulti's **R$1.500,00 is the TRIMESTRAL plan** (3
   months); R$4.000 semestral; **R$5.000 ANUAL = R$417/month**; R$8.000/yr for up to 50 users. The observed
   ceiling for a subscription in this market is **~R$420–500/month/user, not R$1.500.** At that price 5
   subscribers + 20 extracts × R$390 ≈ **R$9.900–10.300/month — below the R$12.300 domestic bar.** It needs
   ~30 extracts/month or ~10 subscribers. **MARGINAL on arithmetic after correction.**
2. **Masking is per-auctioneer, not universal.** SOLD/MM Leilões lots show the hammer for weeks; the 234911
   auctioneer masks instantly. The archive's value is the union across auctioneers, and the window differs.
3. Superbid's Terms §9.2 (fetched, PDF): *"exploração comercial… reprodução, cópia… rigorosamente proibidas."*
   A sourced comparable is arguably not a reproduction of the listing — but counsel before the cron.
4. Whether a *registered non-bidding* account unmasks closed lots has never been tested. The cheapest test is
   a free bidder account (ID upload + selfie) and five closed lots.
5. Whether an NBR 14653 laudo can cite a third party's sealed capture as market data is unresolved.

**Arithmetic (corrected).** 20 extracts × R$390 + 5 subs × R$450 ≈ R$10.050/month. Hours ~50/month claimed,
likely understated. Capital modest (VPS + tooling).

**First thing this week.** Free Superbid account; open 5 closed competitive agro lots logged in; and bank
`evento/234911`-class events daily for two weeks to measure how many hammer prices survive 30 days. Then weld on
`THE SEEDED-DEFECT BENCHMARK` — 500 known price/lot pairs through the parser, publish the detection rate — so an
engineer has a number to sign against instead of her word.

## #4 — A TERRA VISTA DE CIMA v2 · the cooperative's tenure insert

**What it is.** A machine composites a member's own registered property (SICAR polygon → Sentinel-2 imagery,
both free) into a small personalised printed object — *"[Producer], N safras com a Cooperativa X"* — and rides
**inside a print-and-distribution run the cooperative already pays for**, as an annual institutional contract.
Two tiers: a cheap insert for every member and a framed print as an opt-in upsell.

**Why it works — five for.**
1. **The occasion already exists and is budgeted.** Cooperatives already run tenure-recognition rituals
   (Copasul's *"Plante Sua História"* employee plaque programme, per the hunt — **reported by an agent, not
   re-verified by me; Copasul's site search returned nothing today**). The product extends a ritual, not a
   new gift category pitched cold.
2. **COGS is measured, at both tiers, on Printi's own configurator — re-fetched by me:** the insert (poster
   ladder) runs **R$4,73/unit at 10 → R$0,75 at 1.000 → R$0,53 at 3.000**; the framed *quadro decorativo* runs
   **R$93,49 single → R$58,62 at 20.** ⚠ *The judge's table attached the poster COGS to the framed price; they
   are two tiers. Recorded correctly here.*
3. **The consumer comparable is real:** MyTopo custom aerial photo map, **US$49,95**, re-fetched at
   `mapstore.mytopo.com`.
4. **Nothing has to be believed** — the recipient recognises their own land.
5. **The only candidate where the coffee-farm background is an asset, not a tie-breaker.** *Safra, cooperado,
   talhão* — her vocabulary, no bluffing, no camera, no opinion.

**What is honestly against it — five against.**
1. **The pilot does not clear the bar.** At R$8–15/member, a 3.000–5.000-member coop is R$30–75.000/YEAR =
   R$2.500–6.250/month. It needs 3–4 coops or one very large one.
2. No cooperative has been asked. The 12–16 conversations are all still to have.
3. SICAR polygons and Sentinel tiles are free to the coop too — an agronomy SaaS vendor could add the insert as
   a feature. The defence is the object plus the occasion, not the data.
4. Sentinel-2 history starts June 2015; a *"30 safras"* image cannot show 30 years — the text carries tenure,
   the image cannot.
5. Annual contracts = one renewal decision a year per coop; churn of one coop is 25–33% of revenue.

**Arithmetic.** Insert tier: 3 coops × 4.000 members × R$10 = R$120.000/yr = R$10.000/month, plus framed
upsells at R$220–260 on R$59–93 COGS. Hours ~50/month at scale.

**First thing this week.** One written question to 6 cooperative marketing/relationship directors: *"what did
you print and distribute to members last safra, and what did it cost per member?"* Count the replies containing
a number.

## #5 — A COTAÇÃO · the quote-and-sample desk for foreign brands sourcing in Brazil

**What it is.** ApexBrasil gives a foreign brand a free pre-screened supplier list in 10 business days and
stops there — no prices, no samples, no way to compare. She RFQs 20 named Brazilian makers on one written spec
in Portuguese (Quote Pack, US$950) and places the domestic sample order the foreign buyer is structurally
barred from placing — CPF, local address, local payment (Sample Run, US$360).

**Why it works — five for.**
1. **The free front door is the complement, not the killer.** ApexBrasil's list is *where the buyer stops*;
   the quote and the sample are what the list cannot do.
2. **The exclusion is real:** a Brazilian marketplace checkout needs a CPF — `THE OPEN-STATE INVERSION`'s
   corollary that *exclusion from a transaction* is worth more than difficulty reading a file.
3. **The price sits inside an observed band.** EpicSourcing's pricing page, re-fetched by me today: entry tier
   **US$499 + 15% of order value**, white-label US$1.799 + 12%, private-label US$2.999 + 10%. ⚠ *The judge's
   "$500–1.500" is not on the pricing page; the observed entry is $499 plus a management percentage.* A
   US$950 flat Quote Pack undercuts the entry tier on a US$5.000 order.
4. **The channel is a referral, not cold outreach:** bilateral chambers (Britcham SP, BCC-GB) already field
   *"find me a supplier"* from members with no priced product behind it.
5. **Export, USD, invoiced, 8,1%.** Three Quote Packs a month clear the bar.

**What is honestly against it — five against.**
1. No chamber has been asked to refer a paying member to an outside desk.
2. Whether Brazilian factories answer a cold Portuguese RFQ from an unknown intermediary has never been tested
   — the single cheapest test in the file, still unrun.
3. It is a per-engagement service: `THE MACHINE TEST` limb 1 (a relationship to hold) is only half-passed —
   each engagement ends, but each involves correspondence with 20 factories.
4. The Sample Run puts her in the goods chain on her own account — check `THE AGENT EXEMPTION` (RIR/2018 art.
   162 §2 III): she must buy as agent, never resell.
5. US$950 needs a written spec from the buyer; a vague brief becomes unpaid consulting.

**Arithmetic.** 3 Quote Packs = US$2.850 gross → ≈US$2.620 net. 8 Packs + 4 Runs = US$9.040. Hours 32–40/month.

**First thing this week.** Send one real spec to 20 Brazilian makers of one product category in Portuguese and
count replies with a number inside 10 days. PASS ≥8/20.

## Did the domain matter?

Not as a hunting ground. A CLÍNICA and MOLDE PÚBLICO are domain-agnostic machines found by crossing a built
pipeline or a production cell against a distribution law. O MARTELO and A TERRA VISTA use the farm background
as **vocabulary and comfort** — she will not be bluffing — but the structural insight in each (a masked price, a
coop's print run) came from testing a mechanism. **Fashion and modelling produced zero of the five, for the
third time in this file.** Domain is a tie-breaker between machine-shaped businesses, never their source.
