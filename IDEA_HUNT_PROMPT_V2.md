# Brief v2: find a digital product for a specific founder

You are being asked to find a **business idea**, not to evaluate one. Twelve candidates have already
been killed, and the reasons are documented below. They are given to you as **mechanisms**, so you can
reason past them — not as a map of where to search.

Work from evidence. Every number, price, market size, legal rule or competitor claim must either cite
a source you actually consulted or be marked **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent statistics. An honest
"I could not confirm this" beats a confident figure that dissolves on contact.

---

## 1. The founder — fixed facts, not preferences

- Woman, ~26, São Paulo, Brazil. Native Portuguese. Functional written English.
- **She will not do unscripted live calls in English.** She **will** sell by telephone in Portuguese.
  This asymmetry matters: Portuguese-language markets can be sold by direct human contact; English
  ones must be entirely self-serve.
- **She has no domain expertise in anything.** Do not design a product that requires her to be an
  expert, hold a proprietary dataset, or make professional judgements. **Her capability is directing
  an AI coding agent to research and build.** Treat "can produce working software and research
  quickly" as TRUE; treat "knows an industry" as FALSE.
- **Faceless is permanent.** No camera, no personal brand, no influencer or creator work, no webinars,
  no audience-building as the route to market.
- **Her personal network is excluded as a channel.** Cold outreach to strangers is fine; leaning on
  who she knows is not.
- New Brazilian company (CNPJ) plus access to a **US company**, so USD/GBP/EUR card payment, Stripe,
  Paddle and Lemon Squeezy are all available. Payment is solved.
- A few hundred US dollars of budget. **No employees, no contractors, no inventory, no audience, no
  email list, no customers.**

## 2. The target

**R$30.000/month net to the founder**, roughly **US$7.400–8.300/month revenue**, reachable within a
year and sustainable. Note the base rate: across two independent datasets (Indie Hackers Stripe-
verified, n=937; Gumroad, n=146.271), **44–54% of solo digital products earn exactly zero, and only
about 5% exceed US$8.300/month.** The target is roughly the 95th percentile. Plan accordingly.

## 3. The requirement set

> **An enumerable, contactable, solvent population that repeats a task — plus something to sell that
> a funded incumbent is not already giving away.**

The second clause is where twelve candidates died. Treat it as the primary filter, not a footnote.

## 4. THE DOMINANT KILLER — read this before generating anything

Content and templates now cost almost nothing to produce, so **the free tier has become the marketing
budget of every funded incumbent.** A solo operator selling an informational artefact is not competing
with a product; they are competing with someone's *customer acquisition strategy*, which is designed
to give that artefact away.

Six verified instances, all from real audits:

| Incumbent | Gave away free | Killed |
|---|---|---|
| **Levelset** (Procore, ~$500M) | lien-deadline calculator, state notice forms, waiver creation — as SEO lead-gen on the exact target keywords | a US construction paperwork kit |
| **GleanMark** | free tier: monitoring + AI-drafted responses, marketed at solo practitioners | a US trademark response tool |
| **Sympla** (Brazil's largest ticketing platform) | a certificated course covering proposal structure, tier pricing, contract clauses | a Brazilian sponsorship kit |
| **ConLicitação** | free recurso templates *and* an AI that drafts petitions in under a minute, unlimited, inside the subscription bidders already hold | a Brazilian procurement tool |
| **iNymbus / SPS Commerce** | free deduction audits | a US retail-deduction worksheet |
| **Insparisk** | **free forever**, unlimited buildings, deadline and violation alerts | a US property-compliance monitor |

In a compliance or reference domain, **a free template is genuinely as good as a paid one.** That is
why price does not rescue these ideas.

## 5. THE ALERT FINDING — the most useful rule discovered

The evidence splits on exactly one variable: **who benefits from knowing.**

**When the recipient is an OPPORTUNIST who profits by acting on the information, the alert sells by
itself, at real sustained prices:** PropertyRadar **US$119–599/month** (foreclosures → investors),
RealtyTrac from **US$49,95/month**, Owler **US$39–350/month** (funding and hiring events → sales
teams).

**When the recipient is the SUBJECT of the problem — their own violation, liability or cost — the
alert is free or near-free and every dollar is in the remedy:** ViolationWatch charges **$9/building/
month** for alerts against **$350–$1.800 per cure**; Insparisk gives monitoring away **free** and bills
thousands for inspections; Alt Legal's own marketing tells law firms the watch's value is **the
enforcement billables it generates**, not the subscription.

> **No example was found of anyone selling a pure alert, at a meaningful price, to the party the alert
> is about.**

**The rule:** if you build anything around detecting an event, identify who makes money *by knowing
first*, and sell to them. If the only interested party is the one it happened to, you are building a
lead magnet, and the business is the remedy — which usually needs credentials or per-customer work
she cannot supply.

**This is a constraint, not a destination.** Do not simply propose lead-list businesses because of it.

Two related facts. The widely-cited success stories for trigger-based cold outreach (Baird Hall's
iTunes-API story, Jake Atwood's $1,2M ARR) were checked against primary sources and **neither
survives** — both trace to cold-email tool vendors' content marketing. And outreach framed as *"we
noticed you have a public compliance problem"* is a **known fraud vector**: USPTO and the FTC publish
active warnings about solicitors who mine a business's own filing data, so legitimate outreach gets
pattern-matched to scams.

## 6. THE VISE — it has appeared three times

In regulated domains, **the reserved perimeter and the pricing premium are the same feature.**

- The version that is legally safe has no differentiation.
- The version that differentiates is the reserved activity.

Verified instances: **CFC Resolução 1.640/2021 Art. 3º** (Brazilian accounting — and Art. 3º XXX
reaches *the parameterisation of fiscal rules inside management software*, i.e. product design, while
**DL 9.295 Art. 20** makes *advertising* the service the infraction); **Lei 8.906/94 art. 1º, II**
(Brazilian legal consultancy — OAB-SP has filed 34 civil actions with 23 injunctions since 2022); and
UK employment tribunals, where the corpus was legal to publish but **first-instance decisions bind
nobody**, so the lawful product had no authority to sell.

Check the reserved perimeter *before* designing, not after.

## 7. Channel reality

- A click costs the same whatever the product costs, so acquisition cost does not fall with price.
  At ~US$1,50/click with acquisition capped at 30% of price: $29 needs ~17% conversion (impossible),
  $97 needs ~5,2%, $197 ~2,5%, $297 ~1,7%. Cold high-intent pages convert 1–5%. **Paid traffic is
  generally only viable from about US$97 up.** Some verticals price clicks at US$10–30, closing paid
  acquisition entirely.
- **Brazilian click prices are roughly a quarter of US ones** (~US$0,35 median), which genuinely
  changes the arithmetic and can justify sub-$97 pricing there. This is a legitimate reason to break
  the price floor in Brazil.
- With no audience, discovery comes from **search or marketplaces** — which is precisely where funded
  incumbents place their free content. This is the structural trap. Any proposal that quietly assumes
  "we'll rank for the obvious keyword" is competing with an incumbent's marketing department.
- **Hotmart's affiliate marketplace is not a free salesforce.** New products enter at a default
  temperature of 21°, affiliates filter by temperature, and Hotmart's own prescribed fix is personally
  recruiting promoters — which requires the network she does not have.
- Cold email: US CAN-SPAM permits opt-out B2B email but carries **US$53.088 per violating email,
  uncapped** — an ordinary security-camera company paid **US$2,95M** in 2024. UK PECR workable for
  corporate subscribers; Canada CASL consent-based; **Germany effectively closed** (UWG §7(2),
  competitor-enforced).

## 8. Twelve post-mortems, as mechanisms

Read for the mechanism. **Do not propose variants, and do not stay in these industries.**

1. **US charity compliance monitor** — a sample of 150 found ~70% under US$50k revenue. *The visible
   defect selected for buyers too poor to pay.*
2. **US tariff-ruling currency index** — the government's own guidance already prescribed the check,
   and the filing system auto-rejected the bad value. *The defect had no unpriced consequence; an
   unserved problem is often unserved because it costs nobody anything.*
3. **UK employment tribunal corpus** — the buyer was invisible during the 28-day response window, buys
   once a decade, and first-instance decisions are not precedent. *Unreachable buyer, and the asset
   was not what it appeared to be.*
4. **US retail deduction worksheet** — two vendors offer free audits. *Free guard already in place.*
5. **US construction lien kit** — the dominant incumbent gives the templates away on the exact
   keywords. *The incumbent owned both the product and the channel.*
6. **Amazon reinstatement tool** — $30–100 freelancers and free custom GPTs produce the same
   deliverable. *Priced above the human and infinitely above free.*
7. **US trademark office-action tool** — an incumbent free tier already ships both halves, and USPTO
   emails the attorney automatically. *Feature, not product.*
8. **NYC building-compliance radar** — a competitor gives unlimited alerts away free forever, and the
   city prints the obligation on the November tax bill. *The claimed gap did not exist.*
9. **Brazilian exam-study planner** — the incumbents publish free planners at the exact trigger moment
   and bundle one inside the subscription buyers already hold. *Occupied at all three layers: free,
   bundled, and cheaper-paid.*
10. **ISO 9001 audit pack** — its contents are mandatory retained records under clauses 9.2.2/9.3.3/
    10.2.2, so possession is a precondition of being the buyer. *It sold a copy of the thing that
    defines the customer.*
11. **Brazilian procurement appeal tool** — the incumbent ships AI drafting inside its subscription, a
    human sells the finished document for R$190, and the statute requires intent to be declared
    in-session, so the buyer is committed before any outreach can land. *Statutory clock closed the
    sales window.*
12. **Brazilian sponsorship kit** — cultural-sector income averages R$2.815/month, and the law lets a
    commission fundraiser's 10% fee sit inside the approved project budget, payable only from money
    raised. *Adverse selection plus a zero-upfront substitute written into the statute.*

## 9. The questions that have actually killed things

Answer all of these **for your own idea**, with evidence.

1. **Who loses a specific amount of money when this goes unsolved, and by what mechanism?** Name the
   person and the number. "Wasted time" means stop.
2. **Is something already catching this free — automatically?** A government system, a platform
   validation, an included service, a professional they already pay. **Search for the free tier
   explicitly. This is the question that killed six candidates.**
3. **Who profits by knowing first?** If the answer is only "the person it happened to", see §5.
4. **Can the buyer be reached?** Is there a public, timely, lawful signal, or an enumerable list with
   contacts? Or must you win contested search traffic?
5. **Does the same buyer need it again?**
6. **What does the human substitute charge?** Find a published price. If a regulated, insured human
   charges the same or less, stop.
7. **Can the buyer verify the output is right after opening it?** Two values that differ is
   information; "you are non-compliant" is borrowed authority.
8. **Does the arithmetic reach the target?** Show price × units against a defensible population and a
   reachable-per-year figure.
9. **What is the shelf life?** A published schedule of rule changes destroys a static file — but
   *funds* a maintained subscription. Say which you are building.
10. **Is everything you would bundle lawfully redistributable, and does the valuable part sit inside a
    reserved profession?** See §6.

## 10. The commodity-checker test — run this before any build

Adopted from an external review; it is the cheapest way to answer question 2 for a *tool*:

> Build one synthetic fixture with seeded defects — a missing record, a duplicate, a formatting edge
> case, a renamed column, a total mismatch. Send it to **three freelance specialists** with a written
> spec. **If two can build the matched-scope checker for ≤US$100 in ≤2 hours with no continuing work,
> kill the idea unconditionally.**

Roughly US$300 and a few days, and it settles whether a tool is genuinely hard to reproduce.

## 11. Rules

**Hard — do not break.**
- No live English calls anywhere in sale, delivery or support. Portuguese calls are permitted.
- No camera, no personal brand, no audience-building as the channel.
- No selling through her personal network.
- Nothing requiring a professional licence or credential she lacks, in the target market.
- Nothing requiring her to hold proprietary expertise or a private dataset.
- No third-party content that is not lawfully redistributable commercially.
- No claim that a buyer is legally obliged to pay or act, without real authority.
- No outbound channel unlawful in its market.
- No employees, no inventory, no capital beyond a few hundred dollars.

**Soft — break with justification.** Name the rule, explain what makes this case different, and state
what evidence would prove you wrong.
- Sell to a professional who repeats the task, not an individual who meets it once.
- Price at US$97+ *(in Brazil, cheaper clicks legitimately relax this)*.
- Prefer businesses to consumers.
- Prefer buyers who compare your price to an hour of professional time rather than to a book.

## 12. How to think

The founder's own critique, which you should take seriously:

> "AI often kills business ideas because it is much better at listing risks than discovering hidden
> opportunities. It defaults to consultant mode. **It confuses 'not proven' with 'bad.'** Almost every
> new business looks uncertain before testing."

So sort every objection:

- **Structural — these kill.** An identical product already shipping cheaper or free; a named law
  reserving it; a buyer list that cannot exist; a free automatic system that already solves it;
  arithmetic that cannot reach the target.
- **Adoption — these may not kill.** Price acceptance, conversion rate, problem awareness, trust in an
  unknown vendor. If you raise one, attach a test costing under US$200, taking under two weeks, with a
  pass threshold.

Do not produce a risk list. Commit to an answer.

**Do not anchor.** Twelve candidates were all the same shape — *a tool, template or monitor helping a
professional complete a recurring task* — across nine industries, three countries and two languages.
The industries varied; the shape never did. That shape is the most commoditised category that exists.
Generate from **structurally different shapes** and say what makes each one different. If your final
three resemble each other, you have elaborated rather than searched.

## 13. Do not propose

Variants of the twelve above: US charity registration; customs or tariff classification; UK employment
tribunal defence; retail chargeback or deduction recovery; construction lien paperwork; Amazon seller
reinstatement; trademark office-action responses; municipal building-compliance monitoring; exam-study
planners; ISO 9001 or management-system audit packs; public-procurement appeal tools; event
sponsorship kits.

## 14. Output

**Part 1 — the field.** Six to ten candidates, a paragraph each. Include at least two structurally
unlike the rest, and say what makes them structurally different.

**Part 2 — the shortlist.** The three strongest, each answered against all ten questions in §9, with
sources and explicit UNVERIFIED tags, and the arithmetic shown as a visible calculation.

**Part 3 — the recommendation.** One idea. Why it beats the other two. The single assumption most
likely to be wrong, and the cheapest check that settles it — cost, duration, and the result that would
make you abandon it.

**Part 4 — what you could not verify.** Everything asserted without a source, listed honestly.
