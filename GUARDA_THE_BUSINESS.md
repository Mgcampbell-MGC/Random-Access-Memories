## 1. The business, in one paragraph

**A foreign brand sells online. Brazilians copy its products, steal its campaign photographs, and sell the
fakes on Mercado Livre and Shopee — in a language the brand cannot read, on platforms it does not monitor, in
a country where it has no one.** It already owns the Brazilian trademark rights that would stop this; it has
simply never used them. **Sol runs the perimeter: every week a machine sweeps both marketplaces for the
brand's name and its own photographs, sorts the real infringements from the lawful resellers, puts the batch
in front of the brand for one-click approval, and files the removals through the platforms' own free portals.
The brand gets a monthly report of what came down and what came back.**

> ### She is not an investigator and not a lawyer. **She runs a perimeter.** The brand owns the right, the platform does the removing, and she is the operation in between that neither of them will do.

## 2. Why this exists at all — three facts that have to be true together, and are

1. **The brand already holds the weapon and never fires it.** Measured in four weeks of INPI's gazette:
   **1.007 foreign companies hold a *granted* Brazilian trademark and have nobody appointed here at all** —
   469 of them in consumer classes. They are legally armed and operationally absent.
2. **The platforms hand the trigger to anyone properly authorised, free.** Mercado Livre's Brand Protection
   Program: *"Ser membro do programa é totalmente grátis"*, open to *"titulares de direitos de propriedade
   intelectual **e seus procuradores**"* on documented authority, provided the mark is *"deferido ou
   concedido"*. **The enforcement infrastructure is free, and it admits an agent.**
3. **Nobody serves this size of brand.** MarqVision, Red Points and Corsearch sell to enterprises. Brazilian
   IP law firms want matters, not a US$400 monthly perimeter. **The independent US$5–50M brand is above
   "ignore it" and below "hire a firm," and that gap is the customer.**

## 3. Who buys it, precisely

**Not "fashion."** The measured class mix of foreign holders with granted Brazilian marks is 65% non-fashion:
electronics (09), software (42), services (35), pharma (05), cosmetics (03), tools (07), medical devices (10),
clothing (25). **The buyer is defined by economics, not by category:**

- an independent brand, roughly **US$5–50M revenue**, selling direct to consumers
- **holds a granted Brazilian trademark** — this is a hard filter and it is machine-checkable
- **has no Brazilian entity, no Brazilian staff and no enforcement vendor**
- **has counterfeit listings live right now**, which is provable before the first contact
- the person who signs is a founder, head of e-commerce or general counsel

**Every one of those four conditions can be verified from public data before a single email is sent.** That is
unusual and it is the reason this business can be sold in writing.

## 4. What actually happens, weekly

```
MONDAY   ── one script, all clients ──────────────────────────────────
   sweep      Mercado Livre + Shopee: brand name, product lines, common misspellings
   compare    vision model puts each listing image beside the brand's own catalogue
   classify   A: uses the brand's OWN photograph        → copyright, objective
              B: counterfeit product carrying the mark  → trademark, objective
              C: genuine stock, resale, second-hand     → EXCLUDED, lawful (LPI art. 132 III)
              D: unrelated word match                   → EXCLUDED
   ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SOL      review the A/B batch, drop anything doubtful                    ~20 min/client
CLIENT   one email, one approval, in writing                             their decision
SOL      bulk CSV into the free portals                                  ~10 min/client
MONTH    automated report: submitted · removed · reappeared              ~5 min/client
```

**Steady state: about one hour per client per month.** Month one is heavier — clearing the standing backlog is
the setup fee's job.

> **The automation is not a nice-to-have; it is what makes the business exist.** Hand-run, this is 6–8 hours
> per client per month and she caps at four clients. Automated, it is ~1 hour and she caps at fifteen to
> twenty. **The build is the business.**

## 5. ★★ THE LIABILITY ARCHITECTURE — the part that could actually hurt her, designed rather than hoped

Brazil's public complaint record on the Mercado Livre programme is **not** about counterfeiters escaping. It is
about **wrongful removals**: *"denúncias aceitas de forma automática, sem análise técnica mínima quanto à
titularidade real"*, honest sellers with purchase invoices losing listings, accounts suspended, and Brazilian
lawyers openly advertising *Ação de Obrigação de Fazer* to reverse them. **A seller who is taken down wrongly
has a real claim, and the operator who submitted is named on it.**

**Six rules, all structural, none of them a promise to be careful:**

| | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Only two evidence classes are ever submitted:** the listing uses the brand's **own photograph**, or it sells a **product bearing the mark that the brand did not make**. | Both are objective and provable from the listing itself. Neither requires an opinion |
| **2** | **Grey market is never touched.** A seller with genuine stock and invoices is generally lawful. | It is the lawful case, and it is the one that generates the lawsuits |
| **3** | **★ The client approves every batch in writing before anything is filed.** She prepares and recommends; **they decide.** | This is the whole defence. She is a preparer, not the party asserting a right. *The buyer supplies the domain; she supplies the throughput* |
| **4** | **Every submission keeps its evidence pack** — screenshots, URLs, the comparison, the timestamp, the approval. | If a removal is ever challenged, the file answers it |
| **5** | **Counter-notice = stop.** Any push-back goes straight to the client and their counsel. She never argues with a seller. | Arguing infringement is practising law |
| **6** | **One counsel opinion before the first notice**, on her exposure as an authorised submitter. The only capital worth spending. | The proposal's *"zero personal liability"* was an assertion with no source |

## 6. ★★★ THE ASSET — what makes this compound instead of just repeat

Every listing removed is logged: the seller, the storefront, the CNPJ where visible, the category, the date,
the brand attacked, and whether they came back.

> ### **After a year that ledger is the business.** Counterfeit sellers are not one-per-brand — the same storefronts rotate across categories and reappear under new accounts. **A new client's very first sweep starts with a list of known offenders in their category, built from every client before them.** Detection gets faster and more accurate with each brand added.
>
> **A competitor starting today cannot buy this, and the brand cannot commission it.** It is not scraped data — it is the *outcome history* of enforcement actions, which exists nowhere else because nobody else has run them. `THE HEAD-START ASYMMETRY`, on the right side of it for once: the asset is empty on day one and unassailable by year two.

**And it is the same artefact twice:** the ledger that finds a new client's infringements is the ledger that
proves to an existing client why they should keep paying.

## 7. Why they keep paying — the honest answer, and it is measurable in month one

The obvious objection: clear the fakes in month one, the brand cancels in month two. **The answer is not a
minimum-term clause. It is a number she will have by day thirty:**

> **Of the listings removed in the first sweep, how many reappear within thirty days — under a new storefront,
> a new account, or a slightly altered title?**

If that number is high, the monthly fee sells itself every month, in the client's own data. If it is near
zero, **the business is a one-off cleanup and should be priced as one** — a US$1.500 sweep, not a retainer.
**This is the single most important unknown in the whole model, it costs nothing to measure, and it is
answerable on the first client before any pricing is fixed.**
