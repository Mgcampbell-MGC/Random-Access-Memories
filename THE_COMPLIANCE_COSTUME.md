# THE COMPLIANCE COSTUME — mandated replacement cycles, and the ones that are fake

**17 Aug 2026. S2 hunt, agent 2 of 6. The first search run under CLAUDE.md's shape check.**

> **The thesis held — genuine mandated cycles exist — but with a twist that reorders the shortlist
> completely, and with six vendor overstatements caught, four of them near-universal industry lore.**

---

## 1. THE STRUCTURAL FINDING — worth more than any single row

> **The most useful mandates are the ones that INCORPORATE THE MANUFACTURER'S OWN CALENDAR BY REFERENCE.**
> The rule says *"maintain according to the manufacturer's guidelines."* **The statute supplies the
> obligation; the manufacturer supplies the date.**
>
> **That converts a private expiry sticker into a legal deadline — and those consumables arrive in a box
> rather than with a van.**

California Health & Safety Code 1797.196(b)(1)(C) is the clean example: an AED must be *"maintained and
tested according to the operation and maintenance guidelines set forth by the manufacturer."* The
manufacturer says the pads expire in two years. **The state has now made that two-year sticker
enforceable.**

## 2. THE DECISIVE FILTER — service-incumbent versus self-serve

**This kills the categories that look best on paper.**

**CLOSED — a licensed service company with a physical route owns it.** A drop-ship reseller cannot enter:
fire extinguishers · kitchen hood *cleaning* · grease traps · backflow *testing*.

**OPEN — the obligated party performs the swap themselves and the item ships in a box:**
**respirator cartridges · spore-test consumables · AED pads and batteries · calibration gas · hood baffle
filters · DOT emergency equipment.**

## 4. THE COMPLIANCE COSTUME — six overstatements, and this is the law

> **THE COMPLIANCE COSTUME — a large share of "mandatory" replacement cycles are manufacturer or
> trade-association recommendations sold as law by the people who profit from the replacement. Before
> building on a mandate, read the rule text and check whether the standard was actually ADOPTED by the
> regulator.**

**OSHA's own words, from its interpretation letters:** *"ANSI standards become mandatory OSHA standards
only when, and if, they are adopted by OSHA"* — and **Z358.1 was not adopted.**

| Claim sold as law | Reality |
|---|---|
| **Eyewash weekly activation / annual inspection (ANSI Z358.1)** | **Not federal law.** 1910.151(c) sets no interval and no consumable spec. **Z358.1 was never adopted by OSHA.** The 24-month cartridge expiry is manufacturer-set. *(And every vendor page citing Z358.1 clause numbers **contradicted the others** — 4.6.3 vs 4.6.2 vs 4.6.5. All clause numbering **UNVERIFIED**)* |
| **First-aid kit contents and expiry (ANSI/ISEA Z308.1)** | **Not federal law.** 1910.151(b) says only *"adequate first aid supplies shall be readily available,"* and the CFR titles Appendix A **"First aid kits (Non-Mandatory)."** Agent's verdict: *"a commercial practice wearing a compliance costume"* |
| **Harness "5-year expiry"** | **No such rule exists.** Traces to ANSI/ASSE A10.32 and **was removed in the 2012 revision.** Manufacturer practice diverges wildly — 3M DBI-SALA and FallTech publish no fixed life at all *(those figures came from vendor pages — **UNVERIFIED**; the well-supported part is the absence of any regulatory calendar)* |
| **"OSHA requires daily bump testing"** | **False.** The document everyone cites, SHIB 09-30-2013, says of itself: *"**not a standard or regulation, and it creates no new legal obligations.** The Bulletin is advisory in nature."* Daily bump testing is **ISEA's recommendation** |
| **"Calibration gas expires by rule"** | **No.** Expiry is the gas manufacturer's certification. OSHA's language — *"never use a test gas after its expiration date"* — is advisory. **Effectively forced by audit practice, not by law** |
| **"OSHA requires 30-day cartridge change"** | **Not in the rule.** 1910.134(d)(3)(iii)(B) requires that *"the employer implements a change schedule… based on objective information or data."* **It mandates having a schedule, not any interval.** Only substance-specific standards set hard intervals |
| HVAC filter intervals | **No fixed mandate.** ASHRAE 180 is condition-based; the IMC governs installation, not frequency. Lease- and warranty-driven |
| Smoke alarm 10-year replacement | **Partly overstated.** CA H&SC 13114 requires a 10-year battery *for a device to be approved and listed* — it does not impose a replacement duty on owners |

### And the ethical consequence, which is not optional

> **The fake mandates are a KILL LIST, not an opportunity list.** Selling a consumable on a compliance
> obligation that does not exist is misrepresentation, regardless of how many competitors do it. **She does
> not build on rows the rule text does not support.** That removes eyewash, first-aid refills, harnesses,
> and generic bump-test gas from consideration entirely.

## 5. Where this leaves the S2 hunt

**Two rows survive the full filter — mandated, unconditional, self-serve, and the buyer is on a
downloadable public list:**

**A · Spore-test vials → dentists.** Weekly and unconditional. Dentists are publicly licensed in every US
state *and* most other countries. Single-site, card-paying, email-reachable — **D5 sub-procurement** exactly.
Partially occupied by mail-in monitoring services, which is the thing to size.

**B · AED pads and batteries.** The highest-margin, most-forgotten consumable in the table, made binding by
a statute that adopts the manufacturer's expiry date. **Its only flaw is the buyer list** — and **G3 says
the fix is to hunt the event that commits someone: buying an AED.** Worth checking whether any state or
local EMS agency publishes an AED registry, since several require registration.

**Deliberately set aside:** formaldehyde cartridges — per-shift consumption and a downloadable CLIA register
make it the strongest row on paper, but the obligation is **conditional** (1910.1048(g)(1) requires
respirators only where engineering controls are infeasible or insufficient), so the population must be sized
before anything is built. **And its largest named segment is embalming, which is inside the death veto.**
