# THE MERCHANT-OF-RECORD KILL — and the escape, from the same document

**17 Aug 2026. S2 hunt, agent 5 of 6. Red team on the structure itself.**

> **VERDICT: FALSE as specified.** And it does not fail first on foreign VAT, which is where I expected it.
> **It fails in Brazil, on the two words "sole trader."** Two Brazilian statutes void the premise before any
> US, UK or EU rule is reached.

---

## 1. FATAL — "a sole trader with no company" is not a legally available state in Brazil

**Receita Federal's own published guidance:** a natural person who *"em nome individual, explor[a], habitual
e profissionalmente, qualquer atividade econômica… mediante venda a terceiro de bens ou serviços"* is
**equiparada à pessoa jurídica** — and expressly *"**quer se encontrem regularmente inscritas ou não** junto
ao órgão do Registro de Comércio."*

> *"O fato de a pessoa física caracterizada como empresa individual não se encontrar regularmente inscrita
> no CNPJ… **é irrelevante** para fins de pagamento do imposto de renda pessoa jurídica."*

**Basis: RIR/2018 (Decreto 9.580/2018) art. 162 §1.** Mandatory consequences RFB lists: CNPJ inscription,
complete commercial bookkeeping in registered and authenticated books, ECF and DCTF filings, IRRF
withholding, DIRF.

> **She does not get to CHOOSE to be a sole trader. She becomes a taxable company by operation of law the
> moment she trades habitually** — with all the compliance cost, and none of the "few hundred dollars"
> covering a Brazilian accountant plus ECF and DCTF.

**MEI is not the escape** — capped, and its foreign-trade permission is limited to importing for retail
resale to final consumers, not wholesale-to-business. *(Current MEI ceiling: **UNVERIFIED**, do not quote a
number.)*

## 2. FATAL — two unrecoverable turnover taxes stack in OPPOSITE directions

**This is the killer I did not list, and it is the single most likely reason the structure fails.**

**Leg A — Brazil taxes her GROSS SELLING PRICE and gives no credit for what she paid.** Because the goods
never enter or leave Brazil, **it is not an export**, so PIS/COFINS export immunity does not apply.
**SC COSIT 306/2017:** the base of calculation is *the value of the commercial invoice issued to the buyer.*
**SC COSIT 31/2023:** **no PIS/COFINS credits arise**, because the goods were never in Brazilian territory.
So **3,65% cumulative, or 9,25% non-cumulative on gross with zero credits**, plus IRPJ and CSLL.

**Leg B — the USA taxes her COST**, because she has no resale certificate (see §3).

**The arithmetic — modelled, assumptions stated. 25% gross margin, 7% average state rate:**

| On a sale of 100 | |
|---|---|
| Cost of goods | 75,00 |
| US state sales tax charged to her by the drop-shipper | 5,25 |
| Brazilian PIS/COFINS on gross revenue (cumulative 3,65%) | 3,65 |
| **Remaining, before any other cost** | **16,10** |

Non-cumulative: **10,50.** And CDTFA computes the supplier's tax on **110% of its price to her**, making it
worse. Out of that she must fund acquirer fees (3–4%+ on international cards), FX spread, IRPJ/CSLL,
accounting, freight exceptions and returns.

> **At the 15–20% gross margin normal for industrial replenishment consumables, the two turnover taxes alone
> consume most or all of it. Neither is recoverable and neither can be shown to the customer as a line
> item.**

**And a correction to prior research:** her structure is **not "back to back."** BCB's own FX classification
notes distinguish nature **12029** (buy in one foreign country, sell in **another**) from nature **12823**
(*"Comércio de mercadorias sem trânsito aduaneiro no Brasil"* — buy in one foreign country, sell in **that
same** country). **Buy US, sell US is 12823.** The FX leg is lawful and classifiable — but the entire body
of back-to-back rulings applies only **by analogy**, so her tax position is *less* settled than the
literature suggests.

## 3. FATAL — the US kill is the RESALE CERTIFICATE, not economic nexus

**I had the crux wrong.** Nexus is a later problem — at $500–5.000 per order she would need 20–200 orders
into a single state to cross typical thresholds (California's is $500.000).

**The order-one problem is that she cannot buy for resale.** The MTC Uniform Sales & Use Tax Certificate
requires the buyer to certify it *"is **registered** with the below listed states and cities,"* and its own
instructions: *"If the seller does not have this certificate, it is obliged to collect the tax."*

- **Illinois:** *"no other state's registration number is acceptable… the seller must charge the purchaser
  tax."*
- **District of Columbia:** *"not valid as a resale certificate unless it contains the purchaser's D.C.
  sales and use tax registration number."*
- **CDTFA Reg. 1706:** a drop shipper engaged in business in California is **reclassified as the retailer
  and is liable for tax**, and may compute the retail price as *"its selling price to the true retailer plus
  a mark-up of 10 percent."*

**And the mirror problem hits her customer.** Unregistered, she collects no tax on a taxable domestic
delivery, so the US buyer owes **use tax** and must self-assess.

> **A US buyer's tax function reads a foreign vendor invoicing a domestic delivery with no tax line as an
> audit exposure. The tax defect is visible to exactly the person who signs the PO.**

## 4. FATAL — the UK is closed from the first sale, and is worse than most of the EU

**HMRC's internal manual:** *"**NETPs must notify HMRC of their liability to be registered regardless of the
value of the taxable supply**."* **The £90.000 threshold does not exist for her. One sale triggers
registration.**

No general reverse charge rescues her — Notice 735 covers phones and chips over £5.000, wholesale gas and
electricity, wholesale telecoms and renewable certificates. **Not industrial consumables.**

And HMRC may **direct** an NETP with no EU establishment to appoint a UK VAT representative who is
*"responsible **to the same extent as the taxable person**… including **liability for VAT**."*

> **Finding a fit-and-proper UK person to accept unlimited joint VAT liability for an unknown Brazilian
> individual is not a few-hundred-dollars problem.**

## 5. FATAL — France: Brazil is on the wrong side of a published list

**CGI art. 289 A** requires a non-EU taxable person liable for French VAT to appoint an **accredited fiscal
representative established in France who undertakes to pay the tax on her behalf** — unless her country is
on a published exemption list.

**The agent read the list** (arrêté du 15 mai 2013 as consolidated, after the 16 Feb 2021 arrêté added 18
countries including the UK) and enumerated all 42 entries.

> **Brazil is not on it. Argentina, Australia, India, Mexico are. Brazil is not.**

**Germany** separately requires registration: **§ 13b UStG** shifts liability only for enumerated cases —
Werklieferungen, construction, grid gas and electricity, scrap, gold, mobile devices and integrated circuits
over €5.000, Annex 3 and 4 goods, building cleaning, emission allowances. **An ordinary supply of goods
already in Germany by a foreign supplier is not in the list.**

## 6. FATAL — "or her own branding" makes her the manufacturer

**The most expensive four words in the structure.**

- **USA — Restatement (Third) of Torts: Products Liability § 14:** one who *"**sells or distributes as its
  own** a product manufactured by another is subject to the same liability as though the seller or
  distributor were the product's manufacturer."* § 1 already puts any commercial seller of a defective
  product in strict liability.
- **EU — Directive (EU) 2024/2853** defines "manufacturer" to include anyone who *"presents themselves as
  manufacturer through their name or trademark."* **Transposition deadline 9 December 2026.**
- **EU hard blocker — Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 art. 4(1):** a product within art. 4(5) *"may be placed on
  the market **only if there is an economic operator established in the Union**,"* and **art. 4(4) requires
  that operator's name and postal address on the product or its packaging.** Art. 4(5) covers **PPE (Reg.
  2016/425)**, machinery, ATEX, pressure equipment, low voltage, EMC, radio equipment, construction products.

> **Respirator filters are PPE. Machine safety components are machinery. Own-branded, she cannot lawfully
> place these on the EU market at all** without appointing and paying an EU authorised representative.

*(In her favour: GPSR is scoped to consumer products, so pure industrial B2B parts largely fall outside it —
but art. 4(5) does not care whether the buyer is a business.)*

## 7. FATAL — the insurance pincer: Brazilian law forbids the policy her customers require

**Brazil closes it from inside. LC 126/2007 art. 20** restricts residents buying insurance abroad to four
situations — and the parágrafo único reads: *"**Pessoas jurídicas** poderão contratar seguro no exterior para
cobertura de riscos no exterior."*

> **Legal entities may buy foreign cover for foreign risks. Natural persons may not. As a sole trader she is
> statutorily excluded from the only insurance market that can write the policy her customers demand.**

**The USA closes it from outside.** Texas A&M System standard vendor language: coverage must be underwritten
by insurers *"authorized to do business in the State of Texas or eligible surplus lines insurers"* with
**A.M. Best A- or better and financial size VII or better** — CGL $1M per occurrence, $2M aggregate,
**Products/Completed Operations $1M**, buyer as additional insured. **A SUSEP-regulated Brazilian insurer is
none of those.** She cannot produce a compliant certificate of insurance.

## 8. SEVERE — the supplier side, from a real application

Fed Tax ID or SSN · **DUNS #** · years in business · bankruptcy history · *"must include current completed
resale certificate"* · bank reference **plus account number** · *"**four (4) credit references**"* with
account numbers · and a **"PERSONAL GUARANTEE: I personally guarantee any and all outstanding balances"*
signed by a principal.

And the term that settles working capital outright:

> *"Payment in full shall be due and payable **prior to shipment**… via wire payment, ACH or (**for US
> Dealers/Resellers only**) credit card."*

**Cards are for US resellers only. She must prepay by wire.** Net terms exist only after a US credit
investigation she cannot pass — no US credit file, no US trade references, no US bank, and a personal
guarantee worth nothing because enforcing it means suing an individual in São Paulo.

**Returns: she pays twice, plus 25%.** Supplier's own terms make her pay return freight, **insure the return
for full value**, and absorb a **25% restocking fee**. On a wrong part on a $2.000 order that is outbound
freight + return freight + insurance + $500 restocking + a full refund. **One mis-ship wipes out ten clean
orders.**

**And an honest correction on chargebacks:** Visa's VAMP is **not** a realistic threat — the Excessive
Merchant threshold needs ≥220bps **and ≥1.500 monthly disputes**. *(Visa's own footnote: programmes for
**Brazil**, Chile and India "will be announced later.")* The binding constraint is her acquirer's risk
appetite. **The agent refused to give a chargeback rate:** *"every figure circulating on this is vendor
marketing. Do not let a number into the file."*

## 9. SEVERE — the W-9 problem is a hard SYSTEM block, not a soft trust problem

**IRS Instructions for the Requester of Form W-9:** *"A foreign person… **may not provide a Form W-9**."*

Payments for **merchandise** are not 1099-reportable and W-8BEN is technically correct — **but US AP and
procurement systems are built around a W-9 field, and the vendor-master workflow will not create a vendor
record without one.**

> ***"She is not failing a judgement call; she is failing a required field."***

**Australia adds a 47% haircut.** GST Act s 9-25(1) makes goods delivered in Australia connected with
Australia regardless of where the seller sits, and GSTR 2018/2 confirms the supplier's place of business is
irrelevant. Then **TAA Sch 1 s 12-190**: the payer must withhold unless the supplier quotes an ABN.
**Default behaviour is to withhold at the top rate — 47%.**

Below the small-supplier threshold — **C$30.000** in a single quarter and across the last four — **no
registration at all.** If she must register with no permanent establishment, security is mandatory:
minimum **$5.000**, maximum $1M.

## 11. MODERATE — getting paid works, and two prior assumptions were wrong

**Stripe Brazil accepts individuals** — *"the CNPJ is optional"* for Pessoa Física — requiring a CPF, a
Brazilian address, *"your main support phone number,"* and a **BRL-denominated Brazilian bank account.**

**Two corrections:** settlement is *"30 calendar days; **5 calendar days for international charges**"* — her
customers are foreign, so **5 days, not 30.** And **Wise lets Brazilian residents hold USD**, escaping the
double-FX round trip — **but Wise's AUP: *"You may not use your personal Wise account to receive business
payments."***

**On the merchant-of-record question — no clean prohibition found.** Stripe's restricted list catches
*"receiving settlement proceeds for goods or services that you did not provide, **on behalf of one or
multiple third-party sellers**"* — she contracts on her own account, so on its face it does not catch her.
**Treat as account-termination risk, not a documented ban.** But Stripe's list *does* hit the product set:
regulated medical devices, pesticides requiring certified application, **toxic, flammable, combustible or
radioactive materials.**

## 12. THE ESCAPE — from the same RFB document that kills the structure

Among the activities that do **NOT** trigger equiparação to pessoa jurídica:

> *"o **representante comercial** que exerça **exclusivamente a mediação** para a realização de negócios
> mercantis, como definido pelo art. 1º da Lei nº 4.886, de 1965, **uma vez que não os tenha praticado por
> conta própria**."*

> **A commercial representative who ONLY mediates and NEVER trades on her own account stays a pessoa
> física.**

**That single change removes, simultaneously:** equiparação and the CNPJ obligation · PIS/COFINS on gross
sale value (revenue becomes a commission) · **the US resale-certificate problem entirely — she never buys
the goods** · UK NETP registration · the French fiscal representative · seller strict liability under
Restatement § 1 · deemed-manufacturer status under § 14 and the PLD · Reg. 2019/1020 art. 4 · the certificate
of insurance · chargeback and return-freight exposure.

**What she gives up:** gross margin, for a commission rate. And **pricing control**, which is what
merchant-of-record status was for. **And the exemption is fragile — one own-account order and she is
equiparada.**

### THIS CORRECTS G7, AND THE CORRECTION MATTERS

**Parts bin G7 — the router position — concluded that the escape from THE STAMP LAW § 5b was to RESELL
rather than broker: *"take the transaction rather than brokering it."***

> **That is exactly backwards for a Brazilian. Resale is what makes her a company with 9,25% on gross and no
> credits. Brokerage is the only lawful shape.**

**And the two constraints do close on each other — but not everywhere.** § 5b kills paid introductions into
**licensed professions** (AICPA, ABA 7.2(b), Cal. B&P 5061, 19 CFR 111.36(b), ISO 17065). **Industrial
consumables have no referral-fee rule.**

> **THE COMMISSION-AGENT RESOLUTION — resale is closed to her by Brazilian tax law; brokerage is closed to
> her by professional referral-fee rules. The surviving intersection is COMMISSION AGENCY IN AN INDUSTRY
> WITH NO REFERRAL-FEE RULE. That is lawful on both sides, and it is a much narrower target than either law
> alone suggested.**

## 13. THE ONBOARDING LAW — the constraint nobody separated before

> ***"The unfixable constraint is 'never speaks to anyone.' Not for legal reasons — for ONBOARDING ones.
> Reseller account setup at Global Industrial is a phone call. Fiscal representative appointment, CRA
> security posting, Stripe risk review and any dispute escalation are all conversations. Written-only is
> compatible with SELLING; it is not compatible with BEING ONBOARDED."***

> **THE ONBOARDING LAW — C1 has been tested against the SALES motion in every candidate in this file, and
> never against the SETUP motion. Selling in writing is common. Being ONBOARDED in writing — by a supplier,
> an acquirer, a tax authority, a representative — is rare, and it is a one-time gate that no product design
> can route around.**

**Promote to the permanent filter. Every future candidate gets asked: can she be onboarded in writing?**
