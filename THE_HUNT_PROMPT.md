# THE HUNT PROMPT — paste this into any capable LLM

*A cold-start brief. Everything a fresh model needs to beat the current board without re-walking
~130 dead candidates. Last updated 14 Sep 2026.*

---

You are helping a real person choose a real business. Read all of this before proposing anything.
I have already run ~130 candidates into the ground. Your job is to beat three survivors, not to
restart the search from zero.

## 1 · THE OPERATOR — these constraints are hard and do not bend

- **Sol, 26, São Paulo, Brazil.** Native Portuguese, fluent written English. Former runway model
  (small name, small network). Grew up on a coffee farm. **Not a developer.**
- **No employees and no contractors. Ever.** Vendors and suppliers are fine — a print bureau that
  quotes from an uploaded file is a vendor; a freelancer on a retainer is not.
- **Permanently faceless.** No camera, no personal brand, no audience-building, no paid ads that
  publish an advertiser name. A company brand or pen name is allowed if it is not what rescues the idea.
- **Never takes custody of a customer's credential** or standing access to their systems. Work
  arrives as a file they send. (Her *own* logins to her *own* suppliers are fine.)
- **~87 hours a month.** Her hours must be selection, oversight and QA — not per-unit production.
- **US$3.000 of capital, one time. It does not reload.**
- Calls are now permitted — but only ~10–15 spoken hours in year one, i.e. roughly
  **15–20 first conversations, ever.** Origination must be written.
- **She can never be believed as an expert.** Any idea requiring her to be credible as an authority,
  hold a professional credential, or assert a "before/after" is dead on arrival.

## 2 · THE TARGET

**US$2.000–2.500/month NET take-home (R$10.400–13.000), within 12–18 months.**
Not a unicorn. Not a lifestyle brand. A durable small business she still wants to run in year three.

## 3 · THE BOARD YOU MUST BEAT

1. **Government supply desk — office supplies.** Bids whole baskets (~33 line items) of pens, folders
   and paper on Brazilian public tenders; a São Paulo wholesaler ships direct to the buyer under
   *venda à ordem*, so she holds zero stock. Two real one-person operators already do this — one with
   **exactly R$50.000 of capital doing R$1,1M/year.** Honest floor at her volume: R$110–140k/year.
2. **Same desk, newborn kits.** Assembled baby kits to municípios. Market measured at R$66M/year,
   median kit R$322 awarded, needs 12–23 tender wins a year. Needs a co-packer; a São Paulo firm
   already holds 10,4% of the market across 15 states.
3. **Ultrasound keepsake machine.** A clinic hands the mother a printed keepsake generated from her
   scan, ≤3 minutes of machine time per unit. Wounded: medical-council rules mean the clinic may not
   advertise it, may not display an example, and may not edit the image.

**A proposal that does not clearly beat these on the scorecard in §6 is not worth sending.**

## 4 · THE GRAVEYARD — twelve laws that killed ~130 candidates

Screen every idea against these BEFORE you write it up. Most proposals die here, and if yours dies,
say so and move on rather than arguing with the law.

1. **THE FREE FRONT DOOR.** *Sole cause of 77% of surviving kills.* If anyone upstream earns margin
   on the future flow, they fund the thing you want to sell to zero. Grainger gives away part
   identification; Frazer gives funeral homes free tribute software; Mercado Livre gives away its
   takedown portal. **Ask: who earns on the flow behind this, and what do they already give away free?**
2. **THE CATALOGUE SCISSORS.** An item either sits in a catalogue the buyer reaches in 90 seconds
   (no margin) or it does not, in which case it is custom — artwork, proofs, approvals — meaning her
   hands on every unit. **No third door.**
3. **THE TEMPLATE FLOOR TEST.** A legal obligation proves *demand*, never *price*. The duty is public
   and discharged by any conforming artefact, so price falls to the cheapest conforming supplier —
   for a document, a template. Measured floor for a Brazilian mandatory document pack: **R$97,90 for
   186 editable Word files.**
4. **THE ATTENTION TRILEMMA.** If revenue is rent on attention, stop. Ad sales needs a relationship;
   affiliate needs to rank for "which vendor should I use"; subscriptions need ~36–45.000 subscribers
   at measured conversion rates. All three are closed to a faceless operator.
5. **THE STEADY-STATE KILL.** One-time capital cannot fund a subscription business. Churn is
   perpetual; US$3.000 is not. The base decays geometrically to zero. **Ask: how many customers must
   she add every month forever, and what channel produces that after the money runs out?**
6. **THE REPLACEMENT-COST CEILING.** A machine's price is capped by what it costs to rebuild. If the
   buyer can commission the same thing, the price is the build cost. The only inputs that escape:
   **accumulated history, a credential, an access right, or physical possession of a unique object.**
7. **THE OBSERVED-PRICE CEILING.** A price anchor is only usable if the party earning it has no
   advantage she lacks — a name, a credential, an address, a sales team, scale. Otherwise you have
   measured your own maximum and called it validation.
8. **THE MARKET-MULTIPLE RULE.** A revenue target needs a *reachable, contract-priced* pool 3–5× its
   size. She needs ~R$150k/year, so the reachable pool must be **R$550–900k/year minimum** — real
   spend in the actual niche, never a global TAM.
9. **THE MACHINE TEST** *(her own revealed preference, from a real ranked list she cut).* Ask three
   things: **does a MACHINE make the unit; can the buyer VERIFY it without trusting her; does selling
   it create a RELATIONSHIP she must then hold?** She rejected every candidate that was
   relationship-heavy or hands-on-every-unit.
10. **ORIGINATION ONCE vs FOREVER.** After the first yes, does the next unit of demand arrive by
    itself, or must she go and fetch it? She cut three good candidates purely for failing this.
11. **INFORMATION DOES NOT PRICE.** Only four things price, and information is merely their delivery
    medium: **accepted liability for a rejection · a physical original that exists once · an access
    right the buyer is barred from holding · a signature.** Strip the information away; if nothing is
    left, the price is zero.
12. **THE LEGIBILITY–MARGIN ANTI-CORRELATION.** Transactions that are easy to observe (tender awards,
    rate cards, job ads, salary surveys) are priced per unit of commodity labour — that is *why* they
    are published. **Margin lives where price is quoted bespoke and never published.**

**Also settled, do not re-propose:** AI-generatable assets on marketplaces (banned, or price → 0) ·
written deliverables on freelance marketplaces (observed $5–45) · app stores (12–18 months, organic
discovery ≈ 0) · Brazilian SaaS (median published plan R$299; above R$1.000 the price disappears
behind a sales call) · Brazilian info-products (affiliate marketplaces gate on sales history she
cannot manufacture) · faceless YouTube (entry threshold doubles Feb 2027; Brazil has no US tax
treaty so ~27% is withheld) · reselling labour (measured at **−R$79 per head per month**).

## 5 · EVIDENCE STANDARD — non-negotiable

**Four fabricated statistics have already been caught in this project, two of them inside polished
documents from confident sources.** Therefore:

- **Every number needs a primary-source URL** — the company's own pricing page, a statute, a
  government dataset. Not a blog, not an SEO page, not a vendor's marketing claim.
- **Write "UNVERIFIED" explicitly** wherever you cannot confirm something. A flagged gap is worth
  more than a plausible figure. I would rather have five honest numbers than twenty confident ones.
- **Observed listings beat published claims.** What something actually sold for beats what a page
  says it costs.
- **A count is not a check.** If you report that a market, register or dataset exists, quote a real
  row from it.
- **Never invent a statistic to make a candidate work.** If the idea needs a number you do not have,
  say what the number is and how to get it.

## 6 · WHAT "BETTER" ACTUALLY MEANS — score every candidate out of 9

1. A **machine** makes the unit, not her hands
2. The buyer can **verify** it without trusting her
3. Selling it creates **no relationship** she must hold
4. After the first sale, **demand arrives unbidden**
5. The **second purchase is forced** by a rule or a clock she does not control
6. **Nobody upstream** has a reason to give it away free
7. The **reachable pool is ≥R$550–900k/year** of real, contract-priced spend
8. It survives a **frontier model release** — capability makes it *better*, not *rebuilt*
9. She could still be running it, alone, **in year three**

## 7 · WHAT TO SEND BACK

Give me **5 to 8 candidates**, no more. For each, in this order and nothing else:

- **Name + one sentence.** What it is, plainly.
- **The buyer.** Who pays, and why they cannot easily not pay.
- **The unit.** What gets delivered, and what makes it.
- **The money.** Price per unit, units needed for US$2.000–2.500/month net, and the reachable pool —
  with a source for each figure or an explicit UNVERIFIED.
- **Score /9** against §6, with the failures named.
- **The graveyard check.** Which of the twelve laws in §4 come closest to killing it, and why they
  do not.
- **Which of my three it beats, and on what.** If it beats none, say so.
- **The cheapest test.** One thing, under R$500, no phone call, that would tell us whether this is
  real — and the numeric bar it must clear, committed in advance.

**Then end with the honest verdict:** if none of your candidates beats the board, say that plainly.
A well-evidenced null is worth more to me than an eighth plausible idea. The failure mode I am
guarding against is not a bad idea — it is a *confident* one.
