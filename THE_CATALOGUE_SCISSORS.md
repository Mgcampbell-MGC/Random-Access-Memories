# HR SMALL-PURCHASE SUPPLY — KILLED, 19 Aug 2026

**The founder's hypothesis: HR departments buy small things at US$150–500; the buying process costs more than
the item; so a frictionless supplier can charge a large margin, drop-shipped, no inventory.**

**Verdict: the premise is TRUE and thirty years stale. The friction is real, the buyer already fixed it on their
own side with a card, and the fix required no supplier to change anything. Four independent kills — and
notably, the tax was not one of them.**

---

## 0. ★ THE FOUNDER WAS RIGHT ABOUT THE TAX, AND THE FILE WAS ABOUT TO BE WRONG TWICE

The founder's objection — *"never let taxes get in the way of a great business"* — **is vindicated on the
evidence.** Two corrections follow from it:

**(a) The tax is a component of a cost stack, not a verdict.** Verified verbatim at Planalto, **Decreto
9.580/2018 art. 162 §1º II** does make her *equiparada* (*"pessoas físicas que, em nome individual, explorem,
habitual e profissionalmente, qualquer atividade econômica… por meio da venda a terceiros de bens"*), and
**§2º III** does exempt agents who *"não os pratiquem… por conta própria."* **Drop-shipping is trading on her
own account, so the exemption does not reach it — confirmed at source.** But the Brazilian turnover load is
**~5,9% of revenue**, which on its own is survivable. **It only kills in combination.**

**(b) ★ THE PAYMENT RAIL IS NOT THE KILL EITHER — and this file was about to assume it was.** Wise Business
supports Brazilian **Empresário Individual (including MEI)** and **Sociedade Limitada Unipessoal**, with
**receiving details in 18+ currencies including USD**, one-time **R$250**. **A US buyer therefore pays by
domestic ACH at near-zero cost.** *(The wire alternative would have been fatal: Chase's own schedule charges
**$40–$50** for an outgoing international USD wire — **13–17% of a $300 order.**)*

> **So `INVOICE, DON'T CHECKOUT` survives but must be restated: the rail EXISTS and is cheap. What is expensive
> is the CARD, and the market leader says most customers use one.**

## 1. THE PREMISE IS TRUE — and that is precisely why there is no business

**Primary, fetched: [GAO/NSIAD-96-138](https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-96-138.pdf)**, on the 1994 interagency
Purchase Card Council study across 17 organisations:

> *"Per transaction savings for the 17 organizations ranged from **$1.42 to over $142**, with an average saving
> of about **$54**."*

**So a small PO genuinely costs $22–$142 more to process than a card — on a $150–500 item, 4–95% of its value.
The founder's instinct was correct.**

**And it has been fully arbitraged, by the buyer, without any supplier.** [FAR 2.101](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/2.101):
the micro-purchase threshold is **$15.000**, raised from $10.000 effective 1 Oct 2025. Below it: **no PO, no
competition, no vendor record — a card swipe.** Computed from **GSA SmartPay's own statistics workbook**:
**FY2025 Purchase business line = $28.429.337.462 across 19.578.363 transactions, average $1.452.**

> ### **★ THE SELF-SERVED FRICTION LAW — when a buyer's friction is INTERNAL to the buyer, they fix it on their own side and capture 100% of the saving. Every dollar of that $54 accrued to the buyer, not to a vendor. Before selling relief from a process cost, ask WHOSE process it is: if the buyer can fix it without changing suppliers, they already have.**

## 2. ★★ THE CATALOGUE SCISSORS — the structural kill

> ### **In corporate gifting an item either (a) EXISTS in a catalogue the buyer reaches in ninety seconds without speaking to anyone — Amazon Business, 4imprint, Snappy, Goody — in which case THERE IS NO MARGIN; or (b) it does NOT, in which case it is CUSTOM, which means artwork, proofs and approval cycles, which means HER HANDS ON EVERY UNIT.**
>
> ### **The only items with margin are the ones that need her hands; the only items that don't need her hands have no margin. No third door found.**

*(The same shape as the settled AI-on-marketplaces law — banned so her skill is worthless, or allowed so price
→ 0 — in a different market.)*

**And what would have to be true for the hypothesis to survive is a contradiction, stated plainly:** a category
**(1)** with no visible catalogue comparable, **(2)** paid by ACH not card, **(3)** structured so she never
takes title, and **(4)** handled in under ten minutes per order. **(1) forces custom, and custom breaks (4).**

**Observed, live:** 4imprint's public catalogue prices cluster **$0,38–$2,99 per unit**. **A $150–500 promo
"order" is not one frictionless item — it is 100–500 cheap units, each needing artwork, a proof and a
decoration run. The opposite of a frictionless purchase.**

## 4. THE ARITHMETIC, AND THE WORKING-CAPITAL KILL

Net margin after the stack: **~13%** paid by transfer, **~7,8%** paid by card.

| To net US$10.000/mo | Revenue | Orders @$300 | Orders @$500 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transfer (13%) | **$76.900** | **256/mo** | 154/mo |
| Card (7,8%) | **$128.200** | **427/mo** | 256/mo |

**Against ~78 productive hours that is 11–18 minutes per order** — quote, artwork proof, placement, tracking,
exception, invoice, collection. **She personally produces every unit. `THE_REBUILD`'s arithmetic-dead shape in a
drop-shipping costume.**

**And it dies at order one, independently, on float.** Promo suppliers require prepayment from new foreign
accounts; corporate AP does not reciprocate — **FMC Corp publishes *"FMC's standard payment terms are 90 days or
longer."*** At $76.900/month she must fund **~$52.000/month of prepaid goods against 30–90 day receivables — a
float of $52k–$156k. She has $2.500.**

## 5. ★ THE WHOLESALE ESCAPE WAS TESTED HARDEST, AND IT FAILED — with a law

The partner already owns the warehouse, decorators, ERP and supplier accounts. **The only thing it lacks is
sales — the one thing C1 and C2 forbid.** What remains for her is **order administration: manual, per-unit.**

> ### **★ THE WHOLESALE ESCAPE DOES NOT TRANSPOSE TO PHYSICAL GOODS. It pays only where the throughput is A MACHINE THAT RUNS ONCE AND SERVES MANY — a cron, a corpus, a watch. Where the throughput is PER-UNIT HUMAN HANDLING, white-labelling changes who gets the credit and nothing else.**
>
> ### **TEST FOR EVERY FUTURE WHITE-LABEL PROPOSAL: does the partner's marginal order cost her marginal minutes? If yes, it is not the wholesale escape. It is a job.**

**Also dead:** made-to-order only *(does not change the tax position — she still takes title — and adds a proof
cycle to every order, the exact per-unit human step the arithmetic cannot afford)* · commission agency
*(Sendoso publishes only a **"one-time commission"**; a one-time commission cannot produce recurring revenue,
and the motion into HR is a conversation)* · a software layer *(the free front door above)*.

## ★ AND `THE ONBOARDING LAW` IS RESTATED — it bites as THROUGHPUT, not law

For a single $200 gift **no onboarding happens at all — she gets a card.** The wall appears only when she wants
*recurring* revenue. Then: **ATI** requires *"W-8BEN-E (Entity) or W-8BEN (Individual)"* and a Non-US Source
Designation Statement — *"Your company's account cannot be created in our system until this information is
received in full"*; **Walmart** requires a **DUNS number**, W-9/W-8, Certificate of Insurance, and **EDI-B2B
configure-and-test**. Insurance stays closed: **LC 126/2007 art. 20**.

> **She does not need to be onboarded once. She needs to be onboarded 150–400 times, once per customer, and
> again on every bank-detail change.** *(No figure for phone-callback verification of supplier bank details was
> obtained — **UNVERIFIED, do not claim a call is required.**)*

## 6. WHAT HR ACTUALLY BUYS — one genuinely favourable finding, and one that reframes the band

**PPAI 2024 U.S. Distributors' Sales** (primary): industry **$26,78bn**; **Employee Relations 9,1% + Employee
Incentives 7,3% = 16,4% ≈ $4,4bn**, plus Business Gifts 14,9%.

**★ The volume drivers pass `THE CONSTRAINT-SIDE LAW` cleanly** — onboarding kits track hiring rate, service
awards track anniversaries, offboarding tracks attrition, holiday gifting tracks the calendar. **HR controls
none of them.** *(This is why the instinct was good.)*

**But the band is wrong.** **IRF 2025 Industry Outlook** (412 interviews): *"the average spend per instance for
merchandise has decreased to **$177** this past year, compared to **$191** in 2023"*; gift cards average **$142**.
**The modal HR recognition purchase is ~$177 — the bottom edge of the hypothesised band, not the middle** — while
4imprint's AOV is **$654**, above it. **The $150–500 band is the trough between the two.**

## 7. THE TEST THAT WOULD HAVE SETTLED IT IN AN AFTERNOON, FREE

> **Take 20 items HR actually buys in the band. For each record (i) the price the buyer already sees in a
> catalogue reachable without speaking to anyone (4imprint's public prices, Amazon retail) and (ii) the best
> landed cost from a published no-account blind-drop-ship or print-on-demand list. Count how many of the 20
> leave ≥25% gross margin AT OR BELOW the price the buyer already sees. Bar: fewer than 10 of 20 ⇒ dead.**
>
> **Then the second blade, same twenty, no extra cost: how many need no artwork proof and no approval cycle? If
> the intersection of "has margin" and "needs no proof" is EMPTY, the catalogue scissors is confirmed.**
