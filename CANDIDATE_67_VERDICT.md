# CANDIDATE 67 — VERDICT: DEAD

**14 Aug 2026. Killed the same afternoon it was generated, on primary law.**

---

## 1. The backlist has until 2030

**France, in hard law — Décret n° 2023-778, Article 7:**

> *"Les dispositions du présent décret sont applicables aux livres numériques… édités à partir du
> 28 juin 2025. Les dispositions du présent décret sont applicables **à compter du 28 juin 2030**
> aux livres numériques… **édités avant le 28 juin 2025**."*

**Unconditional, prospective, and it is the decree's own entry-into-force provision.** ARCOM
confirms it publicly. Italy is reported the same. Germany is genuinely unsettled — the Börsenverein
tells members there is no legal clarity and advises documenting effort to support a
*disproportionate-burden* defence.

**And a nuance that matters:** Article 32 of the Directive does **not** shelter the backlist. It
covers *products used to deliver* a service, and Art. 3(41) defines an e-book as **a service**. The
2030 relief comes from national entry-into-force provisions, not from the EAA. Anyone citing Art. 32
for a blanket reprieve — and the trade press does — is over-reading it.

**Net: no deadline for four years in the markets that have actually ruled.**

## 2. Three more kills, any one sufficient

**Frontlist is already closed.** Accessibility is now a standard composition deliverable —
*"built in, not bolted on"* — at S4Carlisle, Newgen KnowledgeWorks and every serious production
house, **inside contracts publishers have already signed.** There is no line item to win.

**The reachable buyers are legally exempt.** Art. 4(5) exempts microenterprises *providing
services*, and Art. 3(23)'s financial limb is an **OR**, not an AND — a wider exemption than I
stated. **The small indie who might gamble on an anonymous foreign supplier has no obligation. The
publisher with the obligation already has a vendor.** The overlap is close to empty.

**There has been no ebook enforcement anywhere.** Every confirmed EAA action is grocery
e-commerce — formal notices to Auchan, Carrefour, E.Leclerc and Picard in France in November 2025,
and a court order against Carrefour in June 2026. **Zero publishing actions.** France's ebook
sanction is a 5th-class contravention capped at **€7.500**.

## 3. Two claims of mine that were wrong

### a) The verifiability premise — the entire reason I liked this candidate — is false

I wrote that Ace by DAISY gives the buyer a machine verdict, and called it *"the strongest
verifiability of anything in the file"* and *"the first time in 67 attempts that facelessness costs
nothing at the point of purchase."*

**DAISY says the opposite, in its own documentation:**

> ***"Passing Ace validation is not the same as meeting requirements of WCAG. A clean report only
> means the issues Ace can check for have passed (i.e., the publication still may not be
> accessible)."***

**Ace deliberately issues no score and no percentage of conformance.** Roughly **30% of WCAG
criteria are automatically verifiable**; PDF/UA's Matterhorn Protocol is 89 machine-checkable of 136
conditions. DAISY ships **Ace SMART** as a manual companion — the existence of which is proof by
design that the automated layer is insufficient.

**So she could honestly promise "passes Ace." She could not promise conformance. The gap between
those two sentences is the entire liability — and a sophisticated buyer knows the difference.**

### b) "Titles must be withdrawn from sale" is vendor marketing

I called it *"lost revenue, not a hypothetical fine"* and *"the strongest possible motivator."* It is
**untraceable to any legal instrument**, appears uncited in the marketing of companies selling
remediation, and the one retailer with a published policy says the opposite:

> **Google Play Books: *"Play Books will continue to make titles available for sale regardless of
> their accessibility details."***

**Two smaller corrections:** the microenterprise test is **AND** on headcount but **OR** on the
financial limb. And the 37,4% figure counts **publishers who already produce ebooks**, not all
publishers.

## 4. And she cannot do the half that is worth money

**Measured effort — ABELAB, the best primary data that exists: ~1 hour per average title.** Simple
fiction 43–67 min; medium complexity 55–73 min.

**Fiction alt text is nearly free and nearly worthless** — Accessible.org prices *human* alt text at
$2,25 an image. **The alt text worth paying for is charts, diagrams and equations in scholarly
work**, where a 2026 comparative study found LLM output *"fluent but with substantial limitations in
conceptual accuracy and informational completeness"*, and **PNAS routes AI-generated alt text to
scientific editors before publishing it.**

> **The failure mode is confidently wrong, and a non-specialist cannot catch a wrong description of
> a figure she does not understand.**

At ~1 hour a title, an offshore composition house does that hour cheaper, with QA, references,
insurance and the ability to absorb 5.000 titles at once.

## 5. The one thing that survived — and it is a different business

**ABELAB's own finding:** *"no remediation technological solution or service on the market that can
process a large collection of files in one go."*

**And the toolchain is free** — Ace, EPUBCheck, Ace SMART, veraPDF, the DAISY Knowledge Base, all
open source. **Zero capital.**

> **The honest case for life is a case for a BATCH TOOL WITH A PUBLISHED PRICE, in a market that is
> 100% quote-gated — not for a person remediating files by hand.**
>
> **That is a product build against a 2030 deadline. It is not this candidate, and it is not
> something to start this week.**

**The free check that would decide it, if ever revisited:** run Ace over ~30 real EU-publisher
backlist EPUBs from **OAPEN and DOAB** — openly licensed, freely downloadable, genuine EU academic
backlist — and measure what share of failures are **deterministic** (metadata, language, nav, page
lists) versus **judgement** (alt text, reading order, MathML). High deterministic share means a
batch tool exists to be built. Low means a labour business she loses. **Cost: a weekend.**

## 6. What this changes

**Nothing about the recommendation.** Candidate 67 was one afternoon old and cost nothing. It is
filed.

**Candidate 64 remains the cheapest question in the file, and 66 remains the main build.**

**And one law is reinforced hard:** I generated 67 from the verifiability law, and the verifiability
turned out to be false — *because I read a tool's marketing rather than its documentation.* **The
law is sound. My check of it was not.** Before any future candidate is built on "the buyer can
verify it themselves", the verifying tool's own docs must be read first.
