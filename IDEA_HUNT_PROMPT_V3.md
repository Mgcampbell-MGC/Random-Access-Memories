# Brief: find a business for a specific founder

You are being asked to **find an idea**, not evaluate one. Your output is a recommendation you would
stake your reputation on, with the reasoning that makes it checkable.

Work from evidence. Where you assert a number, a price, a law or a competitor's existence, either
cite a source you actually consulted or mark it **UNVERIFIED**. Never invent a statistic, a company
or a price. An honest *"I could not confirm this"* is worth more than a confident figure that
dissolves on contact. A vendor's claim about itself is not independent evidence — label it as such.

**Forty-four candidates have already been killed.** Everything below was paid for with one of them.
Read it as a map of walls, not as a map of where to search.

---

## 1. The founder — fixed facts, not preferences

- Woman, ~26, São Paulo, Brazil. **Native Portuguese.** Written English is functional.
- **She sells by telephone and WhatsApp, in Portuguese.** This is the one asymmetry she has that a
  global competitor cannot copy cheaply. **She will never take an unscripted live call in English.**
- Background: modelling, then luxury and premium brand events. **Assume no professional expertise in
  anything** — not law, medicine, accountancy, engineering or finance. Her edge is that she directs
  an AI coding agent to build working software, and she researches well. Do not build a candidate on
  her knowing a field.
- **Faceless is permanent.** No camera, no personal brand, no influencer or creator work, no
  webinars, no audience-building as the route to market. She *may* trade under a company name with a
  CNPJ — normal and expected for a Brazilian B2B vendor.
- **Her personal São Paulo network is excluded** as a sales channel. Deliberately.
- New Brazilian CNPJ plus access to a US company. Stripe, Pix and boleto are all workable.
- **No employees, no contractors, no inventory, no audience, no email list, no customers.**
  Budget: a few hundred US dollars, total.
- **No per-transaction founder labour** beyond selling and account management. If the product needs
  her to personally do work on each customer or each transaction, it is an agency and it fails.
- **No custody of any customer credential, certificate or password.** This is not squeamishness: a
  hunt proved that facelessness and credential custody are structurally incompatible, because asking
  a stranger to hand an unnamed seller the key to their whole identity inverts the trust gradient.

## 2. The target

**R$30.000/month net to the founder**, within about twelve months. Recurring revenue strongly
preferred — a one-shot sale pays full acquisition cost forever, which is why eighteen candidates
could not reach the target even when everything else worked.

## 3. THE FOUNDER-FIT FILTER — ranks equal with every kill test below

Forty-four candidates were screened on whether they *work*. Not one was screened on whether she could
**stand to do the job for two years.** She has already rejected a fully-validated candidate — chasing
labour paperwork from construction subcontractors — on exactly that ground.

She will be on the phone and on WhatsApp with this buyer several times a day, in Portuguese, for
years. **The buyer must be someone a 26-year-old woman from the São Paulo events and fashion world
would find natural and pleasant to talk to.**

**Prefer:** solo practitioners and small-practice owners, mostly women — psychologists,
nutritionists, dentists, aesthetics and beauty owners, physiotherapists, event and party organisers,
trainers and course providers, trade-marketing agencies, wedding and production people, boutique
retail owners.

**Avoid, even where the economics work:** heavy construction, freight and trucking, industrial
maintenance, waste, mining, agricultural machinery, security firms — and any buyer whose daily
vocabulary is labour litigation, tax retention or industrial safety.

**A candidate that is economically perfect but points her at a buyer she would dread is a failed
candidate.** State explicitly, for each proposal, why she would enjoy this buyer.

**The price band, measured:** **R$150–700/month.** Bling publishes R$60 / R$120 / R$650; Conta Azul
R$159,90–719,90. Anything above ~R$700 must justify itself against those two by name.
**R$30.000/month means ~100 customers at R$300, or ~46 at R$650.**

**Do not price in dollars.** A US$299 product lands on a Brazilian buyer at ~R$2.100 — three to five
times the incumbent — and dies on price alone.

**Channel arithmetic:** cold B2B phone/WhatsApp converts at roughly 1–3% to paid, so 100 customers
means touching 3.300–10.000 qualified companies in a year.

**Channel legality:** cold *telephone* in Portuguese is unrestricted and cannot be banned. Cold
*WhatsApp* to a number scraped from a public register is not — Meta's Business Messaging Policy
requires opt-in, and unsolicited outreach risks number bans. Design the motion so the prospect
messages first (click-to-WhatsApp ads, or a link the customer sends).

## 5. The laws — every one bought with a dead candidate

**Apply these yourself before proposing anything. Do not outsource them to a critic.**

**THE CHAT-WINDOW TEST — the most lethal.** A general model was *measured* finding **38 of 38 planted
numeric discrepancies across three blind fixtures, zero false positives, zero silently-wrong
arithmetic across ~380 checks**, with checkable citations. Both ChatGPT and Claude execute Python over
uploaded files, so this will not regress.
> **Any candidate whose value is the analysis, the advice, the report, the template or the document is
> dead.** And the wedge must not be a prompt — a prompt needs no funding to distribute, which makes it
> worse than a funded incumbent. Value must sit in an **action**, a **data position**, a
> **permission**, a **channel**, or **continuity that runs unattended**.

**LAW 0 — THE CLEARING PARTY. Check first; it is the cheapest and it has killed the most.**
Name every party earning a margin on goods, payments, bookings, credit, insurance or advertising
flowing past your product. **If one exists, they will give your software away free, permanently, as
customer acquisition cost.** Verified: Object & Order ships AI inbox sync free and states *"there is
no paid plan"*, funded by 5% goods margin; Booksy ships a per-client commission Claim button free,
funded by payments; Momence ships contribution margin free; RangeMe auto-computes buyer gross margin
free, funded by event fees; Insparisk gives compliance alerts free forever, funded by insurance.
> **Do not sell software into a transaction you are not taking a cut of.**

**LAW C — hunt the free competitor separately, by funding model.** A competitor search is implicitly a
search for *vendors*, and a vendor is someone who charges — so a free competitor has no pricing page,
no comparison-site row and no keywords, and is **structurally invisible** to a normal search. A
candidate cleared by seventeen agents was killed by the founder with one link. Search free-shaped in
Portuguese — *gratuito, grátis, sem mensalidade, plano gratuito* — plus GitHub, plus the clearing
party's own product pages.

**LAW A — "no competitor found" is a claim about the searcher.** Vendors write in the formal register:
the lei or portaria number, the system's name, the trade acronym. Nine times in one run an agent
reported a clear space and the incumbent surfaced on the verifier's *first* formal query. **Ship your
exact query list**, in Portuguese, and check **B2B Stack, Capterra Brasil and GetApp Brasil** category
pages by name.

**LAW B — the regulator builds its own filing system, free, and mandates it.** The recipient of a
filing wants the data more than the filer wants to send it. Verified: Inmetro's PSIE, the National
Board's EDT. In Brazil also check gov.br, Receita, Sebrae (which gives away training *and*
consultancy free) and the relevant conselho.

**THE ENUMERABILITY TRAP.** A population enumerable *because a regulator registers it* is a population
whose recurring task that regulator has already systematised for free. Prefer populations enumerable
because they **transact, list themselves, publish, or hold an asset**.

**LAW 1 — THE LEAD-MAGNET MECHANISM (six kills).** Content costs nothing to produce, so the free tier
*is* every funded incumbent's marketing budget. Templates, planilhas, guides, checklists, corpora,
courses, monitors and alert feeds are presumed dead.

**LAW 6 — THE HUMAN SUBSTITUTE.** Brazilian labour is cheap: a secretária runs R$1.600–2.500/month, a
contador serving an SME R$300–800/month, an analista ~R$3.400. **If a part-time human absorbs the job,
there is no product.** Find the published price.

**LAW 7 — THE VISE (five appearances).** In a regulated domain the legally safe version has no
differentiation and the differentiated version is legally exposed — the reserved perimeter and the
pricing premium are the same feature. Watch **CFP** (psychology), **CFO** (dentistry — Res. 196/2019
bars before/after publication outright, which killed a candidate), **CRN**, **CFM**, **CFC**
(accountancy) and **OAB** (art. 1º II reserves *consultoria, assessoria e direção jurídicas*).

**THE ALERT FINDING.** Alerts sell only to the party who **profits from knowing**. They never sell to
the party the alert is *about*.

**THE PRECONDITION TRAP (three appearances).** If the product requires the customer to already possess
the discipline whose absence is the problem — clean records, a structured file, consistent naming —
the qualified population and the needy population are disjoint.

**GATE 0 — MEASURED SOLVENCY, AS A DISTRIBUTION.** Aggregate revenue divided by firm count is not a
distribution and will be rejected. A visible defect *selects for* organisations that are defective
because they are under-resourced. Two candidates died here: US charities at 70% under US$50k revenue,
and Brazilian cultural workers averaging R$2.815/month.

**THE TWO-BUCKET RULE.** **Bucket A kills:** an identical product already shipping to the identical
buyer; a named law reserving it to a credential she lacks; the list cannot be built; the physics fail;
a cheaper substitute does the whole job or someone gives it away free. **Bucket B may not kill:** will
they pay this, will the page convert, do they know they have the problem, will they trust an unknown
vendor. A Bucket B objection must arrive with a test under US$200 and under two weeks with a numeric
threshold. **A false kill costs more than a missed risk, because a false kill never shows up again.**

## 7. How to think — and how not to

The founder's own critique, which you should take seriously:

> *"AI often kills business ideas because it is much better at listing risks than discovering hidden
> opportunities. It defaults to consultant mode. **It confuses 'not proven' with 'bad.'** Almost every
> new business looks uncertain before testing."*

That is correct, and it coexists with everything above. Structural facts kill; adoption unknowns do
not. Do not produce a risk list. Do not hedge every sentence. **Commit to an answer.**

**Do not anchor on the shapes in this document.** The dead candidates were overwhelmingly *tools that
help a professional complete a recurring task* — one shape among many, searched to exhaustion. Deliberately
generate candidates from **structurally different shapes**, then choose. If your final three resemble
each other, you have not searched, you have elaborated.

**Returning zero candidates with a clear explanation of what you eliminated and why is a genuinely
useful answer.** It is better than a weak one, and it will be treated as such.

## 8. Output

**Part 1 — the field.** Six to ten candidates, a short paragraph each. Show your range. Include at
least two that are structurally unlike the others.

**Part 2 — the shortlist.** The three strongest, each answered against every law in §5 and the
founder-fit filter in §3, with sources and explicit UNVERIFIED tags. Show the arithmetic to
R$30.000/month as a visible calculation, with every customer count **derived** from a stated
conversion rate and retention assumption — never asserted.

**Part 3 — the recommendation.** One idea. Why it beats the other two. The single assumption most
likely to be wrong, and the cheapest check that settles it: what it costs, how long it takes, and
what result would make you abandon it. Thresholds must require **money committed**, not interest —
positive replies, "sounds interesting", free pilots and refundable reservations do not count.

**Part 4 — what you could not verify.** Everything asserted without a source, listed honestly.
