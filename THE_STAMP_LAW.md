# THE STAMP LAW — and its inverse, THE GATE FRAME

**14 Aug 2026. Derived from a $23M/month operator describing, on camera, exactly why he paid
$33.600 without negotiating.**

> **This law retroactively explains ten kills in one sentence. Its inverse is the first genuinely
> new search instruction this file has produced since the transposition law.**

---

## 1. The primary observation

An e-commerce operator needed a mini-split product line certified for US sale. Intertek quoted
**$33.600 for six months**. His account of the decision:

> *"I almost said no. 33 grand for seemingly nothing. You're not building anything. You're not
> shipping me anything. Then I thought about it for another second. If we got this wrong, we can't
> sell the product at all or worse, we get sued. So I paid it. **I didn't even negotiate.**"*

And his own analysis of what he was buying:

> *"That $33,600 is not a labor bill. It was an **insurance policy**. Someone with the stamp saying:
> we took the 10% chance you got it wrong and we made it zero."*

He then names three traits shared by every good version of this business — **recurring, painful,
document-heavy** — and a fourth that separates the ordinary version from the expensive one:

> ***"The customer's name is on the line if you get it wrong."***

And finally, the part that decides everything for a faceless seller:

> *"AI handles the repetitive parts of the workflow… **but the expert still has to sign off. The
> expert still takes the risk. The stamp still belongs to a person.**"*

---

## 2. THE LAW

> **THE STAMP LAW — the property that makes a document business worth $33.600 instead of $99 is
> that a person is standing behind it. That person is legally exposed, insured, and reachable.
> A faceless seller with no credential and no professional indemnity cannot hold the stamp, and
> therefore cannot capture the price the stamp commands.**

**Corollary, and this is what makes it useful:**

> **Every one of this file's ten compliance corpses was the B-tier artefact — recurring, painful,
> document-heavy — priced as though it carried the S-tier stamp. That is the single sentence
> explanation for all of them.**

The dead: HTS Ruling Currency Index · ISO 9001 Countdown Pack · ET Respondent's Corpus · NYC
Building Compliance Radar · 50-State Solicitation Monitor · Cosmetics regulatory · Recurso em 3
Dias · Sistema de Ciclo · Form 5500 delinquency feed · Dossiê Patrocínio.

**The law also explains the two prior kills that looked like different failures but are not.**
PRUMO died on CFC Res. 1.640/2021 and Recurso died on OAB Lei 8.906/94 art. 1º II. Both were
recorded as *"the reserved perimeter and the pricing premium are the same feature."* **That is the
Stamp Law stated in Brazilian regulatory terms.** The reserved profession is simply the state
deciding who is allowed to hold the stamp.

### The test, to run before designing anything

> **Name the person who gets sued if this is wrong. If the business only works when that person is
> the seller, stop.**

## 3. The evidence that it is not a hypothesis

The tier-list category he calls S-tier was checked item by item. **Nine for nine are stamp-gated.
Not most — all.**

| His category | Who holds the stamp |
|---|---|
| UL / Intertek / ETL testing | The lab. It is the entire product |
| R&D tax credits | The CPA signs; most-audited credit in the US code |
| Cost segregation | Engineer's certification |
| 179D / 45L energy credits | Licensed engineer must certify |
| WOTC | Employer attests, and it lives inside their hiring system |
| HIPAA | The covered entity or business associate — a signed instrument |
| OSHA | The employer |
| SOC 2 / ISO / PCI | A licensed auditor issues the report |
| Implementation partner | No stamp — but requires admin access to their instance (integration tax) |

**That uniformity is the finding.** It is not that compliance is a crowded market. It is that
compliance *is* the stamp business, definitionally, and the price is the liability transfer.

## 4. THE INVERSE — the gate frame, and it is the useful half

If the fourth trait is what carries the price, and a person cannot supply it, the question is what
else can.

> **THE GATE FRAME — a machine can adjudicate in place of a person. Where a portal, validator or
> automated system ACCEPTS or REJECTS a submission, the buyer never has to trust the preparer. The
> gate decides. The stamp is replaced by an adjudication the buyer can watch happen.**

This satisfies the verifiability law at its strictest reading — the 6 Aug correction that asked
*"after the buyer opens the file, can they tell whether it is right?"* Under a gate, they can. Not
by judgement, but by submission.

**Three conditions, all required:**

| | Condition | Why it binds |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** | The gate is **automated and binary** — accepted or rejected, not a human's opinion | A human reviewer reintroduces the stamp |
| **G2** | Rejection **costs the buyer real money**, not minutes of rework | The dead-code lesson: a defect a free system catches has no market |
| **G3** | The **preparation** is separable from the **submission** | She prepares a file; the client submits it. Otherwise the integration tax binds |

### The kill that G1 already delivered, for free

**Candidate 67 failed exactly this test and the file did not have the language for it at the time.**
Ace by DAISY looks like a gate and is not one — its own documentation says *"Passing Ace validation
is not the same as meeting requirements of WCAG. A clean report only means the issues Ace can check
for have passed."* It emits **no score and no conformance percentage**, and roughly **30% of WCAG
criteria are automatically verifiable**. It is an advisory tool wearing a gate's clothing.

> **G1 has teeth: a validator that reports findings is not a gate. A gate returns a decision the
> submission cannot proceed without.**

---

## 5. What it does NOT license

**The gate frame does not repeal the B2B buyer law.** Almost every machine gate sits inside a
business process, and 28 B2B models have produced zero survivors. A gate candidate still has to
survive: the buyer bringing a negotiation, a diligence, an incumbent and a $5–50/month price anchor.

**It also does not repeal Law 1.** If the gate is important, someone funded is probably giving away
the tool that clears it, as lead generation.

**And a convergence worth knowing about:** run the gate frame honestly and it produces *preparing a
file that a machine will accept or reject.* Candidate 66 already has that shape. That is a fact
about the frame — the stampless residue of a compliance business is always data preparation against
a gate — not evidence that 66 is the answer.

## 6. The unexploited pricing mechanism

Separate from the gate, and possibly worth more.

**Every price in this file across sixty-seven candidates is set cost-plus or against a substitute.
Not one is set against what the buyer loses.** The $33.600 was unnegotiated because it was priced
against the downside.

Absorbing a **liability** downside requires the stamp. But there is a downside that requires no
stamp and no credential:

> **A MISSED WINDOW. A launch date, a season, a filing deadline, a shipping cut-off. Nobody signs an
> indemnity for speed — you either hit the date or you do not, and the buyer can see which. That is
> a downside a faceless seller can be paid to close.**

Untested. Recorded as a mechanism, not a finding.
