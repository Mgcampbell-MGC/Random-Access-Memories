# PERMITCLOSE — assessed, 17 Aug 2026

**Municipal construction-deposit recovery. Externally proposed, scored 9.0/10 by its author.**

> **Verdict: a real business, well reasoned, factually accurate on its headline claims — and it
> produces two new laws for this file, both of which kill it. Not for her, and not mainly because of
> her constraints.**

---

## 1. What was verified, and it holds up

Credit where it is due. The load-bearing facts were checked against primary sources and **most of
them are correct**, which is rare for an externally supplied proposal in this record.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| AB 2559 enacted, approved 16 Jul 2026 | **TRUE.** Chapter 97, Statutes of 2026. Adds Art. 3 (§ 48030) to Ch. 2, Pt. 7, Div. 30, Public Resources Code |
| Three-year window from final inspection | **TRUE.** § 48030(b): *"if, within three years of the final inspection on the work or project that was subject to the deposit…"* |
| Refund deniable on documented noncompliance | **TRUE.** § 48030(c) |
| Pasadena: $10,8M in the deposit account at 30 Sep 2020 | **TRUE.** City audit, *C&D Waste Program Needs Improvement* |
| $7,5M retained for future refunds, $3,33M escheatable, $628k needing research | **TRUE**, same audit |
| ~1.170 deposit-bearing permits/year 2015–2019 | **TRUE** |
| Non-attorney contingency fees for refund recovery | **Probably lawful.** The tax-consultant analogue (cost segregation, R&D credit, property-tax appeal) is established and uncapped |

**And its central instinct is right.** *"I moved away from businesses whose value can be demonstrated
entirely in a browser"* is a genuine advance over UNITLEASE and over most of what this file has
considered. The payer rule, the refusal to take custody of client money, *"AI may explain a result.
It cannot alter a weight,"* and the numeric kill gates are all better discipline than the average
candidate here has had.

### One factual correction

**The statute is not operative yet.** A non-urgency bill chaptered 16 Jul 2026 takes effect
**1 January 2027** — Cal. Const. art. IV, § 8(c)(1): a statute enacted at a regular session goes
into effect on the January 1 following a 90-day period from enactment. The proposal's entire
section 1 is titled *"Why this opportunity exists **now**."* It exists in four and a half months, and
until then the historical wedge rests on local ordinances rather than on AB 2559 — which is exactly
the retroactivity question the proposal correctly flags but then markets past.

## 2. THE UGLY-WORK LAW — the proposal's central error, and it is new to this file

The thesis, stated plainly in section 3: *tech founders prefer clean software problems, therefore
operational ugliness is a moat.*

**That is false, and it is false in a specific and instructive way.**

Operational ugliness is not an unoccupied niche. It is the **core product of the offshore staffing
industry** — mature, enormous, and structurally cheaper than she can ever be. Verified rates:
construction virtual assistants at **$5–15/hour**; a full-time Filipino operator at **$800–1.200/month**
in contractor pay.

And it is not adjacent. **"Permit expediting outsourcing" is a named, marketed SKU** with published
savings claims covering *"application assembly, submission tracking, plan check response
coordination, and approval documentation management"* — 60–70% cost reduction. That is PERMITCLOSE's
Evidence Factory and Filing cell, sold by name, today, by companies with recruiting pipelines.

> **THE UGLY-WORK LAW — work that is too operationally messy for software is, for that exact reason,
> the core market of the labour-arbitrage industry. Choosing ugliness to escape software competition
> does not remove the competitor. It changes which industry the competitor comes from — and trades a
> competitor who might never arrive for one who is already there, advertising your SKU, at a price
> you cannot match.**

The proposal spends a full section on why tech bros won't come. **They were never the threat.**
ShoreAgents was.

**Corollary, and it generalises past this candidate:** every future proposal that justifies itself
with *"this is too messy/manual/unglamorous for software founders"* must now also answer *"and why
is it not already being done at $8/hour by people who do exactly this?"* That question has no good
answer for any pure document-shuffling business.

## 3. THE MOAT-IS-A-CONTRACT TEST — the second new law

Section 2 closes with: *"That inconsistency is not a defect in the business model. It is the moat."*

Twelve municipalities, twelve deposit formulas, twelve portals, twelve deadlines. True today. But
**Green Halo Systems is a funded vendor being paid by cities to eliminate precisely that
inconsistency, jurisdiction by jurisdiction** — and where cities adopt it, they *mandate* it:

- **All** building and demolition projects in unincorporated **Sonoma County** are required to use it
- Many **Alameda County** jurisdictions; **RecycleSmart** member cities
- **Lafayette, Fremont, Lakewood, Pinole** and others publish step-by-step Green Halo instructions
- The plan is **submitted via Green Halo at permit application and must be finalised before the
  permit is closed**

Read what that sentence does to the business. In a Green Halo jurisdiction the waste-management plan
is already in the system, the tickets are uploaded during construction rather than reconstructed
years later, the diversion percentage is computed by the platform, and **the permit cannot close
without it**. That deletes workstations 8, 9 and 10 — Ticket Recovery/OCR, Compliance Reconciliation,
and the Deterministic Diversion Engine — three of the twelve, and the three that carry the skill.

It is free to the contractor because the city pays.

> **THE MOAT-IS-A-CONTRACT TEST — before calling market fragmentation a moat, name who is being paid
> to eliminate it. Fragmentation maintained by nobody's interest is a moat. Fragmentation that a
> funded vendor is winning municipal contracts to remove is not a moat — it is a countdown, and the
> business inherits whatever is left of it.**

This is the same shape as **LAW 1** (if the gate matters, someone funded is giving away the tool that
clears it) but on the supply side rather than the demand side. Law 1 asks who is giving away the
solution. This asks who is being paid to abolish the problem.

## 4. THE ARBITRAGE INVERSION — the discovery engine points the wrong way

The whole customer-acquisition machine (Cell B: Permit Harvester, Closeout Trigger Monitor, Entity
Resolver) harvests public permit records to **infer that a refundable deposit probably existed**.

But a construction deposit is **a cheque the contractor wrote and recorded in their own books.** It
is a line item in their accounting system with a date, an amount, and a payee.

> **The information is on the buyer's side of the table.** She would be selling a $395 guess to
> someone holding the actual receipt.

The proposal concedes this in its own guardrail: *"The system must never claim an exact deposit
amount from a public-record estimate. The amount is verified using the municipal receipt, permit fee
statement, accounting ledger, or agency confirmation."* Every one of those four sources is **the
customer's own document**.

That inverts **THE ARBITRAGE LAW**. She must know something the buyer cannot cheaply learn. Here she
knows strictly less: a maybe, versus their ledger.

The behavioural fact — that thousands of deposits genuinely go unclaimed, per the Pasadena audit — is
real, and it is not an information problem. It is an **attention** problem. Nobody cared enough to
run the report. Selling attention is a different and much worse business than selling information,
because the buyer's alternative is free and takes an afternoon.

> **THE INVERSION CHECK, to run on every list-based candidate: is the fact I am selling on my side of
> the table or the buyer's? If the buyer holds the primary record, I am not an arbitrageur. I am a
> reminder.**

This retroactively explains the trigger-list corpses. Every one of them sold the buyer a fact the
buyer's own files already contained.

## 5. The Stamp Law — the proposal finds it, names it, and then buys it

Section 16, on founder fit: credibility comes from *"a qualified closeout specialist."* Section 10
recruits a **fractional California closeout director** who *"provides professional credibility"* and
*"handles difficult municipal conversations."* Section 9 requires that *"a trained human approves
every filing during the initial operating period."*

**That is the Stamp Law, correctly identified and then solved by purchase.** The test stands: *name
the person who gets sued if this is wrong.* The proposal names them — and puts them on payroll.

## 6. Her constraints, counted

Not the main argument, but it is not close.

| | Constraint | What the proposal requires |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | Never speaks to anyone, ever | § 10: *"A part-time U.S.-based person handles **telephone calls**, mailed forms, **check-status inquiries**."* § 10: the director *"handles difficult **municipal conversations**."* And the Santa Monica claim form itself requires a **telephone number** |
| **C3** | No employees or contractors, ever | **Six hired roles**: fractional closeout director, two case analysts, U.S. municipal liaison, California attorney, local field vendors |
| **C4** | Never standing access to a customer's system | Closeout Guard *"prepares portal entries"* and maintains the client's ticket uploads — Green Halo and municipal portal accounts, continuously |
| **C5** | A few hundred dollars, one time | § 14, days 1–14: *"Retain California counsel."* A California municipal/construction attorney is $400–700/hour. § 7 carries **$3.500–4.500/month fixed overhead** — 15× her entire lifetime budget, monthly |
| **C6** | ~30 h/week | 15–25 h/week for her, **on top of** a team doing the actual work |
| **Veto** | **Construction as a buyer segment** | The buyer is a California general contractor. This is the single most-vetoed segment in the file |

Two internal contradictions worth noting, because they matter for how much weight to give the
scorecard: **"Capital efficiency 9.6"** sits in the same document as $3.500–4.500/month fixed
overhead and retained counsel, and **"Founder independence 9.1"** sits in the same document as six
hired roles and a phone-based liaison. Revenue arrives at 85–145 days, with months 1–3 *"close to
zero."*

And a tell: § 16 opens *"Her age is not the core issue."* Nobody raised her age. That is the second
externally supplied proposal to rebut an objection nobody made while missing C1 and C3 entirely —
a reliable sign the constraint set never reached the author.

## 7. Trying to make it work — five attempts

The obligation was to try, not to dismiss. Each of these was built and then broken.

| | Version | Why it dies |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Sell only the list.** No filing, no percentage, no phone. $395 for "these 40 permits may have refundable deposits" | § 4, the inversion. Plus this is the trigger-list corpse, which has died three times in this file on the same missing element: an actionable row |
| **2** | **Sell to the municipality.** Pasadena needed $628k of transactions researched; cities are drowning in unreconciled deposit accounts | Government procurement, RFP, no cold-email path, foreign natural person with no US entity. Dead on contact |
| **3** | **Jurisdiction closeout kit**, $149 — the form, the approved-facility list, the ticket-field checklist, the deadline | The city publishes the form free. Template tier, $20–50 against free. Law 1 |
| **4** | **White-label to permit expediters** — the user's own distribution insight, applied. They own the contractor and have no post-completion product | She becomes the back office, competing at the exact SKU offshore staffing advertises, at $5–15/hour. § 2 |
| **5** | **The escheatment claim service** — see below. The best idea in the vicinity, and the proposal missed it completely | Five reasons, below |

### Attempt 5 in full, because it deserved it

The proposal never mentions escheatment, and escheatment is where the actual asset is.

**Gov. Code §§ 50050–50057**: money not belonging to a local agency that stays unclaimed for three
years becomes the agency's property **after notice**. The notice must be published in a newspaper of
general circulation, once a week for two successive weeks, and the funds vest **not less than 45 nor
more than 60 days** after first publication. Santa Monica runs a dedicated process for exactly this
class of money — *"How to Claim Unclaimed Construction and Demolition Property."*

And here is the part that made it worth an hour: **Santa Monica's claim requires only** *"the
claimant's name, address, telephone number, amount of claim, and the grounds on which the claim is
founded."* **No weight tickets. No diversion calculation. No Green Halo.** The entire Evidence
Factory — the expensive, ugly, three-workstation core of PERMITCLOSE — is not required to claim a
deposit that is about to escheat. It is a different and vastly simpler legal act.

So: monitor city notices, match payee names to reachable contractors, write to them, file a
five-field claim. A dated deadline, a named person, a specific amount, and real money.

**Why it still dies:**

1. **There is no list to buy.** Santa Monica's page publishes the *process*, not the *inventory* —
   the form comes from a named staff member on request. The list exists 500 times over, behind 500
   individual email requests and CPRA responses, and cities are under no duty to hand a foreign
   stranger their unclaimed-payee roll promptly.
2. **The window is 45–60 days.** Inside it she must find the notice, obtain the list, resolve the
   entity, locate a current contact for a firm whose project ended 3+ years ago and which is often a
   dissolved LLC, obtain signed authority, and file. At scale. In writing only.
3. **CCP § 1582 caps locator compensation at 10%** of property returned, inclusive of all services.
   On a $10.000 deposit that is $1.000 — workable, but it is a hard statutory ceiling on a business
   whose acquisition cost is entity resolution against dead companies.
4. **The claim form requires a telephone number**, and the process runs through named city staff who
   respond by phone. C1.
5. **Law 1, and this one is decisive.** Paid recovery of unclaimed funds is not whitespace — it is a
   **licensed industry**. The California State Controller licenses "Investigators," publishes an
   Investigator Handbook and forms, and maintains consumer-facing pages about them. They already work
   local-agency unclaimed funds. The niche is occupied by people with licences, US presence, and
   telephones.

**What survives from attempt 5, and it is genuinely worth keeping:**

> **THE FORCED-PUBLICATION LIST SOURCE — when a statute compels a party to publish a list of people
> it owes money to, by a deadline, that publication is a free, named, dated, dollar-denominated list
> with a built-in urgency clock. Gov. Code § 50050 is one instance. The mechanism is general and this
> file has never systematically hunted for it.**

Other instances to look for later: unclaimed dividends and demutualisation proceeds, court-held
funds (Contra Costa and Solano courts both publish escheat notices), utility deposits, class-action
residuals, abandoned retirement accounts. The pattern is *a law that forces a debtor to advertise its
debts.* Recorded as a search instruction, not a candidate.

## 8. Verdict

**PERMITCLOSE is a real business and someone should build it.** It is better reasoned than most of
what this file has evaluated, its headline facts survive verification, and its instinct to flee
browser-demonstrable value is correct.

**The founder it fits** is California-based, has a construction-administration background or can
borrow one, has $50–100k, can hire three people, and can pick up a telephone. That is not a criticism
of the plan. It is a description of the plan.

**What it is not** is a business for a faceless solo operator in São Paulo with $300, no phone, no
employees, and a standing veto on construction buyers. And the two laws it produced — **the ugly-work
law** and **the moat-is-a-contract test** — would have killed it for anyone building it as an
information-arbitrage play, constraints or no constraints. The moat is a shrinking inventory of
un-won municipal software contracts, and the labour is a commodity priced at $8/hour by an industry
that advertises this exact SKU.

### What goes into the permanent filter

1. **THE UGLY-WORK LAW** and its corollary question — *why is this not already being done at $8/hour?*
2. **THE MOAT-IS-A-CONTRACT TEST** — name who is paid to eliminate the fragmentation.
3. **THE INVERSION CHECK** — is the fact on my side of the table or the buyer's?
4. **THE FORCED-PUBLICATION LIST SOURCE** — a hunting instruction, not a candidate.
