## 2. The Airtight Niche & Offer

### The micro-niche

**Done-for-you AI-drafted, human-QA'd federal proposal production for small and set-aside US government contractors ($3M–$75M revenue) with no in-house proposal staff.** RECOMPETE sells finished, compliant proposal drafts — not software seats. An agentic pipeline monitors SAM.gov, shreds RFPs into compliance matrices, drafts full volumes grounded in each client's own past-performance corpus, and runs adversarial color-team review; a named human proposal engineer QAs every deliverable.

### The buyer

The CEO or lone BD lead of a US federal/SLED services contractor: NAICS 541512, 541611, engineering/logistics/facilities, diversifying to 9–10 codes by month 18. Purchase triggers: own contract expiring ≤270 days; first-ever recompete; 8(a) graduation window (~1,200–1,500 firms within 3 years of graduation above $3M revenue — a beachhead supplying at most 15–20% of the month-36 base); SDVOSB verification; new MAC/IDIQ on-ramp. CUI-heavy pursuits are screened out at intake and at corpus onboarding.

### The quantified pain

- B&P costs run **1–4% of contract value**; **150–400 hours per bid**.
- Consultants charge **$10k–$40k per bid**; the real alternative is a $300/mo tool plus the owner's nights.
- Firms self-throttle to **6–10 bids/yr**; one missed Section L "shall" is disqualification.
- Honest market sizing: of ~30–40k "actively bidding" firms, segment (a) — current consultant-payers — is ~6–9k firms; segment (b) — tool-users with demonstrated bid volume — is ~12–18k. The subscription sells to (a) and upper (b): a **true-payer SAM of ~9–14k firms**. 550 subscribers is 4–6% penetration of actual payers.

### The offer — marketed on controllable outcomes

Because the whole AI-proposal category pushes more submissions into a fixed solicitation pool, per-bid win rates fall category-wide. RECOMPETE therefore contracts on what it controls: **100% on-time submission with zero disqualifications, recompete-capture readiness, and 150+ owner-hours returned per quarter** — never "wins." Bid/no-bid discipline is a headline feature: **~15% of requested pursuits are declined** when they can't be grounded in the client's corpus. Deliverables: recompete radar; compliance matrix + outline next business day; full draft in 3 business days from queue entry; every factual claim cited to the client's corpus, every forward-looking claim flagged for client attestation; Pink/Red/Gold color passes scored against Section M; named reviewer and dedicated account manager per account.

### Eliminated candidates

- **OVERTURN — Autonomous Denial-Appeals Recovery Engine.** Eliminated on capital and adversary risk. Its own capital critique was arithmetic, not opinion: a $60k GTM line asked to buy accounts costing $200–300k at its own stated CAC; contingency-only design partners against a true 120–180-day contract-to-cash cycle its $35k buffer could not bridge; unbudgeted QA-coder and implementation labor. Honest re-underwriting put cash-flow positive at month 16–20 and made $1M/mo by month 36 a "coin-flip stretch goal." It also sits in the most crowded AI category in US healthcare (Waystar, Epic Penny, RapidClaims, Adonis, R1, Commure), with an inverted data moat and a revenue counterparty (payers) who is an adversary controlling the load-bearing overturn rate.
- **DUNN — Autonomous First-Party Invoice-to-Cash (AR) Department.** Eliminated on a hidden-human-army catastrophic flaw plus the weakest year-1 cash bridge. Promising cash application and three-way matching for ~320k invoices with 2 exception leads — when decade-tuned incumbents achieve only 80–90% straight-through *with* offshore exception teams — implied 20–40 unbudgeted ops humans, destroying the 93% margin claim and the non-linear-scaling constraint. Month-10 "MRR" blended one-time fees; stated CAC contradicted its own rev-share math; its own critic concluded it needed a $250–400k extension before month 6, effectively violating the $250k constraint — with Intuit's near-free native agent sitting inside its flagship channel.

**Why RECOMPETE won:** it is the only finalist whose worst-case year 1 fits inside $250k while the repaired month-36 math remains within striking distance of the target — B&P is a pre-budgeted, FAR-31.205-18-recognized expense line; annual contracts billed quarterly upfront generate float; the TAM is enumerable from free SAM.gov/FPDS data; and paid pilots produce revenue by month 2–3. No build-vs-buy violation.

## 3. The AI Infrastructure Stack

**Design principle:** one Temporal spine runs every proposal as a durable DAG; every inference dollar, QA minute, and client edit is metered per proposal ID. Client-edit-distance and QA-hours per deliverable are kill/scale signals from day 1 — quality is measured, not asserted.

| Layer | Tooling | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Orchestration | Temporal + Python workers | Amendments = re-shred + diff |
| Ingestion | Two independent extraction passes (Sonnet + rules/Haiku); human adjudicates diffs | Plus a **document-completeness gate**: SAM.gov attachment manifest and amendment list reconciled against ingested files; incorporated-by-reference clauses (e.g., FAR 52.252-2) expanded; matrix delivery **blocks** on any unresolved reference. Recall published stratified (clean/scanned/amended); low-confidence packages route to a mandatory billed full human pass |
| Retrieval | Postgres + pgvector, row-level security, per-client keys | Cross-client learning limited to public agency/FPDS patterns |
| Routing | LiteLLM-style router; second provider warm at 10% traffic | Migration = a budgeted 2–4-week quality-regression cycle, not "days" |
| Data terms | Launch on standard API no-training terms; contractual ZDR negotiated by the SOC 2 milestone (mo 9); ZDR marketing gated on the executed agreement | |
| Evals | Golden set of 100+ real solicitations; provenance checker; AI-use-clause detector | |
| Monitoring | SAM.gov daily bulk extracts + FPDS mirrors | |

### Fulfillment cost arithmetic — inference per full proposal, no batch on the SLA path

Every stage on a client deliverable runs real-time; batch is permitted only off the critical path (golden-set regression runs, corpus indexing), because batch windows can consume 48 of a 72-hour draft promise.

| Stage | Cost |
|---|---|
| Triage (Haiku) | $0.25 |
| Dual extraction (Sonnet ×2) | $3.60 |
| Drafting (Sonnet, corpus cached; corpus-dependent calls batched per stage inside one cache window; cache-write premiums included) | $24.00 |
| Pink team (Sonnet, real-time) | $42.00 |
| Red team (Opus, real-time) | $55.00 |
| Gold pass (Opus) | $22.50 |
| Deterministic shall/page/FAR checks | $3.00 |
| 2 revision rounds (cached) | $10.50 |
| **Subtotal** | **$161** |
| +30% retries/fallbacks | **≈$209** |

**Planning number: $250/proposal (year 1) → $220 (mature).** Pad discipline, stated so it isn't double-spent: the $209→$250 pad absorbs *either* full cache-miss degradation (worst case +$27) *or* ~1.2x provider price drift — **not** a 2x price shock; a sustained 2x shock is a separate sensitivity with a model-substitution response.

### Human-in-the-loop

QA per deliverable: full draft 4.0 hrs (includes a 30-min client walkthrough), task order 1.5, matrix 0.5 → **blended 3.0 hrs including rework allowance**, falling to **2.4 hrs** only via three named, individually measured levers: confidence-ranked review queues (−20 min), diff-only revision review (−15 min), automated provenance/citation verification (−10 min) — gated on two consecutive quarters of measured QA-minutes. Ratios: **1:12 accounts year 1 → 1:15 mature at 78% target utilization** (109 of 140 productive hrs) — deliberate slack for a seasonal business. All non-technical client contact is owned by a pooled AM layer; QA engineers are shielded. Support is agent-first (<15% escape rate, time-to-human gated).

## 6. The Risk Mitigation Protocol

### Core failure modes and backup strategies

1. **Seasonal demand vs SLAs** (federal Q4 clusters 1.5–1.6x): staffed to P90, not mean; 78% target utilization; pre-contracted 1099 bench; published queue-priority policy; SLA keyed to visible business-day queue position.
2. **Draft quality physics:** quality is a Block-1 spend gate measured before GTM scales; QA-hours and client-edit-distance metered from day 1; rework scenario priced (≈$850k).
3. **Same-RFP collisions at density:** exclusivity applies only to a client's own incumbent recompete (structurally collision-free); open competitions default to disclosed dual-service with firewalled reviewers; per-NAICS density caps; collision churn carried as 0.4 pts of the 2.9% budget.
4. **Extraction misses:** dual extraction + adjudication + document-completeness gate; stratified recall published; billed human pass on low-confidence packages.
5. **Category win-rate saturation:** contracts on controllable outcomes; win-rate-collapse modeled (churn 3.8% → ≈$780k).
6. **CUI in the corpus:** dual-pass classification + human adjudication; GovCloud trigger on any flagged incident; CMMC L2 re-costed at $100–150k with a month-18 fund-or-scope-out decision.
7. **FCA exposure:** reasonable-procedures record; quantitative claims extracted verbatim only with source pinning, never generated; client indemnity for client-supplied facts.
8. **AI-use solicitation clauses:** clause detection in the shred; bid/no-bid flag + client-counsel notice + human-authorship-review tier.
9. **Outbound platform shutdown:** dedicated compliant infra, redundant pools, 6-week outage in the bear case, referrals live month 6.
10. **Model price/deprecation:** router, regression harness, second provider warm, substitution plan for a 2x shock.
11. **Key person/closer transition:** underwritten close rates with floors, playbook by mo 6, key-person insurance at first outside hire.

### Residual major flaws from final certification — explicitly acknowledged and mitigated

These 22 majors survived Round 2. None is a plan-breaker; each is carried as a monitored risk with a named countermeasure.

1. **Referral channel rests on an unbuilt 80-partner network with partially adverse incentives** (16 of 44 adds). *Mitigation:* underwrite the partner funnel like outbound (outreach → signed → activated, power-law production); pull the partner manager to month 12; Block-4 gate on measured intros-per-active-partner; redesign consultant/CPA economics toward white-label/subcontract so partners keep the client relationship. At half-yield, month 36 lands ~450 subs/~$780k — inside the bear case.
2. **À-la-carte flywheel is load-bearing but its demand engine is under-specified.** *Mitigation:* decompose acquisition (Recompete Radar conversion, SEO, paid CPC) with buyer-stock flow accounting; underwrite the upgrade rate at 3%/mo base / 7% bull; redesign onboarding as "guided first purchase" with costed human-assisted corpus setup.
3. **Base case holds $4,200 ARPA and $6.1k CAC despite assumed month-12 entrant arrival.** *Mitigation:* adopt a competition-adjusted planning case (ARPA glide to $3,800–3,900, CAC drift to $8–9k, close rates −5 pts post-entrant); treat month-36 $998k as the upside where differentiation fully holds; add a joint ARPA+CAC stacked sensitivity.
4. **Willingness-to-pay evidence conflates capture consulting with volume drafting.** *Mitigation:* the design-partner phase becomes a pre-registered WTP experiment (X of 12 converting to annual at list price); report the true-payer SAM in episodic vs. annual-commitment sub-bands; re-derive the 45/40/15 tier mix from pilot data before it drives ARPA.
5. **30% annual churn in a small, reputation-dense community will degrade the referral GTM it feeds.** *Mitigation:* model the cross-term (referral yield as a declining function of churned logos per NAICS territory); formal offboarding program (exit QBR, prorated wind-down, keep-the-radar-free); ex-customer NPS gated alongside collision rate in Block 5.
6. **Block-1 quality gate cannot fully resolve on its own timeline; sample too thin.** *Mitigation:* split the gate — month 5 measures acceptance, rework hours, QA minutes, zero matrix escapes on ≥25 deliverables; a second explicit month-9/10 gate certifies award-rate parity once cohort-1 outcomes exist, with closer hiring conditioned on it.
7. **À-la-carte COGS uses blended unit cost for all-full-proposal work; 66% GM overstated; month-36 QA short ~2 FTE.** *Mitigation:* restate à-la-carte GM at ~52% year 1 → ~60% mature; add ~2 QA FTE (net ≈ $970k) or raise à-la-carte to $1,650 to preserve margin. Path to ~$1M survives with a 1–2 month slip.
8. **Q4 peak inference capacity/SLA exposure — 10%-warm secondary can't absorb primary throttling.** *Mitigation:* negotiated enterprise throughput commitments before ~5B tokens/mo; pre-contracted burst allocation with the secondary at ≥60% of P90 Q4 daily peak; quarterly full-traffic failover drills; force-majeure/queue-pause SLA clause.
9. **Correlated extraction misses bypass dual-pass adjudication; 100-solicitation golden set can't certify 98% recall at disqualification stakes.** *Mitigation:* third structurally-decorrelated pass on high-stakes sections (inverse-form requirement enumeration); recall stratified by requirement type; golden set grown from every production adjudication; mandatory full human pass above an incorporated-by-reference threshold.
10. **Months 6–14 carry zero engineering cost during the highest-iteration phase.** *Mitigation:* declare the engineer of record — either a founder (with sales/QA hours re-budgeted) or a retained part-time contractor (~$6–12k/mo) added to the cash curve; if the bear-case trough goes negative, shrink SOC 2 timing or the conference line to restore buffer.
11. **À-la-carte buyer-replenishment math never shown** (sustaining 230 actives needs ~25–35 new buyers/mo on $18k). *Mitigation:* pool-flow model (inflow, conversion + lapse outflow, steady-state actives) with an underwritten per-buyer CAC and a named channel (Recompete Radar lead magnet); sensitivity row for actives stalling at 50% of plan.
12. **"Net profit" is pre-tax by definitional choice; strict post-tax reading is missed by ~5 months.** *Mitigation:* disclosed openly (post-tax ≈ $750k at month 36; $1M post-tax ~month 41); build 5–10% headroom into the month-36 target (e.g., 580 subscribers or ~$4,300 ARPA via +8% monthly-billing uptake) so pre-tax $1M holds against minor leakage.
13. **E&O cannot actually stand behind the FCA tail** (standard tech E&O excludes fines, government investigations, qui tam). *Mitigation:* stop describing E&O as the FCA backstop; obtain a specimen policy in months 2–3 and map each risk to covered/excluded; lean on reasonable-procedures documentation, verbatim-only quantitative claims, attestation, and liability caps plus an explicit reserve; budget $100–150k/yr insurance at month 36 in the sensitivity table rather than $84k.
14. **Provenance of the outbound contact universe unstated; SAM.gov POC data carries use restrictions.** *Mitigation:* explicit data architecture — FPDS/SAM public data supplies only the *trigger*; contact identities come exclusively from licensed commercial B2B vendors under marketing-permitted terms; SAM POC fields never enter the sending system; opted-out-entity suppression.
15. **Dual-service collisions create Procurement Integrity Act and trade-secret exposure firewalls alone don't retire.** *Mitigation:* auditable protocol — written firewall attestations per collision; hard assignment-system blocks (no reviewer or 1099 touches both sides of a live solicitation); verified prompt-cache/index isolation; PIA training in the QA bootcamp; mutual informed consent in the MSA at signing; solicitation-level exclusivity sold as an upsell.
16. **The permitted-cold-email ESP tier is thinner and lower-quality than implied.** *Mitigation:* name the provider tier; underwrite replies net of an inbox-placement haircut (model 60–75% placement on M365 tenants, not 95%+); two unrelated cold-infra providers on unrelated IP ranges from day 1; seed-account placement monitoring gated alongside the 0.3% spam governor.
17. **À-la-carte self-serve has the weakest CUI/ITAR intake gate.** *Mitigation:* dual-pass CUI/ITAR classifier runs pre-inference on every self-serve upload with automatic quarantine (no model call) until human adjudication; defense-NAICS detection at checkout forces human intake; ITAR added explicitly to both the classifier taxonomy and the rep-and-warranty.
18. **3.0 deliverables/mo consumption contradicts the ICP's 6–10 bids/yr tempo — the largest unpriced churn driver.** *Mitigation:* underwrite utilization from measured design-partner cohort data before tier scaling; add a consumption-DOWN sensitivity (1.5/mo → step-down cascade → effective ARPA); restructure Core as banked credits so low-tempo quarters don't read as "paying for nothing" at renewal.
19. **Q4 surge bench under-sized ~2x, sourced from a pool that craters in Jul–Sep.** *Mitigation:* re-size to ~20–25 reviewers on contractual minimum-availability retainers (retainer in COGS year-round); raise the accrual to ~$30–35k/mo; cap core-team peak utilization at ~90%; shift residual peak absorption into the published queue-priority policy with explicit peak-season SLA terms.
20. **QA hiring engine can't produce heads at the months-26–36 rate; attrition unmodeled.** *Mitigation:* model 15–20%/yr QA attrition; size recruiting to ~3 hires/mo in months 24–36; rolling monthly onboarding or 8–10-person cohorts with a dedicated trainer (not the client-facing leads); extend the hiring trigger to a 16-week pipeline-weighted forecast.
21. **1:79 AM ratio is 1.5–2.5x thinner than benchmark for a $50k-ACV service where renewal defense carries the churn assumption.** *Mitigation:* staff CS/AM at ~1:45–55 (10–12 AMs, ~$120–135k/mo, accepting ~$45–55k/mo lower net) or explicitly segment — AM-led for Growth/Scale, digital-led QBRs for Core — and validate that churn holds in the digital-led segment.
22. **À-la-carte fulfillment ops unmodeled for a channel supplying 16% of revenue and 36% of subscription adds.** *Mitigation:* price first-order onboarding explicitly (setup fee or +$100–150 unit COGS with a relaxed first-order SLA); underwrite the à-la-carte funnel with the same pass-by-pass rigor as subscription channels; make 230 actives a modeled output, not an assumption.
