## 2. THE UPGRADE THAT MATTERS — it defeats the verifiability law

My candidate 71 was *"she writes the narrative."* Prose is unverifiable, which is why prose commoditises.

**RISKSTORY ships two documents instead of one:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Carrier Pack** | Concise, polished, agency-branded — the readable artefact |
| **Broker Proof Sheet** | **Every material statement paired with its source file and page.** Contradictions made visible rather than silently resolved. Amounts reconciled across application, loss run and notes. **Unsupported claims excluded and listed in a "not used" section.** Source hashes and document versions |

> **THE VERIFIABILITY LAW asks: after the buyer opens the file, can they tell whether it is right? With a
> proof sheet, YES — and that converts unverifiable prose into a checkable artefact.**

**This is the single best idea in the document, and it generalises to every factual written deliverable in
this file.**

**And the editorial rule underneath it is clean:** *"Persuasion is permitted only through truthful
organization and documented context. **Unknown means unknown. Missing evidence means the statement does not
appear.**"*

## 3. Four more parts worth keeping

### P1 · THE GAP CARD — max five questions, one round

> *"A file that requires fifteen separate emails is not Green. RISKSTORY consolidates the five most material
> questions into one broker form."*

**This is the mechanism that makes written-only delivery survive at volume.** An unbounded email thread is
what kills asynchronous service work; a hard question cap converts it into one bounded exchange.
**Directly usable in candidates 70 and 72.**

### P2 · THE SLOT MODEL — sell reserved capacity, not projects

$1.500/month for four slots · $2.800 for eight · $5.200 for sixteen, with **limited 30-day rollover and no
unlimited-work plan.** Prepaid monthly.

> **Converts a project business into a subscription without promising unlimited work.** Candidate 70's
> funeral homes and 72's celebrants could both be sold this way, and it fixes the lumpy-revenue problem in
> both.

### P3 · THE TWO-LEVEL PASSPORT — better than my one level

| **Agency Voice Passport** | Style, tone, structure, approved and prohibited phrases — **per partner** |
| **Account Risk Passport** | Facts, loss chronology, controls, versions, **next expiration date** — **per end client** |

**Style memory makes each new PARTNER cheaper. Fact memory makes each RENEWAL cheaper.** I had only the
first. The second is what creates renewal recurrence, and it is the better half.

### P4 · GREEN / AMBER / RED, with a numeric slot definition

One insured · 1–5 locations · ≤3 material losses · **≤150 source pages** · one clarification cycle · one
revision. **A numeric definition of "one unit of work"** — which is the thing every candidate in this file
has lacked, and the reason estimates drift.

**Plus, worth noting:** *"Privacy-Max vs Renewal-Memory"* as a **customer-selected retention mode** turns a
constraint into a product option. And the **hard 80-slot solo ceiling** — *"'More revenue' is not success if
the owner becomes the permanent exception desk"* — is exactly right.

## 4. Where it breaks — three structural failures

### FAILURE 1 · C5, and the document states it plainly

**Its own number: $5.000–13.000 cumulative to launch**, with $1.900–5.100 before a single live file.

**Her constraint is a few hundred US dollars, one time. This is 10–40× over.**

And the Gate 0 budget is not credible on its own terms. It bundles an **Ohio insurance-regulatory scope
opinion** — covering deliverables, communications, compensation *and* approval workflow — together with
sample creation, a landing page, a secure workspace month and a 100-account outreach test, for
$1.900–5.100. **Ohio insurance regulatory counsel is not priced that way.** The memo alone plausibly
consumes the whole range.

### FAILURE 2 · THE ONBOARDING LAW — today's law, and this is its test case

The entire document is built on written-only, no calls. **But every gate it must pass is a conversation:**

| Gate | Why it is a conversation |
|---|---|
| **Ohio scope opinion** | Regulatory counsel engagements start with a call |
| **E&O / cyber binding** | Underwriters ask questions of a novel risk |
| **Agency security diligence** | **NAIC Model 668** makes her a *third-party service provider* and requires the agency to exercise due diligence. **A vendor security review is a conversation** |
| **Payment processor onboarding** | Risk review |

**The blueprint anticipates this** — it wants *"a diligence packet a small agency can review without a
month-long enterprise process"* — **but a packet is an answer to a review, not a replacement for one.**

> **Written-only is compatible with SELLING. It is not compatible with BEING ONBOARDED. This blueprint sells
> in writing beautifully and is onboarded four separate times by voice.**

### FAILURE 3 · The insurance is probably not obtainable

The blueprint budgets *"E&O/cyber/accounting/legal reserve $300–900/month"* and instructs her to obtain
technology E&O, cyber and general liability quotes.

**LC 126/2007 art. 20, parágrafo único:** *"**Pessoas jurídicas** poderão contratar seguro no exterior para
cobertura de riscos no exterior."* **Legal entities may buy foreign cover for foreign risks. Natural persons
may not.**

- As a **pessoa física** she is statutorily barred from the foreign policy.
- Forming a **Brazilian company** fixes that leg — and a SUSEP-regulated insurer is **not admitted in Ohio
  and will not carry an A.M. Best rating** a US vendor-onboarding process accepts.

**This is the identical pincer that killed merchant-of-record today**, arriving from the same statute.
*(Softer here than for goods — a small agency may never demand a certificate — but NAIC Model 668 diligence
pushes the other way, and the blueprint's own kill gate #11 names it.)*

### And the licensing gate is a genuine coin-flip that must be paid for first

**Ohio Revised Code 3905** requires a licence to *sell, solicit or negotiate*. **The blueprint is honest
that the clerical exemptions attach to EMPLOYEES of insurers and agents, not to independent vendors** — and
correctly refuses to assume it is exempt. That honesty is to its credit.

**But it means thousands of dollars of counsel must be spent before it is known whether the business is
legal at all.** This file has been killed by exactly this class of question three times — PRUMO on CFC Res.
1.640/2021, Recurso on OAB Lei 8.906/94 art. 1º II, and trademark maintenance on 37 CFR 11.14(b).

## 6. Verdict

**A real business, correctly designed, for a founder with $10.000 and a telephone.**

**The upgrade from candidate 71 is genuine and I am adopting it:** the product is not the narrative, it is
**the narrative plus the proof sheet** — an artefact whose correctness the buyer can check. That defeats the
verifiability law, and it is the first time anything in this file has.

**What to keep regardless of whether this specific business is ever built:**

1. **The proof sheet** — source-linked, contradictions surfaced, unsupported claims listed as *not used*
2. **The Gap Card** — one round, five questions maximum
3. **The slot model** — reserved monthly capacity with limited rollover
4. **The two-level passport** — style per partner, facts per end client
5. **The numeric unit definition** — Green/Amber/Red with a page count
6. *"Unknown means unknown. Missing evidence means the statement does not appear."*

**What kills it for her:** $5.000–13.000 against a few hundred · four separate onboarding conversations ·
and an insurance requirement Brazilian law forbids a natural person from meeting.
