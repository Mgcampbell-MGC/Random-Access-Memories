# TITLE FORGE — Business Overview & Execution Plan

**The AI-Native Land Title & Curative Factory for the Energy, Transmission, and Data-Center Land Rush**

## 1. The Business in One Paragraph

TITLE FORGE sells finished, liability-bearing land-title work product — certified patent-to-present runsheets, mineral-severance reports, heirship research, and counsel-supervised curative programs — to the buyers racing through the US energy, transmission, and data-center land rush. It is not software and not day-rate labor: fixed-price, SLA-backed, E&O-insured deliverables produced by a vision-LLM pipeline with **100% instrument-level human verification.** We are built to survive a flood of funded AI copycats because our core asset — an adjudicated title plant, built county by county from paid work — cannot be bought or rushed; it can only be earned one certified tract at a time.

## 2. The Problem We Solve

Utility-scale solar/storage/transmission developers, data-center site teams, and mineral/E&P land departments all need proven chain of title on rural land — on the critical path to site control and project financing.

- **Landmen bill $400–600/day** and produce **1–3 tracts per week.**
- A typical 200-tract project costs **$300,000–$600,000 and takes 3–6 months** the old way.
- **Delay costs $10,000–$300,000 per day** once a project is financed and waiting on title.
- The professional abstractor workforce is **aging out exactly as demand peaks** — 2,600+ GW of projects sit in interconnection queues today.

The budget already exists and already flows to day-rate brokerages — brokerages that structurally cannot automate without cannibalizing their own billable-day P&L. TITLE FORGE redirects that existing spend instead of creating a new category.

## 5. Why Copycats Can't Just Show Up (The Flood-Test Moats)

Every AI category eventually floods with funded competitors. Here's what survives that, with honest time-to-replicate:

1. **The compounding title plant (the core moat — ~2 years to replicate, capital cannot compress it).** Roughly a third of relevant county records — precisely the rural counties where energy projects land — have no complete online index before the 1970s. Someone has to physically send runners into courthouse basements. Raw scanning isn't the moat (a funded copycat can blanket-scan 100 counties in months); the moat is the **adjudicated chain graph** — which only gets built by delivering *paid, certified* work. After ~150–200 delivered tracts in a survey area, repeat orders reuse 60–80% of prior work, cutting our cost *and* turnaround time simultaneously. A copycat has to win that same paid volume, county by county, earning zero revenue while they catch up.
2. **Courthouse grit + counsel trust (18–36 months).** Handwritten pre-1970 deeds, state-specific mineral-severance doctrine, clerk-by-clerk quirks (fax-only counties, per-page fees), a vetted paid runner network. Conservative energy land departments switch vendors on their attorney's recommendation — not because a demo looked good.
3. **Monitoring lock-in.** Portfolio monitoring is default-on in every contract and embeds us in the customer's financing diligence chain. Ripping us out mid-project jeopardizes the deal. 1%/mo churn, 85% margin.
4. **Licensing/bonding (an operational prerequisite, not a moat by itself).** Disclosed-AI E&O coverage and credentialed signers are cleared in year 1. True regulatory moats — Oklahoma/North Dakota abstract-plant certificates — are deliberately staged as year-2/3 capital projects; the flood-test case doesn't depend on them.

**The honest verdict:** 50 funded copycats can replicate our LLM pipeline in a quarter and will crater prices in the easy, already-digitized counties. They cannot replicate our scanned-county corpus, our licensing/claims history, or our counsel trust inside two-plus years. We price against landman day rates, not software comps — and our cost floor in plant-covered counties sits below any entrant's marginal cost.

## 6. How It Works — The AI + Human Stack

**Design principle:** correctness is conjunctive. At 99% per-instrument accuracy, a 120-instrument chain is fully correct only ~30% of the time. The killer error class is **omission** — the document the model never extracted — which a simple diff UI is structurally blind to. So verification is redesigned per instrument type:

| Instrument type | Verification method | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Typed/digitized | Field diff + AI-generated clause inventory, checked against highlighted source image | ~1.5 min |
| Handwritten pre-1970 | Mandatory full read with transcription overlaid on the source image | 4–8 min (5 budgeted) |
| All instruments | Cross-vendor AI ensemble (two independent vision stacks) so disagreement flags real errors | — |
| Ongoing QA | Canary seeding: known-defect instruments injected into every reviewer's queue as a catch-rate SLA | — |

**Data provenance rule:** nothing enters our permanent plant except our own-capture images and direct-from-county copies at statutory fees. Third-party data sources (Enverus, Tracts, Landman.ai) are used only for lead targeting and per-order cross-checks — never stored in the plant.

**Cost per tract (COGS, at $70/hr loaded QC labor):**

| Tier | Compute | Capture/acquisition | QC labor | **Total COGS** | Price (mo 36) | GM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scanned first touch | $12–18 | $300 (remainder capitalized into plant) | 6.0 hr = $420 | **$830** | $1,950 | 57% |
| Digitized first touch | $8–12 | $85 blended | 2.0 hr = $140 | **$285** | $385 | 26% |
| Plant-cached repeat | $3–6 | $10 | 1.0 hr = $70 | **$110** | $450 | 76% |

The real rate-limiter is the **abstractor supply program**, not compute: a 90-day paid apprenticeship converts title-company escrow staff and landmen into reviewers; senior signers are recruited from retiring plant principals and licensed abstractors.

## 11. Top Risks & How We've Built Against Them

1. **E&O insurance is the single point of failure.** *Backup:* month-1 three-part carrier gate, with a fallback ladder (parallel E&S quote → fronting through a licensed title agency → high-retention structure from reserves) if the primary carrier balks.
2. **QC/liability exposure.** *Backup:* per-instrument-class verification redesign, canary-seeded QA, and a kill-switch — any claim over $250k or two quarters below 52% gross margin freezes county expansion until resolved.
3. **Unauthorized practice of law (curative work).** *Backup:* all instrument drafting happens under licensed counsel; we compress counsel hours, we never displace counsel.
4. **Licensing gaps.** *Backup:* counsel-verified 50-state matrix from month 1; Oklahoma/New Mexico/North Dakota openly waitlisted until licensed — never oversold.
5. **Market contraction** (energy policy shifts, commodity price swings). *Backup:* a published stress ladder with dual early-warning triggers that force a GTM pivot within 30 days.
6. **Incumbent/copycat flood.** *Backup:* price decay modeled on every exposed tier from day one; seeding shifts toward harder-to-replicate scanned corridor counties; retention locked via bundling and monitoring attach.
7. **Abstractor labor supply.** *Backup:* formal apprenticeship pipeline, published wage sensitivity, hiring gated to a 3-month lead per county launched.

## 12. Why This Wins

- **The pain is real, quantified, and already budgeted** — we redirect existing landman spend, we don't evangelize a new category.
- **We chose the gritty version on purpose.** Courthouse basements, handwritten deeds, county clerks, and licensed signers are exactly the friction that keeps venture-funded "move fast" teams out. That friction *is* the moat.
- **The moat compounds with every dollar of revenue** — paid work builds the plant, the plant lowers costs, lower costs win more paid work.
- **We fact-checked our own moats before betting on them.** This niche survived an adversarial process that killed two other candidates specifically because their claimed moats turned out to be already broken by funded competitors.
- **Built, not bought.** Every asset — the plant, the pipeline, the licenses, the client base — is built from scratch. No acquisitions, ever.
