================================================================================
IDEA HUNT V10 — AN ADVERSARIAL SEARCH PROTOCOL
Not a briefing document. A sequence you execute in order.
Written 19 Aug 2026, after V7 failed in a specific and instructive way.
================================================================================

WHY THIS VERSION EXISTS — READ THIS FIRST
------------------------------------------
V7 of this prompt was given to a competent frontier model. It returned a
personalised pregnancy memoir sold as a hardcover gift book.

That answer was already in the file. It had been proposed five days earlier,
assessed, and narrowed — and the direct competitors had already been found and
priced ($47.88/yr, $49.99/yr + $99, from $129, with a published comparison
article between the three). The model found none of them, cited two unrelated
products instead, and concluded "no structural kill found."

It did not fail from incompetence. IT FAILED BECAUSE THE PROMPT LED IT THERE.
The earlier prompts opened by describing an operator's limits in exhaustive
detail and then asked for a business. Every model given that ordering solves it
as a constraint-satisfaction problem, and the global optimum of that problem is
always the same object: A CUSTOMER FILLS IN A FORM AND THE OPERATOR WRITES
SOMETHING. Wrapping it in a hardcover, an app, a PDF or a subscription does not
change what it is.

THIS VERSION IS BUILT TO MAKE THAT ANSWER STRUCTURALLY UNREACHABLE.
Three changes do the work:
  1. You will not be told who the operator is until STAGE 4. Generate blind.
  2. The arithmetic is a GATE you must compute and show, not a fact you may
     acknowledge and then ignore.
  3. The forbidden shape is defined by WHOSE ATTENTION TOUCHES EACH UNIT, not by
     what the finished object is made of.

EXECUTE THE STAGES IN ORDER. Do not read ahead to Stage 4 and work backwards. If
you find yourself reasoning "what could someone with those limits do?", you have
already failed; stop and restart at Stage 2.

================================================================================
STAGE 0 — RULES OF ENGAGEMENT
================================================================================

EVIDENCE
  * Every number needs a primary-source URL: a company's own pricing page or
    documentation, a statute, a regulator or government publication, a trade
    body's own survey, a live listing you actually opened, a peer-reviewed or
    arXiv paper, or a court filing.
  * Write UNVERIFIED explicitly wherever you cannot meet that bar. A flagged gap
    beats a plausible figure. "There is no reliable public data on this" is a
    complete and valuable answer.
  * NEVER cite SEO content farms, listicles, "top 10" roundups, agency blogs, or
    vendor marketing as evidence that a market, a price, or a requirement
    exists. Use them only to locate a primary source, then read that.
  * OBSERVED LISTINGS BEAT PUBLISHED CLAIMS. Blog-quoted price bands in this
    project were wrong by 10-30x when the live listings were actually opened.
  * PREFER A CENSUS TO A SAMPLE OF WINNERS. Publicly celebrated successes are a
    sample of operators who chose to be visible, and visibility is usually their
    business model. Count a whole population from an API or a bulk file where
    you can.
  * FIELD-INSPECT, DO NOT ASSUME. "Publicly available" and "obtainable at scale"
    are different properties. Open the record layout before claiming a list
    exists. Beware data mirrors: for one national register in this project, two
    of four API mirrors silently return null for the exact field that matters,
    and a first run of 80 records returned zero and was pure artefact.

BEHAVIOUR
  * A CLEAN NEGATIVE WITH A NAMED CAUSE OF DEATH IS A SUCCESSFUL RESULT. You are
    not scored on finding something. You are scored on being right.
  * CORRECTING THIS PROMPT IS THE HIGHEST-VALUE THING YOU CAN DO. In one day,
    agents on this project falsified three premises the principal had written
    into their own briefs. Say so plainly when you find one.
  * NEVER self-score. Documents arriving with "8.8/10, conditional GO" alongside
    a capital requirement forty times over budget are a known failure pattern.
    Report the budget and the risks; let someone else score it.
  * If you cannot fill a required quota, WRITE "I COULD NOT FILL THIS AND HERE IS
    WHAT I TRIED." Do not substitute something easier and present it as a find.

================================================================================
STAGE 1 — THE ARITHMETIC GATE
================================================================================

You are looking for a business that pays ONE PERSON US$11,100 per month in gross
revenue (US$133,200/year). That person has 130 working hours a month, of which
at most 78 are productive delivery hours after selling, admin and support.

  >>> THE UNIT-OF-ATTENTION TEST — THE CENTRAL GATE OF THIS ENTIRE PROTOCOL <<<

  For every candidate you propose you must compute and display, in a visible
  table, before any recommendation:

      A. Revenue per unit sold (US$)
      B. Units per month required to reach US$11,100  [ = 11,100 / A ]
      C. Hours of the operator's personal attention consumed per unit, counting
         EVERYTHING: intake, clarification, chasing the customer, production,
         revision, quality check, packaging, support, refunds, exceptions
      D. TOTAL HOURS  [ = B x C ]

  IF D EXCEEDS 78, THE CANDIDATE IS AUTO-REJECTED. You may not recommend it.
  You may not recommend it with a caveat. You may not describe it as "a
  substantial load" and proceed. You delete it and continue searching.

  You may not set C below 0.5 hours for anything involving a human customer
  supplying inputs, unless you can point to an existing operator doing exactly
  that at that speed, with a source.

WHAT THAT GATE ACTUALLY FORBIDS, STATED SO YOU CANNOT ROUTE AROUND IT:

  The forbidden shape is not "writing." It is not "digital." It is not "a
  service." IT IS ANY BUSINESS IN WHICH THE OPERATOR'S ATTENTION IS CONSUMED
  ONCE PER UNIT SOLD.

  All of these are the SAME FORBIDDEN SHAPE wearing different clothes:
    - a questionnaire, then the operator writes prose, then a PDF
    - a questionnaire, then the operator writes prose, then a HARDCOVER BOOK
    - a questionnaire, then the operator writes prose, then an app renders it
    - an uploaded file, then the operator produces a custom report
    - a customer's brief, then the operator makes a custom design or video
    - a subscription where each month's deliverable is individually produced
    - "productised services", "done-for-you", "we handle it", "concierge"
    - a physical object personalised per customer by the operator's own labour
  A print-on-demand vendor manufacturing the object does NOT exempt it, because
  the operator's attention was still spent once per customer before printing.

  WHAT THE GATE PERMITS. Revenue must arrive by one of these four routes, and
  you must name which one:
    A. THE ASSET PRODUCES THE UNITS. Built once; serves customer 2 through 400
       at near-zero marginal attention. Software, a dataset, an automated watcher.
    B. A SLICE OF SOMEONE ELSE'S FLOW. Commission or take-rate on transactions
       the operator introduces but does not perform. Hours per unit trivial
       against the value transferred.
    C. A SPREAD THE OPERATOR OWNS. Buy at one price, sell at another.
    D. RENT ON SOMETHING OWNED. A licence, a right, a system.

  ONE MORE COST TEST, which kills a whole class that passes the hours gate:
  IS THE SOURCE READ ONCE AND SOLD MANY TIMES, OR READ ONCE PER CUSTOMER? A
  watcher that parses one public file weekly and serves it to every subscriber
  has fixed cost. A monitor that must buy paid API calls per customer forever
  has cost that scales with customers, and a solo operator entering last always
  loses that race. State which yours is.

================================================================================
STAGE 2 — GENERATE. YOU DO NOT YET KNOW WHO WILL RUN THIS.
================================================================================

Do not ask who the operator is. Do not optimise for anyone. If you catch
yourself narrowing toward a person's convenience, stop.

DEPLOY AGENTS IN PARALLEL. Recommended: eight to twelve. Give each agent Stage 0
and Stage 1 in full and ONE of the mandates below. Do not give any agent Stage 4.

MANDATORY QUOTAS. Return at least THREE distinct candidates in each of the five
structures below. Fifteen minimum. If a structure yields nothing, write "I could
not fill this" and show what you searched.

  STRUCTURE 1 — SOMETHING WATCHED CONTINUOUSLY
    The product is that a public, external thing was monitored and someone was
    told the moment it changed. Hunt: statutory publication that starts a legal
    clock; registers whose status changes cost someone money; deadlines nobody
    is notified of. Ask what rule makes the date matter and who suffers if it
    is missed. Ask: does one alert, acted on, pay for a year of subscription?

  STRUCTURE 2 — A DATASET THAT COMPOUNDS
    Accumulate over time what nobody else has. Hunt CLOCK-STARTS, not datasets:
    a register that opened this year, a regime that just took effect, a mandate
    whose first filings are landing now. If a dataset is worth accumulating,
    someone already started, and their head start points at you. And note that
    a register keeps CURRENT STATE, not history — it overwrites — so its
    change-log is the one artefact the holder will never produce and nobody can
    back-fill.

  STRUCTURE 3 — MATCHING OR COMMISSION ON SOMEONE ELSE'S TRANSACTION
    Two enumerable populations, a hard-to-make match, a percentage. Map first
    where referral fees are PROHIBITED or licence-gated, then work in what is
    left. Compute required monthly flow at every commission rate you can source.
    Ask what stops the two sides transacting directly next quarter.

  STRUCTURE 4 — SOFTWARE OR A SYSTEM SOLD MANY TIMES
    Built once. Census the distribution channel before believing in it: pull the
    whole catalogue from a public API and compute the median, not the winners.
    In this project the median paid app in the best-fitting business marketplace
    had 13 installs and the median vendor had 8.

  STRUCTURE 5 — A SPREAD, A RENT, OR AN ARBITRAGE
    Buy and sell, licence, or capture a differential. Include: buying an
    existing cash-flowing asset rather than building one. Include: geographic,
    regulatory, linguistic and temporal arbitrage.

AND A HARD DIVERSITY RULE. At least SIX of your fifteen must come from
industries you consider boring, unglamorous or unfashionable. Waste handling,
freight, insurance claims, industrial fasteners, payroll, calibration,
agricultural inputs, port logistics, laboratory consumables, municipal
utilities, textile finishing, cold chain, scrap metal, bonded warehousing.
The emotionally appealing candidate is nearly always the crowded one. Money
lives where nobody wants to look.

FOR EACH CANDIDATE, RETURN EXACTLY THIS AND NOTHING MORE:
  - One sentence: what is sold, to whom.
  - Which revenue route (A/B/C/D from Stage 1).
  - The Stage 1 arithmetic table. A, B, C, D. Show the numbers.
  - The named buyer. Not "SMEs" — a job title in a kind of firm.
  - What breaks for that buyer if they do not have it.
  - An OBSERVED comparable price, with a URL.
  - Whether the source is read once and sold many, or once per customer.
  - The single honest reason it might fail.

================================================================================
STAGE 3 — THE KILL SCREEN. RUN THIS BEFORE YOU FALL IN LOVE.
================================================================================

Every candidate that survived Stage 1 now faces these. Answer each EXPLICITLY.
One failure is not automatically fatal, but an unanswered question is.

  1. WHO IS ALREADY PAID TO GIVE THIS AWAY FREE? "Knowing which one" is usually
     the customer-acquisition cost of whoever holds the stock or the paying
     work, so they give it away. Name the party and check.
  2. COULD THE BUYER PRODUCE THIS ANSWER IN AN AFTERNOON? Not "do they have the
     data" — could they get it themselves before lunch?
  3. WHO IS BEING PAID TO ELIMINATE THE FRAGMENTATION YOU ARE CALLING A MOAT?
     Funded nonprofits, regulators, standards bodies and platform owners are
     all actively abolishing exactly the messes that look like opportunities.
  4. IS THE BUYER SHORT OF CUSTOMERS, SHORT OF CAPACITY, OR SHORT OF MARGIN?
     Ask: what breaks if their volume doubled next month? A buyer short of
     CUSTOMERS will not pay to be given more hours — they already have spare
     hours and would rather keep them and the money. Sell time savings only to
     someone turning work away, or someone whose volume is fixed by something
     outside their control and whose cost is their own hours.
  5. WHAT IS THE CHEAPEST EXISTING SUBSTITUTE, WITH A URL AND A PRICE? Include
     free tiers and bundled features. If a large incumbent gives this away to
     sell something else, say so.
  6. DOES THE PROPERTY THAT GIVES LEVERAGE ALSO DESTROY PRICING POWER? If it is
     repeatable enough for a machine to do it, it is repeatable enough for
     anyone's machine.
  7. IS THE LIST ACTUALLY CONTACTABLE? Public registers give name, street
     address, phone and an expiry date. They rarely give email. Of nine national
     registers field-inspected in this project, two published email at scale.
     OPEN THE RECORD LAYOUT. Do not assume.
  8. HOW MANY SEPARATE PARTIES MUST ONBOARD THE OPERATOR, AND CAN EACH BE DONE
     IN WRITING? Selling in writing and BEING ONBOARDED in writing are different
     problems. Supplier accounts, payment-processor risk reviews, vendor
     security diligence and professional-indemnity questions are conversations.
     Give a count.
  9. HOW DOES THE MONEY ARRIVE, AND WHAT DOES THAT COST? A bank-transfer invoice
     costs ~0.5%. A card checkout costs ~8% all-in. That difference can exceed
     the entire tax bill. INVOICE, DON'T CHECKOUT.
 10. IS THE CATEGORY EMPTY OR CROWDED? Empty is WORSE evidence than crowded. A
     crowded market with visible prices proves demand; an empty one usually
     proves someone already tried and stopped.
 11. IF THE NEAREST COMPARABLE DOES NOT PUBLISH A PRICE, THE CATEGORY IS
     SALES-LED. That is a ten-second screen and it kills a lot.
 12. WHAT DOES THIS LOOK LIKE IN A GLOBAL DOWNTURN IN 2027-2028?

  AND THE DUPLICATE CHECK, WHICH V7 FAILED. Before recommending anything, state
  explicitly: "This is not a rediscovery of X." The following have already been
  proposed, assessed and killed or narrowed in this project. Anything materially
  resembling them is a REJECTION, not a find:
    - memoir, legacy, life-story, keepsake or milestone books of any kind, for
      any occasion, in any format, digital or physical
    - pregnancy, baby, wedding, funeral or anniversary personalised gifts
    - anything sold on general asset marketplaces: templates, stock, fonts,
      presets, prompt packs, mockups, icon sets
    - written deliverables sold on freelance marketplaces
    - consumer mobile apps, and business software marketplaces
    - reselling physical goods held in inventory
    - "AI visibility" or brand-mention monitoring
    - documentation, lost-knowledge capture, or institutional-memory products
    - anything whose buyer segment is construction, or whose subject is death

================================================================================
STAGE 4 — NOW, AND ONLY NOW, MEET THE OPERATOR
================================================================================

Do not read this section until Stages 2 and 3 are complete and written down.

Filtering last is deliberate. A constraint applied early deletes the COMPONENT
along with the candidate, and the component is usually the valuable half.

THE OPERATING UNIT IS NOT A PERSON WITH LIMITATIONS. It is this machine:

  * A program that runs unattended, on rented compute, indefinitely.
  * Plus 78 hours a month of one literate, careful, bilingual human
    (Portuguese and English, resident in Brazil) who communicates only in
    writing and never appears in any form.
  * Plus US$500-2,500 of one-time capital. Not monthly.
  * Plus unlimited purchased software, APIs, data and vendor services.

THIS MACHINE CAN: read, parse, structure, match, monitor, compute, correspond,
contract, invoice, and buy anything from any vendor. It can operate in
Portuguese natively, which is an advantage nobody else in its market has.

THIS MACHINE CANNOT:
  * Speak. No calls, video, meetings, voice notes or webinars, ever, in any
    language. (One narrow exception: a small, bounded number of ONE-OFF SETUP
    calls at launch — a supplier account, a processor risk review, instructing
    counsel, signing a partner. Ongoing customer contact is written forever.)
  * Appear. No camera, photograph, personal brand, audience or name-based
    reputation. A company brand or a pen name is available ON ARGUMENT, but it
    must never be the thing that rescues an otherwise weak idea.
  * Employ. No employees or contractors, ever. Vendors and software are
    unlimited. CAPACITY IS PURCHASABLE AS MACHINES, NEVER AS PEOPLE.
  * Hold a customer's credentials or standing access to their systems. Work
    arrives as a file they send, or is observed from outside in public.
  * Be believed. No credential, no industry background, no track record. It can
    never assert an expert opinion, a determination, an assurance, or a
    professional judgement.
  * Be present anywhere physically, or testify to anything.
  * Sell to construction as a buyer segment, or build anything around death.

FOUR MECHANISMS ARE KNOWN TO SURVIVE THE "CANNOT BE BELIEVED" LIMIT. If your
candidate needs credibility, it must use one of these or die:
  1. THE ARTEFACT PROVES ITSELF. Every material statement paired with its source
     and page; contradictions surfaced rather than resolved; unsupported claims
     listed in a "not used" section. Nothing rests on the operator's authority.
  2. THE BUYER SUPPLIES THE DOMAIN, THE MACHINE SUPPLIES THE THROUGHPUT.
  3. THE BUYER SUPPLIES THE "BEFORE." They send work they already did with their
     own hours attached; the machine returns the after; the two objects sit side
     by side and the buyer does the comparing. Nothing is claimed.
  4. A RESELLER SUPPLIES THE FACE. The machine is sold wholesale to a firm that
     puts its own name, credential and liability on the output. This is the only
     arrangement found in which being anonymous is what the buyer WANTS. Price
     is then set by the reseller's client revenue, not by the retail comparable:
     seventeen resellers at US$650/month reaches the target, not three hundred
     customers.

NOW RE-SCORE EVERY SURVIVING CANDIDATE against this machine. State PASS or FAIL
per line, and where a candidate fails, ask whether the COMPONENT can be kept
even though the candidate dies. Record the component.

================================================================================
STAGE 5 — WHAT YOU MUST HAND BACK
================================================================================

  1. THE CONTRADICTIONS FIRST. Where two of your agents disagree, name it and
     resolve it on evidence, saying which you believe and why. Agents correcting
     each other, and correcting the principal, has produced more value in this
     project than agreement.
  2. THE LAWS, SEPARATED FROM THE CANDIDATES. Every new structural rule you
     discovered, with the evidence that produced it, whether or not any
     candidate survived. The reusable finding outlives the business.
  3. ONE NUMBERS LEDGER, three columns: VERIFIED PRIMARY / SECONDARY-FLAGGED /
     UNVERIFIED-AND-NOT-USED. Anything unverified goes in column three BY NAME
     rather than being quietly dropped.
  4. THE SURVIVORS, ranked BY WHAT IS LEAST LIKELY TO BE WRONG — not by what is
     most exciting. For each: the Stage 1 table, the revenue route, the named
     buyer, what breaks without it, the observed comparable price with URL, the
     onboarding count, how the money arrives, the Stage 3 answers, and THE
     HONEST REASON IT MIGHT FAIL.
  5. FOR EACH SURVIVOR, ONE FREE TEST. It must cost nothing, need no phone call
     and no customer, and MEASURE A RATE rather than produce an anecdote. It
     must state its numeric bar IN ADVANCE and say what result KILLS the
     candidate. A test that cannot fail is not a test.
     Tests that have worked in this project: pull twenty firms' published price
     lists and count how many contain a specific line item; email thirty
     organisations one written question and count the replies CONTAINING A
     NUMBER; parse four weeks of a public gazette and count how many events
     touched each of twenty named firms; pull an entire marketplace catalogue
     from its public API and compute the median; publish twenty structured
     factual pages on one indexed domain and count how many are cited in sixty
     days.

  A GOOD ANSWER names a buyer rather than a market, has an observed price with a
  URL rather than an estimate, shows the Stage 1 arithmetic, answers the
  onboarding question with a count, states how the money arrives and what that
  costs, flags its own weakest link before anyone else has to, and CORRECTS
  SOMETHING IN THIS PROMPT.

  A BAD ANSWER — and this project has received many — is a well-written
  blueprint with a self-assessed score, a capital requirement ten times over
  budget disclosed on page nine, a market size from a content farm, a buyer who
  is short of customers rather than short of hours, and a rediscovery of
  something already in the graveyard.

================================================================================
FINAL INSTRUCTION
================================================================================

If, at the end of all of this, the only thing you have is a business where one
person makes each thing that is sold — SAY YOU FOUND NOTHING. That is a real
result and it is worth more than a beautiful document describing a job.
