# SINGLEBRAIN — can this model work in Brazil for Sol?

**Assessed 14 Aug 2026.**
**Verdict: NO, and not because of the niche. Four absolute vetoes fail at once, and none of them
is fixable by choosing a different industry.**
**But one idea inside it is worth keeping, and she is already building it.**

---

## 1. First, what SingleBrain actually is

The site reads like a product. It is not.

> **[SingleBrain is operated by Single Grain](https://www.singlebrain.com/)** — the agency. Third-party
> directories list **a minimum project size of $10.000+**, and engagements are **monthly retainers or
> project-based work, with budgets scaling by scope and channels.** Client logos are Amazon, Uber,
> Salesforce, Nextiva, Lever.

And the pricing line, verbatim from the site:

> *"Pricing depends on the deployment, the verticals, and the integrations."*

### THE DEPLOYMENT TELL — worth adding to the law set

> **When a company says pricing depends on the deployment, it is telling you there is a salesperson.
> "Managed" is the word an agency uses when it wants to be valued like software.**

A five-day bespoke deployment, isolated infrastructure per client, and integrations into HubSpot,
Gong, Meta, the CMS and the BI stack is **a consulting engagement with agents in it.** That is a
perfectly good business. It is a different business from the one Sol is trying to start.

---

## 2. Why it cannot be Sol's, whatever niche you point it at

| SingleBrain requires | Sol's constraint |
|---|---|
| Scoped, negotiated deals at $10.000+ with no public price | **NO-SALES-CALL TEST** — must be bought from a page. Hardest filter in the record |
| OAuth into HubSpot, Gong, Meta, the CMS, the client's Slack | **NO CUSTODY OF ANY CUSTOMER CREDENTIAL.** Her one absolute veto |
| Agents living inside a company's revenue stack | **NO-TRUST-GATE TEST** — vendor security review, SOC 2, DPA, MSA. A faceless one-person company cannot pass |
| Isolated infra per client, bespoke five-day builds | **"Build yes. Bugs and such after the build."** Per-client infrastructure is the opposite |
| An agency with staff behind the fleet | **NO EMPLOYEES, NO CONTRACTORS. EVER.** |

**Five failures, four of them absolute.** Swapping the vertical to estética, odontologia or moda
changes none of them — they are properties of the *business model*, not of the industry.

---

## 3. The Brazilian version, built anyway — and why I would not run it

Because the question deserves a real answer and not just a refusal, here is the strongest version
I can construct, followed by the thing that kills it.

**Niche:** clínicas de estética avançada and harmonização facial in São Paulo — heavily
woman-owned, WhatsApp-native, high spend per client, no procurement department.

**KISS product:** R$1.200/month, price published, bought from the page, no call, no login. She never
touches their systems. Each month they receive a finished pack: 12 posts, 8 stories, 4 WhatsApp
campaign scripts, written against the advertising rules that bind their profession.

**Why I would not run it — three kills, any one sufficient:**

1. **LAW 1.** A Brazilian social-media freelancer does this for R$800/month, and Canva does the
   safe version for R$0. Content is the thing incumbents give away.
2. **LAW 7, the vise.** The only real differentiation is *compliance* with CFM/CFO/CFN advertising
   rules — and the compliant version requires her to be **believed as an expert on those rules**,
   which is one of her own vetoes. Safe version undifferentiated, differentiated version reserved.
3. **The price floor.** At R$1.200 she needs **10–12 clients** for the near-term target, each one
   won cold, each one churning, each one a monthly delivery obligation. That is an agency with one
   employee.

**And every adjacent variant dies to doctrine already in the file.** Client reactivation for salons:
the booking software (Trinks, Avec, Belle) already owns it — clearing party. Supplier quotes for
party planners: the suppliers compete to sell, so someone charges them and gives the buyer the tool
free — seller-side funding law. Document chasing for accountants: vetoed outright.

**Brazilian micro-business B2B is where this record's 65 candidates went to die.** Nothing about
adding AI to it changes the arithmetic.

---

## 4. The one idea worth keeping — and she is already building it

Strip the agency and the enterprise buyer away, and what is left is genuinely good:

> **Managed execution. No tool to learn, no dashboard, no login. The customer sends inputs, work
> happens invisibly, a finished thing arrives.**

That is *exactly* what Sol said she wanted: **"AI does the work while she watches the system run."**
SingleBrain's instinct is right. Its buyer is what makes it impossible.

**Change the buyer from a company to a consumer and every veto dissolves at once:**

| | SingleBrain | Candidate 64 |
|---|---|---|
| Promise | Work arrives done, no tool to learn | **Identical** |
| Buyer | A company with a security review | **A person with a credit card** |
| Price | "Depends on the deployment" | **$149 on the page** |
| Access needed | OAuth into the revenue stack | **A form and some photos** |
| Sales motion | Scoping calls | **None** |
| Delivery | Bespoke per client | **One template, one API call** |
| Trust gate | SOC 2, MSA, DPA | **None** |

**Candidate 64 is SingleBrain with the trust gate removed.** Same core promise, a buyer who cannot
demand a security review, and a deliverable a chat window cannot produce.

That is not a coincidence and it is the useful finding here: **the thing that makes managed AI
execution impossible for a faceless solo founder is the B2B buyer — not the AI, and not the
industry.**

---

## 5. Recommendation

**Do not build a Brazilian SingleBrain.** It fails four absolute vetoes before the niche is even
chosen, and the best version I can construct dies to Law 1, Law 7 and the price floor.

**Keep the sentence, not the model:** *no tool, no login, work arrives done.* Put it on the
candidate-64 storefront as the promise, because it is the correct description of what she is
selling — **and it is the reason the product is worth $149 rather than $29.**
