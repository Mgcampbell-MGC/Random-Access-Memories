# THE ANALOGUE CHECK — and what it does to SHELF RECORD, 2 Sep 2026
**Founder: *"is there a close example of someone doing this somewhere in the world, and how do they do it?"*
The right question, and the answer costs the candidate most of its price.**

## 1. Yes. Two analogues, and they sit at opposite ends

**A · The consultant version — real, and not sold standalone.**
Kathryn Read, international sales consultant, 30+ years in emerging markets, publishes a store-check
methodology that is line-for-line what SHELF RECORD proposes: placement, shelf price against RRP, stock and
out-of-stocks, expiry dates, competitor SKUs and promotions, share of shelf, planogram and brand-guideline
compliance. Her stated rationale is exactly the thesis — ***"you need to see the shelves yourself to get a
feel for the market"***, rather than relying on distributor reporting, and *"take lots of photos."*
**But it is a component of a consulting practice performed when the brand's own person visits the market, not
a product with a price. No rate is published.**

**B · ★ The gig version — and this is the one that hurts.**

| Platform | What it pays for | Rate | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Field Agent** | walk into a store, photograph a shelf or display, submit | **US$3–20/task** | retail audit, shelf compliance |
| **Gigwalk** | same shape | **US$3–100/task** | US-centric |
| **Premise** | same shape | per task | **140+ countries** |
| Roamler | same shape | per task | multi-country, localised |
| **VISU** | same shape | per task | **US and BRAZIL** |

> ### **The core labour of SHELF RECORD — go to the shop, photograph the shelf, upload it — is an established global gig task priced at three to twenty dollars, and at least one platform already runs it in Brazil.**

## 2. What that does to the candidate

**It does not kill it. It caps it, and it moves what is being sold.**

| Sold as | Verdict |
|---|---|
| *"Photographs of Brazilian shelves"* | **Dead.** A brand can buy those for a few dollars a store from a platform that already operates here |
| *"The same person, the same route, every month, comparable over time, with a named human accountable and an archive that accrues"* | **Alive, and much smaller than US$600–900/month.** Gig platforms send a different stranger each time and sell no continuity |

**⚠ AND THE PATTERN, which is now three for three in two days:**
> **CONFORMIDADE — a R$679 kit does the same job. GUARDA — the portal is free and a VA can run it. SHELF
> RECORD — the walk is a US$5 gig task. All three reduce to selling ACCOUNTABILITY WRAPPED AROUND A COMMODITY.**
>
> ### **That is the precise definition of what the founder called a hustle: her hours convert into money one-for-one, nothing accumulates, and the moment she stops, it stops.**

## 3. So what separates a hustle from a business, stated once

**A business owns an asset that produces value on a day she does not work, and that a buyer could in principle
purchase from her. A hustle owns her calendar.**

**Every asset-creating route available to a solo founder, against her rules:**

| Asset | Status |
|---|---|
| Software people pay for monthly | capped by what it costs the buyer to rebuild it |
| An audience | C2 |
| A credential | the expert veto |
| Capital assets | US$3.000 |
| A compounding dataset | needs a head start or a clock-start she does not have |
| **Relationships that are slow and physical to form** | **available, and never once hunted** |

**Five of six are shut. The sixth just opened this afternoon when she agreed to leave the house — and in ~130
candidates it has never been the basis of a single proposal.** *(SHELF RECORD's archive was an attempt at the
fifth row. The gig platforms show the capture is commodity, so the archive is thinner than I claimed.)*

# THE MAKER DESK — the new candidate, built on the sixth row

## What it is
**Foreign brands increasingly want to manufacture outside China.** Brazil makes leather goods, ceramics,
homeware, swimwear, jewellery, cosmetics and packaging at real quality — and a small foreign brand cannot use
any of it, because it cannot find the makers, cannot judge them, cannot speak to them, cannot manage samples,
and will not fly to São Paulo to try.

**Sol is their production person in Brazil.** She finds candidate makers, visits them, photographs the
workshop, pulls samples, manages the back-and-forth in Portuguese, checks the run before it ships, and reports
in English.

## Why it is a business and not a hustle
> ### **The asset is the vetted maker network, and it is built by walking.** Client one pays her to find and qualify five makers. **Client two gets those five on day one** and pays for three more. By client ten she holds a verified, visited, photographed network of Brazilian manufacturers that will work at small-brand volumes — **which no competitor can assemble without making the same visits, and no client can commission from abroad at any price.**
>
> Each client makes the asset more valuable to the next. That is the property every candidate so far has lacked.

## The money — and it is the first observed band in this whole direction
Sourcing and production agents charge **5–10% of order value**, or **US$300–1.000/month on retainer** for
ongoing supplier management, new product development and quality visits. *(3% for simple, up to 15% for small
or complex runs.)*

**At the retainer band: 3–4 brands at US$600–800 reaches the target.** On a US$50.000 production run, a 7% fee
is US$3.500 — one order.

## Why her
Portuguese, São Paulo, an eye for materials and finish, and a willingness to walk into a workshop. **She is
never the expert on manufacturing** — she reports what she saw, carries the samples, and the brand decides.

## What has to be checked before this is believed
1. **Is there demand?** Foreign brands wanting Brazilian production is asserted from the nearshoring trend, not
   measured. **Fifteen emails to small foreign brands and sourcing consultancies settles it.**
2. **Do Brazilian makers take small foreign orders?** Brazilian minimum order quantities may be far above what
   a small brand wants. **This is the likeliest kill and it is answerable by visiting five makers.**
3. **Quality liability** — if a run is bad, whose fault is it? The scope line must put approval with the client,
   exactly as GUARDA's did.
4. **Is it fashion?** Leather and swimwear are; ceramics, homeware, packaging and cosmetics are not. **Steer to
   the non-apparel categories.**

**Honest rating: I do not know yet.** It has the first thing on this board that compounds, the first observed
price band in this direction, and a physical moat that only she can build. It also has an unmeasured buyer and
a plausible minimum-order kill. **It deserves the fifteen emails before it deserves any more design.**
