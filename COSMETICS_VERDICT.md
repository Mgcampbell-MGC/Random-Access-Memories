# COSMETICS REGULATORY WORK — VERDICT: NOT HERS

**14 Aug 2026. Measured against primary law and fetched seller pages.**

> **DEAD. The preparer layer exists but is retail, not wholesale; the price floor is $30 for the
> entire document set; preparation is deliberately given away free by the firms that hold the
> mandate; and there is no verified backlog anywhere.**

---

**The source, checked properly, describes itself as *"Structural intelligence on Asia's capital

It is not a cosmetics regulatory publication, and no cosmetics content was visible on it.**

**That quote was the emotional centre of my last two messages and it is unsourced. My error — I
should have checked the publication before repeating it.**

### b) The ANVISA "saves R$15–25K" figure is refuted

The BioCore document said dossier support *"saves you R$15–25K in regulatory consulting."* I built
the redirect on it.

> **ANVISA's own service page: *"Este serviço é gratuito para o cidadão"* — the notification is
> FREE and takes up to three calendar days.** ([gov.br](https://www.gov.br/pt-br/servicos/solicitar-regularizacao-de-cosmeticos-isentos-de-registro))

The R$3.000–15.000 per-product figure that circulates traces to **a single content-marketing blog**,
and it is dominated by **sworn translation, laboratory reports and the retained Técnico
Responsável** — all three of which need Brazilian accreditation, physical samples or a licence. **A
document preparer supplies none of them. Brazil is the weakest leg and should be dropped entirely.**

## 2. Why the business is dead

**The price floor is public and at the bottom.**

| Provider | What you get | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Fiverr `michael_hogwart` | **CPSR + PIF + SDS + GMP cert + CoA + label + CPNP** | **$30** |
| [Phoenix Compliance](https://phoenixcosmeticsafety.com/products/all-in-one-compliance-documentations-cpsr-pif-sds-scpn) | CPSR A&B + PIF + SDS + notification + certificate, **72 hours** | **£131,25** |
| [CosmeticsPIF](https://www.cosmeticspif.com/price.aspx) | Complete PIF, 1–5 ingredients | €300 |
| [Certified Cosmetics](https://www.certifiedcosmetics.com/product/cosmetic-safety-assesment/cpnp-notification/) | CPNP notification | **$28,90** |

**There is no room underneath this**, and the incumbents at that price have reviews, histories and
platform badges she does not.

**The wholesale channel I hypothesised does not exist.** Zero published instances of an RP firm
buying preparation. [Obelis](https://jobs.obelis.net/) hires salaried consultants in Brussels
requiring a law, pharmacy or biotech degree plus three years in sector.
[Biorius](https://biorius.com/careers/) recruits toxicologists to an inbox. Taobe states plainly it
does not subcontract outward.

**And where third-party preparation does circulate, the market prices it at NEGATIVE value:**

> **Intertek's commercial offer is to *audit* subcontractor-prepared PIFs.** *"That is not a preparer
> layer. That is a cleanup market for the damage preparers cause."*

**Preparation is a deliberate loss-leader.** [EU Compliance Partner](https://eucompliancepartner.com/eu-responsible-person-cosmetics-service)
gives CPNP notification, UK SCPN notification **and** label review **free** with a $250/year RP
retainer — charging $40/$40/$150 only to non-clients. **She would be selling something an incumbent
has a structural reason to price at zero forever.**

**Liability is indivisible, and it is the actual product.** Art. 11 of Reg. 1223/2009 puts the PIF in
the Responsible Person's custody for **ten years after the last batch**; Art. 10(2) puts the
assessment on a credentialed signature. The RP cannot outsource the consequence, so it has little
reason to outsource the input.

**There is no verified backlog. None.** CPNP is a free, self-service portal with no queue. Not one RP
firm published a capacity complaint. And the headline statistic cuts both ways: **BMHCA was banned in
March 2022 and still drove ~80% of cosmetic Safety Gate alerts in 2025** — which is *at least as
consistent with brands who will never pay anyone* as with brands who cannot find help.

**The deadline already passed.** The "compliance bill lands in August" is
**Regulation (EU) 2023/1545** on fragrance allergens: compliance for placing on the market was
required **31 July 2026**. Today is 14 August. **If there was a surge it ran in Q1–Q2 and is behind
us.**

## 3. What genuinely survived — and why it still isn't enough

**The legal opening is real, and it was verified in primary law rather than commentary:**

> **Art. 10(2) restricts the safety assessment. Art. 11 restricts custody. Nothing in Regulation
> 1223/2009 restricts who may COMPILE the file.** Annex I Part A — composition, physico-chemical
> characteristics, stability, microbiological quality, impurities, packaging, exposure — carries **no
> author restriction.**

**And the fragmentation is striking: 24.112 UK Responsible Persons hold 861.686 notifications — ~36
products each.** The mass of the industry is one- and two-person operations, which is exactly where
overflow subcontracting would live and exactly the segment too small to publish a partner programme.

**Two tasks pass every filter:**

1. **Allergen and IFRA documentation mapping** under Reg. 2023/1545 — document-chasing and
   table-mapping against 82 Annex III entries, corrected for the **November 2025 corrigendum**
   (Damascone→Damascenone, the *Pelargonium graveolens* split, *Pogostemon cablin*). **The
   existing-stock deadline runs to 31 July 2028 — a two-year tail, not a passed spike.** And
   [Certified Cosmetics already sells it standalone at "from €160"](https://www.certifiedcosmetics.com/shop/),
   proving the task is separable and salable.
2. **Claims screening against Regulation 655/2013.** The Commission's own surveillance found
   **10% of 38.995 claims non-compliant, and 70% of the non-compliance was ONLINE, only 17% on the
   product itself.** Website copy, not carton artwork — **auditable from anywhere on earth, needing
   no lab, no signature and no EU establishment.**

**The inversion that makes those two work, stated by the agent better than I had it:**

> **"Faceless is fatal for the RP mandate and survives for checklist work. Nobody will let an
> anonymous foreigner hold a ten-year liability. But ingredient screening against Annexes II–VI,
> label review, claims screening and allergen mapping all have RIGHT ANSWERS that a buyer can verify
> in minutes without trusting the seller at all."**

**But the arithmetic still fails.** €160 per allergen document means **~15 documents a month for
R$15.000** — sold against $30 Fiverr bundles with review histories, into a market with no measured
backlog, after the deadline. **That is not a business. It is a gig.**

## 4. One correction in her favour, worth keeping

Two beliefs that circulate in this industry are false, and both are checkable:

- **An ISO 22716 certificate is NOT required.** Art. 8 requires GMP; ISO 22716 gives presumption of
  conformity; **the European Commission has confirmed the Regulation "does not require any kind of
  certificate to demonstrate GMP compliance."** A declaration suffices. *"The Korean factory won't
  give us an ISO certificate"* is frequently a non-problem sold as a problem.
- **Full formula disclosure is NOT required for CPNP** — a frame formulation suffices. **But the PIF
  and CPSR do need the true quantitative composition**, and that is the real friction: it requires
  the factory to trust someone. **A preparer cannot resolve it.**

## 5. The check that would reopen it — free, and it is the only thing left

**Take 20 K-beauty SKUs listed on EU-facing e-commerce and check two things needing no lab, no
credential and no call:**

**(a)** Does the ingredient list carry the **expanded post-corrigendum 82-entry Annex III allergen
declarations** required since 31 July 2026?
**(b)** Does the EU-facing web copy carry a claim failing 655/2013 — *"whitening"*, a curative
claim, *"free from parabens"*, or a *"not tested on animals"* statement?

> **Kill criterion: fewer than 3 of 20 show a defect and the thesis is dead. No amount of pricing
> work saves it.**

**And it produces the only asset a faceless seller can lead with: a specific verified defect on a
named product, found before the buyer knew about it** — no credential asserted, no liability
assumed, checkable by the recipient in under a minute.

## 6. Where this leaves the file

**Candidate 66 — the catalogue landing — stands, and nothing in this sweep displaced it.** The one
piece of the cosmetics work worth carrying across is the **claims-and-label screening layer**, which
belongs *inside* a marketplace documentation package for beauty listings rather than as a
standalone regulatory product.

**The clean lesson, and it cost nothing to learn:** *a huge industry is not the same as an
addressable one.* The demand here is enormous, statutory and real — and it is already served at $30
by people with credentials, or given away free by the firms that hold the liability.
