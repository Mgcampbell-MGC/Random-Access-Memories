### ★★ Two new laws from the kills
> **THE PROTOCOL-NOT-PRODUCT LAW — when an industry's own guidance says a defect "cannot be fixed in editing,
> it has to be solved at capture," a post-processing product is dead on arrival. And where a REGULATOR forbids
> the post-processing, the fix is the violation.** Thirty-second screen on any clean-up-the-data candidate.

> **THE COMPELLED-BUT-CREDENTIALLED TRAP — inside her own domain, the compelled obligations are precisely the
> ones reserved to credentialled persons** (cosmetic safety assessor, Responsible Person). **Domain affinity and
> compulsion point at the same door and the expert veto locks it.** This is the structural reason
> `THE DOMAIN-DRIFT DIAGNOSIS` happens: compulsion in a *nice* domain is credentialled; compulsion that is
> *uncredentialled* is industrial.

## ★ THE SURVIVOR — iris / retinal art through a capture-owning channel

**The observation.** Iris photography is a real, priced, growing consumer category, and its economics are good:

| Product | Observed retail |
|---|---|
| Digital single image | **from $79** |
| 20×30 matte, framed | **$309** |
| 20×30 metal, framed | **$359** |
| 24×36 matte | **$409** |
| Hi-res digital with effects | **+$229** |
| Session length | **~10 minutes** |

**Every operator found is a STUDIO** — the customer comes in, the operator's macro rig captures the eye, the
operator processes and sells the print. **The binding asset is the capture rig and a physical location.**

**And the gap: a search for any programme where an optometry practice sells keepsake prints from images it
already captures returned NOTHING.** Optometry practices run ultra-wide retinal imaging as routine
(*"200° single capture in less than ½ second"*), on millions of patients, with the hardware already paid for.

**The structure, if it holds:** the clinic or studio captures; **she never captures anything, never meets a
customer, and never touches a system**; her machine turns the supplied image into the art file; a print lab
ships it.

### Why it scores well on the current screens
- **Inaccessible subject — the strongest instance in the file.** Nobody can photograph their own iris or
  retina at that magnification, at any price, ever. `THE INACCESSIBLE-SUBJECT SCREEN` passes outright.
- **Arithmetic is not close — it is comfortable.** At **$300 net ⇒ 42 units/month ⇒ 2,1 h/month** at a 3-minute
  pipeline. Even at **$100 net ⇒ 176 units ⇒ 8,8 h/month.** Against a 43 h budget, that is 5–20% utilisation.
- **C4 clean** (a file is uploaded, no system access) · **no expert claim** (decoration, not diagnosis) ·
  **zero prose** · **C8 fine** — it is an aesthetic object.
- **Not a catalogue item** — it is personal to one person's eye, so `THE CATALOGUE SCISSORS` does not bite, and
  unlike the craft escape it needs no hands per unit.

### ⚠ The open questions, stated honestly — this is a hypothesis, not a finding
1. **★ THE CRUX: is a clinically-captured image artistically usable?** Iris studios use dedicated macro rigs
   with specific ring lighting. A fundus camera produces an orange-red *medical* image; a slit lamp is
   different again. **If the art depends on the capture rig, the clinic channel does not exist and only the
   studios do** — and the studios' own processing IS their product, so they will not outsource it.
2. **"Nobody is doing it" is weak evidence of an opening and strong evidence of a wall.** The file has applied
   this discipline repeatedly. A category with obvious demand and no clinic-channel entrant probably has a
   reason.
3. **Medical images are patient data.** Moving a retinal image to a third party for a non-medical purpose
   needs consent and possibly a data agreement — friction, and adjacent to C4.
4. **An eye exam is not an emotional moment.** Every prior channel candidate leaned on one. This one leans on
   beauty alone, which is closer to *decoration* — the half `THE INACCESSIBLE-SUBJECT SCREEN` warns loses.
5. **Partner count** — the perennial wall. 42 units/month is small, but it still needs partners.

### The test, and it costs nothing
**Question 1 answers questions 2–5.** Obtain sample images of the three types clinics actually capture
(iris macro, fundus/retinal, slit-lamp), run each through a candidate art pipeline, and put the outputs
beside the studio work at $309. **Bar: would a reasonable person pay $150+ for this print?** If the answer
needs the studio rig, the candidate is dead and the sweep returns empty. **No money, no phone, no partner
required — she can settle the crux alone in a day.**
