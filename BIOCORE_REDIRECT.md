## 1. Why the plan as written cannot be run

It is a **physical import and distribution business**: buy Korean professional ampoules FOB, land
them in São Paulo, sell to estheticians. Good plan. Not her plan.

| The plan requires | Her constraint |
|---|---|
| **R$80–150K starting capital**, first order $12–18K | **A few hundred US dollars.** This alone is **50–75× out** |
| *"Start the Instagram account today — just her face, talking about Korean aesthetic trends, posting daily"* | **PERMANENTLY FACELESS.** Absolute |
| *"Book a flight to AESTHETICA… 40 conversations on Day 1"*, plus scripted booth interviews | **Never speaks to anyone.** Absolute |
| *"Runs a 2-hour protocol masterclass once per quarter"* | Speaking, in a room, to people |
| *"Part-time content/admin assistant: R$3,5K"* | **No employees, ever** |
| Inventory, import, customs broker, 3PL, AFE, ANVISA notification, **RT farmacêutico at R$2,5K/month** | Physical-goods veto at maximum intensity, plus a mandatory licensed professional |
| Month 7–9 before she pays herself *"meaningfully"*; target at month 18–24 | Month 8 at R$3.000 is her stated failure line |

**Seven absolute vetoes. This is not a repair candidate — it is somebody else's business.**

## 2. But the document names the opportunity, twice, and looks straight past it

> *"Do you provide ANVISA technical dossier support? **Critical — saves you R$15–25K in regulatory
> consulting** if they do."*
>
> *"**Hire an importação consultant** for 4 hours (R$800–1.500) to walk her through the AFE + ANVISA
> + customs process before she commits any capital. **This single call prevents 80% of the expensive
> mistakes** first-timers make."*

**Both lines are written from the buyer's chair, as costs to be dodged. Read them from the seller's
chair and they are a business:**

> **The regulatory dossier is expensive, document-shaped, mandatory, per-product, recurring, and
> nobody wants to do it. The importer pays R$15–25K to make it go away — and it needs no inventory,
> no capital, no premises and no face.**

**The plan's whole risk is inventory. The dossier has none of it.**

## 4. The shape that could fit her — stated as a hypothesis, not a conclusion

**She cannot hold the licensed roles.** The EU Responsible Person must be established in the EU. The
Cosmetic Product Safety Report must be signed by a qualified safety assessor. **Both are closed to
her, permanently, and the plan must never pretend otherwise.**

**The open question — the only one that matters — is whether a PREPARER layer exists beneath them:**

> **Ingredient screening against the EU Annexes. Claims screening against Regulation 655/2013.
> Translation of Korean technical documentation into the format Brussels expects. Label element
> compliance per market. Assembly of the Product Information File around the assessor's signed
> report. Chasing certificates the Korean manufacturer has not supplied.**
>
> **None of that is licence-reserved. All of it is document work. And the buyer might be the brand —
> or, more interestingly, the Responsible Person firms themselves, who are the ones drowning in
> badly-documented Korean dossiers.**

**And note what this is:** it is **candidate 66's machinery** — structured document work, per-market
rule sets, localisation, an acceptance test — **pointed at the highest-rule-density, fastest-growing,
highest-margin vertical available.** The competitor council seat said it in as many words:

> *"She goes vertical. Own one vertical's judgement completely, and charge $5.000 without anyone
> blinking. **That is the version I would genuinely fear.**"*

## 5. What is being measured now

Two agents are running against the two things that decide it:

1. **Does the preparer layer exist, and what does it pay?** Published prices for RP service, CPNP
   notification, PIF compilation, INCI screening, label review, MoCRA registration — and whether RP
   firms and consultancies subcontract preparation, at what rate.
2. **What document work in this industry is paid, recurring, and NOT licence-reserved** — including
   the Fiverr and Upwork evidence, where review counts prove real demand rather than assert it.

**Both agents are instructed to destroy their own findings in the last third**, and the specific
adversarial case is already written for them: *this is a licensed profession with no unlicensed
layer; liability is indivisible so RP firms do it all in-house; brands buy the RP and get the
paperwork thrown in free; and a faceless foreign non-expert cannot sell anything with the word
"regulatory" on it.*

**If that case holds, this dies and it dies quickly.** If it does not, this is a better vertical than
anything in the file — because the demand is measured, dated, growing 27% a year, and its own trade
press says the bottleneck is paper.

## 6. What I am not doing

**Not touching the consumer or clinic side.** Selling to estheticians needs Instagram, a face,
congresses and product. Selling protocols or marketing to clinics needs her believed as an expert —
her own veto, and Law 7's vise on regulated professional advertising.

**Not touching anything with a vial in it.**
