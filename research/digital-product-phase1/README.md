# Digital downloadable product — Phase 1 research

Three parallel research agents, run 6 Aug 2026, against six candidate downloadable
products for a faceless Brazilian founder selling into the USA/UK/EU at US$250–750.

Every claim in these files is tagged `[VERIFIED]` (primary source fetched, URL given),
`[PLAUSIBLE]` (secondary source only) or `[UNKNOWN]`.

| File | What it settles |
|---|---|
| `adoption_format.md` | Which delivery formats a non-technical buyer can actually use — and why a Claude Skill is the **worst** option, not the obvious one |
| `outbound_reach.md` | Whether each candidate's purchase trigger is **visible from outside**, which decides whether outbound is possible at all |
| `competitor_sweep.md` | The cheapest *adequate* substitute for each candidate, with live prices |

## The three findings that matter

**1. AI belongs on the seller's side of the transaction, not the buyer's.**
A Claude Skill needs a paid plan, code execution enabled, and an org-admin toggle on
Team/Enterprise — the exact approval step the US$250–750 price band exists to avoid.
Worse, Anthropic's own documentation instructs buyers to *"use Skills only from trusted
sources: those you created yourself or obtained from Anthropic."* A cold-emailed ZIP from
an unknown vendor is what that sentence exists to stop.

The "Anthropic Skills Marketplace with revenue share" widely reported for May 2026 is
**unverified** — `marketplace.anthropic.com` does not resolve, and no primary source exists.

Best formats, in order: a template pack for software the buyer already owns; a PDF system
plus a formulas-only spreadsheet; one self-contained `.html` opened locally.

**2. The trigger must be visible from outside, or fire often enough that being remembered
is enough.** Because the buyer is on no marketplace and does not know the category exists,
outbound is the only channel — so the business rests on whether the founder can see that a
named company has the problem *now*.

Untriggered cold email converts ~0,2–0,5%, needing 3.200–8.000 emails/month for 16 sales.
Triggered converts ~1,5–3%, needing 530–1.070. A new domain safely sends 600–1.000/month.
**The trigger is what makes the required volume physically sendable by one person.**

**3. Five of the six candidates died on price, not on idea quality.** The ceiling is the
cheapest *adequate* substitute — a free spreadsheet that does 80% is adequate.

## Verdicts

| Candidate | Verdict | Killed by |
|---|---|---|
| LINELOCK | **KILLED** | Adequate substitutes at $0, $19,99 and $79,99; the claimed differentiator is a marketed feature of ManageArtworks ($399/mo, self-serve); invisible trigger |
| RIO BLOCKS | **KILLED** | Stella Luna & Co ships the identical package; DXF at $3; needed a named patternmaker; trigger fires once per buyer ever |
| CREDIT LAB | **KILLED** | ELFA $475–500, CFI $497/**year**; needs a Python runtime and the buyer's own LLM inside a bank; worst approval-threshold exposure |
| PITCH & PAID | **DEAD** | Etsy sells the same at $20,55; B2C; it is a prompt file |
| WRAPCHECK | **WOUNDED → dead on trigger** | Free Google Sheet + COUNTIF is adequate; trigger is invisible, monthly at best, and useful only for the hours the set is live |
| BUYERROOM | **SURVIVES, wounded** | Rehearsal commoditised (Yoodli $8/mo; a real ex-buyer at $300 already inside ECRM/RangeMe); list only 240–540 brands/year |

## The unclaimed ground

> No dedicated retail-buyer conversation simulator exists, and everything AI-adjacent in
> CPG pitch prep is analytics — *what to say*, not rehearsing it. And nobody sells the
> **numbers-verification** half: machine-checking the brand's own deck for contradictory
> margins, wrong case counts, unsupported claims. **That combination is unclaimed.**

That fragment is strong for reasons the rehearsal half is not: it produces an **artifact**,
its value is **objectively provable** ("it found N errors"), it needs **no LLM on the buyer's
side**, and it attaches to a **visible dated trigger**.

## Two corrections to the source dossier

- RangeMe's "15.000" is **buyers, not brands** (~90.000 brands are on the platform).
- RangeMe *Immediate Opportunities* is a **retailer-side** feed: it names the retailer,
  category and deadline but never which supplier brands applied or were selected. It says
  which categories are hot; it does not say whom to email. **ECRM, not RangeMe, is the
  trigger list.**
