### Why the two died — the same death, twice

**Lien-Waiver.** Levelset (acquired by Procore, ~US$500M) publishes a **free** lien-deadline
calculator, **free** state-specific preliminary notice forms and **free** lien waiver creation — as
SEO lead-gen pages targeting *exactly* the long-tail keywords the candidate named as its only
channel. The incumbent gives the product away **and owns the road to the buyer.** Secondary: real
unauthorised-practice exposure — Texas bars non-attorneys from charging to prepare instruments
affecting title to real property, including release of lien.

**Amazon Reinstatement.** The substitute is not the US$1.000–2.500 agency. It is **US$30–100 Fiverr
freelancers** and **free custom GPTs** producing the identical deliverable. Priced 2–6× above the
human and infinitely above the free AI.

Both carried `trigger: NONE`. That field has now predicted the outcome in every case.

## The survivor, and the prescription that matters

**Trademark office-action response tooling**, sold to paralegals and junior associates at small
trademark-prosecution firms. The red team refused the one-time static form and prescribed:

> Abandon the one-time static library. Ship it maintained — updates pushed at each TMEP revision and
> USPTO form/fee change, **turning the shelf-life liability into the retention mechanism** — plus a
> TSDR-derived near-real-time office-action alert as the moment-of-need trigger.

**This is the same inversion the unlock sweep derived independently for an unrelated market:** a
published schedule of rule changes destroys a static file and *funds* a subscription. Two blind
processes, one conclusion.

Supporting facts it surfaced: USPTO retired TEAS — the Trademark Center is now the only filing path
(verified 10 Aug 2026) — which is precisely the drift a maintained product monetises and a static one
dies of. Firms bill US$300–1.500 per office-action response at attorney rates of US$200–600/hour, so
the buyer compares the price to billable capacity. And there is **no unauthorised-practice problem,
because the buyer is the lawyer.**

## Hunt B — the external model's memo

Recommended **DeductionDelta**: a US$299/month self-serve KeHE deduction variance worksheet for small
US CPG brands.

**Genuinely better than my own work in two places.** Its test design uses a *paid* gate with second-
month prepayment — *"Intent does not count; only a second prepayment does"* — which measures what
matters, unlike the 60-email test I kept proposing. And it separated *contrary evidence* from *mere
unknowns* explicitly, killing CloseoutPack on PDFsam/Bluebeam/Submittal.com pricing and
CapacityPromise on OrderRules pricing plus Shopify gating checkout extensions to Plus stores.

**But it fails its own Question 2.** By its own text: iNymbus offers a **free** audit, SPS Commerce
advertises a **no-cost KeHE audit**, and K-Solve already lets suppliers search, sort, export, inspect
documentation and file disputes. That is the free-automatic-guard pattern that killed the tariff
index — present as an established fact, not an open question. Its money story also rests on a single
vendor-reported customer at a brand with tens of millions in sales, and its 850-prospect floor is
unverified.

**Reordering fix:** run its own cheapest kill first — request the iNymbus and SPS free audits as a
prospective supplier and see what they deliver. US$0, one day, before any email or code.
