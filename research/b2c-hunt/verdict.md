## VERDICT: one candidate is worth putting beside ADV-CHECK, but only as a fast, cheap, high-information launch — not as a proven path to R$30.000/month.

**PICK: AGENCY ATLAS — the verified model-agency submission database.** I am merging AGENCY ATLAS and SIGNED KIT, which are the same product reached from two lenses (a compiled, license-checked, per-agency submission-requirements corpus for aspiring/working models). Two independent lenses converging on one corpus is mild positive evidence; keeping them as two candidates was double-counting.

### What it is (two sentences)
A downloadable, filterable database of model and talent agencies — for each one: how they actually accept submissions, what digitals/measurements they require, height/age/board rules, tattoo and size policy, whether they accept foreigners, and whether their talent-agency licence is publicly verifiable, each row dated and linked to the source page. Sold as regional editions (US, UK/EU, Asia-for-foreigners, and a Portuguese Brazil edition) plus a global bundle, as an instant download on Gumroad and Etsy.

### Who buys, and the exact moment
An aspiring or newly working model (16-28), or a parent, in the week they decide to submit — sitting with a spreadsheet of half-scam listicle results, about to email 40 agencies, afraid of both rejection for a wrong-format submission and of a pay-upfront "agency". Secondary and higher-intent: a working model targeting Tokyo/Milan/Seoul who needs to know which agencies take foreigners.

### Demand evidence — verified vs not
**Verified by fetch (this session and the check pass):** dense live Google autocomplete families from `suggestqueries.google.com` — `legit modeling agencies near me / for kids / nyc / for beginners / reddit`; `modeling agencies that accept short models / tattoos / 5'5 / all sizes / 13 year olds`; `do real modeling agencies charge money`; `modeling in japan for foreigners`; `modeling agencies in korea that accept foreigners`; plus city-level completions across a dozen US cities. Competitive field, all fetched directly by me just now: `modelindex.gumroad.com/l/GlobalFashionAgencyList` = **US$5.00**, `modelindex.gumroad.com/l/ModelLaunchKit` = **US$20.00**, `actory.gumroad.com/l/LAagentdirectory` (200+ verified LA agencies, actors) = **US$20.00**. `dir.ca.gov/dlse/dlse-databases.htm` → the California DLSE public talent-agency licence search exists, so licence verification is a real scriptable check. `pgdirect.com` (Peter Glenn, the US$29.95 paid directory since 1956) is a 114-byte parked page — the paid incumbent is dead.

**My spot-check materially changed one thing, and it is the load-bearing fact.** I pulled the embedded product JSON from those pages: **every one of them has `ratings: {count: 0}`** — the US$5 list, the US$20 Model Launch Kit, Model Index's US$25 development guide, and the US$20 LA actor directory. And `gumroad.com/products/search?query=modeling agency list` returns **total = 9**, none of them Model Index — the exact-match incumbent is invisible in its own marketplace's search.

**Not verified, and it is the thing that would settle this:** Etsy. Every route (search, /market, listings, curl, WebFetch, jina.ai proxy) returns 403 behind DataDome — confirmed independently in three separate check passes. Amazon KDP search is behind an Akamai interstitial. Reddit's API is blocked. So **no purchase of a compiled agency directory has been observed anywhere, by anyone, at any price.** What is verified is intent, not money. Treat that as a heavy discount, not a kill — and note it applies to *every* candidate in this round except FLOW DECK.

### N6 at the proposed price
The competitor is the **US$20 Model Launch Kit**, not a SaaS. It is a ~100-agency list plus a narrative guide, no per-agency requirement fields, no licence check, no stats filtering, no Brazil/Asia editions, no compile date. That is a partial substitute, so per N5 it does not kill the price point — but it caps it. **Price: US$29 per regional edition, US$49 global bundle.** At US$29 (≈US$25,50 net) the target needs ~300 sales/month; at US$49 ~160. The channel does not plausibly supply that in months 1-3. Honest projection: **US$300-1.500/month** from a first edition, with the target a 6-12 month multi-edition-plus-KDP-print question. Say that to her plainly.

### Which of ADV-Check's four weaknesses it fixes
**B4 only, and decisively.** ADV-Check's first dollar is behind cold email from a reputationless domain at an unknown reply rate — weeks, unbounded. This one's first dollar is behind a listing in a query stream I have verified is live, and the whole experiment costs under US$20 and ten days. **B1 is not fixed** — search intent is verified, purchase is not; that is the same class of ignorance as ADV-Check's unmeasured mismatch rate. **B2 is not fixed** — a model applies once; repeat purchase is near zero, same permanent-hunt shape. **B3 is worse than ADV-Check** — three verified comparables at US$5-20 sit under the proposed price. Reachable demand is larger than ADV-Check's fixed 16.779 advisers, but I have no fetched number for it, so I will not claim a multiple.

### Is her background real here, or decoration?
**Genuinely load-bearing — the only candidate in the set where it is.** The content that makes this more than a scraped list is: what digitals agencies actually accept versus what blogs say, measurement-sheet conventions, mother-agency and board mechanics, the scam patterns aimed specifically at young women, and the Brazil-to-international pipeline. The Portuguese Brazil edition is uncontested and no US Etsy seller can produce it. Her face and name never appear; credibility comes from dated, source-linked rows. (By contrast: ESTATE RUNBOOK's founder edge is zero, and its author admits it.)

### The single cheapest test
**Two stages, US$20 total, under two weeks.**
Stage 0, free, 30 minutes: **she** opens Etsy in a browser from São Paulo — the wall that blocked every automated check does not block a human — and searches `modeling agency list`, `model agency submission`, `modeling digitals`, recording for the top 20 listings: price, review count, and whether it is an *information* product or a *design template*. This resolves the one unknown that has defeated four verification passes.
Stage 1, ~US$20, ten days: build **one** edition only — 60 US agencies with real per-agency submission specs and licence status — list it on Gumroad (free) and Etsy (US$0,20 × 5 listing variants), plus three free SEO pages on the verified long-tail (`modeling agencies that accept tattoos`, `modeling agencies in tokyo for foreigners`, `do modeling agencies charge money`).
**Pass threshold: 5 paid sales in 21 days at US$29 with zero promotion.** That proves a stranger-supplied channel converts at this price, and only then does she build the 800-row global corpus. **Fail: 0-1 sales in 21 days → do not build the corpus.** Build-time kill criterion, separate from sales: while compiling the first 60 agencies, count how many have requirements that differ materially from generic free blog advice. **If fewer than half do, the product has no content edge and should be abandoned even if it sells** — that is the one number here that is genuinely measurable and nobody has measured.

### Weakest link, one sentence
Every compiled agency directory I could actually inspect — three of them, at US$5, US$20 and US$20 — has zero ratings and near-zero marketplace search visibility, so willingness to pay for this specific product is verified nowhere; only the searching is.

Worth *recording* as the strongest B2C demand shape found, not worth building second.
- **THE PRODUCTION BOOK (US$34 wedding)** — the cheapest adequate-ish substitute was fetched at **US$0,99**; Etsy's most saturated digital category; N4 clears only on craft, imitable in a month.
- **PRODUÇÃO KIT (US$29)** — nine free-template SEO competitors including Rundown Studio's 15 free CSVs (no signup), Notion's category is 591 templates and free-dominated, paid comparables show 0-1 ratings. The revival is honest but the free layer does each slice of the job.

**The meta-finding, which is worth as much as the pick:** loosening to B2C and marketplaces did not produce a hell-yes, because it moved the unknown rather than removing it. B2B's unknown was "will a stranger reply to cold email"; B2C's unknown is "will a stranger buy from an unreviewed listing" — and the one marketplace where that question is answerable (Etsy) is bot-walled to every automated method, so it can only be answered by her, in a browser, in 30 minutes. That reconnaissance should happen before another analysis round is commissioned.
