# Bancada 100 — assessment, and what to keep (11 Aug 2026)

**Verdict on the business: not hers.** Four reasons, in severity order.

1. **LAW 0 fires, and the document supplies the evidence itself.** Distributors and retail technical
   centres already fund professional haircare education *because they earn margin on the products* —
   Ikesaki with 50 free courses, Danny with weekly **brand-titled** courses. The plan concedes they
   control *"the venue, the audience, the enrollment data, the sponsorship rights, the product shelf,
   existing brand relationships."* They are not a later partner. They are the free incumbent, and they
   own the shelf the brand needs anyway.
2. **Two-sided cold start.** Gate 1 needs 12 unrelated Hosts and 20 class dates in 14 days; Gate 2
   needs 3 prepaid brands from 75 prequalified in 30 days — both from zero, simultaneously, by one
   faceless person. This is what killed CrossHire Circle. Calling the network "closed" does not change
   the physics.
3. **The autonomy claim does not survive its own state machine.** *"Under four total human hours per
   flight"* against product preflight, Anvisa checks, allocation across 8–11 classes, a multi-address
   shipping manifest, delivery and lot verification, Host training, proof scoring, anomaly review,
   make-good, payout and reporting — plus a human call on every first brand purchase. Physical product
   to eleven addresses on a deadline is per-transaction founder labour.
4. **The brand's ROI rests on an unmeasured conversion:** 100 mannequin demos → 5–10 wholesale
   accounts. Unsourced, and it sits on the *buyer's* side of the table, so she cannot test it cheaply.

# WHAT TO KEEP — this is the valuable part, and it is portable to any candidate

## 1. THE PREFILLED-CLAIM ACQUISITION ENGINE — the strongest idea in the document

Do not ask a prospect to sign up and fill an empty form. **An AI process finds their public activity —
Instagram, Google, Sympla, their own site — and builds a page that is already about them:** their
business name, their real class, the date, the city, the topic, the public source. Then:

> *"Your class on [date] may qualify for a paid 12-minute Product Lab. At 15 verified professionals the
> expected payment is R$725. May I send you the prefilled page?"*

**This is the screenshot sale applied to acquisition rather than to selling** — the first contact
carries an artefact about *them*, not a pitch — and it dissolves the blank-form problem that kills
every cold start. **Portable to every candidate in this file, and it should be the default cold-call
shape from now on.**

## 2. THE AI / DETERMINISTIC BOUNDARY — the cleanest statement of it yet found

> **AI for:** finding public signals, prefilling records, extracting information, classifying, drafting
> messages, summarising anomalies.
> **Deterministic rules — never AI — for:** eligibility, deduplication, **money**, deadlines, payouts,
> completion, refund calculations.

Same principle as DeckProof's *"margins never come from pixels"*, generalised: **AI reads and drafts;
code decides and calculates money.** This is the design rule that makes a product safe to sell without
domain expertise, and it should govern any build.

## 3. THE SELF-DISQUALIFYING BUYER CALCULATOR

Before checkout the buyer computes, themselves: *accounts needed to break even = price ÷ contribution
per account*, and is **rejected** if the number is unreachable. Paired with an automatic rejection list
(prototypes, therapeutic claims, unregularised products, anyone demanding raw attendee lists or
guaranteed sales).

**This is the buyer-side proof-of-loss idea made self-serve** — the buyer produces the solvency
evidence instead of us estimating it, and disqualifies themselves with arithmetic before consuming any
of her time.

## 4. Smaller, still worth taking

- **Two-stage onboarding** — two minutes to claim, eight minutes only for the promising. Protects her
  time by construction.
- **The exception console** — name the small set of cases that reach a human; everything else advances
  on deterministic rules. This is the concrete design pattern for *"no per-transaction founder labour"*.
- **The timer state machine** — T−21, T−14, T−10, T−7, T−2, event day, T+1, T+7, day 45, day 60.
  Anything with a deadline needs this shape.
- **Concentration cap** — *"no Host should represent more than 25% of one campaign."*
- **Referral bounties paid on the OUTCOME, not the introduction** — R$150 after another Host completes a
  valid activation; R$750–1.000 to a private-label manufacturer **after the brand pays**.
- **The private-label-manufacturer insight, generalised:** *find whoever touches your buyer at the exact
  moment of need but does not compete with you.* A manufacturer meets a challenger brand precisely when
  it needs distribution and credibility.
- **The Brazilian no-code stack, named:** Softr/Glide/WeWeb · Airtable · **Supabase with row-level
  security** · Fillout/Tally · **Make or n8n** · **Clicksign** (the Brazilian contract standard) ·
  **Asaas** (Pix, boleto and payouts) · Metabase. *"Seven-day no-code build; custom software waits until
  volume justifies it."* Directly usable by her, with the right Brazilian rails.
- **Gate 4's wording, adopted verbatim as the standard pass bar:** *"Interest, deposits, positive
  feedback and 'we would use this again' do not count."*
- **And the closing honesty** — *"If brands cap their willingness around R$12.000 while Hosts require
  approximately R$6.000 per 100 professionals, the two sides do not economically overlap, and the model
  should be abandoned rather than converted into a coordination-heavy agency."* A document that names
  its own throat is worth more than one that does not.
