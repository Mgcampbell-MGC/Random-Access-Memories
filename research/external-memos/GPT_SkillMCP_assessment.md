# Assessment — "Sol Skill/MCP Business Hunt" (10 Aug 2026)

**Verdict: the best external document received. Its five kills are worth more than its survivor, and
its survivor is an agency that dies on Law 0 and on per-transaction human work. One idea inside it
generalises further than the document itself claims, and that idea is the real prize.**

## It killed the Expediting Desk independently — with two competitors we never found

Beyond **Object & Order** (free, no paid plan, funded by 5% goods margin), it names:

- **Alcove** — *"extracts proposed changes from vendor email and applies them after review"*
- **Techstyles** — *"watches vendor email, updates PO timelines, summarizes actions, and drafts replies"*

So the space had **three** occupants, one of them free. Our seventeen-agent sweep found none. This is
the third independent confirmation that the sweep's price-shaped blind spot (Law C) was the failure,
not bad luck.

## MCP Release Sprint — KILL, and the document nearly says so itself

A US$4.900 productized service delivering a merge-ready MCP release PR for API-first SaaS companies:
tool-surface memo, OAuth resource-server adapter, narrow scopes, negative tests, cross-client
compatibility matrix. US$2.450 non-refundable deposit, no calls, customer deploys.

The document is honest — **"PAID TEST ONLY", "not build approval"** — and records that it **fails its
own Q1** with Q2, Q5 and Q10 unresolved. Four things finish it:

**1. LAW 0 — the clearing party exists and is already funding this to zero.** The API-tooling
platforms earn on the SDK relationship, so they give MCP generation away: **Stainless ships it on the
free tier.** The model providers publish the SDKs, quickstarts, registry and security guidance free
because MCP adoption *is* their distribution. The document's own §2 concedes *"the raw server is
absolutely commoditized"* — and the claimed residual wedge (curation, authority boundaries, negative
tests, compatibility evidence) is precisely the layer those funded platforms absorb next, because it
is their differentiator, not a gap.

**2. Per-transaction human work, and the human is her.** Every sale is bespoke engineering on a
stranger's repository. That is the shape that killed **Aval** (a per-transaction step at ~1.440
reception desks) and **REAVER** (which outsourced every submission). Here it is worse: she *is* the
step. The document's own closing line concedes it — *"If Sol must manually operate delivery after the
PR, this is an agency and fails the intended model."*

**3. The trust requirement is maximal and her trust position is zero.** The document names this as
its own likeliest failure: a US/UK API company paying a faceless unknown US$2.450 for
**security-adjacent work on their repository** with no live call. A single security questionnaire ends
it, and answering one is a conversation she is not permitted to have.

**4. Worst possible buyer.** An API-first SaaS with 11–200 employees has engineers holding Claude
Code and Codex. MCP hardening against a written spec is exactly what those tools now do well. The
document's own commodity gate — *abandon if two freelancers deliver the hardened scope for ≤US$500 in
three days* — is very likely to fire.

Add the arithmetic: 24 one-shot wins a year at US$4.900, needing 600–1.200 qualified contacts. Same
treadmill that defeated every one-shot candidate in this file.

## THE IDEA WORTH KEEPING — and it generalises further than the document claims

Buried in the recommendation:

> *"MCP Release Sprint is the only one where current free automation **exposes — rather than
> erases — a separately checkable acceptance layer.**"*

That is a real and general mechanism, and it is the same thing that made DeckProof survive:

> **WHEN GENERATION BECOMES FREE, VERIFICATION BECOMES SCARCE.**
> If anyone can produce an artifact in seconds, nobody can tell which artifacts are *correct* — and
> the volume of unverified artifacts rises exactly as fast as the cost of making them falls.

Why this is the strongest position available to this founder, tested against the whole law set:

- **Law 1 cannot reach it.** Checking is not content. There is no publishable artifact, because the
  output is a fact about *this* document, on *this* day.
- **Law 0 usually cannot reach it.** The party who profits from generation being free has no
  incentive to tell you their output is wrong. Stainless will not ship "your generated server is
  unsafe"; RangeMe will not tell you your deck contradicts your cost sheet.
- **R4 does not bind.** A discrepancy verifies itself — two values agree or they do not.
- **No credential.** Verification against the customer's own inputs asserts nothing about the world.
- **And the supply of work grows on its own**, because free generation is increasing the number of
  unchecked artifacts every month.

**This is now a first-class lens for the running hunt:** find a place where free AI generation has
recently multiplied the volume of artifacts, and where being wrong costs money.

## Methods adopted from this document

1. **The commodity gate, exactly as specified.** Seed one public fixture with deliberate defects, give
   the identical fixed scope to three freelancers, abandon if two deliver for ≤US$500 in three days.
   *"Cheap promises without repository evidence do not count."* And the line that should govern every
   test here: **"Do not reinterpret a failure as 'we need better positioning.'"**
2. **A hard pass-threshold definition.** *"Positive replies, video views, 'interested,' free pilots,
   and refundable reservations do not count."* Our tests have never been this strict.
3. **The non-refundable deposit as substitute for a measured loss.** Where no universal dollar loss
   can be verified, require a non-refundable deposit as proof of allocated budget instead of claiming
   an ROI. **Two independent external documents have now converged on this** — the CotaMRO memo asked
   the buyer to produce a prior lost quotation; this one asks for cash. Adopt it as the standard
   Gate 0 substitute.
4. **UNVERIFIED tagging on gross-vs-net.** It flags US$10.290 as *gross before Brazilian/US tax,
   Stripe fees, tools, refunds, insurance and founder time.* Every arithmetic in this repo should
   carry that line.
