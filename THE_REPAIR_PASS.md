# THE REPAIR PASS — six candidates, one repair

**17 Aug 2026. Instead of hunting, take the candidates that genuinely fit her and apply the parts.**

> **Every one of the six repaired to the same structure. They are not six candidates. They are one
> business with six verticals.**

## 1. The repair, candidate by candidate

### Candidate 70 — written memorial → funeral homes

| What was broken | The part that repairs it |
|---|---|
| **4 hours of writing per unit** | **G2 PASSPORT — qualify the HOME once**, not each family: their voice, package tiers, intake form, turnaround SLA, approval path, branding. Family #1 costs hours; family #20 costs minutes |
| Must be believed as a writer | **G10 EXPERT BUYER — the director is the expert.** Her pitch is not *"I write beautifully."* It is *"you will never miss a deadline and never write another one at 11pm"* |
| How to pitch it | **G5 INVISIBLE DEPARTMENT** — *"You own the family. We are your invisible writing department."* Triple-confirmed wording, and Full-Circle already sells this channel with *"We do the hard work. You get the credit."* |
| Privacy nervousness | **G12 CUSTODY REFUSAL AS A FEATURE** — *"We never touch your case-management system or hold family data. Everything arrives and leaves as a document."* In a channel this privacy-sensitive, that is a selling line |
| Finding homes to approach | **G3 TRIGGER GRAPH** — observable commitments: joined Life Story Network · bought aftercare software · added "celebration of life" or "life tribute" to their site · advertises personalised services |
| Validation design | **G17 — twenty real instances with a count.** Approach 20 homes, count how many will read a sample |

**Still unrepaired:** whether a director will onboard **entirely in writing.** Aftercare.com selling at
$150/month with *"no contracts"* suggests self-serve exists in this channel, but it is unverified. **This
is the C1 test at channel level, and it is the thing that killed the referral play.**

### The deadline document (eulogy · speech · vows) — direct

**Broken: the channel.** Speechy and The Eulogy Writers sell from their own sites on years of SEO. On
Fiverr the same work is $20 and the ceiling-holder law says she gets the floor.

**The repair is not to fix the channel. It is to stop selling direct.**

> **This is not a separate candidate. It is candidate 70's retail twin, and its channel problem is solved
> by candidate 70's channel.** Sell it wholesale through the people who already stand next to the deadline.

**And it opens a second partner population nobody has looked at: wedding celebrants and officiants.** They
own the couple, they face a fixed date, they deliver *words*, and many of them dislike writing. Speechy
charges **£445 for a ceremony script.** An officiant could resell at a fraction and still make margin.

### Genealogy book → professional genealogists

| Broken | Repair |
|---|---|
| StoryTerrace's ceiling (~25 books/month with 600 writers) hangs over book-length products | That is a **direct-to-consumer, interview-driven, $3.250** product. Selling wholesale to a genealogist **who already did the research** is a different, cheaper, faster product. The ceiling may not bind — **but this is an assumption, not a finding** |
| 4 hours per book | **G2 — qualify the GENEALOGIST once:** their research format, GEDCOM export, client package, branding |
| Credibility | **G10 — the genealogist is the expert.** She never claims genealogical expertise. She is writing capacity |
| Is the information on her side of the table? | **G6, CORRECTED INVERSION CHECK.** The genealogist *has* the GEDCOM and the research and **cannot turn it into narrative in an afternoon.** Passes the corrected test where it would have looked marginal under the blunt one |

### Milestone birthday, multi-copy

| Broken | Repair |
|---|---|
| **$899 is a jewelry-shaped bet** — Shutterfly's recession-proof AOV was $30,74; real jewelry PCE fell −19% | Price down, and lean on the per-head frame: **$12,84 a head is not a luxury object** |
| Etsy's clock plus post-purchase collaboration | **The red team's only two doors:** a finished fast-shipping SKU on Etsy with the bespoke version sold off-Etsy, **or** a 10-week EDD that kills the occasion |
| Which door | **G10 — party planners, event venues, milestone photographers.** They own the customer and the date. That removes the Etsy clock entirely |

**Weakest of the six on the recession filter.** Keep, don't lead.

### Before You (pregnancy)

**Broken, fatally as structured: the giver buys and the mother does the work.** Etsy's clock starts the day
after checkout and never waits for her.

> **G10 dissolves it completely. Sell through maternity photographers, doulas and childbirth educators.**
> The doula sells to the **mother**, who is already engaged and already talking to her. **The gift framing
> was the bug.** There is no giver, no stalled third party, no non-delivery case.

### The filled-in Nokbox

| Broken | Repair |
|---|---|
| Everplans and Trustworthy give the empty container away at $0–99/yr | **G10 — sell wholesale to estate attorneys, financial advisors and insurance agents.** The advisor is not comparing you to Everplans. They are comparing you to **their own unbillable hours** |
| Pitch | **G5** — *"You own the client. We're your invisible documentation department"* |

> **FLAG, HONESTLY: this one may fail C4 in spirit.** The artefact is *where everything is* — account
> locations, document locations, possibly numbers. Even if the client fills the form, **she would be
> holding a map to a stranger's assets.** That is closer to custody than anything else on this board.
> **Do not build it before that is resolved.**

### Fiverr

**Unrepaired.** The ceiling-holder law is structural: the top of every category is held with a credential
and a name. **It stays a cash channel, not a business** — though at $20 it could cheaply test whether
anyone buys written memorials at all. That measures *demand*, never *economics*.

## 4. The honest risk list

1. **Will a professional onboard entirely in writing?** Unverified. This is what killed the referral play,
   and no amount of design fixes it — it has to be observed.
2. **The chatbot.** Every one of these partners can generate a draft themselves, and Tribute Technology
   already auto-generates memorial books from obituary data. The only evidence the human premium holds is
   the Association of Ghostwriters' 61%-use-AI-but-7%-generate finding — **a perception with an expiry
   date. Anyone building here is racing a clock.**
3. **Price is unknown in every vertical.** Retail comparables exist ($275–575 for eulogies); **wholesale
   does not.**
4. **Passport reuse is a hypothesis.** If the second family inside a partner still costs four hours, the
   fourth key fails and this is a job.

---

## 5. Sequence, and the first test

**Lead with funeral homes.** Agent 6 verified the channel already buys third-party vendors, already sells
written life stories, and **has no vendor.** Demand is actuarial. Genealogists are second — the only
vertical with two independent channels.

**Two tests, both free, in this order:**

1. **The price.** Pull a **General Price List** — mandatory under the FTC Funeral Rule — from a Life Story
   Network member and find the Life Story line item. *The one number nobody publishes.*
2. **The Passport.** Write two memorials for the **same** hypothetical home from two real obituaries, and
   time both. **If the second takes less than half the first, the fourth key is real.** If it doesn't,
   this is a well-paid job and should be run as one.

**Do not build anything until test 2 has a number.** It is the whole thesis, and it costs an afternoon.
