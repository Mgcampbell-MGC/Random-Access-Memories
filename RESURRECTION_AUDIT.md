# Resurrection audit — every kill in the record, re-scored (10 August 2026)

The question asked three times and deferred three times: **is any past kill worth reopening with
new eyes?**

Answer: **three are, and the strongest of them was never a kill at all.** The rest stay dead, and
they stay dead for the right reason — each died on a Bucket A fact (an identical shipping product,
a named law, a measured wallet, a free automatic system), not on a judgement call.

---

## The re-scoring rule

Each kill is re-read against the tests that did not exist when it died:

- **Two-bucket rule** (6 Aug) — was the cause of death structural, or an adoption guess wearing the
  costume of rigour?
- **R4 correction** (6 Aug) — the credential only binds when the buyer *cannot verify the output*.
- **The lead-magnet mechanism** (10 Aug) — does the kill depend on the product being *content*?
- **The alert finding** (10 Aug) — was it sold to the party who profits from knowing?
- **Test A** (10 Aug) — was the competitor search run in the vendor's vocabulary?
- **Test B / the enumerability trap** (10 Aug) — was the population enumerable only because a
  regulator registers it, and did that regulator already ship the tool free?

## The three that reopen

### Tier 1 — DECKPROOF was never killed. It was abandoned mid-test.

`research/digital-product-decision/DECISION_DeckProof.md` is titled **FINAL PROPOSAL** and opens:
*"The decision: build DeckProof… at US$750, wedged on the supplement/HBC sellers named on ECRM's
published session rosters."* The parser gate then returned 2/14 against a 15/20 threshold — and the
test's own write-up concludes, **in bold: "Does not prove the product fails."**

Its two reasons were correct and were never rebutted:
1. It could only assemble **published marketing catalogs**, which omit wholesale pricing *for
   editorial reasons* — a different artifact from an internal buyer-meeting deck.
2. Three of fourteen were flattened images, called *"unrecoverable without OCR"* — in a test run
   with **pdfplumber**. That sentence has an expiry date, and it has passed.

The write-up then prescribed a design change (spreadsheet primary, PDF opportunistic, typed
fallback) and a **US$200 email test that was never run.** The session pivoted to the HTS hunt and
DeckProof was orphaned. It has been sitting in this repo as the standing decision ever since.

**And it passes every test built after it was shelved:**

| Test | DeckProof |
|---|---|
| Gate 0 — measured solvency | The buyer has **already paid US$7.000–16.900** to attend. Solvency is *observed*, not estimated — unique in this record |
| Lead-magnet mechanism | Not content. **Nobody can give away a computation on your confidential COGS as a blog post** |
| Test B — regulator built it | No regulator, no mandatory filing |
| Enumerability trap | Enumerable because they **bought a ticket** — the non-regulatory axis, exactly |
| Two enumerable populations | The buyer is the payer |
| Alert finding | The buyer profits from knowing; the signal is about their own meeting |
| R4 — can the buyer verify? | They check the arithmetic against their own numbers. Nothing rests on the author |
| Reserved profession | None |
| CAC arithmetic | US$750 × ~11/month. Cold outbound, ~200 emails/month against a new domain's safe 600–1.000 |
| Faceless | All written English, fully scripted. No calls, no camera, no name |

**Its one untested exposure is the one that has killed six candidates: the competitor sweep.** The
decision document tags its own sweep `PLAUSIBLE`, not `VERIFIED`. That sweep is running now, in
vendor vocabulary, along with a re-verification of the roster stream and an assessment of whether
ECRM already gives paid attendees the prep material free — which is the single most likely way it
dies.

### Tier 1 — the licitação POPULATION, with a different product

Recurso em 3 Dias died four times over. But the graveyard entry records, under *"What did NOT kill
it"*, the best buyer evidence in the entire record:

- **700.000 SICAF suppliers**; over **1 million public purchases worth ~R$1 trilhão in 2025**
- ME/EPP alone: **481.700 purchases worth R$272,6 bilhões** — an average around **R$566.000 per
  ME/EPP contract**
- **PNCP exposes CNPJ-level data through a no-login public API**, and atas name every participant

Critically: **this population is enumerable because it transacts, not because it is licensed.** It
is the non-regulatory axis, already verified, in Portuguese, where phone selling is permitted and
CPC is ~US$0,35.

What died was the *product* — legal drafting, which is reserved, commoditised and clock-blocked.
What did not die is that these companies stake **R$566.000 of contract value on a document full of
numbers they produced themselves**: the *planilha de custos e formação de preços*. Wrong numbers
there mean desclassificação. That is the DeckProof shape pointed at a population already proven.

**Not yet a candidate — a lead.** It needs the same treatment DeckProof is getting.

### Tier 2 — LINELOCK's kill was an artifact of the constraint set

LINELOCK died on the invisible trigger: *"the moment an agency sends artwork to print is invisible
from outside."* True. But **the requirement for a visible trigger is a consequence of "no audience,
therefore cold outreach," not a property of the market.** A subscription does not need a *moment*;
it needs a buyer with a *continuous* need — and every print job has one.

It is also, structurally, the DeckProof shape: it computes on the buyer's own files, its output is
self-verifying (two values differ, or they don't), and it is not content. Reopened as a lead, behind
the two above.

### Tier 3 — AGENCY ATLAS's 30-minute reconnaissance was never done

The B2C verdict prescribed a free, 30-minute, human-only step: **Sol opens Etsy in a browser from
São Paulo** — the wall that blocked every automated check does not block a person — and records
price, review count and product type for the top 20 listings. Four automated verification passes
failed at this; nobody asked her. It stays tier 3 because it is an information product (law #1),
US$29 breaks the price floor, and a model applies to agencies once. But the reconnaissance costs
nothing and answers a question that has defeated the machine three times.

## What the audit actually found — a product shape, not a candidate

All three reopened items are the same shape, and it is the first shape in this record that the
mechanism which killed six candidates **cannot touch**:

> **A tool that runs on files the buyer already has and cannot share, recomputes something the buyer
> is about to stake money on, and hands back a corrected artifact plus a discrepancy report.**

Why it is immune, stated precisely:

1. **The content is not the product — the buyer's own data is.** A funded incumbent's marketing
   department can give away a template, a checklist, a guide, a corpus or an alert feed, because
   producing those costs nothing. It cannot give away *a computation performed on a stranger's
   confidential cost sheet*, because there is nothing to publish. Law #1 has no purchase.
2. **The output is self-verifying.** Two numbers agree or they don't. The buyer checks it against
   documents they wrote. Nothing rests on the author's credential, so R4 does not bind and the
   founder's lack of domain expertise is genuinely irrelevant.
3. **No regulator wants the data**, so Test B does not fire.
4. **It is engineering, not content** — which is exactly the asymmetry this founder has, because her
   build cost is one-time and near zero while a marketing department's content cost is also near
   zero. Against content she has no edge. Against software she has parity, and against a solo
   competitor she has speed.

Sixteen candidates were an information or tooling product helping a professional complete a
recurring task. **Not one of them computed on the buyer's private data.** DeckProof did — and it is
the one that was never killed.
