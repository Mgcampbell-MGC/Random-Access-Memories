# THE CEILING-HOLDER LAW, and the Veto Law's failure condition

**17 Aug 2026. Agent 11 — dispatched to test the Veto Law corollary against four private-party vetoes.
All four died; one died differently, and the difference is the useful output.**

---

## 1. Insurance agency perpetuation — the purest corollary kill in the record

**The candidate veto-holders are the ones paying to give the artefact away.**

**The PIA Partnership** — a carrier council — launched **Agency Journey Mapping**, a perpetuation-planning
programme covering valuation, internal and external succession, and contingency buy-sell agreements, with
an on-demand library, **4 CE hours**, and **a 30-minute private consultation with a named consultant.**

**The ten funding carriers, named:** Central · Encompass · Erie · Liberty Mutual · MetLife Auto & Home ·
National General · Progressive · Selective · State Auto · The Hanover.

Plus: PIA of Indiana publishes a Perpetuation Toolkit **including sample plans**; Big "I" publishes ten-plus
free member perpetuation resources; Liberty Mutual publishes free perpetuation content separately.

> **The insurance agency owner has a trade association funded by the very counterparties who benefit from
> her having a plan. There is no room in that market.**

*(Honest flags: agencyjourneymapping.com returned 503 twice and pianational.org's wording says members may
"purchase" the programme — **whether it is literally $0 is UNVERIFIED.** The one claim that carriers

## 2. Private key-person vetoes demand a PERSON, not a document — now three legs

From an **actual filed agency agreement** (ProFormance Insurance, SEC EDGAR): assignment is permitted
*"only to the Agent's successor or successors provided such successor or successors meet the Company's
requirements for appointment,"* and the agreement ends *"on the effective date of sale, transfer or
merger."* **Nothing in it requires the Agent to maintain a written succession plan.**

| Veto-holder | What it actually demands |
|---|---|
| Iowa Ct. R. 39.18 | A **licensed attorney** as designated successor |
| Cal. B&P § 7068.2 | An approved **RMO/RME** within 90 days, or automatic suspension |
| Carrier appointment | **A successor who meets the Company's requirements for appointment** |

> **Three independent verticals, one shape: where a private party holds a genuine key-person veto, what it
> demands is an APPROVED HUMAN BEING, not a document.** The Stamp Law arriving from a third direction — now
> a pattern with three legs rather than an inference.

## 3. The one counterexample — GFSI food-safety documentation (SQF / BRCGS)

The corollary **failed to fire** here, for the first time anywhere in this file.

- **Veto: private and two-layer** — a **certification-body auditor** refuses certification without a
  documented system, and behind it a **retail buyer** refuses to purchase without the certificate.
  **No regulator anywhere in the chain.**
- **Required, in the scheme owner's own words.** SQFI: *"The SQF system elements… must be documented. This
  requires preparing policies, procedures, work instructions, and specifications."* Mandatory clauses
  *"may not be reported as not applicable during audits."*
- **The veto-holder does NOT publish the artefact free — the only "no" in this entire file.** SQFI publishes
  the Code and guidance free but states guidance documents *"are not auditable documents."* **BRCGS goes
  further and sells its own standard** ($177,12 / £140 unlocked).
- **Paid kits, published prices, instant download, no call:** IFSQN SQF Ed.10 package **$995** ·
  documentationconsultancy BRCGS Food Issue 9 **$550** · certificationtemplates ISO 22000 **$399**
  (procedures only $140).
- **The credentialed-person requirement lands on the BUYER's site, not the vendor** — the buyer must employ
  a *"trained SQF Practitioner."* And the incumbents are **already faceless document shops with no personal
  brand**, which answers the expert-credibility objection *empirically* rather than by argument.
- **It has the only recurring mechanic found in this whole family:** annual recertification audits plus
  **forced code-edition rewrites** (SQF Ed.9→10, BRCGS Issue 9).

**And it dies anyway — to the Fiverr floor, third independent confirmation.** Live HACCP and
food-safety-plan gigs at **$5, $20, $30, $50, $100** and one at **$350**, several titled *"audit ready…
FDA compliance."* Same buyer, attacked from below at 10–200× cheaper, plus the AI supply vise — a
food-safety plan built from a questionnaire is precisely what an LLM does.

> **Survives THE VETO LAW. Fails THE REPEATABILITY TRAP.** Not a clean survivor, but the first
> counterexample to the corollary — which is worth more than the vertical.

## 4. THE REFINEMENT — when the corollary does NOT fire

The corollary is not universal. The free artefact appears whenever the veto-holder is a **many-to-one public
gate** — a regulator, or a trade association funded by the counterparties who benefit from compliance. It
**fails to appear only when all three of these hold at once:**

1. **The standard-setter is a private body that monetises the standard or its licence.** BRCGS *sells* its
   standard; SQFI licenses a brand and an auditor economy. Neither can publish compliant documents without
   destroying what it sells. **A regulator has no such conflict — publishing the template is pure mission
   accomplishment, which is exactly why the State Bar of California and NASAA do it.**
2. **The required artefact is large and site-specific** — 89 to 215 documents. Free lead-generation
   artefacts are **small by construction**: cheap to produce, generic enough to attract everyone. A one-page
   checklist cannot satisfy an auditor, so the free artefact and the required artefact are **not
   substitutes.** Wherever a single downloadable template *can* clear the gate, someone gives it away.
3. **The payers have no trade association funded by their own counterparties.** Independent agents have
   Big "I", PIA, *and* a ten-carrier council. Food manufacturers have associations that lobby but do not
   publish audit-ready document sets.

**The search rule going forward:** *a private standard-setter that sells its standard + an artefact too big
for a free checklist to substitute + payers with no counterparty-funded association.* **Unexamined places
that rhyme:** IATF 16949 (automotive OEM veto) · AS9100 (aerospace primes) · FSSC 22000 · Sedex/SMETA ·
**EcoVadis** — the one lead the agent deliberately handed back unexamined · PCI DSS for larger merchants ·
GDPR Art. 30 records where a large customer's DPA is the gate.

## 5. THE CEILING-HOLDER LAW — the Fiverr floor, sampled three times, and it is not flat

| Category | Observed live gig prices |
|---|---|
| SOPs / operations manuals | **$5 · 10 · 20 · 25 · 25 · 40 · 45** |
| HACCP / food-safety plans | **$5 · 20 · 30 · 50 · 100 · 350** |
| Succession plans | **$350** |
| Business continuity plans | **$550 · $1.500** |

Price on that channel rises with **audit exposure** and with the **seller's claimed certification** — the
$550 BCP seller advertises DRII CBCP and ISO 22301. **Audited artefacts clear 10–30× the SOP band.**

> **THE CEILING-HOLDER LAW — on a commodity channel, audit exposure raises the CEILING but never creates a
> FLOOR. The $5 gig exists in every category, including the ones with $350 and $550 gigs. And the sellers
> holding the ceiling hold it with a CREDENTIAL AND A NAME — the two things she does not have. She therefore
> gets the FLOOR of every category she enters, whatever its ceiling.**

That is the Stamp Law appearing in the **price distribution of a commodity channel.** It closes the Fiverr
price question permanently: the channel is real, the demand is real, and **the portion of the price curve
available to her is the bottom of it.**

## 6. Everything else hunted, and how it died

| Veto-holder | Why it died |
|---|---|
| **Surety companies** (contractor bonding) | Real — sureties underwrite "Continuity" as one of the four C's and *"prefer a written plan to continue the business upon the death or retirement of key personnel."* But construction is a vetoed segment, brokers publish the guidance free as lead-gen, **NASBP/SFAA run SuretyLearn free with open registration**, and the core artefact is a **buy-sell agreement — a lawyer's instrument** |
| **ISO certification bodies** (9001, 22000) | **The corollary at its hardest. Oxebridge publishes a complete free ISO 9001:2015 kit** — 20+ procedures, ~30 forms, three manual variants, a 60-slide training deck — with the words *"Never pay for the Oxebridge kit; it's free now, and will always be free."* iseoblue gives away 32 templates. Paid toolkits sit at **$199** |
| **Amazon** (dietary supplements, 2026 policy, 90 days to comply) | The deliverable is a **third-party certificate from an accredited TIC provider** — NSF, Eurofins, SGS, USP, Intertek. A lab and audit business. Amazon's Compliance Fast-Track has certifiers file directly, **cutting the seller out of the document path entirely** |
| **Sponsor banks / BaaS** (BSA/AML programme) | A genuine double veto — FinCEN plus the sponsor bank as onboarding gate. But the FFIEC manual is free and the buyer demands a **named, credentialed compliance officer's signature.** Fails the expert-credibility constraint outright *(sponsor-bank requirement sourced only to a vendor blog — UNVERIFIED)* |
| **DSO / VSO roll-up acquirers** (dental, veterinary, optometry) | Identical to the business-sale vertical. Every required item is produced by a CPA, an attorney, or a percentage-fee broker. Written SOPs are explicitly **advisable, not required** — they *"make your practice more appealing"*, the same soft-discount shape. *(QofE prices and the "30–50% of LOIs renegotiated" figure come from M&A advisory blogs — **UNVERIFIED, not recorded**)* |

## 7. The constraint none of it clears

**Not one artefact in any vertical here can produce $99–199/month.** Every one is a **one-time document.**
The single recurring mechanic found in the entire search is **forced re-documentation on annual audit cycles
and scheme-edition changes.** If this family is ever revisited, that hook is the only reason to.

**What the agent could not reach:** agencyjourneymapping.com (503 ×2) · **IIAT's Guide to Agency-Company
Agreements** (bot wall) — *the single document most likely to settle whether any carrier contract mandates a
perpetuation plan, and it is unread* · Live Oak (404) · **no consultant price obtained for perpetuation
planning anywhere, from any of five firms — the absence is itself the finding** · Registrar Corp (403),
Sirocco (bot check), TCI (404), so the SQF/BRCGS kit prices rest on two observations rather than a survey ·
**and no evidence that any SQF/BRCGS kit vendor sells at volume — a published price proves a price, not a
market.**
