# ORDINARY Audit & Competitive Verdict

## Executive Verdict

**TITLE FORGE remains the winner — no change to the reigning champion.** ORDINARY, and the two fresh challengers brought in to unseat it (DRAWBRIDGE, BACKFEED), were built, audited, and red-teamed specifically to try to beat TITLE FORGE's certified numbers, and none of them did. **ORDINARY, as pasted, does not reach $1,000,000/month net profit by month 36 — it falls short by roughly $510,000–$710,000/month (51–71% of the mandate), and the shortfall is structural, not a matter of better execution:** the plan's own maximum stated monthly *revenue* ($875,000) is already below the $1,000,000 monthly *net profit* target, so no cost assumption, tax treatment, or growth curve can close the gap. TITLE FORGE clears the mandate pre-tax at month 36 and clears it after-tax by roughly month 42–46 on the current mix — the only entry in this round whose numbers land within honest striking distance of $1M/month, and the only one holding real structural moats.

### Economic & Feasibility Audit

**The dispositive arithmetic requires no assumptions at all.** ORDINARY's own §32 revenue table tops out at 500 matters/month × $1,750 blended fee = **$875,000/month in REVENUE**. The mandate requires **$1,000,000/month in NET PROFIT**. Net profit can never exceed revenue. Even a fantasy version of ORDINARY with zero payroll, zero overhead, and zero tax still misses the mandate by $125,000/month at its absolute maximum stated capacity — before a single dollar of cost is subtracted.

Working the plan's own numbers honestly, at its two stated operating points:

- **At the plan's declared north star (300 matters/month, its actual "operate toward" target):** $555,000 revenue − 25% direct cost − non-delivery opex (8–12 person team per the plan's own §34 honesty check) ≈ **$290,000/month pre-tax → ~$220,000/month after tax. This is 22% of the mandate.**
- **At the plan's absolute ceiling (500 matters/month, which §42 explicitly refuses to forecast):** $875,000 revenue, 75% contribution margin, ~$150–195k/month non-delivery opex ≈ **$490,000/month pre-tax → ~$370,000/month after tax. This is 37% of the mandate — the best case anywhere in four independent recomputations.**

To actually net $1,000,000/month after tax, ORDINARY would need roughly **$2.1–2.2M/month in revenue, i.e. ~1,150–1,215 matters/month — 2.3–2.4x its own maximum stated capacity envelope**, or roughly 61 attorney-ready files every single business day, forever. The only volume denominator the plan cites (17,493 adversary proceedings of *all* types filed nationally in FY2025, ~1,458/month) is explicitly disclaimed by the plan itself as "not ORDINARY's TAM" — meaning the business would need a national share approaching 34% of *every* adversary proceeding of any kind at the 500-matter ceiling, and roughly 83% at the volume actually required to hit the mandate. No credible denominator exists anywhere in the document to support this.

**Flood-test moat verdict: 0.5–1 moat held, against a hard requirement of at least 2.** Scored against the project's six-category rubric:
- **(a) Proprietary compounding data — FAILS by choice.** §41 explicitly renounces the one asset that would genuinely compound (a cross-firm outcome corpus) as "not the moat... unnecessary."
- **(b) Regulatory/licensing barrier — FAILS structurally.** ORDINARY holds no license, bond, or certification by design — the UPL posture requires staying outside any credentialing regime, which is precisely what makes the space floodable.
- **(c) Grit — WEAK.** The workflow is entirely digital (PDFs, Excel, AR exports); no county offices, no field operations, no physical friction that deters a remote AI-native team.
- **(d) Physical adjacency — none.**
- **(e) Distribution/trust network — FAILS.** Three anchor firms is three revocable partner preferences, not a network; the plan itself names routing as the #1 binding constraint it must fight to win, not an asset it holds.
- **(f) System-of-record switching cost — FAILS by design.** Enterprise integrations are explicitly deferred; output ships as portable Word/Excel files the firm owns — the same choices that build law-firm trust guarantee zero lock-in.

Worse, the moat runs backwards: Stretto, named in the plan's own citations, sits on the estate side holding the debtor's transfer data at the source — a category-(a) position ORDINARY can never obtain having sworn off the estate side.

**Catastrophic flaws:**
1. **Revenue ceiling below the net-profit mandate.** $875,000 max revenue < $1,000,000 required net profit — unfixable by any cost, tax, or margin assumption.
2. **No net-profit figure, no opex line, no tax line, and no months-13–36 plan anywhere in the document.** The roadmap stops at month 12; the $1,000,000/month target is never once restated against the plan's own numbers, making the shortfall structurally invisible on a friendly read.
3. **Flood test fails outright** — 0.5–1 of the required 2 structural moats, with the one genuinely compounding asset explicitly disclaimed.
4. **No addressable-market denominator.** The only volume statistic cited is explicitly disclaimed as "not ORDINARY's TAM," leaving the entire volume model — and therefore the entire profit model — unsupported.

**Major flaws (severity-ordered):**
5. **Pricing benchmarked against the wrong rate.** §12 justifies the $2,500 file against Clio's blended $311/hr rate, but the work described is paralegal-grade and the #1 ICP (fixed-fee boutiques) compares against internal cost (~$60–90/hr), not billing rate — making ORDINARY 2.5–5x more expensive than in-house for the exact matters that make up 70% of the mature mix.
6. **Small-dollar claims cited as the opportunity, priced out of reach.** The Yellow Corporation $12,000 claims cited as proof of volume cannot economically absorb a $1,500–2,500 preparation fee — spending it would consume 30–45% of the entire disputed amount before negotiation begins.
7. **Revenue is structurally lumpy and unmodeled as such.** §546(a)'s two-year limitations period mechanically produces boom-bust "wave" revenue tied to a credit cycle two years upstream; the plan presents flat monthly figures with no trough scenario or cash-reserve policy.
8. **E&O/indemnity exposure named but never sized or capped**, against a product whose errors flow directly into a law firm's litigation advice and malpractice exposure.
9. **The 12-month ramp requires implausible client concentration** — 200–300 matters/month from 3–5 anchor firms implies each anchor routes 50–100 matters/month, a practice size inconsistent with the plan's own "small/medium bench" ICP.
10. **Day-60 kill line can be satisfied without revenue** — an "authorized replay" (an unpaid historical test) counts as clearing the gate meant to test willingness to pay.
11. **ABA Op. 512 / Model Rule 1.6 consent requirements attack the plan's own claimed moat** — per-client informed consent cannot be pre-granted by one partner for 10–50 not-yet-existing defendant clients, undermining the "firm routing habit" the plan relies on.
12. **Wave-block co-defendant concentration creates unaddressed privilege/discoverability exposure** — many co-defendants of the same estate sharing one commercial vendor is the plan's stated scale mechanism, and it is never tested against confidentiality or common-interest privilege risk.

## The Challenger Field

Two fresh challengers were brainstormed and red-teamed this round specifically to try to beat both ORDINARY and TITLE FORGE. Both were eliminated.

**DRAWBRIDGE** — an AI-native duty-drawback recovery service, filed under the company's own licensed customs brokerage and distributed exclusively through the customs-broker/freight-forwarder referral channel. **Eliminated.** Its core demand premise was falsified by law that changed before the plan was written: the IEEPA tariffs it monetizes were struck down by the Supreme Court on 2026-02-20, collection ceased 2026-02-24, and the Section 122 stopgap expired 2026-07-24 — two weeks before this plan's date. The tariff growth that survives (Section 232) is statutorily *not* drawback-eligible and covers three of the plan's five named verticals. Its central licensing moat is self-contradictory (claiming both an 18–24 month barrier and a 7-month filing date for itself) and was already crossed by a live competitor (Zollback, launched March 2026 with an identical thesis). Under the $250k capital constraint, the honest month-36 net profit is **$0** — the company is projected insolvent before its first filed claim.

**BACKFEED** — a done-for-you utility interconnection application and study-management service for C&I energy storage, EV fleet charging, and commercial solar, built on a claimed proprietary per-utility outcome library. **Eliminated.** The plan's headline moat claim ("utility outcomes are not scrapable or purchasable at any price") is factually false in the states that carry the revenue — California, New York, and other high-volume states already publish application-level interconnection data, and incumbent software vendors already hold the equivalent dataset on the utility side. Its own volume denominator was off by roughly an order of magnitude (its "single-digit market share" claim is actually 26–70% of every qualifying national filing), and its PE-stamp "pass-through" arrangement is unlicensed plan-stamping in most states, adding an unbudgeted compliance cost that collides with the mandate's legality requirement. At a defensible market share, the honest month-36 net profit is **~$200,000/month pre-tax (~$150,000 after tax)** — and even that figure falls **$150,000–250,000 short of the plan's own stated month-6 milestone** under the $250k budget.

## Final Recommendation

**TITLE FORGE wins, with a binding condition attached.** None of the three alternatives evaluated this round reaches the mandate on an honest recomputation — two are not merely short, they are falsified by facts checkable at the time of writing (DRAWBRIDGE by a Supreme Court decision six months old at drafting; BACKFEED by state data-publication mandates eight years old). ORDINARY is the most intellectually honest document in the entire project — its citations check out, its arithmetic is clean in every row, its refusal architecture is genuinely good design, and its own honesty check corrects its headcount estimate upward rather than downward. It fails not because it is careless, but because every place it is careful, it is careful in the direction of a smaller business, and it never once restates the $1,000,000/month target it exists to serve — making its shortfall structurally invisible on a friendly read. That invisibility is the defect, not the honesty. ORDINARY is a credible $3.5–6M/year net-profit specialty firm; it is not this mandate's business.

TITLE FORGE wins by being the only entry whose numbers land within striking distance of the mandate *and* the only entry holding independently certified structural moats — but this round surfaced a convention problem that cannot be ignored: every ORDINARY auditor subtracted income tax, while TITLE FORGE was previously certified pre-tax. Applying the same after-tax standard to the champion yields ~$750,000/month at month 36 (75% of mandate), with the honest post-tax $1M/month crossing closer to month 42–46 on the current revenue mix. This does not change the ranking — every alternative is worse on the identical convention by a factor of 2.5–5x — but the committee should stop describing TITLE FORGE as meeting the mandate "at month 36" and start describing it accurately: pre-tax at month 36, post-tax at month 42–46, with a clear path to close that gap.

## If You Proceed With The Winner

Before TITLE FORGE can honestly be called "airtight" against this mandate, the following are binding conditions, not suggestions:

1. **Restate the target post-tax.** Publish a full month-36 P&L with revenue, COGS, gross profit, each opex category, EBITDA, tax, and NET PROFIT AFTER TAX, with the $1,000,000 line drawn explicitly on the chart — do not let a pre-tax figure read as success.
2. **Close the ~30% post-tax revenue gap with margin mix, not more volume.** Grow portfolio monitoring, financing-reliance certification, and curative work to carry ≥45% of gross profit by month 36 (versus runsheet-volume today) — these are the recurring, high-margin, moat-protected lines, and they lift blended margin toward 68%.
3. **Publish the true headcount split between COGS and opex at fully-loaded market wages** — the current implied opex per non-delivery head is thin and needs cross-footing before the next capital decision.
4. **State the plant-seed amortization policy explicitly** and test the mandate on the conservative (expensed-as-incurred) treatment, not just the capitalized one.
5. Add a mandate-level kill line (something ORDINARY conspicuously lacked) that tests whether the *objective* is still reachable at month 18, not just whether pilot-stage quality gates are passing.
6. Quantify licensure/bonding honestly as a real supplement to the moat, with actual state-by-state timelines for a funded copycat.
7. Publish logo concentration and cyclicality exposure (top-5/top-10 revenue share, trough scenario).
8. Republish month-36 net at the decayed price floor across all exposed pricing tiers.
9. Publish the month-by-month cash trough to the claimed breakeven at month 16, with founder compensation stated explicitly.

A follow-up certification pass should re-run the Lead Critic's adversarial audit specifically against the corrected post-tax P&L and the rebalanced revenue mix from fix #2, confirming the $1M/month after-tax crossing date lands where the rebuild claims it does before any further capital commitment.
