# BRAZIL — THE ARBITRAGE LAW

**14 Aug 2026. Written after checking two more Brazilian angles and finding the pattern behind
sixty-five deaths.**

---

## 1. The high-ticket document category, checked: dead

The most promising Brazilian shape left was **high-ticket consumer document work where the money is
already committed** — the citizenship-by-descent industry being the largest example.

**The market is real and the tickets are large:** assessoria for Italian citizenship runs
**R$7.000–30.000 per applicant**, with a full range of **R$5.000–80.000**
([cidadania4u](https://www.cidadania4u.com.br/blog/quanto-custa-tirar-cidadania-italiana/)).

**And it dies on five things at once:**

1. **The market was legislated away this year.** **Decreto-Lei 36/2025 and Lei 74/2025 restricted
   recognition to children and grandchildren of Italians.** Great-grandchildren and beyond — by far
   the largest Brazilian cohort — are now cut off from the administrative route and must go
   **judicial**, which requires an Italian lawyer. **Spain closed entirely: the Ley de Memoria
   Democrática lapsed on 22 October 2025.** Only Portugal's route survived intact, *"with the
   largest queue at the IRN."*
2. **The core deliverable is a reserved profession.** *Tradução juramentada* requires a
   **tradutor público matriculado na Junta Comercial**. She cannot do it. **Law 7's vise.**
3. **The judicial route is a lawyer's monopoly.**
4. **The trust profile is the worst possible one in this entire record.** A stranger hands over
   R$7.000–30.000 to an anonymous provider with no face, no office and no phone. The merchant seat
   already gave the verdict on this exact shape: *"premium to the anonymous is asking me to fund
   their reputation."*
5. **It is a relationship sale.** These assessorias sell with faces, testimonials, WhatsApp calls
   and offices, to people making one of the biggest purchases of their lives.

**Filed. And with it, the whole "high-ticket Brazilian consumer document" category** — inventário,
regularização, previdenciário — because every one of them is either reserved to a profession or
requires being trusted with life-changing money by someone who has never seen your face.

## 2. THE ARBITRAGE LAW — what actually killed sixty-five candidates

Here is the pattern, and I should have stated it a long time ago.

> ### THE ARBITRAGE LAW
> **A Brazilian cost base is only an advantage against a buyer who does not have one.**
>
> **Selling Brazilian work to Brazilian buyers converts her single durable edge into exactly
> nothing.**

**Check it against the record.** Her one advantage that cannot be competed away is that
**R$30.000/month is US$5.780** — a number no American, British or German solo operator can live on.
That advantage is worth everything against a buyer paying in dollars and **worth zero against a
buyer paying in reais, because he has the same cost base she does.**

Now look at what survived:

| | Buyer | Currency | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Candidate 64** — pregnancy memoir | US consumer | **USD** | Alive |
| **Candidate 66** — catalogue landing | US/UK/EU merchant | **USD/GBP/EUR** | Alive, planned |
| **The other 64** | Overwhelmingly Brazilian | **BRL** | Dead |

**Both survivors sell into hard currency. Almost every corpse sold into reais.** That is not a
coincidence and it is not about Brazil being a bad country to sell in — it is arithmetic. The
Brazilian price ceiling and her cost floor are the *same number*, so there is no gap to live in.

**This also explains the two most painful individual deaths in the file.** The pregnancy book at
R$349 needed 411 units a month; at $179 it needed 45. **Same object, same labour, same founder.**
The only thing that changed was the currency of the buyer.

> **Brazil is where she lives. It is not where she should sell.**

## 3. The one Brazilian angle that survives the law

There is exactly one way to have a Brazilian buyer without destroying the edge:

> **Sell to Brazilians who EARN in hard currency.**

They are Brazilian — so she has cultural proximity, native Portuguese, the same time zone, and Pix
as a payment rail. **And their revenue is in dollars, so the price ceiling is not Brazilian.** It is
the only configuration where being Brazilian is an asset on *both* sides of the transaction.

**And the population is real and enumerable:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Brazilian exporting companies, 2025** | **29.818 — a record** ([Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/brazil-hits-record-29818-exporting-companies-in-2025/)) |
| Brazilian importing companies | 60.115, up 7,6% |
| **MPEs directly supported by ApexBrasil** | **15.000+, virtually all micro, small or medium** |
| **Amazon Global Selling — individual Brazilian sellers** | **US$10.000–50.000/year**; structured companies well beyond |
| Etsy | Brazilians can sell, routed through Payoneer |

**And the work is candidate 66 run in reverse.** A Brazilian selling on Amazon US has exactly one
visible, verifiable, expensive defect: **their English listings are bad.** They were written by a
Portuguese speaker or by machine translation, and the marketplace punishes both. She is the only
person who can take the brief in Portuguese, understand the product natively, and return
**native-quality English that passes the platform's validator.**

**Why this partially solves the trust problem too:** a Brazilian seller trusts a Brazilian supplier
in a way a Manchester merchant never will. **Anonymity costs less when there is cultural proximity
and a Pix receipt.**

### The honest limits, stated up front

1. **Most of them are too small.** An Amazon Global Selling individual at **$10.000/year of revenue
   cannot pay $899 for anything.** The buyer must be at the top of that band or a structured
   exporter.
2. **The exporter pool is small and mostly commodity.** Much of the 29.818 is soy, meat and ore —
   businesses with no listings and no need for this.
3. **It is still a B2B buyer**, so the B2B buyer law still applies: negotiation, diligence,
   incumbents, price anchor.
4. **The cross-border seller population is unmeasured.** Nobody publishes how many Brazilians sell
   on Amazon US or Etsy, and that number decides whether this is a segment or a handful.

## 4. What I would actually do with this

**Not start a new candidate.** Add it as a **second beachhead for candidate 66**, because it costs
nothing to test alongside the first:

> **The same deliverable — a catalogue prepared to land on a new channel — sold to Brazilian sellers
> going to Amazon US, Etsy and eBay, in Portuguese, priced in dollars, paid by Pix or Wise.**

**It fixes three of 66's weakest points at once:**

- **The channel.** She cannot reach a Manchester merchant. She can reach a São Paulo seller — they
  are in Brazilian e-commerce Facebook and WhatsApp groups, at Brazilian events, and enumerable
  through ApexBrasil's own programme lists.
- **The trust gate.** Same nationality, same language, same payment rail, and she can be a named
  Brazilian company to a Brazilian buyer without breaking the faceless rule for the wider market.
- **The localisation line — the biggest item on the invoice — becomes the whole pitch**, because
  PT→EN is precisely where a Brazilian seller is provably weakest and where her being bilingual is
  worth something no offshore BPO can match.

### The free check, and it settles whether this is a segment or a rounding error

**Search Amazon.com for products sold by Brazilian sellers** — filterable by seller location — and
**count how many have listings written in obviously non-native English.** Then check whether
ApexBrasil, Correios Exporta Fácil, or the state export agencies publish participant lists.

> **If fewer than a few hundred Brazilian sellers are visibly on international marketplaces with bad
> English, this is a rounding error and it closes. If there are thousands, it is the warmest channel
> in the entire file — because they are reachable in her own language, by a compatriot, about a
> defect they already know they have.**

---

## 5. The correction this forces on everything before it

**Every Brazilian-buyer idea in this file was dead before it was written**, and I kept testing them
one at a time on their individual merits. The arithmetic was always the same and I never named it.

**From here: any candidate whose buyer pays in reais must first explain why the Brazilian cost base
is not its only advantage.** If it cannot, it does not get researched.
