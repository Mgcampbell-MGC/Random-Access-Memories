# SYNTHESIS — 14 AUG 2026: three independent searches, one answer

**Three probes ran today from three different directions. All three came back with the same
finding, and none of them was looking for it.**

| Probe | Direction | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **My keepsake hunt** | Consumer artifacts, transposition law | 5 kills, 1 contested survivor |
| **SingleBrain** (user-supplied) | Managed AI execution, B2B enterprise | Fails 4 absolute vetoes before the niche is chosen |
| **External LLM, 28-model sweep** | Every business model except keepsakes | **28 no-survivor verdicts**, 2 near-misses, both dead on price sheets |

**The convergence is the finding. It is not the niche, not the model, and not the AI.**

## 1. The external report independently rediscovered four of our laws

This is worth recording because it is the strongest validation the law set has ever received — a
different model, a different search space, no access to the graveyard, same conclusions:

> *"A fixed service became a report or spreadsheet that the customer could make with ChatGPT"* →
> **the chat-window test**
>
> *"A subscription utility became a feature inside an incumbent platform"* → **feature-not-product**
>
> *"A licensed data product either relied on public/free information or required a trust-heavy
> sales process"* → **LAW 1 and the no-trust-gate test**
>
> *"A transaction-fee product attracted a clearing party that could fund the utility to zero"* →
> **LAW 0 and the seller-side funding law**

Four laws, bought with corpses over weeks, re-derived from scratch in one sweep. **They are real.**

And its executive summary states the constraint problem more cleanly than anything in our own file:

> *"The combination of low founder labor, faceless operation, no calls, no credentials, no staff,
> no regulated expertise, and a path to US$2,400/month removes most markets where a new solo
> entrant could obtain durable pricing power."*

Correct — with one crucial amendment, which is the point of this document.

## 2. Both of its near-misses are dead, and the price sheets it found are what kill them

The report is honest about both and recommends no build budget for either. I would go further:
**neither is worth the test either**, and its own citations are why.

### Candidate 1 — Commercial lease operating file at $300/lease. **DEAD: a 92× price gap.**

| Provider | Price | Per lease |
|---|---|---|
| [LeaseAbstracts](https://leaseabstracts.com/pricing) — source-linked abstracts, critical dates, OCR, exports, **human review**, email support | $49/mo for 15 | **$3,27** |
| Same, 75 leases | $149/mo | **$1,99** |
| [LeaseWizard](https://leasewizard.ai/pricing) | $20/lease + $350/mo | ~$37 at volume |
| **The proposal** | **$300** | **$300** |

**LeaseAbstracts already sells the entire proposed deliverable — including the human review — at
one ninety-second of the price.** The report calls this "a fraction." It is two orders of magnitude.

Corrected Law A1 leg (a) does not fire literally — $3,27 is not zero — but **it is close enough to
zero that the entire willingness to pay must come from somewhere the report cannot name.** Its own
words: *"a trigger hypothesis — not a measured buyer segment."*

Add the second kill: a landlord acting on an **option deadline or rent escalation** supplied by an
anonymous provider with no E&O insurance, no credential, and no entity they can sue. The report
files this under "authority." It is the **no-trust-gate test**, and it fires at exactly the moment
the product becomes valuable.

### Candidate 2 — Association ballot utility at $99/month. **DEAD: three separate kills.**

| Provider | Price | Gap to $99/mo |
|---|---|---|
| [Simply Voting](https://www.simplyvoting.com/pricing/) | **FREE** for ≤10 electors, credit card self-serve | **Law A1 leg (a) fires literally** |
| [eBallot](https://www.eballot.com/pricing) | **$4,99–6,99/month** | **14–20×** |
| [vote.direct](https://vote.direct/pricing) | **$99 per YEAR** for 50 voters, exports and audit trails included | **12×** |

1. **A free competitor exists.** That is the cleanest kill in the law set and it fires on its face.
2. **Maximum trust requirement.** Running an association's contested board election is the most
   trust-dependent utility that exists. Any association large enough to pay will demand a security
   questionnaire, insurance and an MSA — the report says so itself. **A faceless anonymous
   one-person company cannot run someone's election.**
3. **The worst possible bug profile.** Against her own *"build yes, bugs and such after the build"* —
   weighted votes, quorum rules, ballot secrecy, email deliverability, audit integrity. **A bug in
   a voting system is catastrophic, public, and unrecoverable.** This is the single worst
   founder-fit in anything assessed today.

Plus support that clusters into election season, against a 30 h/week ceiling.

## 3. THE B2B BUYER LAW

Three searches, three directions, one shared property. Every dead candidate today — 28 models, 5
keepsake probes, and SingleBrain itself — was killed by something the **buyer** brought, not
something the **idea** lacked.

> ### THE B2B BUYER LAW
> **For a faceless solo founder with no calls, no credentials and no staff, the B2B buyer is the
> disqualifier — not the niche, not the model, not the technology.**
>
> **A business buyer brings four things, every time:**
> 1. **A negotiation** → a sales call, because businesses do not buy from a page above trivial sums
> 2. **A diligence** → a trust gate: security questionnaire, MSA, insurance, references
> 3. **An incumbent** → every B2B software category is already occupied and often free at the low end
> 4. **A price ceiling** → anchored on $5–50/month SaaS, so the ACV is capped below what solo labour needs
>
> **A consumer buyer brings none of the four.** They buy from a page, diligence nothing beyond
> reviews, compare against *nothing* in an empty vertical, and anchor on **gift budgets** rather
> than software budgets.

That is why 28 B2B models produced zero survivors and one consumer artifact cleared fourteen of
fourteen constraints. **It was never a search-quality problem.**

### The amendment to the external report's conclusion

Its summary says the constraint set *"removes most markets where a new solo entrant could obtain
durable pricing power."* Nearly right. The precise version:

> **The constraint set removes most B2B markets. It removes very few consumer ones.** The four
> things that kill her in B2B — negotiation, diligence, incumbency, price ceiling — are all
> properties of *buying on behalf of an organisation.*

## 4. SingleBrain is the same law, stated in reverse

SingleBrain's core promise is exactly right for her: **no tool to learn, no dashboard, no login;
work arrives done.** That is *"AI does the work while she watches the system run,"* verbatim.

It is impossible for her anyway — five failures, four absolute — and **every one is contributed by
its B2B buyer**: scoping calls, OAuth into HubSpot/Gong/Meta, SOC 2 and MSA, bespoke per-client
infrastructure, an agency with staff.

**Take the identical promise and hand it to a consumer and every failure disappears:**

| | SingleBrain | Candidate 64 |
|---|---|---|
| Promise | Work arrives done, no tool to learn | **Identical** |
| Buyer | A company with a security review | **A person with a credit card** |
| Price | *"depends on the deployment"* | **$149, on the page** |
| Access | OAuth into the revenue stack | **A form and some photos** |
| Sales motion | Scoping calls | **None** |
| Trust gate | SOC 2, MSA, DPA | **None** |

**Candidate 64 is SingleBrain with the B2B buyer removed.** Same idea, four vetoes lighter.

## 5. What this changes

**Thinking differently was the right instinct and it paid.** Three probes in one day did not find a
better business — they found the *shape of the wall*, which is worth more than another candidate.
Sixty-five kills had established that things keep dying; today established **what they die of.**

**Practical consequences, effective now:**

1. **Stop searching B2B under this constraint set.** Not "search it more carefully" — stop. Twenty-
   eight models, six CNAE waves and sixty-five candidates is enough evidence. Any future B2B
   candidate must first name which of the four buyer properties it escapes, and *why*, before any
   research is spent on it.
2. **Neither near-miss gets the test.** The lease file would spend two weeks and $300 to relearn
   what LeaseAbstracts' price page already says. The ballot utility has a free competitor.
3. **Candidate 64 stands, and today strengthened it three separate ways** — a six-probe attempt to
   find its equal failed, a 28-model sweep in the opposite direction found nothing, and the reason
   both failed is the exact property 64 does not have.
4. **The next hunt, if there is one, is consumer-only** and applies the transposition law: a mechanic
   proven at $59–199 in one vertical, transposed to the vertical it never crossed into.

**The build decision has not changed all day, and three independent attempts to change it are why
it should now be made.** One book. One volunteer. $30.
