# ★★★ THE THREE, AND THEY ARE THREE DIFFERENT BUSINESS MODELS

## 1 · GARIMPO — the São Paulo buying desk *(acquisition agency — a shape this file has never had)*

**A foreign dealer or designer who wants a piece of Brazilian modernist design cannot legally bid for it, see
it, negotiate in Portuguese, pay in reais, or get it out of the country. She does all five as their appointed
representative, for a percentage of each acquisition.**

**★ The spine is a statutory exclusion, and it is new to this file.** Leilo's own participation regulation:
***"Estrangeiro somente poderá participar mediante a procuração em nome de um representante legal estabelecido
no Brasil"*** — registration requires a **CPF and fiscal regularity with the Receita Federal.** A foreign buyer
is *barred from the transaction* unless a Brazilian resident acts. **This is not the procurador seat that died:
that was a R$810/year filing role; nobody sells this one bundled with viewing, condition-reporting, payment,
collection and export.**

**Export is clear:** IPHAN restricts only tombado items, anything produced or introduced up to **1889**,
archaeological objects, and 16th–19th-century books. **Mid-century is freely exportable**, and IPHAN issues the
declaration free. Statutory leiloeiro commission is a flat **5% from the buyer** (Decreto 21.981/1932 art. 24),
so the cost structure is fixed and knowable.

**The tell in the data:** of nine Jean Gillon listings surfaced on 1stDibs, sellers were in Florida, Tennessee,
California, New York, Nevada, Minnesota, British Columbia, Stockholm and Belgrade. **None in Brazil.** Somebody
is getting these out today, and it is not a findable, English-speaking service.

### ⚠ MY CORRECTION — THE HEADLINE MULTIPLE IS WRONG, AND THE BUSINESS SURVIVES IT
The agent reported a **3,1× spread**: São Paulo dealer **R$15.000 ≈ US$2.882** for a Jangada chair against
**US$8.900** on 1stDibs. **Both numbers are real and both are ASKING prices.** I checked the clearing price:

> **Wright, Design, 13 Dec 2023, lot 331 — Jean Gillon Jangada armchair AND ottoman: estimate US$4.000–6.000,
> SOLD US$7.560.** Against 1stDibs' asking of **US$18.500 for the same pairing.** ⇒ **Foreign dealer asking
> prices run ~2,4× the auction clearing price.**

**Honest arithmetic: a chair alone clears at roughly US$4.500–4.900 abroad against US$2.882 at São Paulo dealer
retail — about 1,6×, not 3,1×.** ⇒ **The business only works because her channel is auction and estate, which
sits BELOW Brazilian dealer retail.** At the agent's own average acquisition of R$8.000 (**US$1.537**), landed
cost with her fee and freight is ~US$2.300 against a foreign clearing price of US$4.500–9.000. **That works —
and it is a different business from the one the headline implied. It is not "buy retail here, sell retail
there"; it is "reach the supply below retail."**

**★★ THE KILL, and it is severe: ATTRIBUTION IS THE VALUE, AND ATTRIBUTION IS THE EXPERT VETO.** An anonymous
jacarandá chair is R$1.500; a *Gillon* is R$15.000. **The entire spread lives in the name.**
**The escape is this file's own rule — the buyer supplies the domain, she supplies the throughput.** She sends
dated photographs, dimensions, joinery detail and maker's marks; **the dealer decides what it is and whether to
buy.** She never attributes.

## 2 · CAMPO — the field-verification desk *(wholesale per-assignment to foreign firms)*
She goes to an address and records what is actually there — occupancy, staff, signage, stock, whether the
"factory" is a room — and returns a dated photographic report. **She never signs a conclusion.** Sold to
US/UK due-diligence and investigation firms who resell it inside their reports.
**Compelled by FCPA and UK Bribery Act third-party diligence and AML onboarding.** Receita, JUCESP and Serasa
tell you a company is *registered*; none tells you the address contains an operation.
**★ The price is a GOVERNMENT SCHEDULE, verified by me at `trade.gov/user-fees`: International Company Profile
US$2.000 full / US$1.050 partial · US$360 per arranged appointment · US$90 PER STAFF HOUR.** Kreller has run
*"more than 250 reports in Brazil"* in two years with site visits as a named method.
**At US$350–500/visit: 6 visits = US$2.100–3.000 in 24 h, +8 h development = 32 h.**
⚠ **The kill:** Kreller's Brazil lead is a former Chief of the Tax Fraud Division with 35 years as a São Paulo
police chief. **These firms may buy SOURCE ACCESS, not observation** — the expert veto in a new suit.
**TEST, free: 25 emails as a prospective supplier — "what do you pay a São Paulo field correspondent per site
visit, and what background do you require?" PASS: ≥5 naming ≥US$300 without requiring ex-law-enforcement.**

# ★★ THE LAW THIS HUNT PRODUCED
> ### **THE OPEN-STATE INVERSION — Brazil's language barrier protects nothing that the Brazilian state publishes, and it publishes almost everything.** LAI, dados abertos, ECADNET, registrocivil, DataJud, SICAR, Comex Stat, and now a free EUDR platform. **The "badly formatted, Portuguese-only, hard-to-reach data" premise is FALSE at the source layer.**
> **Portuguese is a moat only where the record is PRIVATE, UNDIGITISED, or HELD BY A PERSON** — an estate, a workshop, a dealer, a shelf, an object.
> ⇒ **HUNT BRAZILIAN ARBITRAGE IN THINGS AND PEOPLE, NEVER IN REGISTERS.**
> **And its corollary, which is why GARIMPO has the hardest spine of anything found today: a foreigner's EXCLUSION FROM A TRANSACTION is worth far more than a foreigner's difficulty READING A FILE.**
