# CANDIDATE 64 — "ANTES DE VOCÊ CHEGAR"

**Source: external LLM, 14 Aug 2026. Assessed same day against the law set.**
**Verdict: ALIVE. The best founder-fit candidate in 64, and the first one in the whole record
with a distribution mechanism that already exists. Two cheap numbers decide it, and one of them
is currently wrong.**

---

## 1. The shape

After a 3D/4D ultrasound the mother scans a clinic QR code and buys a R$349 hardcover book.
**She** uploads her own cropped ultrasound images and fills a questionnaire — the clinic transfers
nothing. Sol writes and lays out the pregnancy story, the mother approves, a Brazilian photo lab
prints and ships. Clinic takes ~R$70.

Not a blank album. It arrives already written: the discovery, the ultrasound timeline, the story
behind the name, letters from the parents, milestones, a final page for the birth photo.

nulls.** So the search was re-run in the buyer's own words — *diário da gestante*, *livro do bebê

The category is genuinely crowded, and every single competitor found sells the same thing:

## 4. The print cost is wrong, and it is the whole business

The external report costs printing at **~R$80**, citing Digipix. Fetched
[the actual page](https://www.digipix.com.br/fotolivros/plus) on 14 Aug 2026:

> **R$79,90 buys 21x15cm, matte, *20 pages*.** Extra pages run **R$2,59–6,79** each depending on size.

R$80 is the price of **the smallest, thinnest book on the menu.** The proposed contents —
discovery, ultrasound timeline, name story, parents' letters, milestones, final page — do not fit in
20 pages of a 21x15cm book. A realistic build is 30–40 pages.

| Spec | Print cost |
|---|---|
| 21x21cm square, 20pp | **R$64,90** — the value pick |
| 21x15cm landscape, 32pp | R$79,90 + 12 × R$2,59 = **R$111** |
| 21x30cm portrait, 32pp | R$139,90 + 12 extra ≈ **R$194** |
| 30x21cm landscape, 40pp | R$139,90 + 20 extra ≈ **R$230** |

### What that does to the economics

At R$349 retail, R$70 clinic, R$95 everything else (freight, packaging, payment fees, Simples,
remake reserve):

| Print cost | Contribution/book | Books for R$12.000 | Books for R$30.000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| R$80 *(their assumption)* | R$104 | 115 | 288 |
| R$111 | R$73 | 164 | 411 |
| R$140 | R$44 | 273 | 682 |
| R$194 | **−R$10** | **never** | **never** |

**The business is a bet on one number nobody has checked.** It swings from healthy to
loss-making across the ordinary range of the same lab's price list.

**The offsetting fact:** [Digipix Pro](https://www.digipix.com.br/fotografo-vendedor) exists — a
reseller programme for photographers, studios and designers with *"preços especiais de revenda"*
and its own logistics. Reseller pricing on a 32-page 21x21 could plausibly land back near R$80.
**Two unknowns that partly cancel — which is not the same as being right.**

## 5. The ceiling: this is a R$12–15k business, not a R$30k business

Per book she does: review the questionnaire, supervise the AI draft, check the layout, run one
revision round with the mother, place the print order, handle exceptions. **~25 minutes at maturity,
~45 at the start.**

At 30 h/week = 130 h/month, with roughly half her time on production and half on clinic
recruitment and marketing:

- **~156 books/month** is the honest solo maximum.
- At R$73 contribution that is **~R$11.400/month**.
- R$30.000 would need **411 books = 171 hours/month of production alone.** Impossible for one
  person, and she has vetoed employees permanently.

**So it lands almost exactly on the relaxed near-term target (R$12.000–15.000) and forecloses
year three.** It is linear labour with no compounding: every additional real is another book.

### The only path past the ceiling is price, not volume

A better object at **R$549** — linen or foil cover, heavier stock, 40 pages, print R$140:

> 549 − 140 print − 90 clinic − 130 (fees/freight/tax/reserve) = **R$189/book**
> R$30.000 needs **159 books = ~66 h/month.** That works.

**If this is run, it should be built to become the R$549 object, not the R$349 one.** The R$349
version is the test, not the destination.

## 6. The clinic channel — right instinct, wrong wiring in two places

### a) The counterparty-motivation rule fires

R$70 × 5 orders = **R$350/month to a clinic.** That is nothing. No clinic changes its behaviour
for R$350, and the receptionist who actually hands over the QR gets **zero** of it.

The pitch is therefore **not** a revenue share. It is a free patient perk that makes the clinic look
generous, and the R$70 is a rounding error that will not sustain anyone's attention past month two.

**The design fix:** the QR must sit *inside a flow the clinic already runs* — printed on the laudo,
or in the WhatsApp message the clinic already sends with the exam images — not on a counter card
someone has to remember. A counter card decays to zero by month three. That is the difference
between a channel and a favour.

### b) The test channel is wrong — and this is the correction that matters most

The report says *"send the complete written offer to 40 clinics."* By email, that returns noise.
**Brazilian clinics run on WhatsApp**, and they publish that number themselves on their site,
Google Business profile and Instagram bio.

This is the same correction that rewrote TEST_01 on 13 Aug. It is load-bearing here: the report's
own kill criterion is *"if nobody partners without meetings, kill it"* — and **on WhatsApp, a
clínica manager will absolutely negotiate a patient-perk partnership in writing.** By email or
phone, they will not. The channel decides whether the kill criterion fires on the product or on
the medium.

### c) V-Baby is cited as validation. It is also the durability risk

[V-Baby](https://www.vlabhealth.com/v-baby) serves 600+ ultrasound rooms and 40.000+ exams/month,
and already owns the clinic-branded image-delivery flow — which is precisely where the QR belongs.

- **Not a kill under corrected Law A1**: it is not free, and it does not sell this product.
- **It is the proof the channel and the emotional moment are real.**
- **It is also the party best positioned to add the book as a feature**, because it has already done
  the hard part — clinic distribution at scale.

Partnering with V-Baby is the obvious growth move and the wrong entry move: that is a company
relationship sale, which means a trust gate and a meeting. **Enter through independent clinics.**

## 7. LGPD

Ultrasound images are health data — **Art. 11 sensitive category**, and the design correctly puts
the mother in control: she uploads, she crops, she consents specifically, she approves, originals
are deleted after fulfilment. That is the right structure and it is the reason the credential-custody
rule does not fire.

But she is still the **controller of sensitive personal data**. Manageable — a highlighted specific
consent at upload, a stated retention window, deletion on fulfilment — not free, and it must be
right before the first real image is uploaded.

---

## 8. The remaining real risk: physical fulfilment exceptions

Drop-ship removes inventory. It does not remove the damaged book, the lost package, the misprint,
the wrong name. **The emotional stakes on this specific object are the highest in commerce** — it is
a book about someone's baby — and the price point is low.

The R$95 bucket budgets a remake reserve, which is correct. What it does not budget is her time
answering those messages. That is inside the 25 min/book estimate and it is the number most likely
to be optimistic.

---

## 9. Verdict and the next move

**ALIVE.** It clears every hard founder constraint, it survives Law A after the search was redone
properly, and it has the first real distribution mechanism in 64 candidates. Its ceiling is the
relaxed near-term target, not the three-year one, and the path past the ceiling is a better object
at a higher price rather than more books.

**The external report's final recommendation is right and should be followed: do not build an app.**
Build one physical sample, one checkout page, per-clinic QR codes. But with three amendments:

1. **Build the sample at the real spec** — 32–40 pages, at the size you intend to sell — not at the
   20-page R$79,90 spec. Holding it settles the print cost and the R$349 price in one motion.
   **This is the single cheapest way to answer the number the whole business rests on.**
2. **Register for Digipix Pro first** and get reseller pricing on that exact spec, plus written
   confirmation of **direct-to-end-customer shipping in neutral packaging.** If the lab will not
   drop-ship, she is running a fulfilment job, not a design business, and the physical-goods veto
   fires properly.
3. **Send the offer by WhatsApp, not email**, to the number each clinic publishes itself. 40 clinics,
   independent obstetric and 3D/4D ultrasound, written, personalised, low daily volume — the same
   Meta boundary rules as TEST_01 §4.

**Kill criteria, kept from the report and sharpened:**

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Print at real spec > R$140 with Pro pricing | **Kill or reprice to R$549 immediately** |
| Lab will not ship direct to the end customer | **Kill** — physical-goods veto fires |
| Fewer than 4 of 40 clinics say yes in writing | **Kill** — the channel is a favour, not a channel |
| Clinics ask for patient records or credentials | **Kill** |
| Any clinic requires a meeting or a visit to proceed | **Kill** — founder constraint, not negotiable |
| Price must be discounted below ~R$300 to place it | **Kill** — the anchor won, the margin is gone |
