# THE SHAPE WAS WRONG — agents 8 and 9, and the convergence

**17 Aug 2026. The most consequential pair of results in the file. One killed a claim I had asserted as
fact; the other found where the money in this category actually sits.**

> **The book was the wrong SHAPE, not the wrong idea. Everything converges on SHORT, DEADLINE-BOUND,
> WRITTEN-ONLY, NO PHYSICAL OBJECT — and the price band the recession data says survives is the one
> that band already occupies.**

## 1. Corrections to my own claims, first

### I said funeral spending is "famously inelastic." That is FALSE in real terms.

BEA data via FRED, real PCE for funeral and burial services (quantity index, 2017=100):

| | 2007 | 2008 | 2009 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Real** | 116,7 | 111,9 **(−4,2%)** | 104,1 **(−6,9%)** | 92,2 **(−9,3%)** |
| Nominal ($B) | 24,0 | 24,2 | 23,4 (−3,2%) | 29,7 (−5,5%) |
| Price index | 77,6 | 81,5 **(+5,0%)** | 84,7 **(+3,9%)** | 121,4 |

**Real funeral spending fell 10,8% from 2007 to 2009 while US deaths were flat-to-rising.** Providers held
nominal revenue by **raising prices 4–5% a year straight through the recession.** SCI's own FY2009 10-K:
comparable services performed **−6,7%**, average revenue per service **+1,1%** — families did not cut the
per-funeral ticket; volume and mix did the damage. Same mechanic today: SCI Q2 2026, sales average +3% on
services performed −1%.

**And 2020 is not evidence of recession-resistance.** Funeral spending rose 2020–21 because *deaths* rose.
That is a mortality effect, not an income effect.

### I said the crash filter favours an $899 keepsake. The data says the opposite.

**Held up through 2008–09:** Shutterfly FY2009 net revenues **$246,4M vs $213,5M, +15%**; transacting
customers **+18%**; personalised products **56% → 61% → 66%** of revenue while commodity prints fell
**44% → 39% → 33%**. CEWE photo books H1 2009 **+50%**. Substitution *within* the category, measured.

**Fell in the same recession:** real PCE for jewelry and watches **−19,0% cumulative** 2007→2009.
Build-A-Bear FY2009 **−14,4%**. Portrait photography studios **−27,2%** 2007→2012, and still below 2007
*nominal* in 2022.

> **THE SENTIMENT-PRICE SPLIT — sentimental spending survives a downturn when the sentiment is the
> product and the money is not: the emotional payload comes from the customer's own raw material, and the
> price is small relative to felt value. It does NOT survive when the emotion is carried by an expensive
> object.**

**Shutterfly's recession-proof average order value was $30,74. Her range is 6× to 29× that. The $899 tier
is structurally a jewelry-shaped bet, and jewelry lost 19% in real terms.**

### And "the recession is the acquisition channel" is not supported at all

Every documented substitution winner was riding a **pre-existing secular shift**, with the downturn adding
a few points of accelerant:

- **Lab-grown diamonds: 61% of engagement-ring centre stones, +239% since 2020** — and it happened during
  *strong* consumer spending, not a downturn. De Beers H1 2026 revenue **−23%**, Q2 rough sales **−44%**,
  ASP **$105/ct, −32%**.
- **Private label: 21,3% dollar share, an all-time high — but only +2,2pp over 2021–2025, ~0,5pp/year**,
  regardless of cycle.
- **Home improvement went the OTHER way.** Home Depot's Pro business outperformed DIY; homeowners are
  **deferring** remodels, not doing them themselves. **When a job is hard, people postpone — they do not
  downgrade.** If her product is postponable, a downturn hurts her outright.
- **Weddings are not a downturn market.** 85% said the economy affected planning, but **77% of those
  adjusting budgets INCREASED spend**; average held at **$34.000**; 13 vendors; 88% still hired a
  photographer. *(And 52% cite vendor responsiveness as key — bad ground for a never-speaks vendor.)*

**The real competitor is not the expensive incumbent. It is free.** Deloitte 2025 holiday survey (n=4.270):
planned spend **−10%**, retail goods **−14%**, **77% trading down**, and **49% planning to make DIY gifts.**
In her category the free substitute is the customer writing it themselves with a chatbot. The only
documented defence is that buyers currently reject AI prose — the Association of Ghostwriters reports 61%
of writers use AI but **only 7% use it to generate content** — and that is **a perception with an expiry
date, not a moat.**

### One genuine tailwind, and it is real

**Cremation is 63,4% of US dispositions in 2025, projected 82,3% by 2045.** The shift destroys spending on
**hardware** — casket, vault, plot, marker — and on the **service**. NFDA medians: burial-with-viewing
**$8.300** ($9.995 with vault) versus **$6.280** with cremation; direct cremation strips that to roughly
$1.000–3.000 **with no ceremony at all.**

**SCI's own 10-K states its results depend on expanding cremation memorialisation products** — and its
rising sales average shows it worked.

> **Total spend per death shrinks in real terms; the memorial-artefact share of it grows. Her slice of a
> shrinking pie is the growing slice.** Corroborating: NFDA found **58,3%** have attended a funeral at a
> non-traditional location — the ritual is decoupling from the funeral home, leaving no service, no
> casket, no marker, and **nothing to hand the relatives.**

## 3. THE CONVERGENCE — three findings that dissolve her hardest constraints

### 1. Written-only intake is a NORMAL, PRICED DELIVERY MODE in this category

**In Tribute sells the same eulogy two ways: $350 with online-form intake, $450 with a one-hour phone
interview.** The Eulogy Writers' $275 eulogy is **questionnaire-based with 24-hour delivery.**

*(Correcting the agent's own framing: written-only is priced $100 BELOW the phone version, not above it —
it is a discount, not a premium. But the structural point is stronger than the pricing point.)*

> **C1 — never speaks to anyone — is not a handicap here. It is a standard product configuration that
> established sellers already offer and price. For the first time in this file, her hardest constraint is
> a normal option in the market she would enter.**

### 2. URGENCY carries the price, not luxury

Speechy Urgent **£445 vs £345, +29%**. Ghostwriters Central **doubles to $120/page** for rush. The Eulogy
Writers' entire selling proposition is **24 hours.**

> **Every price above $250 found in this category clusters on a DEADLINE-BOUND occasion: a funeral, a
> wedding, a "before it's too late" letter.** That is the MISSED-WINDOW mechanism recorded in the Stamp
> Law §6 as untested — *"nobody signs an indemnity for speed; you either hit the date or you do not, and
> the buyer can see which."* **It has now been observed in the wild, priced, twice.**

### 3. The winners sell HOURS of work, not MONTHS

A 700–1.000-word eulogy at **$275–515**. A speech at **$345–575**. Vows at **$195–345**.

> **That is what makes $110+ contribution at 30 h/week arithmetically plausible, and it is exactly what a
> 300-page book at $99 can never be.** It also fixes the one key the book always failed: **operator
> leverage.** Two hours per order instead of four, at a similar price.

### And the fourth, which nobody asked for

**A eulogy is a document.** No printing, no supplier, no POD, no shipping, no customs, no tariff, no
delivery estimate, no inventory, no returns on a personalised physical good.

> **If the deliverable is a file, the entire physical supply chain and every constraint attached to it
> disappears — including the red team's most dangerous question, whether Etsy computes delivery estimates
> from São Paulo. That question becomes moot for a product with nothing to ship.**

## 4. The unresolved problem, stated plainly

**Channel.** Speechy charges £290 and The Eulogy Writers $275 **from their own websites**, on SEO and
reputation built over years. This file has already verified that **written deliverables on Fiverr price at
$5–45** — twice, on two unrelated categories. So:

> **The product shape is right, the price is proven off-marketplace, and her channel problem is unsolved.
> She has no site traffic, and the marketplaces she can actually reach price this work at $20.**

That is the real remaining question, and it is a different question from the one this file has been asking
all week. It is also narrower and more testable.

**Second unresolved risk, and it is serious:** a chatbot writes a eulogy for free, and **49% of consumers
say they plan to make DIY gifts.** The only defence on record is that buyers currently reject AI-written
prose, and that is a perception with an expiry date. **Anyone building here is racing a clock.**

**Third:** direct cremation removes the ceremony — and a eulogy is delivered at a ceremony. The cremation
tailwind helps the *memorial artefact* and may **cut** the eulogy occasion. These two findings pull in
opposite directions and the net is unresolved.

## 6. What this means for the board

**The book is not dead, but its ceiling is now measured and it is low.** The category leader with 600
writers ships ~25 books a month. That is the number that should have been found in week one.

**The new shape is short, dated, written-only, and file-delivered.** It clears operator leverage, clears
C1 as a *normal market option*, sits in the price band the recession data says survives, needs no
supplier, and crosses no border.

**The gap agent 9 actually identified, precisely:** *"a book-length written life story between $99 and
$999."* Storyworth stops at $59–99. The next book-length written product is **one solo operator's legacy
book at $450–850**, then StoryTerrace's side-products at $499–749, then Swift at $1.200. **One solo
incumbent and one leader's side-product occupy the entire $150–900 short-book slot.**

**Three agents still running:** agent 1 (card-deck market and saturation — the demand half of candidate
69), agent 6 (death-admin incumbents and whether funeral-home aftercare already has a written-memorial
vendor — now the single most load-bearing open question in the file), and agent 10 (the red team on the
Etsy assumption, which the file-delivery finding may have partly defused). Agent 11 is out on private-party
vetoes.
