# STONEPROOF — assessed, 17 Aug 2026

**Externally supplied. Independent Brazilian quartzite pre-shipment inspection and container release,
buyer-paid. Self-scored 8.9/10 rising to 9.1.**

> **Verdict: the best-researched proposal this file has received, and dead for her on the single law
> it was always going to hit. Inspection IS the stamp business — it is literally the example that
> generated THE STAMP LAW. But it produced one genuinely new law that closes a family of fifteen-plus
> candidates, and four design patterns worth keeping.**

---

## 1. Credit first — the market read is largely correct

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Brazil exported ~$1,48–1,5B natural stone in 2025 | **TRUE.** Centrorochas: ~US$1,5B, **+17,5%** year on year, a record |
| **HTSUS 6802.99.00 quartzite is exempt from the additional tariff** | **TRUE and load-bearing.** Centrorochas confirms the exemption covers *"Worked monumental or building stone, nesoi"* — quartzite — which was **~50% of Brazilian stone exports to the US in 2024**. *(Minor misattribution: the reporting ties it to the 40% IEEPA tariff, not the 25% Section 301)* |
| Quartzite is carrying the sector while granite and marble suffer | **TRUE.** Centrorochas' own headline: *"Natural stone sector grows despite U.S. tariffs, but declines in granite and marble exports raise concerns."* Quartzite growth *"helped sustain the overall result"* |
| Australia banned engineered stone | Consistent with Safe Work Australia's silica programme |
| Generic pre-shipment inspection is cheap | **Partly.** Observed: **$200–500 per visit** standard, **$800–3.000** for a full check, $800+ where NDT or lab sampling is involved. So its $1.195 is *inside* the observed range — fairer than I expected |

**The micro-niche selection is genuinely well done.** Picking quartzite specifically — the one category
that survived the tariff wall and is growing — out of a sector that is otherwise contracting, is the
sharpest piece of market reasoning any supplied proposal has shown here. It also **independently
refines** what this file established about the Brazil tariffs earlier the same day.

**And the reframe is the best single idea in the document:**

> *"Do not own the Brazilian product. Own the trust layer around an expensive Brazilian transaction…
> The real Brazil advantage is no longer buy cheaply in reais and sell in dollars. It becomes: deploy
> trusted Brazilian eyes, Portuguese-language access, local field coverage against an expensive
> information problem faced by foreign buyers."*

**That passes THE INVERSION CHECK, which almost nothing does.** A US importer genuinely cannot see
inside a factory in Cachoeiro de Itapemirim; she genuinely could. **This is the first idea in the
entire file where being in Brazil is the asset rather than the obstacle.**

Which is exactly why the way it fails is worth a law.

## 2. THE PHYSICAL-PRESENCE PARADOX — the new law, and it closes a family

Local access is real arbitrage. But local access is delivered by **a body standing in a specific
place.**

> **THE PHYSICAL-PRESENCE PARADOX — for this founder, the strongest available arbitrage is local
> Brazilian access that foreign buyers lack. But that access is delivered by a body in a place. A
> founder who will not travel, will not speak, and cannot hire has exactly one body, and it cannot
> stand in two factories. Every "Brazilian eyes on the ground" business converts her single best
> asset into her single hardest constraint.**

**What this closes, permanently:** third-party inspection · sourcing agent · factory audit · quality
control · permit or export expediting · site verification · mystery shopping · local representation ·
trade-show proxy · supplier vetting · container loading supervision · in-person document retrieval.
Fifteen-plus candidates, none of which need to be generated again.

**And the reason it cannot be automated away:** the proposal's own architecture proves it. Twelve
workstations, and the one thing none of them can do is *be in the room*. Its own honest admission:
computer vision *"cannot independently determine whether a line is a harmless natural fissure, a
structural crack, an acceptable repair, or genuine geological quartzite."*

## 3. The Stamp Law, third time running

**Inspection is not adjacent to the stamp business. It is the stamp business.** The Stamp Law was
derived from an operator paying **Intertek $33.600**, and the first row of its S-tier table reads:
*"UL / Intertek / ETL testing → The lab. It is the entire product."*

STONEPROOF identifies this correctly and then buys it:

- *"Routes ambiguities to a fractional natural-stone expert"*
- *"Fractional natural-stone technical director"* who *"provides professional credibility"*
- *"Humans retain authority over: material identification, crack-versus-fissure judgments, technical
  acceptability"*
- *"She is not positioned as the technical authority. The visible authority is… the fractional veteran
  reviewer"*
- *"The technical director can handle the small number of calls that truly require technical
  credibility"*

**That is PERMITCLOSE's move, made again, by a different author, in a different industry.** Both
proposals found the Stamp Law by reasoning, named the person who carries the liability, and then put
them on the payroll.

**The test is unchanged:** *name the person who gets sued if this is wrong. If the business only works
when that person is the seller, stop.* Here the answer is printed in the org chart.

**And the pricing data confirms the law with a number.** The stampless residue — *"observed condition
against submitted criteria, not an engineering guarantee"* — prices at **$200–500 a visit.** The stamp
prices at $33.600. **That gap is the Stamp Law, measured.**

## 6. What to pull — five things, and they are real

**1. The independence principle, stated cleanly.**
> *"The foreign buyer — not the Brazilian supplier — pays… That independence is the product."*

**Whoever pays defines whom you serve. If the seller pays for the report, the report is worthless.**
That is the correct structural answer to adverse selection, and it belongs in the permanent filter.

**2. Three-state reporting: verified / not visible / access denied.**
Most reports are binary pass-fail. Distinguishing *"we could not see it"* from *"it is fine"* is what
makes a report honest, defensible, and sellable without a stamp. **Directly transferable to any report
product** — including anything she ever writes for a buyer.

**3. "Observed condition against submitted criteria," not a guarantee.**
This states the **stampless residue more crisply than THE STAMP LAW itself does.** A faceless seller
may report what was observed; they may never warrant what will happen. That is the exact boundary, and
it should be folded back into the law.

**4. The evidence-integrity stack.**
Geofenced live capture · timestamps · device identity · random challenge codes · no gallery uploads ·
duplicate-image detection · OCR of seal and bundle numbers. A genuinely good design pattern for **any**
product whose value depends on the evidence being real and not recycled.

**5. Trust sells at the first transaction with an unknown counterparty.**
Its highest-probability buying situations — first order with a new supplier, first order of a new
material, after a prior dispute, buyer cannot travel — is good trigger thinking and generalises well
beyond stone.

**Plus the factual gain:** quartzite is exempt via **HTSUS 6802.99.00** while granite, marble and slate
are exposed and declining. That sharpens what this file established about the Brazil tariff wall the
same day.

## 7. Closing note, in fairness

**This is a better document than PERMITCLOSE.** Its red team is real. Its kill gates include the three
that actually matter — #5 *buyers consistently consider supplier photographs sufficient*, #7 insurance,
#10 *cannot command a premium over generic inspection labour*. It refuses to claim 9.3. It writes
*"No amount of Grok automation repairs the absence of buyer willingness to pay,"* which is this file's
Gate 0 in one sentence.

It is not a bad proposal. **It is a good proposal for a different founder** — one in Espírito Santo,
with a stone background or the money to rent one, $50k, three hires, and a telephone.

**And a competitive note the proposal underweights:** its own competitor table dismisses generic
inspectors as *"usually not deeply specialized in Brazilian slab stone."* But **SGS runs pre-shipment
inspection for minerals and metals in Brazil and sells container loading supervision as a named
service.** That is the stamp holder, already present, with a global brand and an indemnity policy. The
whitespace is narrower than claimed.
